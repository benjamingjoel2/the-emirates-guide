The paddle dips, the kayak slides forward, and within a few strokes of mangrove kayaking in Abu Dhabi the city simply switches off. Towers that were looming over the launch point disappear behind a wall of glossy green leaves, and the only sounds left are water dripping from the blade and the soft click of crabs in the roots.

Then a heron lifts off from a branch a few metres ahead, unhurried, and lands again further along the channel. This is the mangroves at their best: flat water, slow pace and a surprising amount of life, all within easy reach of the capital.

It is one of the gentlest outdoor trips in the UAE, and one of the best for beginners. You do not need experience, only a guide or a calm day, the right tide and a plan for the sun. Here is where to go, how a paddle actually unfolds, and how to make it go smoothly.

## Why paddle the mangroves at all?

Mangroves are trees and shrubs that grow in salt water, their roots standing in the tide. The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi (EAD) explains that they create [a habitat for biodiversity](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/mangrove-national-park) while protecting against tidal surges, purifying the water around them and removing carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Visit Abu Dhabi adds that mangroves trap carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases [in their flooded soils for millennia](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/jubail-mangrove-park).

From a kayak you see them at eye level. You drift past root systems, under low branches and through channels too shallow and narrow for boats. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests that kayaking is [one of the best ways to discover](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/mangrove-national-park) this landscape, with myriad channels to explore, and says the trees reach up to 15 feet in height.

## Where to go mangrove kayaking in Abu Dhabi

### Mangrove National Park

The main forest lies right on the edge of the city. Visit Abu Dhabi places Mangrove National Park on [the northerly shores of the city, close to Reem Island](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/mangrove-national-park), protected by EAD. EAD describes it as one of Abu Dhabi's key ecological assets and part of its [Sheikh Zayed Protected Areas Network](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/mangrove-national-park).

Trips usually start from the Eastern Mangroves area. EAD mentions [a two-hour kayak tour](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/mangrove-national-park) through the middle of the trees, and notes that private operators run excursions from there. Visit Abu Dhabi adds stand-up paddleboarding and boat tours, led by [licensed and ecologically responsible tour operators](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/mangrove-national-park), and says you can choose to go with a guide or on your own. If you are unsure of the channels, follow the guide.

Not paddling? Visit Abu Dhabi says you can walk the [public boardwalk at Mangrove National Park for free](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/eastern-mangroves), while private operators charge for activities such as guided kayak tours. The promenade nearby has cafés and shops, and there is a children's playground at Dolphin Park, which makes the area easy to combine with a family morning.

### Jubail Mangrove Park

On Jubail Island, between Yas Island and Saadiyat Island, Jubail Mangrove Park combines kayaking with boardwalks. Visit Abu Dhabi says the park is famous for boardwalks [floating just above the water's surface](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/jubail-mangrove-park), that kayaking is its most popular activity, and that there is a special night glow tour. It recommends you book ahead through the park's website, says the journey from downtown by taxi is around 30 minutes, and puts the boardwalk at 2km long.

The park's own page lists [a 1.5-hour guided sunrise kayaking tour](https://jubailisland.ae/jubail-mangrove-park/) and a one-hour guided single-kayak tour, both on sit-on-top kayaks, along with a 20-minute electric-car tour for anyone who prefers to stay dry. Visit Abu Dhabi's Jubail Island page says advance booking is [required for kayaking](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/jubail-island), with sunrise, sunset and night options, and describes the park's paths as stroller-friendly.

### Which one suits you?

- **Mangrove National Park** is closer to the city centre and has the larger forest. It suits a quick early-morning paddle before a day of sightseeing, and gives you the choice of several operators.
- **Jubail Mangrove Park** is more self-contained, with boardwalks, set tour times and an electric-car option. It suits mixed groups where not everyone wants to paddle, and families with small children.

### Other paddles

Visit Abu Dhabi's kayaking page also suggests paddling [beneath the dome of Louvre Abu Dhabi](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/water-activities/kayaking) or along remote island shores. If you are spending time on Saadiyat, our [Saadiyat Cultural District guide](/stories/saadiyat-cultural-district-guide/) shows how a paddle fits into a museum day.

## Getting to the launch points

Eastern Mangroves is close to the centre. Visit Abu Dhabi says the drive from downtown takes [less than 15 minutes](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/eastern-mangroves), with direct access from Sheikh Zayed Bin Sultan Street (E10) and parking at the marina and along the promenade. It also lists the free Experience Abu Dhabi Shuttle Bus, whose Route C stops at Eastern Mangroves, and public Bus 56 for nearby stops.

Jubail Island is a little further out. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [30 minutes by car from downtown](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/jubail-island), connected by a bridge, with on-site parking, and that Route D of the free shuttle bus links the airport and Yas Island to Jubail Mangrove Park. Shuttle routes and timetables change, so check them before you rely on one for an early start.

If you are only passing through, Visit Abu Dhabi even lists a [morning layover trip to the mangroves](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/morning-layover-trip-to-the-mangroves) for transit passengers, with a kayak through the Eastern Mangroves.

## How a guided paddle unfolds

Most guided trips follow a similar pattern. You arrive a little before the start time, sign a form, leave anything you do not need in the car or a locker, and are fitted with a life jacket. The guide gives a short briefing on holding the paddle, getting in and out, and what to do if you tip over, which is rare on flat water.

Then you launch, usually from a beach or low pontoon, and spend the first few minutes in open water getting used to steering. Once everyone is comfortable, the group heads into the narrower channels. Guides tend to stop every so often to point out roots, crabs or birds, and to let slower paddlers catch up. You turn back at a set point and return the same way, or loop round on a falling tide. The paddling itself is gentle; the effort is in keeping a steady rhythm and steering around bends.

Expect to get a little wet. Water drips off the paddle into your lap, and sit-on-top kayaks are open to splashes.

## Timing your paddle: tides, light and season

### Go with the tide

This matters more than people expect. Visit Abu Dhabi says [the best time to kayak is at high tide](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/mangrove-national-park), and warns that the channels can be difficult to manoeuvre in some seasons. At low tide you may find yourself scraping mud or stepping out to drag the kayak. A good operator will schedule around the tide tables rather than a fixed clock.

Its Jubail Island page describes the difference well: at high tide the mangroves are [partially submerged, their branches brushing the surface](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/jubail-island), while at low tide the forest floor emerges, revealing tangled roots and tidal pools. Both are worth seeing, but high water is far easier to paddle; low water is better seen from a boardwalk.

### Pick the cool hours

Sunrise and late afternoon are the sweet spots: softer light, calmer air and birds that are more active. The mangroves themselves, EAD notes, are [there 24 hours a day](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/mangrove-national-park) and easy to see in daylight. Night tours are an option at Jubail if you want something different.

## What you might see

Keep your voice low and your paddle quiet, and the mangroves reward you.

- **Crabs** scuttling over the mud and roots. EAD mentions the mottled crab.
- **Wading birds** such as the [greater flamingo and western reef heron](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/mangrove-national-park), which EAD lists among the park's species.
- **Fish** darting in the shallows, and sometimes turtles. Visit Abu Dhabi says the forest is home to [crabs, turtles, herons, flamingos and fish](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/mangrove-national-park), alongside larger creatures such as foxes, dugongs and dolphins, with more than 60 bird species to look out for depending on the season.
- **Gazelles** on Jubail Island, which Visit Abu Dhabi lists alongside turtles and herons among the park's residents.

Sightings are never guaranteed. Bring binoculars if you have them, and a waterproof bag for your phone. Our guide to [birdwatching in the UAE](/stories/birdwatching-in-the-uae/) has more on where and when to look.

### Photography from a kayak

A phone in a waterproof pouch on a lanyard is the simplest option. Keep it tethered; anything dropped in the mud is usually gone. Shoot when the kayak has stopped drifting, and let birds come to you rather than paddling straight at them. At Jubail, the park's page notes that personal photoshoots with professional-grade cameras [carry a fee](https://jubailisland.ae/jubail-mangrove-park/), and that commercial filming needs approval in advance.

## Paddling gently in a protected place

These are protected habitats, and a kayak is one of the lowest-impact ways to see them. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that kayaking is permitted in many of Abu Dhabi's [marine protected areas](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/water-activities/kayaking), where jet skis are prohibited and human-powered activities are encouraged. It adds that official visitor guidelines for those areas do not allow anchoring, and that vessels must use designated mooring buoys.

Help keep it that way:

- Do not grab or break branches, and avoid stepping on roots.
- Keep well back from birds, especially nesting or feeding flocks.
- Take every scrap of rubbish out with you.
- Stay with your guide in the narrower channels.

For more on the wider network, read our [guide to the UAE's protected areas](/stories/uae-protected-areas-guide/).

## Safety on the water

### Life jackets and briefings

Visit Abu Dhabi says that, while not mandatory for kayaks, wearing a life jacket is [strongly recommended for your safety](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/water-activities/kayaking), and that operators may give a safety briefing first. Wear it the whole time, even on flat water. The UK Foreign Office's advice on sea travel is to [make sure life jackets are available](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) for all passengers, and it notes that the safety of tourist boats may not match UK standards.

If you are not a confident swimmer, say so before you set off, and ask for a double kayak with a stronger paddler.

### Sun and heat

There is little shade on the water, and the sun reflects off it. EAD reminds visitors to [put on plenty of sunscreen and stay hydrated](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/mangrove-national-park). The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather and lists tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea and heavy sweating among the signs of heat exhaustion. In the UAE, [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health).

### What to wear and bring

- Quick-drying clothes that cover your shoulders, plus a hat that will not blow off.
- Water shoes or sandals with a strap; the launch area can be muddy.
- A refillable water bottle and sunglasses with a strap.
- A dry bag for your phone, cards and keys.
- A change of clothes and a towel for the drive back.

## Kayaking with children

Calm water and short tours make the mangroves one of the better first paddles for children. A few points help. Book a double kayak so a child can sit with an adult, check the operator's minimum age and life jacket sizes when you book, and choose a shorter tour for a first go. Jubail's boardwalks and electric-car tour give a fallback if a younger child loses interest, and Eastern Mangroves has a playground nearby for afterwards. Our guide to [three days in Abu Dhabi](/stories/three-days-in-abu-dhabi/) shows how a paddle fits around other family stops.

## What catches people out

- **Booking for the wrong tide.** Ask the operator how the tide looks on your date before you pay.
- **Arriving late.** Early tours leave on time to beat the heat. Allow for parking.
- **Overpacking.** You need water, sun protection and a phone in a pouch. Leave the rest on shore.
- **Forgetting the hands.** Sunscreen on the backs of hands and tops of feet is easy to miss, and paddling keeps them in the sun throughout.
- **Paddling too fast.** The wildlife is in the quiet moments. Slow down.

## Quick answers

**Do I need experience to kayak the mangroves?** No. The water is calm and guided tours include a briefing. Tell your guide if you are a weak swimmer.

**What is the best time to kayak the Abu Dhabi mangroves?** At high tide, according to [Visit Abu Dhabi](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/mangrove-national-park), and ideally early or late in the day to avoid the strongest sun.

**Where can I kayak in the mangroves in Abu Dhabi?** The main options are Mangrove National Park on the edge of the city and Jubail Mangrove Park on Jubail Island.

**Do I have to wear a life jacket?** It is not mandatory for kayaks, but Visit Abu Dhabi strongly recommends one.

**Can I see the mangroves without kayaking?** Yes. The public boardwalk at Mangrove National Park is free to walk, and Jubail Mangrove Park has a 2km boardwalk and an electric-car tour.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is the trip timed to high tide on our date, and what happens if the tide is too low?
- Are life jackets provided and worn for the whole trip, and is there a safety briefing?
- How many kayaks are in each group, and how many guides go out with them?
- Are your guides trained in first aid and water rescue?
- Do you offer double kayaks for children or less confident paddlers?
- What do you do to keep groups away from nesting birds and fragile roots?
