On Ajman Creek, a half-built wooden hull sits in a cradle of timber props, its ribs open to the sky. A craftsman runs a hand along a plank, checks the curve, and reaches for a tool. Across the water, the evening fish auction is about to begin.

That is the Ajman most visitors drive straight past on the way north. This Ajman travel guide is for people who want to stop. The emirate is small enough to see the highlights in a day, and it has a museum in an old fort, a long beach promenade, a working dhow yard and, surprisingly, mangroves and mountain enclaves. It moves more slowly than Dubai, and that is the point.

## Ajman travel guide basics: the emirate at a glance

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ajman) calls Ajman the smallest of the seven emirates, covering about 260 sq km, or roughly 0.3% of the country's area excluding islands. It lies on the Arabian Gulf between Sharjah and Umm Al Quwain, with a coastline the portal puts at 16 km.

The emirate has three parts:

- **Ajman city**, on the coast, with the old fort, souks, creek and corniche.
- **Al Manamah**, about 60 km inland, known for valleys and high mountains.
- **Masfout**, about 110 km to the south-east, known for farming, mountains and wide valleys.

The portal also notes that the Al Nuaimi tribe, who migrated to the region around 1775, formed Ajman, and that it was first recognised as an autonomous state in 1820.

## Ajman Museum, inside the old fort

Begin where the emirate began. The fort's thick walls hold the heat out, and inside the courtyard the noise of the city drops away.

According to the [government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ajman), Ajman Fort dates back to the 18th century and has been restored and turned into a museum displaying archaeological finds and evidence of life through different eras. Visit Ajman adds that the fort [served as the ruler's residence until 1970](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/ajman-museum/) before becoming a museum a decade later.

The collection is worth an unhurried hour. The tourism board describes manuscripts, weapons, model wooden dhows and irrigation systems, a section on the pearl trade, and finds from an excavated cemetery at Al Muwaihat, with [pottery and funeral jewellery dating back as far as 3000 BC](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/ajman-museum/). Displays are labelled in Arabic and English. Check the official page for current opening hours before you set off, especially on a Friday.

## The corniche and the watchtower

Late afternoon is corniche time. The heat eases, families arrive with picnic rugs, and the smell of grilling drifts along the beach.

Visit Ajman describes the [Corniche](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/corniche/) as a four-kilometre esplanade overlooking the Gulf and lined with cafés and casual restaurants, and says much of the emirate's social life is centred here. It mentions a sheltered tidal pool for swimming and notes that the area really comes to life in the evening and at weekends.

Halfway along stands the sandy-coloured [Al Murabbaa Watchtower](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/al-murabbaa-watchtower/), which the tourism board says has guarded the city since the 1930s and was restored in 2000. It is a good place to pause and picture the town when it faced the sea rather than the highway.

If you swim, the Foreign Office advises [swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and warns that rip currents can occur at any beach. Swimwear belongs on the beach: cover up before you walk back into town.

## The creek: dhows, fish and gold

### The dhow yard

This is the stop that tends to surprise people. Visit Ajman says the [Dhow Yard](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/dhow-yard/) remains one of the world's largest dhow-building centres, and that you can watch master craftsmen working with tools and techniques handed down through generations. It sits on the north side of Ajman Creek. Be respectful: this is a workplace, so keep clear of tools and ask before photographing anyone.

### The fish market

Across the water is the [Fish Market](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/fish-market/), where the tourism board says haggling is expected and you can have your fish filleted, spiced and grilled at simple stalls nearby. It mentions a lively auction each evening. Go early, as the same page warns that parking is tricky.

### The gold souq

For browsing, the [Gold Souq](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/shopping/gold-souq/) sits near the traditional souq in the city centre. Visit Ajman explains that gold is sold by weight at international rates, while the workmanship is open to negotiation. Our guide to [Dubai's gold and spice souks](/stories/dubai-gold-and-spice-souks/) has tips on polite bargaining that work here too.

## Al Zorah: mangroves on the edge of the city

North of the centre, the city gives way to still lagoons and green mangroves. Visit Ajman describes [Al Zorah Nature Reserve](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/al-zorah-nature-reserve/) as a mix of mangroves, lagoons and sandy beaches, home to bird species including flamingo, egret and heron. It says the best way to explore is by kayak, and that the calm water and network of channels suit even novice paddlers.

Go early in the day, when the water is glassy and the birds are feeding. Bring a hat and long sleeves; the sun off the water is fierce even in the cooler months.

## The mountain enclaves: Masfout and Manama

Most visitors never realise Ajman has mountains. Its two inland enclaves feel like another country from the corniche.

[Masfout](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/masfout/) sits among the Hajar Mountains near Hatta. Visit Ajman says its higher altitude brings cooler temperatures and that the rocky countryside suits walking, mountain biking, picnics and wadi exploration. The same page carries an important routing note: it says non-GCC nationals should take the Sharjah to Kalba road, because the Dubai to Hatta road passes through an Oman border post that it describes as open to GCC nationals only. Check the current position before you travel.

[Al Manama](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/al-manama/) is a quieter farming enclave at the foot of the mountains, which the tourism board says is known for scenery, wild honey and three historic fortresses.

If you plan to walk, read Visit Ajman's [hiking safety advice](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/masfout/masfout-hiking-routes-safety/) first. It recommends carrying plenty of water and electrolytes, a head torch and a first aid kit, not relying on phone signal for navigation, telling someone your route, and avoiding wadis when thunderstorms are around. Our guide to [hiking in the UAE](/stories/hiking-in-the-uae/) has more.

## Etiquette in Ajman

Ajman is relaxed but traditional. The tourism board's [culture FAQ](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/facts/culture-faq/) says swimwear is fine at the beach or hotel pool, but visitors are asked to dress modestly in public places such as malls and to avoid tight or revealing clothing. It also says public displays of affection are not part of local culture, and that you should avoid photographing military areas, government buildings and sites such as ports, and ask before photographing people. ## Quick answers

**Is Ajman the smallest emirate?** Yes. The UAE government portal describes it as the smallest of the seven, covering about 260 sq km.

**What is the main thing to see in Ajman?** Ajman Museum, inside an 18th-century fort that was the ruler's residence until 1970, is the best place to start. Add the dhow yard and the corniche.

**Can you kayak in Ajman?** Yes. Visit Ajman says kayaking is the best way to explore the mangroves of Al Zorah Nature Reserve, and the calm channels suit beginners.

**What should I wear in Ajman?** Swimwear at the beach or pool is fine, but the tourism board asks visitors to dress modestly in public places such as malls and to avoid tight or revealing clothes.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is the museum open at the time we plan to visit, including on a Friday?
- Can you arrange guided kayaking at Al Zorah early in the morning, with life jackets and sun cover?
- Which route will we take to Masfout, and does it involve any border post we cannot cross?
- Is there a guide who can explain dhow building at the yard, and is it a good day to visit?
- Which part of the corniche is best for swimming, and is there lifeguard cover on our dates?
