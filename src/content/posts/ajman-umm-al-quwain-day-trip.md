By mid-morning you are sitting in a kayak with mangroves on both sides and not a tower in sight. By late afternoon you are walking a beach promenade as families lay out picnics and the sun drops into the Gulf. In between, you have seen a fort, a boatyard and an old town that still smells of the sea.

That is the shape of an Ajman and Umm Al Quwain day trip, and it is one of the easiest ways to see a gentler side of the UAE from Dubai or Sharjah. The two smallest emirates sit side by side on the coast, so you can cover the highlights of both in one unhurried day. The plan below starts in Umm Al Quwain while the water is calm and finishes in Ajman for sunset.

## Why pair Ajman and Umm Al Quwain

They are close, they are quiet, and they complement each other. Ajman brings museums, souks and a lively seafront. Umm Al Quwain brings lagoons, mangroves and a small old town.

The government's emirate guide puts Umm Al Quwain [about 20 minutes' drive north of Ajman](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), so you spend your day exploring rather than driving between them. Start early from Dubai and you can be on the water before the heat builds. The two also share a history. Both grew up around fishing, pearling and boatbuilding on sheltered creeks, and you will see that thread in the museums, the boatyard and the nets along the shore. Seeing them together gives a fuller picture of how the northern coast lived before oil, which neither emirate makes a fuss about but both quietly preserve.

## Before your Ajman and Umm Al Quwain day trip

A few things make the day smoother:

- **Go in the cooler months.** Paddling and walking are far more pleasant then. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you pick dates.
- **Check museum hours.** Look at opening times before you go, especially on a Friday.
- **Pack for water and town.** Swimwear and a hat for the lagoon, plus a modest layer for the old towns.
- **Decide how you will travel.** A hire car gives freedom, but a driver means nobody has to navigate.
- **Bring the right kit.** Water, a hat, sun cream, a dry bag for your phone, shoes that can get wet, and a change of clothes for the afternoon.

### Getting there and getting around

Both emirates sit just up the coast from Sharjah, so the drive from Dubai is mostly city traffic and then an easy coastal run. If you drive yourself, set off before the morning rush and plan your parking: the fish market area in Ajman can be tight, and the kayak launch in Umm Al Quwain is easiest to reach with your own wheels. The Foreign Office advises using [only licensed taxis or other recognised forms of public transport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), including ride-hailing apps, and reminds drivers that drink-driving is illegal at any level. Taxis are far less plentiful in Umm Al Quwain than in Dubai, so a driver who waits for you is the simplest way to link the stops.

## Morning: kayaking the Umm Al Quwain lagoon

Arrive early and the lagoon is glassy, the light is soft, and herons pick through the shallows. This is the best part of the day, so protect it.

Emirates Nature-WWF describes [Mangrove Beach, within Khor Al Yeefrah](https://www.emiratesnaturewwf.ae/en/page/khor-al-yeefrah), as an area of dense mangrove forest and rich biodiversity, with kayaking near lagoon-side beaches. It says the lagoon is the breeding ground for the largest Socotra cormorant colony in the Gulf region, and that thousands of saplings have been planted there in recent years to help the mangroves regenerate.

A guided paddle is the easy option. You will slip along calm channels between the trees, hear crabs clicking in the mud and watch the light change on the water. Keep a respectful distance from birds, do not stand on the roots, and wear a life jacket.

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="ajman-umm-al-quwain-day-trip-1" alts="The sandy, sparsely vegetated shoreline of Al Sinniyah Island in the Umm Al Quwain lagoon" caption="Al Sinniyah, the largest of the lagoon's islands, is best enjoyed from the water."></photo-set>

Out across the water lies Al Sinniyah, the largest of the lagoon's islands. In 2023 the emirate's tourism and archaeology department announced the discovery there of what Gulf News reported as [the oldest pearling town yet found in the Arabian Gulf](https://gulfnews.com/uae/arabian-gulfs-oldest-pearling-town-discovered-in-umm-al-quwain-1.94600893), dating from the late 6th to the mid-8th centuries AD and lying near an ancient Christian monastery. Excavation continues and the island is not a visitor site, so enjoy the view from your kayak and let the guide decide where you can go.

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/umm-al-quwain) adds context: it describes the emirate's rich coastal mangroves and islands, notes that the largest island, Al Seniah, is home to Arabian gazelles, falcons and turtles, and says the emirate plans to expand its mangrove cover threefold by 2031.

## Late morning: Umm Al Quwain's old town and fort

Dry off and drive the short distance to the old quarter on the peninsula. The streets are low and quiet, and the fort's walls give welcome shade.

The government's emirate guide says [Umm Al Quwain Fort, once home to the Ruler](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), guarded the entrance to the old town and later became a museum. The same guide describes Umm Al Quwain as the most ancient emirate, with archaeological traces of links to Mesopotamia in the 3rd millennium BC. The museum is the place to see what has been found at sites such as Ed Dur and Tell Abraq. The Ministry of Economy and Tourism describes the [Umm Al Quwain National Museum](https://www.moet.gov.ae/en/-/umm-al-quwain-national-museum) as housed in Al-Ali Fort, one of the emirate's oldest buildings, with artefacts spread through many rooms that show how people here once lived.

Take a slow walk along the waterfront afterwards. Fishing has always mattered here; the [government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/umm-al-quwain) calls it a key contributor to the emirate's economy, and you will see boats and nets along the shore.

## Lunch: a simple, local table

Fish is the obvious choice on this coast. Look for a simple local spot near the water in Umm Al Quwain, or hold on until Ajman, where grilled fish near the creek is part of the experience. Keep it light if there is more paddling or walking ahead. If you are travelling during the cooler months, eating outdoors near the water is part of the pleasure; in warmer weather, look for somewhere air-conditioned and give yourself a proper break before the afternoon. Drink more water than you think you need, especially after a morning in the sun on the lagoon, and keep a bottle in the car for the drive south. Our guide to [Emirati food](/stories/emirati-food-what-to-eat-where/) helps with what to order.

## Early afternoon: Ajman's dhow yard and museum

Head south to Ajman as the day heats up, and move indoors or into the shade.

### The dhow yard

On the north side of Ajman Creek, the [Dhow Yard](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/dhow-yard/) is a working boatyard where, Visit Ajman says, master craftsmen build wooden dhows using tools and techniques passed down through generations. The tourism board calls it one of the world's largest dhow-building centres. Watch quietly, keep away from tools and ask before photographing anyone.

### Ajman Museum

Then step into the cool of the old fort. Visit Ajman says the 18th-century fort [served as the ruler's residence until 1970](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/ajman-museum/) before becoming a museum, and describes manuscripts, weapons, model dhows, a section on the pearl trade, and finds from an excavated cemetery at Al Muwaihat. Check the official page for hours on the day, as they can differ on Fridays.

Dress for both the museum and the souks. The tourism board's [culture FAQ](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/facts/culture-faq/) asks visitors to dress modestly in public places and to avoid tight or revealing clothes. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.

If you have time, the [Gold Souq](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/shopping/gold-souq/) is nearby. Visit Ajman explains that gold is priced by weight at international rates, while the workmanship can be negotiated.

### Saleh Souq and the heritage path

Directly opposite the museum, Visit Ajman describes [Saleh Souq](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/shopping/saleh-souq/) as a traditional market with a palm-frond roof, timber beams and a coral-stone floor, known for tailoring, textiles and small gifts. It says the souq sits on a heritage path linking the museum with the corniche, so you can walk from one to the other as the afternoon cools. Bargaining is customary, and the tourism board notes that the souq is liveliest in the evening.

Just a few minutes away, [Rashid Al Khadar House](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/rashid-al-khadar-house/) is the restored home of the Nabati poet Rashid bin Salem Al Suwaidi, whose verses, according to Visit Ajman, have become proverbs and are still sung by Gulf artists. It is a short, quiet stop that adds another layer to the day.

## Late afternoon to sunset: the Ajman Corniche

Finish where the locals do. As the heat fades, the [Corniche](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/corniche/) fills with families, walkers and the smell of barbecues. Visit Ajman describes it as a four-kilometre esplanade overlooking the Gulf, lined with cafés and restaurants, and says it really comes to life in the evening.

Stop at the [Al Murabbaa Watchtower](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/al-murabbaa-watchtower/), which the tourism board says has guarded the city since the 1930s. Then find a spot on the sand and watch the sun go down.

If you prefer something livelier, the [Fish Market](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/fish-market/) holds an auction each evening, according to Visit Ajman, and you can have fish grilled at simple stalls nearby.

If you want a swim, the Foreign Office advises [swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), since rip currents can occur at any beach. Cover up again before you leave the sand.

## Travelling with children

This route suits families well, as long as you keep the pace gentle. A guided double kayak works for older children, while younger ones often enjoy crab and bird spotting from the shore more than sitting in a boat. Both fort museums are compact, shaded and easy to explore, and the corniche has space to run around in the evening. Pack snacks and plenty of water, and plan a proper rest after lunch.

## What tends to catch visitors out

- **Starting late.** By late morning the lagoon is hotter and busier, and you lose the best of the day.
- **Forgetting about Fridays.** Visit Ajman's own museum page shows shorter Friday hours in places, so check both museums the day before.
- **Walking into town in swimwear.** Change before the museums and souqs.
- **Photographing people without asking.** At the dhow yard, the fish market and the souqs, a quick question first is expected.
- **Trying to do too much.** Two small emirates still deserve an unhurried day. Drop a stop rather than rushing.

### During Ramadan

If your trip falls in Ramadan, the Ajman tourism board's [culture FAQ](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/facts/culture-faq/) says the town is quieter, most restaurants outside hotels close during the day, and not eating in public is a courtesy to people who are fasting. Plan lunch at a hotel or carry snacks to eat discreetly in the car, and expect the corniche and souqs to come alive after sunset.

## Changing the order

The plan works in reverse too, but you lose the calm morning water. Two alternatives:

- **Mangroves closer to Ajman.** If time is short, Visit Ajman says the calm channels of [Al Zorah Nature Reserve](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/al-zorah-nature-reserve/) suit even novice paddlers. You could kayak there and skip the drive north.
- **Camel racing in season.** Visit Ajman says racing at [Al Tallah Camel Racecourse](https://visit-ajman.ae/en/destinations/ajman-city/al-tallah-camel-racecourse/) runs from October to March, usually early in the morning and in the afternoon on Fridays and Saturdays. On a race day you could start there instead of the lagoon. Our guide to [watching camel racing](/stories/camel-racing-uae-guide/) explains what to expect.
- **Stay the night.** An overnight in Umm Al Quwain puts you on the lagoon at dawn and makes the drive on to Ras Al Khaimah easy the next day.

## Quick answers

**Can you visit Ajman and Umm Al Quwain in one day?** Yes. The UAE government's emirate guide puts Umm Al Quwain about 20 minutes' drive north of Ajman, so the highlights of both fit comfortably into a day.

**What is the best thing to do in Umm Al Quwain on a day trip?** Kayaking among the mangroves. Emirates Nature-WWF describes kayaking near lagoon-side beaches at Mangrove Beach, within Khor Al Yeefrah.

**Where should I watch the sunset in Ajman?** The Corniche, which Visit Ajman describes as a four-kilometre esplanade overlooking the Gulf that comes to life in the evening.

**What should I wear for the day?** Swimwear for the lagoon or beach only, and modest clothes for the old towns, museum and souks, as Ajman's tourism board asks.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What time should we start from our hotel to reach the lagoon while the water is calm?
- Is the kayaking guided, with life jackets, and does the route keep clear of bird colonies?
- Will the Umm Al Quwain fort museum and Ajman Museum both be open on our date, including if it is a Friday?
- Can we visit the dhow yard with someone who can explain the boatbuilding?
- Would you suggest Al Zorah instead of Umm Al Quwain for kayaking if our time is short?
- Can the driver wait for sunset on the corniche and take us back afterwards?
