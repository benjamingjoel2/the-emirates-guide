Before the city is properly awake, the camel market on the edge of Al Ain is already loud, and a good Al Ain day trip starts right here. Animals shift and groan in their pens, buyers walk slowly along the rails, and the bargaining is done in Arabic, in low voices, with a great deal of patience.

By mid-morning that scene has gone, and you are walking under a roof of date palms while water runs past your feet in a channel older than most cathedrals. That is the rhythm of an Al Ain day trip: an early start, a quiet middle, and a mountain at the end of the day. It is the easiest way to see a completely different side of the UAE without packing a bag.

Yes, it works as a day trip. Visit Abu Dhabi puts Al Ain [90 minutes' drive from Abu Dhabi city](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and says the drives from Abu Dhabi or Dubai on the E22 or E66 take [approximately 1.5 to 2 hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain). The trick is to arrive early and give the day a clear order.

## Why Al Ain feels like another country

The coast is glass towers and sea breeze. Al Ain is low, green and inland, set against the Hajar Mountains on the Omani border. Visit Abu Dhabi calls it [the Garden City](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) and notes that this is where Sheikh Zayed, the UAE's founding father, was born and raised. The same guide describes the emirate's second city as ringed by date palms and [ruins going back 5,000 years](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

It also holds the country's UNESCO World Heritage listing, the Cultural Sites of Al Ain, which takes in oases, Bronze Age tombs and early settlements. Visit Abu Dhabi describes Al Ain as [one of the oldest continuously inhabited places](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and its Al Jahili Fort page counts [more than 40 forts, palaces and historical buildings](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/al-jahili-fort) in the city. You will not see all of it in one day, and you do not need to. The plan below picks the pieces that fit together well and leaves room to sit in the shade.

If you would rather go slower, our guide to [staying overnight in Al Ain](/stories/staying-overnight-in-al-ain/) shows what a second day adds.

## The Al Ain day trip, stop by stop

### Early: the camel market

The serious trading at the camel market happens early. Visit Abu Dhabi's own day plan suggests you [be there by 8 AM](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) and says the market wraps up mid-morning, so make it your first stop. It calls this the largest camel market in the UAE, with the real business done [before 9 AM](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

One detail catches people out. Visit Abu Dhabi's family itinerary for Al Ain says visits to the camel market [need to be pre-planned and supervised by market staff](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain). Do not simply turn up and wander between the pens. Arrange the visit through your driver, guide or hotel, and check the current arrangements a day or two before you go.

This is a working market, not a show. Stand back from the animals, keep out of the way of trucks and handlers, and ask before you photograph anyone. A polite greeting goes a long way, and so does patience: if a handler waves you away from a pen, there is usually a good reason.

### Mid-morning: into the oasis

From the market, head for Al Ain Oasis in the middle of the city. Walk in and the light changes at once: the palms close overhead, the air cools a little, and the noise of traffic falls away. Visit Abu Dhabi says the oasis has been farmed for [at least 3,000 years](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), and that around 147,000 date palms make a canopy thick enough to block the sun.

What makes it special is the falaj, the network of channels that carries water from the mountains to the farms by gravity alone. Visit Abu Dhabi says [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), that you can explore on foot, by rented bicycle or by golf buggy, and that the free Eco-Centre has interactive exhibits on the oasis's history. The same page warns that the water [can disappear and reappear depending on the season](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), so do not be disappointed if a channel is dry. It also notes that [benches line the pathways](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), which helps if anyone in the group needs regular rests.

Wear shoes you can walk in, and keep to the paths. These are farmed plots, not a garden laid out for visitors. Start at the Eco-Centre if you want the background first, then walk until you find a junction where a channel splits and the water is sent into one plot and not the next. That small sight explains the whole oasis. Our longer guide to [Al Ain Oasis and the UNESCO sites](/stories/al-ain-oasis-unesco-sites/) goes deeper into the history.

If you are travelling with very young children or older relatives, there is a gentler alternative. Visit Abu Dhabi's family day swaps in Al Jimi Oasis, where it says quiet, shaded paths make the walk manageable [for everyone, from toddlers to grandparents](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain).

### Late morning: Al Jahili Fort

A short drive away, Al Jahili Fort is all mudbrick, lime plaster and a calm central courtyard. Visit Abu Dhabi says it was built [in the 1890s](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/al-jahili-fort) for members of the ruling Al Nahyan family, and that [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/al-jahili-fort).

Abu Dhabi Culture fills in the story. It says work on the fort [began in 1891 and was completed in 1898](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-jahili-fort), and that Abu Dhabi's rulers left the humid coast for Al Ain in summer, using the fort as a summer residence. In the early 1950s British forces made it part of their regional headquarters. Look for the separate round tower of four rising, narrowing tiers, and the majlis in one corner where the sheikh received guests.

Inside is the reason to linger: a permanent exhibition of photographs by the explorer Wilfred Thesiger, who crossed the Empty Quarter twice in the late 1940s with local companions. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests allowing [about an hour](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). Abu Dhabi Culture offers complimentary guided tours of [approximately 45 minutes](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-jahili-fort) in Arabic and English, subject to availability. The pictures of pre-oil Arabia make a striking contrast with the drive you have just done on a modern highway.

### Lunch and the heat of the day

Eat somewhere cool and take your time. In the warmer months this is not wasted time; it is how you get through the afternoon. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, drink more cold drinks and wear light, loose clothing.

### Early afternoon: a museum or a palace

You have two good indoor choices, and both are close to the oasis.

- **Al Ain Museum** sits east of the oasis. Abu Dhabi Culture describes it as the UAE's [first purpose-built museum](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-ain-museum), founded in 1969, with stories running from the Palaeolithic era to the 20th century and galleries built around archaeological remains left where they were found. At its heart is Sultan Fort, an early 20th-century building. The same page lists gallery hours as [daily 9 AM to 7 PM](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-ain-museum), with different hours in Ramadan. Tickets are booked through the site, so check before you go.
- **Qasr Al Muwaiji** is a restored mudbrick fort with a glass exhibition pavilion in its courtyard, telling the story of the ruling family in Al Ain. Visit Abu Dhabi says [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/qasr-al-muwaiji), guided or multimedia tours are available, and most visitors need around an hour. It adds that the fort was the residence of Sheikh Zayed [from 1946](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/qasr-al-muwaiji), and that his son, the late President Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan, was born in its north-west tower in 1948.

How to choose? The museum gives you the long view, from prehistory onwards, and helps the tombs and oases make sense. Qasr Al Muwaiji is shorter and more personal, a story of one family and one building. If you are planning to see Hili or the Jebel Hafit tombs later, the museum is the better primer.

If you have children with you, Visit Abu Dhabi notes that Hili Archaeological Park has [playgrounds alongside the archaeology](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), which can make a better afternoon than another gallery. The park sits [10 kilometres outside the city on the road to Dubai](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/hili-archaeological-garden), and Visit Abu Dhabi says the site provides the earliest known evidence of an agricultural village in the UAE.

### Late afternoon: up Jebel Hafit

Save the mountain for last. Jebel Hafit rises from the desert south of the city and is, according to Visit Abu Dhabi, [Abu Dhabi's highest peak](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), at 1,249 metres. The Jebel Hafit Mountain Road to the top is described as an [11.7km fully paved, well-lit highway](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), and the Al Ain guide puts the drive to the summit at [around 20 minutes](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

Pull in at the viewpoints as the light drops. The city spreads out below, the green of the oases shows clearly against the sand, and the ridges turn rust and then grey. Visit Abu Dhabi's family itinerary notes that the road is drivable, so the whole group can take in the views [without needing to walk any of it](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain), and that sunset is generally considered the best time to go.

At the foot of the mountain, Green Mubazzarah has [natural hot springs, grass and picnic areas](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) that fill with families at weekends. For the full story of the mountain, the desert park and its tombs, see our [Jebel Hafit guide](/stories/jebel-hafit-guide/).

## Three ways to shape the day

The stop-by-stop plan above suits most first-timers, but it is worth adjusting to your group.

**The heritage day.** Camel market, Al Ain Oasis, Al Jahili Fort, Al Ain Museum and Qasr Al Muwaiji, with Jebel Hafit only if there is light left. This is the most walking under a roof and the least time in the car, and it works well in the warmer months.

**The family day.** Camel market early, Al Jimi Oasis instead of the main oasis, Al Jahili Fort, then Hili Archaeological Park for the playgrounds, finishing with the drive up Jebel Hafit. Another option is Al Ain Zoo, which Visit Abu Dhabi says is home to [over 4,000 animals](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/al-ain-zoo), including the Arabian oryx. Be realistic, though: the same page recommends [a full day](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/al-ain-zoo) for the zoo, says weekends are busier, and notes that outside food and drink are not permitted. It is a different day trip rather than a stop on this one.

**The late start.** If you cannot face a dawn departure, skip the camel market, start at the oasis around mid-morning, and keep everything else in order. You lose the most atmospheric hour, but the rest of the day still holds together.

## Getting there and getting around

A car gives you the most freedom, because the stops are spread across the city and up the mountain. Visit Abu Dhabi says Al Ain has [a reliable public bus service](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), but many visitors opt for taxis or car rentals, and that major landmarks are well signposted. Its family itinerary is blunter: because the stops are spread out, a car or driver is [the most practical way](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain) to move between them in a single day. Guided tours are another option if you would rather not drive.

If you are hiring a car, remember the mountain road has bends and steep sections. Take it slowly, especially on the way down in fading light, and use a lower gear rather than riding the brakes. If you have driven from Dubai, factor the return journey into your energy as well as your timings; the drive home after sunset is often the most tiring part of the day. Our guide to [driving in the UAE as a visitor](/stories/driving-in-the-uae-as-a-visitor/) covers licences, road rules and what to do after an accident.

## When to go

Visit Abu Dhabi calls Al Ain a year-round destination, but says [October to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) brings the cooler days that suit outdoor sightseeing. In the warmer months it suggests shade in the oasis and the city's museums. Its Al Ain guide narrows the most comfortable window to [November to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), when it says days sit around 22 to 28°C. Rain is rare, but Visit Abu Dhabi notes it is [more common in Al Ain](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) than elsewhere in the emirate because of the nearby Hajar Mountains, so glance at the forecast before you set off.

In summer, flip the day: oasis and market first thing, indoor stops through the middle, mountain at dusk. Know the warning signs. The NHS lists tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, heavy sweating and strong thirst among the [symptoms of heat exhaustion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. In the UAE, the UK Foreign Office says to [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health).

## What catches people out

- **Arriving late at the market.** Leaving Dubai or Abu Dhabi after breakfast means you reach the pens as trading ends. If the market matters to you, leave early or stay the night before.
- **Trying to do everything.** Al Ain has more than enough for two days. Five or six stops, with a long lunch, is a full and pleasant day.
- **Forgetting that heritage sites have hours.** The forts and museums keep set opening hours, and Ramadan hours differ. Check the official pages the day before.
- **Underestimating the oasis.** It is easy to get turned around under the palms. Note where you came in, and keep a map open on your phone.
- **Leaving the mountain too late.** Sunset views need light. Aim to be on the mountain road well before the sun drops, not as it does.

## Small things that make the day easier

- Dress modestly for the forts, museums and market: shoulders and knees covered is a safe rule. Our guide on [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more detail.
- Carry water and a hat, even in the cooler months.
- Keep cash and a card; smaller places may prefer one or the other.
- Check opening times on the official pages the day before, especially around public holidays and in Ramadan.
- Pack a light layer for the summit and for air-conditioned museums.

On accessibility, the paved oasis paths, the benches and the drivable mountain road make much of this day possible for travellers who cannot walk far. The forts have uneven floors, steps and thresholds, so ask at each site what can be reached. ## Quick answers

**Can you do Al Ain as a day trip from Abu Dhabi or Dubai?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says the drive from either city takes [approximately 1.5 to 2 hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and its own suggested day covers the camel market, the oasis, Al Jahili Fort and Hili Park.

**Is Al Ain Oasis free?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), with bicycles, golf buggies and private tours at extra cost.

**What time should I get to the camel market?** Early. Trading wraps up mid-morning, so aim to [be there by 8 AM](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), and arrange the visit in advance.

**Can you drive up Jebel Hafit?** Yes, on a paved, lit mountain road to the summit. Go late in the afternoon for the light.

**When is the best time to visit Al Ain?** Visit Abu Dhabi recommends [October to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) for outdoor sightseeing.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How is the camel market visit arranged, what time will we arrive, and how long do we have before trading winds down?
- Is the driver comfortable on the Jebel Hafit mountain road after dark, and will we stop at the viewpoints?
- Which museum or palace fits our group best, and are any closed or on special hours on our date?
- Can the day be reordered for heat, with outdoor stops early and late?
- Is the vehicle shared or private, and is water provided on board?
- If we want to stay the night, can you change the plan without starting from scratch?
