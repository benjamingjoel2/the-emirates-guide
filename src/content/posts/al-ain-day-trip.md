Before the city is properly awake, the camel market on the edge of Al Ain is already loud, and a good Al Ain day trip starts right here. Animals shift and groan in their pens, buyers walk slowly along the rails, and the bargaining is done in Arabic, in low voices, with a great deal of patience.

By mid-morning that scene has gone, and you are walking under a roof of date palms while water runs past your feet in a channel older than most cathedrals. That is the rhythm of an Al Ain day trip: an early start, a quiet middle, and a mountain at the end of the day. It is the easiest way to see a completely different side of the UAE without packing a bag.

Yes, it works as a day trip. Visit Abu Dhabi puts Al Ain [90 minutes' drive from Abu Dhabi city](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and says the drives from Abu Dhabi or Dubai on the E22 or E66 take [approximately 1.5 to 2 hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain). The trick is to arrive early and give the day a clear order.

## Why Al Ain feels like another country

The coast is glass towers and sea breeze. Al Ain is low, green and inland, set against the Hajar Mountains on the Omani border. Visit Abu Dhabi calls it [the Garden City](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) and notes that this is where Sheikh Zayed, the UAE's founding father, was born and raised.

It also holds the country's UNESCO World Heritage listing, the Cultural Sites of Al Ain, which takes in oases, Bronze Age tombs and early settlements. You will not see all of it in one day, and you do not need to. The plan below picks the pieces that fit together well and leaves room to sit in the shade.

If you would rather go slower, our guide to [staying overnight in Al Ain](/stories/staying-overnight-in-al-ain/) shows what a second day adds.

## The Al Ain day trip, stop by stop

### Early: the camel market

The serious trading at the camel market happens early. Visit Abu Dhabi's own day plan suggests you [be there by 8 AM](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) and says the market wraps up mid-morning, so make it your first stop.

This is a working market, not a show. Stand back from the animals, keep out of the way of trucks and handlers, and ask before you photograph anyone. A polite greeting goes a long way.

### Mid-morning: into the oasis

From the market, head for Al Ain Oasis in the middle of the city. Walk in and the light changes at once: the palms close overhead, the air cools a little, and the noise of traffic falls away.

What makes it special is the falaj, the network of channels that carries water from the mountains to the farms. Visit Abu Dhabi says [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), that you can explore on foot, by rented bicycle or by golf buggy, and that the free Eco-Centre has interactive exhibits on the oasis's history. The same page warns that the water [can disappear and reappear depending on the season](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), so do not be disappointed if a channel is dry.

Wear shoes you can walk in, and keep to the paths. These are farmed plots, not a garden laid out for visitors. Our longer guide to [Al Ain Oasis and the UNESCO sites](/stories/al-ain-oasis-unesco-sites/) goes deeper into the history.

### Late morning: Al Jahili Fort

A short drive away, Al Jahili Fort is all mudbrick, lime plaster and a calm central courtyard. Visit Abu Dhabi says it was built [in the 1890s](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/al-jahili-fort) for members of the ruling Al Nahyan family, and that [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/al-jahili-fort).

Inside is the reason to linger: a permanent exhibition of photographs by the explorer Wilfred Thesiger, who crossed the Empty Quarter with local companions. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests allowing [about an hour](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). The pictures of pre-oil Arabia make a striking contrast with the drive you have just done on a modern highway.

### Lunch and the heat of the day

Eat somewhere cool and take your time. In the warmer months this is not wasted time; it is how you get through the afternoon. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, drink more cold drinks and wear light, loose clothing.

### Early afternoon: a museum or a palace

You have two good indoor choices, and both are close to the oasis.

- **Al Ain Museum** sits east of the oasis. Abu Dhabi Culture describes it as the UAE's [first purpose-built museum](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-ain-museum), founded in 1969, with stories running from the Palaeolithic era to the 20th century and galleries built around archaeological remains left where they were found. The same page lists gallery hours as [daily 9 AM to 7 PM](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-ain-museum), with different hours in Ramadan. Tickets are booked through the site, so check before you go.
- **Qasr Al Muwaiji** is a restored mudbrick fort with a glass exhibition pavilion in its courtyard, telling the story of the ruling family in Al Ain. Visit Abu Dhabi says [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/qasr-al-muwaiji), guided or multimedia tours are available, and most visitors need around an hour.

If you have children with you, Visit Abu Dhabi notes that Hili Archaeological Park has [playgrounds alongside the archaeology](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), which can make a better afternoon than another gallery.

### Late afternoon: up Jebel Hafit

Save the mountain for last. Jebel Hafit rises from the desert south of the city and is, according to Visit Abu Dhabi, [Abu Dhabi's highest peak](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit). The Jebel Hafit Mountain Road to the top is described as an [11.7km fully paved, well-lit highway](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit).

Pull in at the viewpoints as the light drops. The city spreads out below, the green of the oases shows clearly against the sand, and the ridges turn rust and then grey. At the foot of the mountain, Green Mubazzarah has [natural hot springs, grass and picnic areas](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) that fill with families at weekends. For the full story of the mountain, see our [Jebel Hafit guide](/stories/jebel-hafit-guide/).

## Getting there and getting around

A car gives you the most freedom, because the stops are spread across the city and up the mountain. Visit Abu Dhabi says Al Ain has [a reliable public bus service](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), but many visitors opt for taxis or car rentals, and that major landmarks are well signposted. Guided tours are another option if you would rather not drive.

If you are hiring a car, remember the mountain road has bends and steep sections. Take it slowly, especially on the way down in fading light.

## When to go

Visit Abu Dhabi calls Al Ain a year-round destination, but says [October to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) brings the cooler days that suit outdoor sightseeing. In the warmer months it suggests shade in the oasis and the city's museums. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you weigh up the seasons.

In summer, flip the day: oasis and market first thing, indoor stops through the middle, mountain at dusk. Know the warning signs. The NHS lists tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, heavy sweating and strong thirst among the [symptoms of heat exhaustion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. In the UAE, the UK Foreign Office says to [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health).

## Small things that make the day easier

- Dress modestly for the forts, museums and market: shoulders and knees covered is a safe rule. Our guide on [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more detail.
- Carry water and a hat, even in the cooler months.
- Keep cash and a card; smaller places may prefer one or the other.
- Check opening times on the official pages the day before, especially around public holidays and in Ramadan.

## Quick answers

**Can you do Al Ain as a day trip from Abu Dhabi or Dubai?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says the drive from either city takes [approximately 1.5 to 2 hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and its own suggested day covers the camel market, the oasis, Al Jahili Fort and Hili Park.

**Is Al Ain Oasis free?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), with bicycles, golf buggies and private tours at extra cost.

**What time should I get to the camel market?** Early. Trading wraps up mid-morning, so aim to [be there by 8 AM](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

**Can you drive up Jebel Hafit?** Yes, on a paved, lit mountain road to the summit. Go late in the afternoon for the light.

**When is the best time to visit Al Ain?** Visit Abu Dhabi recommends [October to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) for outdoor sightseeing.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What time will we reach the camel market, and how long do we have before trading winds down?
- Is the driver comfortable on the Jebel Hafit mountain road after dark, and will we stop at the viewpoints?
- Which museum or palace fits our group best, and are any closed or on special hours on our date?
- Can the day be reordered for heat, with outdoor stops early and late?
- Is the vehicle shared or private, and is water provided on board?
- If we want to stay the night, can you change the plan without starting from scratch?
