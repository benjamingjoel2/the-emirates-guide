Step off the street into Al Ain Oasis and the first thing you notice is the sound. Traffic fades, palm fronds rustle overhead, and somewhere close by water is moving through a narrow stone channel at your feet.

That water is the whole story. It is why people settled here, why the palms grow, and why the oases and ancient sites around this inland city carry a UNESCO World Heritage listing. Walking Al Ain Oasis is free, shaded and easy, and it is the best way into the Cultural Sites of Al Ain. Add a Bronze Age tomb, one of the smaller oases and a museum, and you have a day that explains how people lived in this desert long before oil.

Here is how the pieces fit together, what to look for on the ground, and how to see them on foot.

## What the UNESCO Cultural Sites of Al Ain include

The listing is not one site but several. Visit Abu Dhabi explains that UNESCO inscribed the Cultural Sites of Al Ain [in 2011](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), covering Hafit, Hili, Bidaa Bint Saud and the oases. Together, it says, they record one of the oldest continuously inhabited landscapes on the Arabian Peninsula.

The oases themselves are a group, too. Abu Dhabi Culture describes [six UNESCO-listed oases](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases) in Al Ain, with Al Ain Oasis the largest of them. It also notes that the UN's Food and Agriculture Organisation has recognised the oases as a store of genetic resources, biodiversity and Emirati heritage.

In practice, think of three layers:

- **The oases**, where palms, fruit trees and old earthen buildings sit around working water channels.
- **Hili**, a Bronze Age landscape of tombs and settlements on the north side of the city.
- **Hafit**, the mountain to the south, with its beehive-shaped tombs.

We do not cover Bidaa Bint Saud, the fourth component, in detail here; this guide concentrates on the three you can easily combine in a day.

## Walking Al Ain Oasis

### Following the water

The water that feeds the oasis comes from wells and from the falaj system, which Abu Dhabi Culture describes as [a complex network of underground and surface channels](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases) bringing water from the mountains to the farms. Two main channels serve the oasis, Al Aini and Al Dawoodi, each watering a different part, and both start some distance to the south-east, towards the Hajar Mountains. Visit Abu Dhabi adds that the falaj carries the water [by gravity alone, no pumps](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), and has run in the same form for thousands of years.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="al-ain-oasis-unesco-sites-1" alts="A1" caption="Palms, fruit trees and water channels share the ground in Al Ain Oasis."></photo-set>

As you walk, look for the places where a channel splits, where small sluices send water into one plot and not the next. Farmers still tend more than 147,000 date palms of [more than 100 varieties](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases), along with fruit trees such as citrus, bananas, guava and figs, and historic boundary walls still separate the plots, according to Abu Dhabi Culture.

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that the water [can disappear and reappear depending on the season](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis). A dry channel is part of how the system works, not a sign that it has stopped. The same page says the oasis was [inhabited almost 4,000 years ago](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), and calls it the largest oasis in the UAE, followed by Liwa.

### What to look for on the ground

Once you know the vocabulary, the oasis reads like a map.

- **The channel itself.** Open stretches of falaj run beside the paths, often lined with stone or plaster. Follow one and you will see how carefully the water is shared out.
- **The point where the water surfaces.** Abu Dhabi Culture calls this the shariaa, where the underground supply [comes to the surface](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases) and is used for drinking and irrigation. It was valuable enough that towers were built to guard it.
- **The walls.** Low earthen walls mark out individual plots. They are part of the heritage, so do not sit or climb on them.
- **The layers of planting.** Palms on top, fruit trees beneath, and sometimes vegetables at ground level. The shade of each layer protects the one below.

### Practicalities for the walk

- Entry is [completely free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), and so is the Eco-Centre, which uses interactive exhibits to explain the oasis's history.
- You can walk, rent a bicycle or use a golf buggy. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests you could spend [up to 2 hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis) roaming, and benches line the pathways if you need a rest.
- Abu Dhabi Culture lists visiting hours for the oases of [9:00 am to 7:00 pm](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases), with free entry and paid tours.
- Go in the morning for [cooler air and quieter paths](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).
- Stay on the paths. The plots are private farmland, and the walls and channels are part of the heritage.

## Beyond the main oasis

Most visitors see only Al Ain Oasis. The smaller oases are quieter and show more of the buildings that once guarded the water.

### Hili Oasis

Visit Abu Dhabi calls Hili [the northernmost oasis in the Al Ain district](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-hili-oasis), covering 60 hectares with about 40,000 mature palms across 252 farmed plots. Abu Dhabi Culture describes two watchtowers at Hili that together formed [a protective gateway to Hili village](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases) and guarded its water. One is square, built at the command of Sheikh Zayed and set on a man-made earth mound; the other, about 50 metres away, is round, 7.4 metres high and probably 19th century. At the centre of the oasis stands the Bin Hadi Al Darmaki House, a fortified tower house of the early 1800s with a large square tower at one corner of its enclosure.

### Jimi and Qattara

Jimi Oasis sits in a sunken basin of palm gardens, with more than a dozen historic earthen buildings, including mosques, fortified houses and watchtowers. Abu Dhabi Culture singles out the late 19th-century fortified residence of Sheikh Ahmad bin Hilal Al Dhaheri, and the round Jimi Watchtower to the south, [14 metres tall](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases), which protected the point where the falaj surfaced. It is also the oasis Visit Abu Dhabi suggests for mixed-age groups, with quiet, shaded paths manageable [for everyone, from toddlers to grandparents](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain).

Qattara Oasis is described as [rich in history](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases), with 19 historic earthen buildings dating from the mid-18th to the early 20th century, among them ancient mosques, fortified houses and a traditional souq. The souq dates back to the 1930s and is a covered corridor of around 35 metres with shops on either side, built with thick mud walls and small openings for light and air. To the east is a Bronze Age tomb, dated to 2000 to 1000 BCE, where excavations found jewellery, weapons, stone vessels and pottery.

Next to the oasis, Al Qattara Arts Centre occupies a redeveloped mud brick tower house and keeps archaeological finds on permanent display in [the Qattara Archaeological Basement](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/culture-centers/al-qattara-arts-centre). It makes a cool indoor stop between walks.

### Al Muwaiji

Qasr Al Muwaiji stands beside Al Muwaiji Oasis, which Visit Abu Dhabi calls [the smallest and most westerly of Al Ain's historic oases](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/qasr-al-muwaiji), with some 21,000 palms still watered by falaj. The restored fort is free to enter and makes a natural pairing with a short oasis walk. Abu Dhabi Culture notes that from 1946 to around 1960 the fort was [the home and diwan of Sheikh Zayed](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/al-ain-oases), the meeting place where community affairs were handled.

### Which oasis suits you?

- **First visit, short on time:** Al Ain Oasis, with the Eco-Centre.
- **Architecture and towers:** Hili or Jimi, for the watchtowers and fortified houses.
- **Archaeology and a market building:** Qattara, with the arts centre's basement.
- **A museum and a stroll:** Al Muwaiji, with the fort.

## Hili: tombs from the Bronze Age

Hili Archaeological Park is the easiest way to stand in front of something truly ancient. Visit Abu Dhabi describes it as a Bronze Age settlement centred on [the Grand Tomb, its carved figures still visible on the stone](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), and says entry is free and it is rarely busy. It calls Hili one of the few Bronze Age sites in the Gulf where you can walk right up to the stonework.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="al-ain-oasis-unesco-sites-2" alts="A2" caption="The Grand Tomb at Hili Archaeological Park dates from the Bronze Age."></photo-set>

Why does Hili matter? Visit Abu Dhabi says the site provides [the earliest known evidence of an agricultural village](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/hili-archaeological-garden) in the UAE, and places the park 10 kilometres outside the city on the road to Dubai. Abu Dhabi Culture explains that discoveries at the nearby Hili 8 site show people [starting to settle and practise agriculture](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park) in Al Ain, growing wheat, barley and date palms. The centre of the community seems to have been around Hili, with large tombs and mudbrick forts. In the same period, it says, the region produced copper, which became a major export, and trade with southern Mesopotamia became very important.

Walk slowly around the Grand Tomb and look for the figures cut into the stone near the entrances. Then picture the same ground with fields and palms around it. The park is also a public garden with playgrounds, which makes it one of the easier heritage stops with children.

## Hafit: the tombs on the mountain

To the south, Jebel Hafit holds the oldest layer. Visit Abu Dhabi says the mountain is home to [more than 500 ancient tombs](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), and that you can see the Jebel Hafit Tombs on a guided hike through Jebel Hafit Desert Park. It adds that the tombs were excavated in 1959 at the request of Sheikh Zayed.

Abu Dhabi Culture quotes UNESCO's own description of the Hafit sites as [exceptional testimony to the development of successive prehistoric cultures](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park) in a desert region, from the Neolithic to the Iron Age. It explains that Neolithic herders came to the mountain for hard rock to shape into tools and arrowheads, and that in the Iron Age, when falaj irrigation developed in the area, people reused the Bronze Age tombs for burials.

Reaching the tombs takes a car and a short scramble, so wear proper shoes. Visit Abu Dhabi lists the desert park's day hours as [8:00 AM to 6:00 PM](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/jebel-hafit-desert-park). Our [Jebel Hafit guide](/stories/jebel-hafit-guide/) covers the mountain road and the desert park.

## Put it in context: the museums

A museum visit ties the outdoor sites together. Al Ain Museum, east of the oasis, was founded by Sheikh Zayed and is described by Abu Dhabi Culture as [the UAE's first purpose-built museum](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-ain-museum). Its new galleries are built around archaeological remains left exactly where they were found, three of them dating from the Iron Age to the Late Pre-Islamic period. Tickets are booked online, and Ramadan hours differ, so check the page before you go.

Al Ain Palace Museum, once a residence of the ruling family, has been [closed for renovation](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-ain-palace-museum) according to Abu Dhabi Culture. Check whether it has reopened before building your day around it.

## A walking day that works

1. **Morning:** Al Ain Oasis and the Eco-Centre, while the air is cool.
2. **Late morning:** Al Ain Museum, for the background.
3. **Lunch and rest** through the hottest hours.
4. **Afternoon:** Hili Archaeological Park, then Hili Oasis and its watchtowers.
5. **Late afternoon:** up Jebel Hafit for the view, if you have a car.

For a fuller plan with the camel market and forts, see our [Al Ain day trip](/stories/al-ain-day-trip/). If you want to see more than one or two oases, a night in town helps; our guide to [staying overnight in Al Ain](/stories/staying-overnight-in-al-ain/) explains how.

### If you have two days

Spread it out. Give the first morning to Al Ain Oasis and the museum, and the first afternoon to Hili. On the second day, walk Jimi or Qattara early, visit Qasr Al Muwaiji and its oasis late in the morning, and keep the desert park and the mountain for the afternoon. You will see four of the six oases without ever feeling rushed.

## Season, heat and respect

Visit Abu Dhabi says [October to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) brings the cooler days best suited to outdoor exploring, while the oasis shade and the museums help in the warmer months. In hot weather the NHS advises you to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), drink more cold drinks and wear light, loose clothing.

Dress modestly, keep your voice down near homes and mosques in the oases, and ask before photographing people. Our guide on [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has the detail.

### Etiquette in a working oasis

- Do not pick dates or fruit, even ones that look fallen. They belong to the farmers.
- Keep out of plots unless a guide invites you in, and close any gate you pass through.
- Do not touch sluices or block channels; the water schedule matters to every plot downstream.
- If you meet someone working, a greeting is welcome; questions are often welcome too, but take the hint if they are busy.

If you want to understand what grows here, our guide to [dates in the UAE](/stories/uae-dates-guide/) explains the varieties and the harvest.

### Families and accessibility

The main paths in Al Ain Oasis are shaded and have benches along the way, and the golf buggies help if walking far is difficult. The smaller oases have narrower, rougher paths. The Hafit tombs involve a scramble over rock and are not suitable for anyone unsteady on their feet. Hili Archaeological Park, with its open paths and playgrounds, is the easiest of the ancient sites for children. For more on the emirate, browse our [Abu Dhabi destination page](/destinations/abu-dhabi/).

## Quick answers

**What are the UNESCO Cultural Sites of Al Ain?** A World Heritage listing [inscribed in 2011](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) covering Hafit, Hili, Bidaa Bint Saud and the oases of Al Ain.

**Is Al Ain Oasis free to visit?** Yes. Entry and the Eco-Centre are free; bicycles, buggies and private tours cost extra.

**What is a falaj?** A system of underground and surface channels that brings water from the mountains to the farms. Al Ain Oasis is served by two main ones, Al Aini and Al Dawoodi.

**How long do I need for Al Ain Oasis?** Visit Abu Dhabi suggests [up to 2 hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis) for the oasis itself; allow a full day to add Hili, a museum and Jebel Hafit.

**Which smaller oasis should I add?** Hili or Jimi for watchtowers and fortified houses, Qattara for its old souq and archaeology, or Al Muwaiji if you are visiting the fort.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which of the six listed oases will we walk, and will the guide explain how the falaj works on the ground?
- Is Al Ain Palace Museum open on our date, and if not, what do you suggest instead?
- Can the guide take us to the Hili watchtowers and the Bin Hadi Al Darmaki House, not just the main oasis?
- How much walking is involved, and is there shade and water along the way?
- Are the Jebel Hafit Tombs part of the plan, and is the scramble suitable for our group?
