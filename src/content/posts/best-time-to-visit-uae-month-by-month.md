The one thing people who know the UAE never leave at home is a jumper. In January they pull it on in the desert after sunset; in August they need it for the cinema.

That small contradiction tells you most of what you need to know about timing a trip here. The UAE is a year-round destination, but the month you choose decides what you can comfortably do outdoors, how you shape each day around the sun, and whether you arrive during Ramadan, which moves through the calendar from year to year.

Picture the same corniche park in two different seasons. In winter, as the sun drops behind the towers, everyone comes outside: families spread rugs on the grass, runners take the beach path, and the smell of grilled meat drifts from a souk lane. In August the same spot is quiet at midday, the city's life has moved into cool malls and museums, and the evening walk starts much later. Neither is wrong; they are simply different trips.

Temperatures below come from official sources linked inline. Treat them as averages, not promises, and check a live forecast before you fly.

## Coast, desert and mountains: how the climate works

The UAE government describes the country as having [a desert climate](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/fact-sheet): warm and sunny in winter, hot and humid in summer, and generally cooler in the eastern mountains. That matters if you are heading for Ras Al Khaimah's peaks or the Hajar range around Fujairah and Hatta, where the air can feel noticeably fresher than on the coast.

Visit Abu Dhabi, the emirate's official tourism board, says the [cooler months, November to April, are the most pleasant time to visit](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), with temperatures around 24°C during the day and 13°C at night. The same page says rainfall is sporadic and falls mainly between November and March, and that rain is more common around the oasis city of Al Ain because it sits close to the Hajar mountains.

At the other end of the year, Visit Abu Dhabi's [summer guide](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) describes intense heat and relatively high humidity from June to September, especially along the coast, with average daytime highs from 38°C in June to 41°C in August.

These figures are for Abu Dhabi. Dubai and the northern coastal emirates follow a broadly similar pattern, but we have not found official monthly figures for each emirate to quote, so we describe them in general terms below.

## The year, one month at a time

### January: say yes to everything outdoors

The heart of the cool season: bright, clean light, shade that actually feels cool, and days that are warm rather than hot. It is the easiest month to say yes to everything outdoors: a long wander through the old creek-side districts of Dubai, a full day at Al Ain Oasis, a hike in the mountains. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that [beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in winter](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi).

The catch comes after dark. Evenings can be cool enough for a jacket, particularly in the desert and the mountains, where the drop after sunset catches people out. Keep that layer in your day bag.

Occasional rain is possible. It rarely lasts long, but when it falls heavily, roads can flood (more on that below).

### February: the classic trip, with one date to check

Much like January: comfortable days, cool nights and the same busy feel at popular sights. If you want the classic outdoor UAE trip, desert camp evenings and mountain viewpoints included, February is one of the safest bets.

Before you book, check Ramadan. The [UAE government portal explains](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan) that the Islamic calendar has 354 days, so Ramadan arrives about 11 days earlier each year in the Gregorian calendar. According to [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/10/06/ramadan-2027-uae-dates-eid-al-fitr-holiday/), the UAE's Astronomy Centre has projected that Ramadan 2027 will begin on 8 February 2027, with Eid Al Fitr expected on 9 or 10 March 2027. These are projections only: the dates are confirmed by moon sighting.

That is no reason to change your plans, but read our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/): it changes the rhythm of the day in ways many visitors end up enjoying.

### March: winter's last weeks

Visit Abu Dhabi places the end of winter in mid-March, after which [spring sees temperatures start to rise](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), with sporadic rainfall in March. Early March still feels like winter. By the end of the month the midday sun has more bite, and you feel it on the back of your neck as soon as you leave the shade. Put outdoor sightseeing in the morning and late afternoon.

The same source describes spring as a busy season for cultural events and trade shows in the capital, so check what is on before you book accommodation.

### April: long lunches and golden evenings

April is the last month in Visit Abu Dhabi's "most pleasant" window. Mornings and evenings are lovely, beach days are easy, and the sea is warming up. Middays are noticeably warmer than in winter, so build in a long, shaded lunch or an afternoon in a museum, then head back out as the light softens and turns gold over the water.

### May: the day splits in two

May is a shoulder month that leans towards summer. Visit Abu Dhabi says [summertime starts in late May](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). From here on, the day divides neatly: outdoors early and late, indoors in the middle. If desert or mountain activities are on your list, ask your operator how they adjust timings at this point in the year.

### June and July: living by the clock

Step out of an air-conditioned lobby and your sunglasses fog over. That is summer here. Visit Abu Dhabi's [summer guide](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) gives average daytime highs of 38°C in June and says the hottest month is generally July or August. Add coastal humidity, and this is heat to respect rather than test.

The country is built for it. Malls, museums, galleries and indoor attractions are air-conditioned, and the same Visit Abu Dhabi guide suggests heading out to the beach or the desert in the evenings as temperatures drop. A summer trip works best if you accept the shape of the day: a slow morning, indoor sights at midday, and life outdoors after dark.

The small habits that make the biggest difference:

- Carry water everywhere, and refill whenever you get the chance.
- Wear a hat, even for short hops between the car and the door.
- Never plan a long walk in the afternoon sun.
- Book outdoor activities for the evening and keep the daytime flexible.

### August: the peak of the heat

Visit Abu Dhabi gives an [average daytime high of 41°C in August](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer), the top of its range. Choose August only if your dates are fixed or indoor attractions are the main point of your trip. If you do come, base yourself somewhere with easy access to indoor sights, keep outdoor activities short and save them for the evening.

### September: summer with the door left ajar

Summer runs on into September, with Visit Abu Dhabi placing it [from late May to September](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), and the same page describing autumn as starting in September as the summer heat begins to subside. In practice, early September still feels like summer, and the change is gradual. Treat it as a summer trip with slightly better odds towards the end of the month.

### October: the year turns

Visit Abu Dhabi describes [autumn's milder weather calling for beach outings and watersports](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). The heat is easing, the sea is still warm, and outdoor plans become realistic again, especially in the second half of the month. For a beach-focused trip it is a strong choice: you get warm water without the hardest of the summer sun.

### November: the season opens

November opens the [cooler months](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) and, for many people who know the UAE, it is one of the most enjoyable times to visit. The outdoor season is beginning, evenings on the waterfront are a pleasure, and the desert starts to feel inviting again. It also opens the main rainfall window, which runs until March, so keep an eye on forecasts.

### December: flags, festivities and full diaries

Visit Abu Dhabi dates [winter from December to mid-March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). December brings comfortable days, cool evenings and a festive, busy atmosphere. The UAE's National Day falls on 2 December, and the government lists [2 and 3 December as public holidays](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays). Expect celebrations, flags on buildings and cars, and busier roads and attractions around those days.

The festive season and school holidays in many countries also make the second half of December a popular time to travel. Book early.

## When the rain does come

Rain is not common, but when it arrives it can be heavy. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking weather forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en) and following advice from local authorities.

If a heavy rain warning is issued during your stay, be ready to change plans, particularly for wadi trips, mountain drives and desert excursions. Leave a little slack in your itinerary in the wetter months so a lost day does not unravel the rest. A good operator will make the call for you.

## Ramadan, Eid and the dates that move

Islamic holidays follow the lunar calendar, which is why they travel through the Gregorian year. The UAE government notes that [Islamic holidays are determined according to moon sighting](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays), and that Eid Al Fitr runs from 1 to 3 Shawwal, the month after Ramadan. Because Ramadan [arrives about 11 days earlier each year](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan), it drifts backwards through the seasons. With Ramadan 2027 projected to start in February, it will keep falling in the cooler months for the next few years, so check the dates whenever you plan a winter trip.

Travelling during Ramadan is entirely possible, and many visitors find the evenings, with iftar gatherings and late-night streets, a highlight. Daytime life is quieter, though, and there are rules of public conduct to respect. Our [Ramadan guide](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains what to expect.

## So which month is yours?

- **Outdoor days, desert and mountains:** November to March, with the coolest weather from December to February.
- **Beach time with fewer crowds than midwinter:** late October and November, or April.
- **Indoor sights, lower demand and long warm evenings:** June to September, if you are comfortable with serious heat.
- **A quieter, more reflective trip:** Ramadan, whenever it falls, provided you are happy to adapt.

Whatever you choose, pack for the air conditioning as well as the outdoors. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests [taking a jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), because indoor spaces can be cold. That is the jumper from the top of this page, and it earns its place in every month.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How do you adjust the timing of desert, mountain and boat trips for the month I am travelling?
- If heavy rain or a weather warning is issued, will you move, refund or cancel my outdoor activities, and who makes that call?
- Does my travel window overlap with Ramadan, Eid or National Day, and how will that affect opening hours and crowds?
- Which of the activities I have planned are realistic in summer heat, and what indoor alternatives would you suggest?
- How cold does it get in the desert or mountains at night in my month, and what should I bring?
