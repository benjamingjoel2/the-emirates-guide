There is a particular moment in a UAE winter that people who know the country wait for. The sun drops behind the towers or the dunes, the air loses its edge, and suddenly everyone is outside: families spreading rugs on the grass of a corniche park, runners on the beach path, the smell of grilled meat drifting from a souk lane. You reach for the light jumper you nearly left at home. Come back to the same spot in August and the scene is different: the streets are quiet in the middle of the day, the life of the city has moved indoors into cool malls and museums, and the evening walk starts much later.

Neither version is wrong. The UAE is a year-round destination, but the month you choose shapes almost everything: what you can comfortably do outdoors, how you plan each day around the sun, and whether your trip overlaps with Ramadan, which moves through the calendar from year to year.

This guide takes the year month by month. Where we give temperatures, they come from official tourism and government sources linked inline. Treat them as averages, not promises, and check a live forecast before you go.

## The UAE climate in brief

The UAE government describes the country as having [a desert climate](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/fact-sheet): warm and sunny in winter, hot and humid in summer, and generally cooler in the eastern mountains. That last point matters if you are heading to Ras Al Khaimah's peaks or the Hajar range around Fujairah and Hatta, where the air can feel noticeably fresher than on the coast.

Visit Abu Dhabi, the emirate's official tourism board, says the [cooler months, November to April, are the most pleasant time to visit](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), with temperatures around 24°C during the day and 13°C at night. The same page says rainfall is sporadic and falls mainly between November and March, and that rain is more common around the oasis city of Al Ain because it sits close to the Hajar mountains.

At the other end of the year, Visit Abu Dhabi's [summer guide](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) describes intense heat and relatively high humidity from June to September, especially along the coast, with average daytime highs from 38°C in June to 41°C in August.

These figures are for Abu Dhabi. Dubai and the northern coastal emirates follow a broadly similar pattern, but we have not found official monthly figures for each emirate to quote, so we describe them in general terms below.

## Month by month

### January

This is the heart of the cool season, and it feels like it. Days are warm and bright rather than hot, which makes it the easiest month to say yes to everything outdoors: a long walk through the old creek-side districts of Dubai, a full day at Al Ain Oasis, a hike in the mountains. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that [beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in winter](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). Evenings can be cool enough for a jacket, particularly in the desert and in the mountains, where the drop after sunset catches people out.

Occasional rain is possible. It rarely lasts long, but when it does fall heavily, roads can flood (see the note on rain further down).

### February

Much like January, with the same comfortable days and cool nights, and the same busy feel at popular sights. If you want the classic outdoor UAE trip, desert camp evenings and mountain viewpoints included, February is one of the safest bets.

One thing to check: Ramadan. The [UAE government portal explains](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan) that the Islamic calendar has 354 days, so Ramadan arrives about 11 days earlier each year in the Gregorian calendar. According to [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/10/06/ramadan-2027-uae-dates-eid-al-fitr-holiday/), the UAE's Astronomy Centre has projected that Ramadan 2027 will begin on 8 February 2027, with Eid Al Fitr expected on 9 or 10 March 2027. These are projections only: the dates are confirmed by moon sighting. Read our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) before you book, because it changes the rhythm of the day in ways that many visitors end up enjoying.

### March

Visit Abu Dhabi places the end of winter in mid-March, after which [spring sees temperatures start to rise](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), with sporadic rainfall in March. Early March still feels like winter; by the end of the month the midday sun has more bite and it is worth planning outdoor sightseeing for the morning and late afternoon. The same source describes spring as a busy season for cultural events and trade shows in the capital, so check what is on when you are booking accommodation.

### April

The last of the months Visit Abu Dhabi includes in its "most pleasant" window. Mornings and evenings are lovely, beach days are easy, and the sea is warming up. Middays are noticeably warmer than in winter, so this is a good month to build in a long, shaded lunch or an afternoon in a museum, then head back out as the light softens.

### May

May is a shoulder month that leans towards summer. Visit Abu Dhabi says [summertime starts in late May](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). Expect to shift your outdoor plans to early morning and evening, and to spend the middle of the day indoors. If you are thinking about desert or mountain activities, ask your operator how they adjust timings at this point in the year.

### June and July

Full summer. Visit Abu Dhabi's [summer guide](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) gives average daytime highs of 38°C in June and says the hottest month is generally July or August. Add coastal humidity and the heat is not something to underestimate: stepping outside from an air-conditioned lobby can fog your sunglasses.

The country is built for this. Malls, museums, galleries and indoor attractions are air-conditioned, and the same Visit Abu Dhabi guide suggests heading out to the beach or the desert in the evenings as temperatures drop. A summer trip works best if you accept the shape of the day: a slow morning, indoor sights at midday, and life outdoors after dark. Carry water, wear a hat, and do not plan long walks in the afternoon sun.

### August

Visit Abu Dhabi gives an [average daytime high of 41°C in August](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer), the peak of its range. It is the month to choose only if your dates are fixed or indoor attractions are the main point of your trip. If you do come, base yourself somewhere with easy access to indoor sights, keep outdoor activities short, and plan them for the evening.

### September

Summer runs on into September, with Visit Abu Dhabi placing it [from late May to September](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), and the same page describing autumn as starting in September as the summer heat begins to subside. In practice, early September still feels like summer, and the change is gradual. Treat it like a summer trip with slightly better odds towards the end of the month.

### October

October is the turning point. Visit Abu Dhabi describes [autumn's milder weather calling for beach outings and watersports](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). The heat is easing, the sea is still warm, and outdoor plans become realistic again, especially in the second half of the month. It is a good month for beach-focused trips.

### November

November opens the [cooler months](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) and, for many people who know the UAE, it is one of the most enjoyable times to visit: the outdoor season is beginning, evenings on the waterfront are a pleasure, and the desert starts to feel inviting again. It also opens the main rainfall window, which runs until March, so keep an eye on forecasts.

### December

Visit Abu Dhabi dates [winter from December to mid-March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). December brings comfortable days, cool evenings and a festive, busy atmosphere. The UAE's National Day falls on 2 December, and the government lists [2 and 3 December as public holidays](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays). Expect celebrations, flags on buildings and cars, and busier roads and attractions around those days. The festive season and school holidays in many countries also make the second half of December a popular time to travel, so book early.

## A word on rain and flooding

Rain is not common, but when it comes it can be heavy. The UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking weather forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en) and following advice from local authorities. If a heavy rain warning is issued during your stay, be ready to change plans, particularly for wadi trips, mountain drives and desert excursions. A good operator will make that call for you.

## Ramadan, Eid and other moving dates

Islamic holidays follow the lunar calendar, which is why they move through the Gregorian year. The UAE government notes that [Islamic holidays are determined according to moon sighting](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays), and that Eid Al Fitr runs from 1 to 3 Shawwal, the month after Ramadan. Because Ramadan [arrives about 11 days earlier each year](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan), it drifts backwards through the seasons. With Ramadan 2027 projected to start in February, it will keep falling in the cooler months for the next few years, so check the dates whenever you plan a winter trip.

Travelling during Ramadan is entirely possible, and many visitors find the evenings, with iftar gatherings and late-night streets, a highlight. But daytime life is quieter and there are rules of public conduct to respect. Our [Ramadan guide](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains what to expect.

## So when should you go?

- **For outdoor days, desert and mountains:** November to March, with the coolest weather from December to February.
- **For beach time with fewer crowds than midwinter:** late October and November, or April.
- **For indoor sights, lower demand and long warm evenings:** June to September, if you are comfortable with serious heat.
- **For a quieter, more reflective trip:** Ramadan, whenever it falls, provided you are happy to adapt.

Whatever you choose, pack for air conditioning as well as the outdoors. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests [taking a jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), because indoor spaces can be cold. For clothing norms more generally, see [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/), and for ideas on how to use the cooler months, our [five-day itinerary](/stories/five-day-uae-itinerary/) and guide to [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) are good next reads.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How do you adjust the timing of desert, mountain and boat trips for the month I am travelling?
- If heavy rain or a weather warning is issued, what is your policy on changing or cancelling outdoor activities?
- Does my travel window overlap with Ramadan, Eid or National Day, and how will that affect opening hours and crowds?
- Which of the activities I have planned are realistic in summer heat, and what indoor alternatives would you suggest?
- How cold does it get in the desert or mountains at night in my month, and what should I bring?
