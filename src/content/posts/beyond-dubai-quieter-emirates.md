Drive north out of Dubai early and the city lets go of you slowly. The towers thin out, the billboards give way to low warehouses, and somewhere past Sharjah the horizon fills with a long grey-brown wall of mountains. By mid-morning you can be standing in a palm garden where the only sounds are birds and the trickle of an irrigation channel, with a mud-brick fort on the hill above you.

That is the part of the UAE many visitors never see. The northern emirates and the east coast are not short on things to do, but they move at a different speed. Lunch is grilled fish by a harbour rather than a reservation. Heritage sites are often open-air and unstaffed, so you wander them in near silence. Roads climb into the Hajar range and drop down to a second coastline facing a different sea.

This guide goes deeper on Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah, Umm Al Quwain, Ajman and Sharjah's lands beyond its city centre. If you want the quick overview of all seven first, start with our [first-timer's guide](/stories/uae-first-timers-guide-seven-emirates/).

## Ras Al Khaimah: forts, pearls and a mountain road

### Dhayah Fort

Of everything in the north, this is the climb we would tell a friend to make first. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/dhayah-fort/) describes Dhayah Fort as the only hill fort remaining in the UAE, set on a hill in the middle of an oasis between date gardens and the mountains, and reached by 239 zigzagging steps. From the top the view runs across the palms towards Oman and down to the sea.

What catches people out is the practical side. The same official page notes there is no lighting and the climb is not accessible to prams, wheelchairs or other mobility aids, so go in daylight, wear shoes with grip, and carry water. Morning light on the mud-brick walls is softer and the steps are cooler.

### Al Jazeera Al Hamra

On the Gulf coast lies a place that feels frozen mid-sentence. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-jazeera-al-hamra/) describes Al Jazeera Al Hamra as the only remaining historical pearling village in the Gulf region, built from coral blocks, beach rock and mangrove beams, and left by its residents after oil was discovered. Its name means "Red Island".

Walking through it, you find courtyard houses with their roofs gone, a wind-tower house still showing how the barjeel funnelled breezes indoors, a fort, a mosque and the outline of a souq. The official page notes the site is open-air and unstaffed, so treat it with care: stay off crumbling walls and bring your own shade.

### The mountain and the old port

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) traces Ras Al Khaimah's story back some 7,000 years and recalls Julfar, a medieval port that was one of the prominent trading posts of the Gulf. Today the emirate's most famous road is the one up Jebel Jais. Beyond the viewpoint, [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) lists six designated mountain trails totalling 16 km, for walkers of different experience levels. Pick a short trail for the morning, then come back down for a late lunch near the coast.

## Fujairah: the east coast and its wadis

Crossing the Hajar range to Fujairah takes you from one sea to another. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/fujairah) describes a coastline on the Gulf of Oman running from Fujairah city north to Dibba, backed by rugged mountains, valleys and oases. The light here feels different in the late afternoon, as the sun drops behind the peaks rather than into the water.

### Al Bidya Mosque and the forts

North along the coast road, the small Al Bidya Mosque is worth slowing down for. The [Fujairah Tourism and Antiquities Authority](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-PlacesOfWorship.html) explains that its four close-set domes rest on a single central column, with no timber holding up the roof. Inside, it is cool and dim. Treat it as the place of worship it is: dress modestly and visit quietly, away from prayer times.

Fujairah also has a string of forts. The authority's [museums and forts page](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-MuseumAndForts.html) describes Fujairah Fort, with its three round towers and one square tower, as one of the largest in the emirate, and lists smaller forts such as Al Hail and Al Bithna in the valleys inland. Fujairah Museum has halls on traditional work such as farming, fishing and trade.

### Wadis

The wadis are the real reason many people come. The authority's [natural sites page](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-NaturalSites.html) describes Wadi Al Wurayah as a boulder-strewn valley protected for its flora and fauna, and notes that Wadi Ham, usually dry, fills with flowing water after rain. That last point matters. Rain in the mountains can turn a dry channel into a fast stream quickly, so check the forecast and local advice, and never drive or camp in a wadi bed when rain is possible.

The beaches here are tempting too. The UK government's advice warns that rip currents can occur at any beach and advises swimming only at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags ([FCDO safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security)).

See our [Fujairah page](/destinations/fujairah/). If the coast road north tempts you towards Musandam, read [combining the UAE and Oman](/stories/uae-and-oman-one-trip/).

## Umm Al Quwain: lagoon life

Umm Al Quwain rewards people who like water and quiet. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/umm-al-quwain) describes rich coastal mangroves and a group of islands east of the mainland, and says the emirate plans to greatly expand its mangrove cover. The town itself sits on a narrow peninsula, so the water is never far away.

The best hours are early. Paddle or walk along the lagoon edge while the air is still, watch for wading birds in the shallows, and look out over the low green line of the islands. Later in the day, the waterfront keeps the unhurried feel of a fishing town. It is an easy overnight stop between Dubai and Ras Al Khaimah.

More on our [Umm Al Quwain page](/destinations/umm-al-quwain/).

## Ajman: fort, corniche and inland valleys

Ajman's coast is short, but the emirate has more depth than its size suggests. In town, Ajman Fort has been restored and turned into a museum displaying archaeological finds and objects from daily life, according to the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ajman). It makes a good hour before the evening walk along the corniche, when families come out to the beach and the heat eases.

The surprise is inland. Ajman also governs Manama and Masfout, which the same portal describes as areas of valleys and mountains, with Masfout known for agriculture and for its castle. They are a world away from the coast and work best as part of a mountain drive with a guide or driver who knows the roads.

## Sharjah beyond the city

Most visitors know Sharjah for its museums and souks. Fewer realise how much of the emirate lies inland and on the far coast.

### Mleiha

In the desert of central Sharjah, Mleiha mixes landscape and archaeology. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) places it near Jebel Fayah, 65 km south-east of Sharjah city, and describes a circular Bronze Age Umm an-Nar tomb, an archaeological centre, and Fossil Rock, a huge outcrop full of marine fossils. Activities range from guided site tours and hiking to evenings in the desert.

Mleiha suits people who want a desert day that is about more than the dunes. Go in the cooler months, start early, and pair a site tour with a sunset over the rock formations. Our guide to [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) helps you decide what kind of desert experience you want. The UK government's advice notes that desert excursions can be dangerous without a properly equipped four-wheel-drive and recommends travelling with other cars, water and a phone ([FCDO safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security)), which is one more reason to go with an experienced guide.

### Khorfakkan and Kalba

Sharjah also reaches the Gulf of Oman. [Visit Sharjah's East Coast guide](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/) describes a stretch where mountains meet beaches and mangroves, including the seaside towns of Khorfakkan and Kalba. In Khorfakkan, a Roman-inspired amphitheatre faces the bay beside a 45-metre man-made waterfall cut into natural rock, which [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/khorfakkan-amphitheater-and-waterfall/) notes is often lit up at night.

Further south, Kalba is the gentler choice. The [Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/khor-kalba-mangrove-centre/) sits within what Visit Sharjah calls the oldest mangrove forest in the UAE, and visitors can look for birds such as the Arabian collared kingfisher.

## Making it work

- **Self-drive or driver.** These areas are easiest to explore by car, so most visitors drive or hire a driver. Mountain and wadi roads reward a careful, unhurried pace.
- **Dress for heritage sites.** Forts, villages and mosques are local places. The UK government's advice says women should cover shoulders and knees in public ([FCDO safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security)), and modest dress suits everyone here; our [etiquette guide](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.
- **Mind the season.** Mountain trails and open-air ruins are far more pleasant outside the hottest months. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps.
- **Bring cash and snacks.** Small villages and heritage sites may have few services.
- **Taste as you go.** Harbour-side fish and local bakeries are part of the trip; see our guide to [Emirati food](/stories/emirati-food-what-to-eat-where/).

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you plan a loop through Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah and Sharjah's east coast that avoids long drives in the heat of the day?
- Which wadis are safe to visit on our dates, and how will you check rain and water levels on the day?
- Can you arrange a guide for Dhayah Fort, Al Jazeera Al Hamra or Mleiha who can explain the history on site?
- Which Jebel Jais trail suits our fitness, and what should we carry?
- Which east-coast beaches have lifeguards, and is snorkelling realistic in the conditions?
- Is an overnight in Umm Al Quwain or Kalba worth adding, and what can you arrange there?
