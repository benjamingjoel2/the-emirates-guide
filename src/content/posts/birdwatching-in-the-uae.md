The first thing you notice in the hide is the noise of the city fading. Then, through the slot in the wall, a line of pink shifts on the water, a few hundred flamingos feeding with their heads upside down, while traffic flows past on the other side of the reeds.

Birdwatching in the UAE is easier than most visitors expect. The country sits on a major migration route, and its wetlands and mangroves pull in huge numbers of birds in the cooler months, often within sight of tower blocks. The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi says the UAE welcomes [nearly two million migratory birds every year](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Discover-Our-Biodiversity/Birds), from flamingos and eagles to tiny shorebirds such as the little stint. The short answer to where to go: Ras Al Khor in Dubai, Wasit in Sharjah, the Abu Dhabi mangroves, and Kalba on the east coast, ideally between autumn and spring and early in the day.

## Why birdwatching in the UAE is so rewarding

Three habitats do most of the work. Coastal wetlands and lagoons feed waders and flamingos. Mangroves give herons, kingfishers and crabs shelter. And the Hajar Mountains in the east hold a smaller set of hardy resident species that you will never see on the coast.

The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi also notes [resident and breeding species](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Discover-Our-Biodiversity/Birds) such as Socotra cormorants, crab plovers and sooty falcons, so even summer is not entirely empty. But for sheer numbers, plan around the migration season.

## Dubai: hides in the heart of the city

### Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary

This is the classic first stop. Dubai Municipality describes Ras Al Khor as a wetland of international importance [immersed in one of the world's most urbanised landscapes](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/wildlife/), and says it became the country's first Ramsar site in 2007. In winter, it says, numbers may reach up to 25,000 birds of 201 species. Flamingos are the headline, but the municipality also lists ducks such as pintails and common teals, waders, songbirds and raptors, plus globally threatened species including the Socotra cormorant and ferruginous duck.

You watch from three hides, each with a different view. The municipality's booking page says the [Flamingo Hide offers flamingos all year round](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/book-a-visit/), the Lagoon Hide opens onto the creek for everything from waders to raptors, and the Mangrove Hide is marked as temporarily closed. Check that page before you go.

Families and individuals can walk in during opening hours, the same page says, with walk-in registration, and you may be asked to show ID. Tour companies must pre-book through the municipality's portal.

Inside, the rules are strict and worth knowing. The municipality asks for a [quiet zone with no phones, pets, food, drinks or smoking](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/plan-your-visit/), allows only 15 people in a hide at a time, and warns that approaching or harming wildlife can bring heavy fines.

### Al Marmoom's lakes

Out in the desert of Saih Al Salam, Dubai Municipality has built [eight bird hides at the Al Marmoom reserve](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/book-a-visit/), where lakes sit among the dunes. It is a good choice if you have a car and want space, and you might see gazelles or oryx on the way. Our guide to [where to see the Arabian oryx](/stories/where-to-see-arabian-oryx/) covers the desert side.

### Hatta's dams

In the mountains, the municipality says the Hatta reservoirs attract [grey herons and western reef herons](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/wildlife/) along their banks, and that the Hajar range is a stronghold for birds such as the sand partridge, desert lark and Hume's wheatear. Combine a walk with a kayak; our [Hatta guide](/stories/hatta-guide/) has the details.

## Abu Dhabi: flamingos and mangroves

### Al Wathba Wetland Reserve

On paper, this is one of the region's great wetlands. The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi says it was declared a [Ramsar site in 2013](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Al-Wathba-Wetland-Reserve), hosts as many as 4,000 greater flamingos in autumn and spring and more than 250 bird species, and has two self-guided trails and a flamingo hide. Its birds page adds that Al Wathba is [the only site in the Arabian Gulf where the greater flamingo breeds regularly](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Discover-Our-Biodiversity/Birds).

The catch: the agency's visitor page says the reserve is [temporarily closed for development until further notice](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Al-Wathba-Wetland-Reserve). Check before you plan around it.

### The city mangroves

Where Al Wathba is closed, the mangroves are wide open. The agency says the Mangrove National Park on the edge of the city holds [hundreds of bird and plant species](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Mangrove-National-Park), including the greater flamingo and western reef heron, and that a kayak tour takes you through the middle of the trees. A paddle at a slow pace, with the tide right, is one of the calmest ways to watch herons feed. Our guide to [kayaking the Abu Dhabi mangroves](/stories/abu-dhabi-mangrove-kayaking/) explains the options.

At Jubail Mangrove Park, Visit Abu Dhabi describes [boardwalks through the mangroves](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/jubail-mangrove-park) where you can see herons, turtles and gazelles, and advises booking ahead through the park's website. It is the easiest option for families.

### Further out

For dedicated birders, the Marawah Marine Biosphere Reserve in Al Dhafra is said by Visit Abu Dhabi to host [70% of the UAE's migratory birds](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marine-protected-areas), reached on specialised eco-tours. It is a serious day out, not a casual stop.

## Sharjah and the east coast

### Wasit Nature Reserve

Inside Sharjah city, Wasit mixes coastal dunes, salt flats, ponds and a lake. Visit Sharjah says it has [over 200 bird species and eight hides with binoculars](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/wasit-nature-reserve/), and that it is closed on Sundays. It is the easiest wetland to reach without a car from Dubai or Sharjah.

### Kalba's mangroves

On the Gulf of Oman, the mangroves at Kalba are the real prize for birders. Visit Sharjah calls them [the oldest mangrove forest in the Emirates](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/khor-kalba-mangrove-centre/), and says the Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre is a place to watch shore birds such as the rare Arabian collared kingfisher. At the nearby Al Qurm Protected Area, it suggests [guided kayak and paddleboard tours](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/al-qurm-protected-area/), or simply binoculars from the shore. Our guide to [Kalba and its mangroves](/stories/kalba-mangroves-guide/) helps with planning.

The UAE government portal lists ten Ramsar wetlands of international importance, including [Al Zorah in Ajman and the Kalba mangroves](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/environment-and-energy/Nature-and-wildlife/topography-and-ecosystems), which is a useful checklist if you want to go further.

## When to go, and what to pack

The migration months, roughly autumn to spring, bring the numbers, and they are also the comfortable months to be outside. Within any day, the first hours after sunrise are best: the light is soft, birds are feeding and the heat has not built. For more on the seasons, see our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/).

Pack light:

- Binoculars, and a field guide or app for Arabian birds.
- Water and a hat, even in winter.
- Neutral clothing and patience in the hides.
- A long lens if you photograph, but read the rules below first.

## Binoculars, cameras and the law

Birding gear can raise eyebrows here. The UK Foreign Office warns that hobbies like [bird watching and plane spotting may be misunderstood](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), particularly near military sites, government buildings and airports, and says you may need a licence for equipment such as powerful cameras or binoculars. It also says not to photograph people without their permission. Keep your lens on the birds, stay in public reserves, and if in doubt, ask. ## Quick answers

**Where is the best birdwatching in Dubai?** Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary, where Dubai Municipality says winter numbers can reach 25,000 birds of 201 species, viewed from public hides.

**Can you see flamingos in the UAE all year?** Dubai Municipality says the Ras Al Khor Flamingo Hide has flamingos all year round. Al Wathba's large flocks gather in autumn and spring, but that reserve is temporarily closed.

**Do you need to book the Ras Al Khor hides?** Families and individuals can walk in with registration; tour companies must pre-book.

**Can I bring binoculars?** Wasit provides them in its hides. If you bring your own, the UK Foreign Office says powerful binoculars may need a licence and that birdwatching near military sites or airports can be misunderstood.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which hides and reserves are open on our dates, and have any closed for works?
- What time do you start, and how does the tide affect a mangrove kayak?
- Is your guide a birder who can identify species by call, and do you provide binoculars?
- How close do you take boats or kayaks to feeding or roosting birds?
- Are there any photography or equipment restrictions where we are going?
- Can you combine wetlands and mangroves in one day without long drives in the heat?
