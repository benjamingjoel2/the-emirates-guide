The message pops up on your phone within minutes of your enquiry: a smiling emoji, a photo of a sunset camp, a price that seems almost too good and a nudge to pay now before the last seats go. It is late, you are tired from the flight, and it would be so easy to tap the link.

Stop there. Most tour operators in the UAE are professional and well run, and booking a desert safari, dhow cruise or city tour is usually straightforward. But the gap between a good operator and a poor one is wide, especially for activities where safety matters: dune driving, boats, quad bikes and long road trips.

So how do you book a UAE tour operator safely? Check who you are actually dealing with, ask about safety in plain terms, get everything in writing, pay in a way that leaves a record, and make sure your insurance covers what you are doing. Here is how to do each, and what to watch out for.

## Start with who you are really booking with

The name on the advert and the company running your trip are not always the same. Resellers, booking sites and freelance agents often pass bookings on to another operator, which is fine as long as you know who is responsible on the day.

Before you pay, find out:

- **The trading name and the legal company name.** These should match the name on your confirmation and invoice.
- **Whether they run the trip themselves.** If not, ask who does and whether that operator is licensed for the activity.
- **A physical address and a landline or official contact.** A business that only exists as a messaging account is harder to hold to account.
- **Their tourism licence.** Tourism businesses in the UAE are licensed by the tourism authority of the emirate they operate in. A reputable operator will tell you how they are licensed without hesitation.

If you are booking through your hotel, it is still worth asking which company runs the trip. Hotels often work with outside operators.

## Ask about safety in plain words

### What the Foreign Office advises

The UK Foreign Office is direct about the activities that carry the most risk. It warns that desert excursions can be dangerous [unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and says that if you join a tour for desert driving or quad biking you should go with a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures. It also advises checking that such activities are covered by your insurance.

On the water, it says the safety of tourist boats [may not be up to UK standards](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises making sure life jackets are available for all passengers.

### The questions that separate good from bad

A good operator answers safety questions calmly and specifically. A poor one changes the subject or tells you not to worry. Ask:

- Are your drivers or skippers trained and licensed for this activity?
- Are seatbelts, helmets or life jackets provided for everyone, including children?
- Is there a first aid kit, and what is the plan in a medical emergency?
- What happens if the weather turns?
- How many people will be in the vehicle, on the boat or at the camp?

### Look for recognised schemes

Some protected areas run their own approval systems. The Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve, for example, has an [approved tours seal](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) and says every approved tour follows strict requirements: guides are certified by the reserve, and tours consider environmental impact and waste management. It describes having carefully chosen the guides and operators allowed to work there. A scheme like this does not guarantee a perfect day, but it does mean someone is checking. See our [Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve guide](/stories/dubai-desert-conservation-reserve-guide/) for more.

## Red flags worth walking away from

None of these proves an operator is dishonest, but each is a reason to slow down.

- **Pressure to pay immediately**, especially with "last seats" messages late at night.
- **Cash only**, or a request to transfer money to a personal account.
- **No written itinerary**, or one that changes every time you ask.
- **Vague answers about safety**, licensing or who runs the trip.
- **A price far below everyone else's** for the same trip. Something has to give, and it is often the vehicle, the guide or the group size.
- **Touts approaching you in the street** or at the beach with a deal that must be decided now.
- **Reluctance to give an invoice** or a booking reference.

## Get it in writing

A written booking protects you if something goes wrong. Ask for a confirmation that sets out:

- the date, pickup time and place, and return time
- exactly what is included and what costs extra
- the vehicle or boat type and group size
- the cancellation and refund policy, including for bad weather
- the operator's name, address and contact number

UAE law gives you a solid basis to ask for this. The UAE government portal says that under the federal consumer protection law, consumers have the right to [obtain correct information about the service they receive](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/consumer-protection) and to fair compensation for damage caused by inadequate or unprofessional services. It also says suppliers must provide a dated invoice including their trade name, address, the type of service and its price. The invoice must be in Arabic, and other languages may be added.

## Paying safely

How you pay matters almost as much as who you pay.

- **Pay by card where you can**, so there is a clear record of the transaction.
- **Avoid paying the full amount in cash in advance**, especially to someone you met in person a few minutes ago.
- **Keep every message, receipt and confirmation** until after the trip.

Check what protection you have if the company fails. The UK government's travel insurance guidance notes that cover if an airline or travel agent [goes out of business is typically not included](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/foreign-travel-insurance) in a policy, and suggests choosing an ATOL-protected holiday or a policy that includes supplier failure cover.

## Check your insurance covers the activity

This is the step most people skip. The same UK guidance says travel insurance should cover [all activities you may undertake](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/foreign-travel-insurance), such as sports or adventure tourism, and that you may need specialist insurance or an add-on for some activities. It notes that the use or hire of quad bikes is not usually covered by standard policies.

Read your policy before you book anything with an engine, a boat or a height. Our [travel insurance guide for the UAE](/stories/uae-travel-insurance-guide/) covers what to check.

## If something goes wrong

Start with the operator: raise the problem calmly, in writing, and keep a copy. If that does not resolve it, the UAE government portal says each emirate's [Department of Economic Development handles consumer rights issues](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/consumer-protection) and receives consumer complaints.

For anything more serious, the UAE government lists tourist police numbers: in Dubai, [Dubai Tourism Police on +97146096239](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Tourist-services-and-information/emergency-help-for-tourists-in-the-uae), and in Abu Dhabi, Abu Dhabi Tourism Police on +97128002626 or +97125127777. In a medical emergency, the Foreign Office says to [dial 998 and ask for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health), then contact your insurer quickly.

## Quick answers

**How can I tell if a UAE tour operator is reputable?**
Ask how they are licensed, who actually runs the trip and what their safety procedures are. The Foreign Office advises using a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures for desert driving and quad biking.

**Should I get an invoice for a tour?**
Yes. The UAE government says suppliers must provide a dated invoice with their trade name, address, the service and its price.

**Does travel insurance cover desert safaris and quad bikes?**
Not always. UK government guidance says quad bike use is not usually covered by standard policies, and some adventure activities need an add-on.

**Who do I contact if a tour goes wrong?**
Raise it with the operator in writing first. The UAE government says each emirate's Department of Economic Development receives consumer complaints.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What is your legal company name, and are you licensed by the tourism authority in the emirate where this trip runs?
- Do you run this trip yourselves, or will another company operate it on the day?
- What safety equipment is provided, and are your drivers, skippers or guides trained and licensed for this activity?
- Can you send a written confirmation and a dated invoice with everything that is included?
- What is your cancellation and refund policy, including for bad weather?
- What is your plan if someone is injured or falls ill during the trip?
