The lift doors slide shut, your ears pop, and moments later they open onto a wall of glass with the whole city laid out below like a model. Cars on Sheikh Zayed Road look like beads on a string. Beyond them, the desert on one side and the Gulf on the other.

Visiting the Burj Khalifa is the moment many people picture when they think of Dubai, and it is worth doing well. The short answer: most visitors are happy with the main decks on Levels 124 and 125, the higher Level 148 suits anyone who wants a calmer, more hosted visit, and the best light comes at sunrise and sunset, so book those slots early. Here is how to choose, when to go, what to expect on the day and how to build a relaxed Downtown evening around it.

## The tower in brief

The Burj Khalifa stands [828 metres tall](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa), which the official page says makes it the tallest building in the world. It rises from the middle of Downtown Dubai, next to Dubai Mall and the lake where the fountain shows play, and the viewing experience inside it is sold under the name At The Top.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="burj-khalifa-visit-guide-1" alts="The slim spire of the Burj Khalifa in silhouette against an orange sky, with the sun setting just beside it at the end of a long straight road" caption="The tower is visible from far across the city, which makes it a useful landmark on the way in."></photo-set>

One thing that surprises first-time visitors is how visible the tower is from almost everywhere. You will see its needle from the road in from the airport, from the beaches and from the far side of the city, often poking above haze that hides everything else. That makes it an easy landmark for getting your bearings, but it also means the tower can look closer than it is. Downtown is a large district, and the walk from a hotel or a metro station to the deck entrance is longer than the skyline suggests.

The other thing to understand is that the visit is about the view rather than the building. The official page describes At The Top as a way to take in [sweeping cityscapes and stories of the city's evolution](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa) from inside the tower, so think of it as a viewpoint with a short story attached, not a museum.

## The Burj Khalifa viewing decks compared

The official page sets out [three tiers](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa), called Silver, Gold and Platinum. Each one gives you the same city. What changes is the height, the crowd and how much is done for you.

### Levels 124 and 125: the classic view

The standard experience covers the public observation decks on Levels 124 and 125, which the official page puts at [452 metres above the city](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa). You ride up in a fast double-decker lift, and Level 125 is the more spacious of the two, decorated with Arabic mashrabiya patterns. Views run a full 360 degrees, and there is an optional virtual reality add-on that simulates a climb to the very top of the tower. The page also lists [telescopes and interactive exhibits](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa) among the things to see, and says free Wi-Fi is available on the standard levels.

For first-time visitors this is plenty. The difference between this height and the decks above is something you feel more than see.

### Level 148: higher and quieter

Level 148 is described as the [world's highest observation deck at 555 metres](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa). The experience includes a lounge with Arabic coffee, traditional sweets and drinks, multilingual hosts, interactive displays and access to an outdoor terrace. Tickets for this tier also include Levels 124 and 125.

Choose this if you want to linger rather than jostle, or if the view is the main event of your trip.

### Levels 152 to 154: the top tier

The most exclusive option takes you to a lounge that the official page says is recognised by Guinness World Records as the [world's highest, at 585 metres](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa), with priority access to all observation levels, food and drinks, and a setting designed to feel uncrowded. It is a splurge, and best kept for a special occasion.

### Which deck is right for you?

- **First visit, tight schedule:** Levels 124 and 125.
- **Photographers and anyone who dislikes crowds:** Level 148, where the terrace and calmer lounge help.
- **Celebrations:** the top tier, if the budget allows.
- **Nervous of heights:** start with the lower decks. You can stand back from the glass and still take in the view.
- **Travelling with young children:** the lower decks are usually enough. Children tend to enjoy the fast lift and the telescopes more than a long lounge stay.

## What you actually see from the top

It helps to know what you are looking at before you go up, because the city reads very differently from above.

Looking towards the coast, the Gulf runs along the horizon, and on a clear day the clusters of towers further along the shore stand out against the water. Closer in, the line of skyscrapers along Sheikh Zayed Road runs away from you like a spine, with the road itself threaded through the middle.

On the inland side, the view changes character completely. Low-rise villa neighbourhoods, parks and highway junctions give way to open sand towards the horizon. Directly below, the lake and the curve of Dubai Mall look small enough to touch, and if a fountain show is playing you can watch the jets from above.

How much of this you see depends on the day. Haze and dust can soften the view, particularly in the warmer months, and sometimes the far horizon disappears altogether. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) will help you pick a season with clearer skies. If your dates are fixed, a morning slot often gives the crispest outline before the day's haze builds, but there is no guarantee.

## When to go: sunrise, sunset or after dark

The decks are open [daily from 7:00 to 23:00](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa), with the last entry to the Level 148 lounge at 22:00, and the official page names sunrise and sunset as the times for the most striking views.

Each slot has its own character.

### Sunrise

Early slots catch the city waking up, the desert turning pink to gold in the east and long shadows reaching across Downtown. It is also a good way to beat the heat of the day before you head anywhere else.

### Sunset

This is the slot most people want. The sun drops towards the Gulf, the towers catch the last light, and then the city switches itself on below you. Plan around it and book well ahead. If you can, choose a slot that gets you to the top a little before the sun sets, so you see the city in daylight, at golden hour and after dark in a single visit.

### After dark

A night visit swaps landscape for lights: the grid of roads glowing, the Marina towers twinkling in the distance, and the water show at the foot of the tower. It is often easier to get a slot.

### During Ramadan

If you visit during Ramadan, the rhythm of Downtown changes. The UK Foreign Office says it may cause offence to [eat, drink, smoke or chew gum in public in the daytime](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) during the holy month, and advises checking the opening hours of shops and restaurants. Check the official page for any changes to deck hours on your dates, and see our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/).

## Booking At The Top

Visit Dubai lists the Burj Khalifa as [the world's tallest building and one of Dubai's top attractions](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/places-to-visit/burj-khalifa), and links through to official ticketing. The tower's own page also suggests booking through its official site, where you can add [combination tickets](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa) for nearby attractions, such as a fountain lake ride or the floating boardwalk.

A few habits make booking smoother:

- **Book timed slots in advance,** especially for sunset, weekends and the cooler, busier months.
- **Check what each tier includes** before you pay, since lounges and terraces only come with the higher levels.
- **Buy from the official site or a trusted operator,** and be wary of resale offers that seem vague about which levels they include.
- **Keep a buffer either side** of your slot. Getting through Dubai Mall to the entrance can take longer than you think.

We avoid quoting prices here because they vary by tier, date and time of day. Check the official page when you book.

## On the day: getting there and getting in

### Finding the entrance

The entrance is not at the foot of the tower itself. At The Top is [accessed via Dubai Mall on the Lower Ground level](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa) in Downtown Dubai, and the official page says it is reachable by metro, car or taxi, with parking at the mall. Follow the signs once inside; the mall is vast and it is easy to lose time.

### Getting there by metro

Dubai Metro is an easy way to reach Downtown. The Roads and Transport Authority notes [lift access to all metro station levels](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro) and dedicated spaces for wheelchair users on all trains, which helps if you are travelling with a pushchair or anyone who struggles with stairs. The same page explains that trains have cabins reserved for women and children and a separate Gold Class cabin, so check the signs on the platform before you board. Eating and drinking are not allowed in areas where they are prohibited, and the RTA lists fines for it, so save your snacks for later. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) covers the network in more detail.

### What to wear

You enter through a busy mall, so dress for that. The UK Foreign Office advises visitors to [dress modestly in public areas like shopping malls](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), with women covering shoulders and knees. Bring a light layer too: the mall and the decks are air-conditioned, and the contrast with the heat outside can be sharp. See [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) for more.

### How the visit unfolds

Arrive with your ticket ready on your phone and expect to wait your turn at busy times, since entry runs by time slot. Once you are through, the lift ride is fast and the rest of the visit is at your own pace: a slow circuit of the glass, a look through the telescopes, and as many photographs as you like. Check your ticket conditions for how long you can stay, and allow extra time if you have booked a lounge or plan to wait for the sun to set.

Downtown is busy in the evenings, so agree a meeting point in the mall beforehand if your group tends to scatter.

### Photos

Photography and video are [encouraged at the top](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa), though professional shoots may need prior permission. Reflections are the enemy of a good shot through glass. Press your lens close to the window, wear dark clothing and switch off the flash. The outdoor terrace on Level 148 avoids the glass problem for at least part of the view. Keep strangers out of your frames: the Foreign Office advises visitors [not to photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

## Access, families and nerves

**Mobility:** the decks are reached by lift, and the RTA says every metro station level has lift access. It is still a long walk through the mall, so if anyone in your group tires easily, plan rest stops and ask about access when you book.

**Children:** fast lifts and a long drop beyond the glass are thrilling for most children, but keep hold of small hands near the windows and on the busy routes through the mall. Our guide to [Dubai with children](/stories/dubai-with-kids/) has more ideas for family days.

**Fear of heights:** on the indoor decks you are behind glass, and you never have to walk right up to it. Many nervous visitors find that standing a few steps back makes the view feel like a large painting rather than a drop.

## Make an evening of Downtown

The tower sits at the centre of a district built for wandering, and it makes sense to plan your visit around the water show on the lake below.

The official page gives the Dubai Fountain schedule as [evening shows daily from 18:00 to 23:00](https://www.emaar.com/en/at-the-top-burj-khalifa), with afternoon shows at 13:00 and 13:30 from Saturday to Thursday, and at 14:00 and 14:30 on Fridays. You can watch from the promenade around the lake, or more closely from the floating boardwalk or an abra ride, both bookable.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="burj-khalifa-visit-guide-5" alts="The turquoise lake at the foot of the Burj Khalifa, seen from a carved stone balustrade on the promenade with Downtown towers behind" caption="Between shows the lake is calm, and the promenade around it is one place to watch from."></photo-set>

A good rhythm is a late-afternoon deck slot for sunset, then down to the lake for a show or two once the lights come on. The promenade fills up quickly in the evening, especially at weekends, so if you want a clear view of the water, take your place a few minutes before a show begins.

If you want a second, very different viewpoint, the Dubai Frame looks across old and new Dubai from a lower height. Our [Dubai Frame guide](/stories/dubai-frame-guide/) explains how the two compare. For more on the city, see our [Dubai destination page](/destinations/dubai/).

## Quick answers

**How high are the Burj Khalifa viewing decks?** The main decks on Levels 124 and 125 are at 452 metres. Level 148 is at 555 metres, and the top-tier lounge is at 585 metres. The tower itself is 828 metres tall.

**What time does At The Top open?** The decks open daily from 7:00 to 23:00, with last entry to the Level 148 lounge at 22:00.

**What is the best time to visit the Burj Khalifa?** The official page names sunrise and sunset as the times for the most striking views. Sunset slots are the most sought after, so book early.

**Where is the Burj Khalifa entrance?** Entry to At The Top is through Dubai Mall, on the Lower Ground level, in Downtown Dubai.

**Can I take photos at the top?** Yes. Photography and video are encouraged, but professional shoots may need prior permission.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which deck would you suggest for our group, and what exactly does each ticket include?
- Can you secure a sunrise or sunset slot on our dates, and what happens if the weather is hazy?
- How much time should we allow to get from our hotel through the mall to the entrance?
- Is there step-free access all the way to the decks for a wheelchair or pushchair?
- Can you combine the visit with a fountain show or another Downtown attraction without a rushed schedule?
- If our plans change, what are the rules on changing the time slot?
