The price on the booking page looked perfect. Then, at checkout, a short list of extra lines appears: a service charge, a municipality fee, a tourism fee. Nothing is wrong. It is simply how hotel pricing works in much of the UAE, and it catches out a lot of first-time visitors.

Choosing a hotel in the UAE is mostly about the same things as anywhere: location, the right kind of room, and a fair rate. But a few local factors make a real difference here. Which emirate your hotel is in can change whether alcohol is served. The total you pay can include charges set by the authorities. And at certain times of year, such as Ramadan, hotel life works a little differently.

The short answer: before you book, check four things. Where the hotel is in relation to what you want to see, what the quoted rate actually includes, which emirate it is in and what that means for you, and how the dates of your stay fit the local calendar.

## Start with location, not the pool

In a country where many sights are a drive apart, location shapes your trip more than any hotel feature.

### Pick the city before the hotel

Most visitors base themselves in Dubai or Abu Dhabi city, with side trips from there. Our guide to choosing [between Dubai and Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) compares the two, and [where to stay in Dubai](/stories/where-to-stay-in-dubai/) breaks Dubai down by area. If you want something calmer, the smaller emirates and the east coast offer a different pace.

### Then pick the neighbourhood

Mark the places you most want to visit on a map, then look for hotels between them rather than simply the cheapest option. A hotel that saves money but adds a long taxi ride twice a day may not save anything at all.

### Ask how you will get around

If you are not driving, check how close the hotel is to public transport and how easy it is to get a taxi. If you plan to explore remote areas, a car matters more. Visit Abu Dhabi, for example, says a car is [the most convenient way to explore Al Dhafra](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra), because attractions are spread over a wide area.

## Understand what the rate includes

This is where most surprises happen.

### Taxes and fees

The UAE government portal explains that restaurants, hotels, hotel apartments and resorts [may charge one or more taxes and fees](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae), including a tax on the room rate, a service charge, municipality fees, a city tax and a tourism fee. Not every charge applies everywhere, and the rules differ between emirates.

Some emirates add their own per-night fee. The same page says that in Dubai, hotels charge a Tourism Dirham fee [per room per night, for a maximum of 30 consecutive nights](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae), with the amount depending on the hotel's category. It also notes a per-room, per-night tourism fee in Ras Al Khaimah.

What to do: when you compare hotels, compare the final price including everything, not the headline rate. If a booking site is unclear, ask the hotel directly.

### Tipping

Tipping is a common question at check-in and check-out. Visit Abu Dhabi says [tipping is not expected but is commonly practised](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/taxes-service-charges-and-tipping), and that gratuities to hotel and restaurant staff are at your discretion. It also notes that service charges are often already included in menu prices, so check the bill before adding more.

### Book through a channel you trust

The government portal links to [official places to search for and book hotels](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae) in Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah, and lists travel agencies linked to the UAE's airlines and airports. Whichever route you choose, keep your confirmation and check the cancellation terms.

## Check which emirate you are in

The UAE has seven emirates, and some rules differ between them. The most important one for hotel choice is alcohol.

The UK Foreign Office says [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that elsewhere in the UAE, non-residents can drink in licensed venues such as hotels, restaurants and clubs. It also notes it is illegal to drink, or be under the influence of alcohol, in a public place anywhere in the country.

This matters because some areas that feel like one place on the map are split between emirates. Sharjah's east coast towns, for example, follow Sharjah's rules. If a drink with dinner is important to you, check the hotel's emirate first. Our guide to [alcohol rules in the UAE](/stories/alcohol-rules-in-the-uae/) has the full picture.

## Know the check-in rules

A few rules catch people out at the front desk.

- **Passports.** The Foreign Office says it is [normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) or Emirates ID. Have it ready at check-in.
- **Young travellers.** The same advice says you [cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and not accompanied by an adult. Bear this in mind for older teenagers travelling with friends.
- **Paying the bill.** The Foreign Office warns that [non-payment of bills, including hotel bills](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), can lead to a prison sentence, a fine or both. Make sure you can settle your bill before you leave.
- **Couples.** A common question from unmarried couples is whether they can share a room. The Foreign Office says consensual sexual relationships between a man and a woman outside marriage are [generally legal if both are aged 18 or over](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Read its full advice, as there are exceptions.

## Time your stay with the calendar

The same hotel can feel different depending on when you visit.

### Ramadan

During the holy month, the Foreign Office notes that hotels and restaurants serving food in fasting hours [may separate you from Muslim guests, for example with screens](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Ask your hotel how its restaurants work during the day and at iftar. Our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains what changes.

### Dress around the hotel

Inside resorts, the atmosphere can feel relaxed, but the Foreign Office reminds visitors that [swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that some venues have their own dress codes. Pack a cover-up for walking through the lobby.

## A short pre-booking checklist

Before you press "book", run through these:

1. Is the hotel close to the places you most want to see, or to good transport?
2. Does the total price include all taxes, service charges and per-night fees?
3. Which emirate is it in, and does that affect what you want from your stay?
4. Do the cancellation terms work if your plans change?
5. Do your dates overlap with Ramadan, Eid or a big event?
6. Does the room fit your group, including cots, connecting rooms or accessible rooms if needed?

## Quick answers

**Why is my UAE hotel bill higher than the room rate?** The UAE government portal says hotels may add taxes and fees, such as service charges, municipality fees and tourism fees, and Dubai hotels charge a Tourism Dirham fee per room per night.

**Do UAE hotels take your passport?** The UK Foreign Office says it is normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport or Emirates ID.

**Do hotels in Sharjah serve alcohol?** No. The Foreign Office says drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah.

**Should I tip hotel staff in the UAE?** Visit Abu Dhabi says tipping is not expected but is commonly practised, and gratuities are at your discretion.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which area would you recommend for our plans, and how long are the transfers to the places we want to visit?
- Does the rate you have quoted include all taxes, service charges and per-night fees?
- Which emirate is the hotel in, and is there anything about local rules we should know?
- What are the cancellation and change terms for this booking?
- How will Ramadan or any public holiday during our dates affect hotel dining and tour timings?
