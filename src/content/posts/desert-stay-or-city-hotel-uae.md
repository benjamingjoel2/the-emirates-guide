The generator goes quiet, the last 4x4 of the evening pulls away towards the highway, and suddenly the only light comes from a lantern and a sky that seems to have twice as many stars as it did an hour ago. Somewhere behind a dune, a camel shifts in the sand.

Compare that with the city: a lift down from a high floor, a metro ride, breakfast with a view of a marina. Both are the UAE, and both make good bases. The real question is not which is better, but which one your trip needs, and for how long.

The short answer for most visitors: base yourself in a city hotel and add one desert night if you can. A city stay gives you transport, choice and easy access to museums, souks and beaches. A desert stay gives you silence, stars and sunrise over the dunes, but it takes you away from almost everything else. Here is how to weigh them up.

## Desert stay or city hotel: what really changes

The UAE government's own visitor guide sums up the range neatly: you can [stay by the sea or in the city, or relax in a resort in the deep desert](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae). What changes between them is less about the room and more about the shape of your day.

### Your days from a city hotel

From a city base, the day is flexible: a museum in the morning, the pool when the heat builds, an evening souk after dinner. Taxis and public transport are on hand, and if a plan falls through, there is always another nearby. You can also change your mind at short notice, which matters more than people expect on a first trip, when jet lag, weather or a tired child can rewrite the day.

### Your days from the desert

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="desert-stay-or-city-hotel-uae-1" alts="The sun low over rippled sand dunes with green desert shrubs in the foreground near Dubai" caption="Early morning on the dunes is the part of a desert night most people remember."></photo-set>

In the desert, the landscape is the plan: dune walks before the sun climbs, shade in the afternoon, stars after dark. You will usually be far from shops and other sights, and may depend on your camp for meals and transfers. That is the point, but one or two nights is plenty for most people.

It also changes how you think about time. Instead of fitting in as much as possible, you plan around light: when the sand is cool enough to walk on, when the sun drops, when the sky goes properly dark. If that sounds appealing, a desert night will suit you. If it sounds like time lost from sightseeing, keep it to one night or choose an evening trip instead.

## How a desert night usually unfolds

Every camp runs its own schedule, so check the details when you book, but most overnight stays follow a similar pattern.

- **Afternoon:** a pickup from your city hotel or a meeting point, often in a four-wheel drive, then a drive out of the city as the light softens.
- **Sunset:** arrival in time to walk up a dune and watch the colour drain out of the sand. This is when photographers are busiest.
- **Evening:** dinner at camp, often grilled food and Arabic dishes served in a tented area, followed by time around a fire or simply sitting out.
- **Night:** the generator and the day visitors fade, and the stars take over. Bring a warm layer even in the cooler months.
- **Dawn:** an early start for sunrise, then breakfast and the drive back. Many people are back in the city by mid-morning with most of the day still ahead.

That rhythm explains why a desert night works best as one part of a trip rather than the whole of it. You see the desert at its best, then return to the city refreshed rather than stranded.

## Where you can actually sleep in the desert

Desert stays come in very different forms, from a tent you pitch yourself to an air-conditioned dome with breakfast included. These are the main areas, using what the official boards and reserves say themselves.

### Inside a protected reserve near Dubai

The Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve describes itself as [the UAE's first national park](https://www.ddcr.org/), working to protect Dubai's last desert wilderness and its native plants and animals. Its activity list includes [overnight camping with professional safari guides](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/), with a tent and a traditional Arabian dinner waiting at camp. The reserve also says a single resort is [the only one offering overnight stays within the reserve](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/), so space inside it is limited. Ask any operator exactly where their camp sits: inside the reserve or simply near it.

The reserve runs an approval scheme for tours: approved operators carry its seal, guides are [certified by the reserve](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) to explain its plants, animals, geology and culture, and tours are expected to manage waste and environmental impact. Its listed activities include wildlife drives to look for free-roaming herds, camel treks, falconry and sand boarding. If you care about how your desert night affects the place, that seal is a useful thing to ask about.

### Beneath Jebel Hafit in Al Ain

At the foot of Al Ain's mountain, Jebel Hafit Desert Park offers [three camping experiences](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit): basic camping with your own equipment, a serviced camp with Bedouin-style tents and breakfast, and furnished glamping tents with air-conditioning. Visit Abu Dhabi confirms that [overnight camping at designated campsites in Al Ain is allowed](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain), and recommends planning for the cooler months, from around October to April.

The same tourism board describes Jebel Hafit as [Abu Dhabi's highest peak, rising to 1,249 metres](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), with the desert park stretching along its foot and offering hiking, mountain biking and horse or camel rides. The camping guide points to the beehive-shaped Jebel Hafit tombs nearby, around 5,000 years old and part of Al Ain's UNESCO-listed sites, so a night here combines easily with a morning of history. Our guide to [staying overnight in Al Ain](/stories/staying-overnight-in-al-ain/) covers the town itself.

### Mleiha, in the heart of Sharjah

Sharjah's tourism board says visitors love to [spend a night under the stars in Mleiha](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/), surrounded by dunes, and that the archaeological centre there can advise on camping spots, including glamping. Transfers from Sharjah are available if you do not have a car. The wider [Central Region](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/central-region/) is described as less than an hour from Sharjah city, with desert safari tours that can show you where to set up camp.

Mleiha suits people who like a bit of substance with their stars. Visit Sharjah lists guided tours of a circular Bronze Age tomb and of Fossil Rock, a large rock formation full of marine fossils, alongside hiking, dune driving and quad biking. Spending the afternoon on the archaeology and the night on the sand makes a well-balanced overnight.

### Liwa and the Empty Quarter

For the biggest dunes, Liwa in Abu Dhabi's Al Dhafra region is in a different league, and a different distance. Visit Abu Dhabi says driving there from Abu Dhabi city [typically takes around two to three hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) and that a car is the most convenient way to explore, because sights are spread across a wide area. It also notes that the Liwa International Festival runs each December, so expect more people then. Liwa rewards two nights rather than one; see our [Liwa and Empty Quarter guide](/stories/liwa-empty-quarter-guide/).

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="desert-stay-or-city-hotel-uae-5" alts="A palm-lined road curving past tall pale sand dunes in Liwa, Abu Dhabi" caption="Liwa's dunes are on a far larger scale than those near the cities, and the drive there is part of the trip."></photo-set>

The same page says the cooler months are ideal for desert experiences there, while the warmer months are quieter and better suited to coastal stays and indoor attractions. That is a good rule of thumb for desert nights anywhere in the country.

### Mountains rather than sand

If it is open space you want rather than dunes specifically, Hatta in the mountains of Dubai emirate is another option. Visit Dubai lists [hiking trails, kayaking, mountain biking and camping](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/beyond-dubai/nature-adventures-in-hatta) there.

## When the city wins

A city hotel is the better main base if:

- **This is your first visit.** You will want to see the big landmarks, and almost all of them are in or near Dubai and Abu Dhabi city.
- **You are relying on public transport.** Desert camps generally need a transfer or a hire car.
- **You are travelling with toddlers or anyone with limited mobility.** Sand and basic toilets can be hard work.
- **You are visiting in the hotter months.** Outdoor desert life is at its best in the cooler season, which is exactly why the official guides above steer campers towards it.
- **You want variety in the evenings.** Restaurants, souks and waterfronts are where city nights come alive.

Our guide to choosing [between Dubai and Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) can help with the city part of the decision.

## When the desert is worth it

A night on the sand is hard to beat if:

- **You want to see the sky properly.** City glow hides most stars. Visit Abu Dhabi's Al Ain guide talks about [dark skies, cool night air and a silence broken only by the wind](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain).
- **You love early mornings.** Sunrise over the dunes, with nobody else around, is the moment many people remember most.
- **You are celebrating something.** A quiet desert night makes a special break in the middle of a city-heavy trip.
- **You have already done an evening safari** and want the desert after the day visitors leave. Our piece on [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) explains how the classic evening trip differs.

## Families, mobility and comfort

Desert nights can work well for children, but it pays to pick the right kind. The Al Ain camping guide suggests family activities such as a camel ride, a short hike on the lower slopes of Jebel Hafit and spotting constellations with a telescope or a stargazing app, and it points to ready-pitched tents for anyone new to camping. A glamping tent with proper beds is usually a gentler first night for young children than a basic camp.

For anyone with limited mobility, ask detailed questions before booking: how far the tents are from where vehicles park, whether paths are on soft sand, and what the toilets and showers are like. A city hotel with a single evening trip to the desert may be the better choice, and there is no shame in that.

Two rules from the UK Foreign Office are worth knowing whichever base you choose. Under-18s [cannot stay in a hotel unless accompanied by an adult](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and it is normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport. If anyone in your group takes regular medication, the Foreign Office notes that some UK medicines are controlled in the UAE and that you may need [approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health). That matters more in the desert, where the nearest pharmacy may be a long drive away.

## The split stay: a city base with one night on the sand

For most trips the best answer is both. Spend the bulk of your nights in a city hotel, then move to the desert for one night, ideally midway through the trip or at the end, when you are ready to slow down.

A few tips make the switch smoother:

1. **Keep your city room if you can** or leave big bags at the hotel, so you travel to camp with a small overnight bag.
2. **Pack for two climates.** The Al Ain camping guide suggests [light layers for the day, warm layers for the night](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain) and sturdy closed-toe shoes, plus a torch, power bank, first-aid kit and bags to take all your rubbish home.
3. **Match the desert to your route.** Staying in Dubai? The reserve area is the natural fit. Heading to Al Ain? Jebel Hafit makes sense. Exploring Sharjah? Look at Mleiha.
4. **Plan your transfer back.** Many camps are set up for an early departure, so check what time you need to leave.
5. **Leave the next morning light.** Do not book an early museum slot or a long drive for the day after; you will be up before dawn.

Before you compare prices, check what is included. The UAE government portal notes that hotels, resorts and restaurants may add [taxes and charges such as service charges and municipality fees](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae), so a desert package and a city room are only comparable once you know what each covers: meals, transfers, activities and fees.

## What catches people out

- **Assuming every camp is the same.** An evening safari camp that empties at night is very different from a camp built for sleeping. Ask whether guests stay overnight and how many.
- **Underestimating the cold.** Desert nights in the cooler months can feel far colder than the day suggests.
- **Booking the desert on the last night before an early flight.** It can work, but a tight transfer back to the airport takes the shine off the morning.
- **Forgetting the road.** Liwa is a long drive, and even camps nearer the cities are out of reach of public transport.

## Staying safe on a desert night

If you are driving yourself, take the official advice seriously. The UK Foreign Office says desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and leaving a copy of your travel plans with friends or where you are staying. If you book a tour, it recommends a reputable company with proper health and safety procedures, and checking that the activity is covered by your insurance.

## Quick answers

**Is it better to stay in the desert or the city in the UAE?** For most visitors, a city hotel makes the best main base, with one desert night added. The city gives you transport and choice; the desert gives you quiet and stars.

**Can you camp overnight in the UAE desert?** Yes, in the right places. Visit Abu Dhabi says overnight camping is allowed at designated campsites in Al Ain, and the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists guided overnight camping.

**When is the best time for a desert stay?** Visit Abu Dhabi recommends the cooler months, from around October to April, for camping in Al Ain.

**How far is Liwa from Abu Dhabi city?** Visit Abu Dhabi says the drive to Al Dhafra typically takes around two to three hours, depending on where you are heading.

**Is a desert night suitable for children?** It can be. Glamping tents and ready-pitched camps are gentler than basic camping, and the Al Ain guide suggests family activities such as camel rides and stargazing.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Where exactly is the camp or desert resort, and is it inside a protected area or simply near one?
- What are the sleeping arrangements, toilets and showers like, and is there any heating or cooling in the tents?
- What time is pickup from our city hotel, and what time do we need to leave the camp the next morning?
- How many other guests will be staying overnight, and does the camp get busier with day visitors in the evening?
- What should we pack for the night temperatures in the month we are travelling?
- What happens if the weather turns, and is the trip covered by a clear cancellation policy?
