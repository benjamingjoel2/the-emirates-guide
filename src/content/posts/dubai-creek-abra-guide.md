You step down from the quay onto a wide wooden deck that rocks gently under your feet. Someone shuffles along the bench to make room, the boatman nods, and before you have quite sat down the engine is running and the bow is swinging out into the Creek. A minute ago you were on a busy street. Now you are on the water, eye level with the cargo dhows, and the city looks completely different.

Crossing Dubai Creek by abra is one of the simplest things to do in Dubai and one of the most memorable. It is cheap, quick and part of daily life for the people who live and work around the Creek. You do not need to book. You need some small cash, a little confidence stepping on and off, and a few minutes. This guide explains the routes, the hours, how the crossing works and how to do it safely with children.

## What an abra is

The Roads and Transport Authority (RTA), which runs Dubai's public marine transport, describes the abra as the city's most traditional form of transport and a symbol of its history and heritage. It explains that the word comes from [an Arabic verb meaning "to cross"](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine) and that abras have been a frequently used way of getting about Dubai for centuries.

The setting explains why. The UAE government notes that the creek [divides the city into Deira in the north and Bur Dubai in the south](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai), and that it was a natural harbour that made Dubai a centre for fishing, pearling and sea trade. For generations, the quickest way from one side to the other was a small boat. Visit Dubai still calls the Creek [the historic heart of the city](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf).

## Which abra crossing to take

The RTA runs several kinds of abra, and the names can be confusing at first.

- **Traditional motorised abras**: the classic open wooden boats with a central bench. These are the ones most visitors mean.
- **Electric traditional abras**: quieter boats used on scenic routes elsewhere in the city rather than for the basic Creek crossing.
- **Air-conditioned and petrol heritage abras**: more modern boats styled on the traditional design, which the RTA says combine [traditional style with modern safety technology](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine).

### The two classic routes

For a first crossing, use one of the traditional motorised routes. The RTA lists:

- [Bur Dubai Abra Station to Deira Old Souq Abra Station, running from 6:00 am to midnight](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/promotion/new-marine-routes-abra).
- Dubai Old Souq Abra Station to Al Sabkha Abra Station, running 24 hours.

The first is the natural choice if you are coming from Al Fahidi and heading for the Spice Souk and Gold Souk, because Deira Old Souq station lands you close to both. The RTA describes these routes as connecting to metro and bus stations, which makes the crossing easy to fit into a day on public transport.

### Heritage routes along the Creek

The RTA also lists petrol heritage abra routes on the Creek, including [Dubai Old Souq to Baniyas, Al Fahidi to Al Sabkha, Al Fahidi to Deira Old Souq and Al Seef to Baniyas](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine). Services and stations can change, so check the RTA page or ask at the station before you set out.

## Paying for the crossing

The traditional routes are cash only. The RTA's route page states that fares on both classic crossings are [paid in cash](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/promotion/new-marine-routes-abra), so carry small coins or notes. Card payments and travel cards are not the way to pay here.

The RTA also offers private charters on the traditional abras for a set time, which is a good option for a family or small group who want to see more of the Creek at their own pace. The current terms are on the same page.

## How the crossing works, step by step

1. **Find the station.** Abra stations sit right on the water, usually with a small covered waiting area and boats moored alongside.
2. **Join the queue.** Follow the line and the boatman's directions to the next boat.
3. **Step on carefully.** The deck sits low and moves with the water. Take your time, use the hand of the person helping you if offered, and step onto the flat deck rather than the edge.
4. **Sit on the bench.** Passengers sit along the central bench, facing outwards towards the water. Keep bags on your lap.
5. **Pay when asked.** Have your cash ready so you can hand it over to the boatman without fuss.
6. **Step off the same way.** Wait until the boat is steady against the quay before you stand.

The crossing itself is short. Use it: look upstream and downstream, watch the dhows being loaded, and see how the two banks face each other across the water.

## Abras with children and limited mobility

The RTA is clear on rules for younger passengers. Its marine transport page states that [children under 12 are not allowed on marine transport unaccompanied](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), that children under 12 travelling with an adult must wear a life jacket, and that a valid ID for the child must be presented at the ticketing and information office. Bring your child's ID with you.

Hold small children firmly while stepping on and off, and keep them seated in the middle of the bench. The traditional boats have no rails along much of the edge.

The RTA page lists accessibility features for some of its other marine services, but not for abras. The traditional boats involve a step down from the quay onto a moving deck, so anyone with limited mobility or a wheelchair should check with the RTA or a local operator before planning a crossing.

## Best time for an abra crossing

Any time is fine for a quick crossing, but two windows stand out. Early morning is cooler and quieter, and the light on the water is soft. Late afternoon into sunset is livelier and more atmospheric, with the Creek glowing and the souks filling up on the Deira side.

There is little shade on the water or the quays in the middle of the day. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm, wear light-coloured, loose clothing and drink more cold drinks](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/). A hat and sunglasses make the crossing much more comfortable. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) shows when the weather is kindest.

## Photos and manners on board

The abra is a commuter boat as much as a tourist experience, and many of your fellow passengers are on their way to work. The UK Foreign Office advises that you [do not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Photograph the water, the boats and the skyline freely, and ask before you include a person. Dress for a public place: the same page advises dressing modestly in public areas. ## Quick answers

**Do I need to book an abra?**
No. On the traditional routes you simply queue at the station and board the next boat.

**How do I pay for the abra?**
In cash. The RTA lists fares on the traditional Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq and Dubai Old Souq to Al Sabkha routes as cash only.

**What time does the abra run?**
The RTA lists Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq from 6:00 am to midnight, and Dubai Old Souq to Al Sabkha 24 hours.

**Can children ride the abra?**
Yes, with an adult. The RTA says children under 12 cannot travel unaccompanied and must wear a life jacket on board, and you should carry a valid ID for the child.

**What does "abra" mean?**
The RTA explains that the name comes from an Arabic verb meaning "to cross".

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which abra station is best for our walking route, and where will you meet us on each side of the Creek?
- Can you arrange a private abra charter for our group, and how long would you suggest?
- Do you provide life jackets for children, and what ID should we bring for them?
- Is there a step-free or easier boarding option for anyone with limited mobility?
- How do you adjust timings in the hotter months so we are not waiting on the quayside at midday?
