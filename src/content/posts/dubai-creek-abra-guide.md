You step down from the quay onto a wide wooden deck that rocks gently under your feet. Someone shuffles along the bench to make room, the boatman nods, and before you have quite sat down the engine is running and the bow is swinging out into the Creek. A minute ago you were on a busy street. Now you are on the water, eye level with the cargo dhows, and the city looks completely different.

Crossing Dubai Creek by abra is one of the simplest things to do in Dubai and one of the most memorable. It is cheap, quick and part of daily life for the people who live and work around the Creek. You do not need to book. You need some small cash, a little confidence stepping on and off, and a few minutes. This guide explains the routes, the hours, how the crossing works, what has changed at the stations, and how to do it safely with children or anyone who finds boats tricky.

## What an abra is

The Roads and Transport Authority (RTA), which runs Dubai's public marine transport, describes the abra as the city's most traditional form of transport and a symbol of its history and heritage. It explains that the word comes from [an Arabic verb meaning "to cross"](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine) and that abras have been a frequently used way of getting about Dubai for centuries.

The setting explains why. The UAE government notes that the creek [divides the city into Deira in the north and Bur Dubai in the south](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai), and that it was a natural harbour that made Dubai a centre for fishing, pearling and sea trade. For generations, the quickest way from one side to the other was a small boat. Visit Dubai still calls the Creek [the historic heart of the city](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf).

### Still a commuter service

It is easy to think of the abra as a tourist ride, but the numbers say otherwise. In 2021 the RTA reported that [148 abras operated by the private sector on two lines on Dubai Creek](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2021/Feb/15-02/Biodiesel-5-proves-successful-in-powering-traditional-abras-announces-RTA-in-cooperation-with-ENOC) ferried about 13 million riders a year, which it said was around 90% of all marine transport riders in Dubai. In 2023 it described the four traditional Creek stations as [used by 14 million riders annually](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/August/31-08/RTA-revamps-4-traditional-abra-stations-along-Dubai-Creek). Many of the people sitting next to you are simply getting to work.

## Which abra crossing to take

The RTA runs several kinds of abra, and the names can be confusing at first.

- **Traditional motorised abras**: the classic open wooden boats with a central bench. These are the ones most visitors mean.
- **Electric traditional abras**: quieter boats used on scenic routes elsewhere in the city rather than for the basic Creek crossing.
- **Air-conditioned and petrol heritage abras**: more modern boats styled on the traditional design, which the RTA says combine [traditional style with modern safety technology](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine).

### The two classic routes

For a first crossing, use one of the traditional motorised routes. The RTA lists:

- [Bur Dubai Abra Station to Deira Old Souq Abra Station, running from 6:00 am to midnight](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/promotion/new-marine-routes-abra).
- Dubai Old Souq Abra Station to Al Sabkha Abra Station, running 24 hours.

The first is the natural choice if you are coming from Al Fahidi and heading for the Spice Souk and Gold Souk, because Deira Old Souq station lands you close to both. The RTA describes these routes as connecting to metro and bus stations, which makes the crossing easy to fit into a day on public transport.

### Heritage routes along the Creek

The RTA also lists petrol heritage abra routes on the Creek, including [Dubai Old Souq to Baniyas, Al Fahidi to Al Sabkha, Al Fahidi to Deira Old Souq and Al Seef to Baniyas](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine). Services and stations can change, so check the RTA page or ask at the station before you set out.

## The stations and the boats today

If you last crossed the Creek years ago, you will notice changes. The RTA set out to upgrade four key stations: [Bur Dubai, Deira Old Souq, Dubai Old Souq and Sabkha](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/August/31-08/RTA-revamps-4-traditional-abra-stations-along-Dubai-Creek), with the Bur Dubai model station designed to keep its historic and cultural identity while adding a sheltered outdoor space and retail outlets for riders.

The Deira Old Souq station reopened in 2024. The RTA said it [serves the Old Baladiya Road area and the Gold Souq](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/february/20-02/rta-opens-deira-old-souq-marine-transport-station), links to Al Fahidi and Bur Dubai stations, and has expanded sheltered waiting areas. The last two stations, Old Dubai Souq and Al Sabkha, were finished in 2025, when the RTA said it had [expanded shaded waiting areas by 50%](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/september/01-09/rta-completes-final-development-phase-of-dubai-creek-stations) and added an air-conditioned rest area for the boatmen.

The boats are changing too. In 2025 the RTA introduced fourth-generation traditional abras that [can carry up to 24 passengers, up from 20](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/february/17-02/rta-unveils-fourth-generation-traditional-abras-to-enhance-marine-transport-network), with improved seating and flooring, digital destination boards and dedicated spaces for people of determination. The look is still the familiar wooden boat, so you may not notice which generation you are on until you sit down.

There has been work on cleaner fuel as well. In 2021 the RTA said a trial running five traditional abras on Biodiesel 5 had been a success, with a reduction in emissions compared with regular diesel, and that its use by the private operators on the Creek was optional. If the smell of diesel on the water is part of your memory of Dubai, it may slowly become less familiar.

## Paying for the crossing

The traditional routes are cash only. The RTA's route page states that fares on both classic crossings are [paid in cash](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/promotion/new-marine-routes-abra), so carry small coins or notes. Card payments and travel cards are not the way to pay here.

The RTA also offers private charters on the traditional abras for a set time, which is a good option for a family or small group who want to see more of the Creek at their own pace. The current terms are on the same page.

## How the crossing works, step by step

1. **Find the station.** Abra stations sit right on the water, usually with a small covered waiting area and boats moored alongside.
2. **Join the queue.** Follow the line and the boatman's directions to the next boat.
3. **Step on carefully.** The deck sits low and moves with the water. Take your time, use the hand of the person helping you if offered, and step onto the flat deck rather than the edge.
4. **Sit on the bench.** Passengers sit along the central bench, facing outwards towards the water. Keep bags on your lap.
5. **Pay when asked.** Have your cash ready so you can hand it over to the boatman without fuss.
6. **Step off the same way.** Wait until the boat is steady against the quay before you stand.

The crossing itself is short. Use it: look upstream and downstream, watch the dhows being loaded, and see how the two banks face each other across the water. On the Deira side you will see the long wharf where cargo dhows tie up; Dubai Municipality finished redeveloping the [two-kilometre Deira wharf](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/may/18-05/dubai-municipality-completes-development-of-dubai-creek) in 2025, adding a public promenade as well as berths for working vessels.

## What a crossing feels like

For first-timers, the anticipation is usually bigger than the experience. The queue moves quickly, the boatman helps you on, and within moments the boat fills up and pulls away. The ride is smooth on a calm day, with spray only when other boats pass close by. The engine is loud, so conversation tends to wait until you land.

The best seats are at either end of the bench, where you get an open view along the Creek. In the middle you will face one bank or the other. Either way, keep your phone and camera on a strap or held firmly: the deck is open and anything dropped overboard is gone.

## Abras with children and limited mobility

The RTA is clear on rules for younger passengers. Its marine transport page states that [children under 12 are not allowed on marine transport unaccompanied](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), that children under 12 travelling with an adult must wear a life jacket, and that a valid ID for the child must be presented at the ticketing and information office. Bring your child's ID with you.

Hold small children firmly while stepping on and off, and keep them seated in the middle of the bench. The traditional boats have no rails along much of the edge.

The RTA's marine transport page lists accessibility features for some of its other services, but the traditional abra still involves a step down from the quay onto a moving deck. The newer fourth-generation boats are designed to the Dubai Universal Design Code, and the upgraded stations were built to meet Dubai's code for people of determination, but you cannot always choose which boat arrives. Anyone with limited mobility or using a wheelchair should check with the RTA or a local operator before planning a crossing, and allow time to wait for a suitable boat.

## What catches people out

- **No small cash.** The single most common problem. Break a note before you reach the quay.
- **Rushing the step.** Most slips happen when people stand before the boat is steady. Wait for the boatman's signal.
- **Big bags.** There is little room for luggage. Leave shopping bags until after the return crossing if you can.
- **The wrong station.** Bur Dubai, Al Fahidi, Deira Old Souq, Dubai Old Souq and Al Sabkha are close together, and routes differ. Check the board or ask before you queue.
- **Midday sun.** The quays and boats offer little shade. Cross early, late or in the cooler months.

## Best time for an abra crossing

Any time is fine for a quick crossing, but two windows stand out. Early morning is cooler and quieter, and the light on the water is soft. Late afternoon into sunset is livelier and more atmospheric, with the Creek glowing and the souks filling up on the Deira side.

There is little shade on the water or the quays in the middle of the day. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm, wear light-coloured, loose clothing and drink more cold drinks](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/). A hat and sunglasses make the crossing much more comfortable. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) shows when the weather is kindest.

## Photos and manners on board

The abra is a commuter boat as much as a tourist experience, and many of your fellow passengers are on their way to work. The UK Foreign Office advises that you [do not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Photograph the water, the boats and the skyline freely, and ask before you include a person. Dress for a public place: the same page advises dressing modestly in public areas. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has the detail.

During Ramadan, the Foreign Office notes that eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime may cause offence. Keep your water bottle in your bag on board and drink discreetly once you are off the boat and out of the way.

## Fitting the abra into your day

The crossing works best as part of a walk. Start in [Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood](/stories/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood/), cross from Bur Dubai, browse the [gold and spice souks](/stories/dubai-gold-and-spice-souks/) on the Deira side and cross back. Our [Old Dubai walking guide](/stories/old-dubai-walking-guide/) sets out the full loop.

On the Deira side, Dubai Municipality's [heritage tourist trails](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/august/24-08/dubai-municipality-completes-landmark-project) now link the Gold Souq, the Spice Souq and other traditional markets with signs and floor markings, so it is easy to find your way from the station into the lanes and back again.

## Quick answers

**Do I need to book an abra?**
No. On the traditional routes you simply queue at the station and board the next boat.

**How do I pay for the abra?**
In cash. The RTA lists fares on the traditional Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq and Dubai Old Souq to Al Sabkha routes as cash only.

**What time does the abra run?**
The RTA lists Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq from 6:00 am to midnight, and Dubai Old Souq to Al Sabkha 24 hours.

**Can children ride the abra?**
Yes, with an adult. The RTA says children under 12 cannot travel unaccompanied and must wear a life jacket on board, and you should carry a valid ID for the child.

**What does "abra" mean?**
The RTA explains that the name comes from an Arabic verb meaning "to cross".

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which abra station is best for our walking route, and where will you meet us on each side of the Creek?
- Can you arrange a private abra charter for our group, and how long would you suggest?
- Do you provide life jackets for children, and what ID should we bring for them?
- Is there a step-free or easier boarding option for anyone with limited mobility, or a way to wait for one of the newer boats?
- How do you adjust timings in the hotter months so we are not waiting on the quayside at midday?
