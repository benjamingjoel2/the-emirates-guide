Look north from the bridge and you see the low roofs of the old town, the Creek beyond them and the districts where Dubai began. Turn around and the skyline of glass towers runs south towards the desert. Same spot, same minute, two cities.

That double view is the whole idea of the Dubai Frame, a giant gold rectangle in Zabeel Park that literally frames the city. It is one of the easier Dubai attractions to plan: open every day, in a park with metro access, and short enough to fit into a half day. In brief: book ahead for weekends, go late in the afternoon if you want sunset from the top, and leave large bags behind. This Dubai Frame guide covers what you see, how the visit flows, the practical rules and how to build it into a day.

## What the Dubai Frame is

From a distance it looks like an empty picture frame stood on its end. Up close it is two towers joined at the top by a glass-floored bridge.

According to its official site, the Dubai Frame [measures 150 metres high and 95 metres wide](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/about-us), and its rectangular design was inspired by the "golden ratio" of 1.618 that many architects and artists consider an ideal balance. More than 15,000 square metres of gold-coloured stainless steel covers the structure, with a ring pattern inspired by the Expo 2020 Dubai logo. The same page says over 9,900 cubic metres of reinforced concrete, 2,000 tons of steel and 2,900 square metres of laminated glass went into its construction.

The point is symbolic as much as visual. The site describes the Frame as a [metaphorical bridge](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/about-us) between the emirate's past and its present, telling the story of Dubai from its early days to its plans for the future. It is run by Dubai Municipality, which gives it a calmer, more civic feel than some of the city's bigger attractions.

It also helps to understand where it sits. The Frame rises from the edge of Zabeel Park, a large public park between the older districts to the north and the towers of Sheikh Zayed Road to the south. That position is what makes the view work: you are standing almost exactly on the line where the old city gives way to the new one.

## Inside the Dubai Frame: how a visit flows

A visit is built as a short story in three chapters: past, present and future.

### Old Dubai Gallery

You start on the mezzanine level with a [multimedia exhibition](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/discover) charting the city's change from a fishing village to a modern metropolis, with insights into Emirati heritage. It sets the scene for the view you are about to see, so do not rush it. If you are travelling with children, this is a good moment to point out the old trading districts and the Creek, so they know what to look for from the top.

### The ride up

The lift to the Sky Deck carries visitors [48 floors in 75 seconds](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/about-us). The official site highlights its panoramic lifts, so stand near the glass if you can.

### Present Dubai: the Sky Deck

This is the main event. On the Sky Deck level you get [uninterrupted views from all sides](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/discover): "Old Dubai" to the north and "New Dubai" to the south.

A few things to look for:

- **To the north:** the older, lower neighbourhoods around the Creek, where the city's trading life began.
- **To the south:** the line of towers along Sheikh Zayed Road and Downtown.
- **Below you:** the green spread of Zabeel Park, a reminder that the Frame sits in a public park rather than a mall.
- **Under your feet:** the [luminous glass walkway](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/about-us) on the bridge, which is either the highlight or the part you edge around, depending on how you feel about heights.

The site also mentions interactive screens, a chance to identify Dubai's landmarks, and the option of a coffee more than 150 metres above the ground. Its discover page lists further [special experiences](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/discover), including a garden, a souvenir shop, a 360 view and a breakfast on the bridge, so check what is running when you book.

If you are nervous about the glass section, you do not have to cross it. The views are just as good from the solid floor either side, and many people simply watch others step out before deciding for themselves.

### Future Dubai Gallery

On the way down, visitors leave the lift onto the mezzanine and pass through a [swirl tunnel with audio and visual effects](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/discover), a short imagined glimpse of the city's future. It is brief and theatrical, and children tend to like it.

## Reading the view: a few tips

The Sky Deck rewards a little preparation. Many visitors rush to the south side for the towers, take a few photographs and leave, missing the quieter story on the other side of the bridge.

- **Start north.** Spend your first minutes looking towards the older districts. Low buildings, tighter streets and the line of the Creek tell you where the city started, and the Old Dubai Gallery you have just walked through gives you the background.
- **Then turn south.** The contrast is the point. Looking from the low-rise north to the towers in the south in a single turn of the head is the experience the Frame was designed to give.
- **Use the screens.** The interactive screens on the bridge help you put names to what you are seeing, which is useful if you are planning where to go next.
- **Look down last.** Once you have your bearings, step towards the glass walkway and look at the park below. It is the moment most people remember.

If the haze is heavy, the far horizon may disappear, but the old and new districts close to the park are usually still clear. That is one reason the Frame can work on days when a much higher viewpoint feels washed out.

## Access and older relatives

The visit is short and mostly indoors, with lifts doing the climbing for you, which makes it one of the more manageable viewpoints in the city for older travellers. The walk through the park from the gate or the metro is the part to plan for in the heat. Strollers have to be left at the Gate 4 counter, so bring a baby carrier if you have a very young child, and ask the Frame in advance if anyone in your group uses a wheelchair or tires easily.

## When to go

The Frame is [open all year round](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), including weekends and public holidays, with opening hours from 8:00 to 21:00. Timings may vary during Ramadan and on holidays, so check the official site if you are travelling then; our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains how the month changes the rhythm of the day. The UK Foreign Office notes that during Ramadan it may cause offence to [eat, drink, smoke or chew gum in public in the daytime](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), which is worth remembering in the park as well as inside.

Each time of day has its appeal:

- **Morning:** usually clear and calm, and a good way to see the old and new districts sharply defined before the heat haze builds.
- **Late afternoon into sunset:** the official site lists [sunset from the top of the bridge](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/about-us) among its highlights. Book a slot that gets you up there before the sun drops.
- **Evening:** the city lights come on, and the Frame lists an illuminated musical fountain among the things to enjoy nearby.

Weekends tend to be busier than weekdays, which is why the official FAQ pushes advance booking most strongly for those days. In the warmer months, remember that you will spend some time outdoors walking through the park to the entrance, so an early or late slot is kinder than the middle of the day.

## Booking tickets

You can buy tickets [at the main park entrance, on the Dubai Frame website or through its app](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/faq). The official FAQ strongly recommends buying in advance, especially at weekends, and asks visitors to arrive at least 10 to 15 minutes before their scheduled time.

A few details worth knowing:

- **Tickets are tied to a date.** The FAQ says a ticket is [valid for one day](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/faq), for entry on the date booked.
- **One entry per e-ticket.** Printing several copies of the same booking does not let more people in.
- **Missing confirmation?** The FAQ suggests checking your junk or spam folder first, and otherwise contacting the Frame's call centre, which it says is available around the clock on 800900.
- **Free entry for some visitors.** Infants under three and [People of Determination with up to two companions](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit) enter free. Companion tickets are issued at the ticket counter.
- **No private guides for general visitors.** The FAQ says private guided tours are [currently only offered for school trips](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/faq).

We do not quote ticket prices here; check the official site when you book.

## Rules to know before you go

The Frame's rules are short and sensible, but a couple catch visitors out. The official list includes:

- [No food or drink](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), no smoking and no pets.
- Toys and sharp tools are not allowed.
- Respectful clothing is expected.
- Items on display in the exhibitions must not be touched or removed.
- Do not lean on or climb the railings, and never sit children on them.
- Children under 12 must be with an adult at all times.
- Large bags and strollers must be left at the Gate 4 counter.

On bags, the FAQ adds that handbags are fine, but [shopping bags, sports bags and backpacks](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/faq) must go in the baggage room. Travel light if you can, and remember that children's toys count under the rules, so leave the scooter or the toy sword in the hotel.

On clothing, the Foreign Office advises visitors to [dress modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), with women covering shoulders and knees. Comfortable shoes help too, since you will be walking through the park and around the galleries.

The FAQ also confirms the Frame [welcomes People of Determination](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/faq). If anyone in your group has mobility needs, contact the Frame before your visit to talk through access, especially as strollers have to be left at the counter.

## Photography at the Frame

The Sky Deck is built for photographs, and the official site even lists taking selfies with the city's landmarks among its attractions. Glass brings reflections, so press your lens close to the window and turn off the flash. For a picture of the Frame itself, step back into the park: from a distance you can line up the opening so that it frames the towers behind.

Keep your camera on the view rather than on strangers. The Foreign Office warns visitors [not to photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

## Getting there

The Frame stands in [Zabeel Park at Gate 4](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), bordered by Sheikh Rashid Road, Sheikh Khalifa Bin Zayed Road and Sheikh Zayed Road. The official site says you can reach Zabeel Park through Al Jafiliya Metro Station. If you take a taxi, ask for Gate 4 specifically, since the park has several entrances.

On the metro, the Roads and Transport Authority notes [lift access to all metro station levels](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro) and dedicated spaces for wheelchair users on all trains. Trains also have cabins set aside for women and children and a Gold Class cabin, so read the signs on the platform before you board. ## Making a day of it

The Frame works best as a bridge between two halves of a Dubai day.

**Old first, then the Frame.** Spend the morning in the lanes of the historic district, using our [Al Fahidi guide](/stories/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood/), then come to the Frame in the afternoon and look back over the area you have just walked.

**The Frame, then Downtown.** Visit the Frame in daylight, then head south for the bigger view. Our [Burj Khalifa guide](/stories/burj-khalifa-visit-guide/) explains how the two compare and how to book a sunset slot there.

**With children.** The glass floor, fast lift and swirl tunnel go down well, and the park around it gives everyone room to run. See [Dubai with children](/stories/dubai-with-kids/) for more ideas, and our [Dubai destination page](/destinations/dubai/) for the wider city.

## Quick answers

**How tall is the Dubai Frame?** The official site gives its size as 150 metres high and 95 metres wide.

**What are the Dubai Frame opening hours?** It is open every day from 8:00 to 21:00, including weekends and public holidays. Hours may vary during Ramadan and on holidays.

**Do I need to book the Dubai Frame in advance?** The official FAQ highly recommends booking ahead, especially at weekends, and arriving 10 to 15 minutes before your slot.

**Where is the Dubai Frame and which metro station is closest?** It is in Zabeel Park at Gate 4, and the official site says you can reach the park through Al Jafiliya Metro Station.

**Can I bring food or a backpack?** No food or drink is allowed inside. Backpacks and shopping bags must be stored in the baggage room, though handbags are fine.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you book a slot that gets us onto the Sky Deck before sunset on our date?
- Will you drop us at Gate 4, and where will you collect us afterwards?
- How long should we allow for the galleries and the Sky Deck with children or older relatives?
- Are opening hours different on our dates because of Ramadan or a public holiday?
- Can you combine the Frame with old Dubai or Downtown so we are not crossing the city twice?
