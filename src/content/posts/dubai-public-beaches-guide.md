The lifeguard's flag snaps in the breeze, the sand is still cool underfoot, and the water is so clear you can see your feet. Behind you the city hums along the coast road. In front of you there is nothing but the Gulf.

Dubai's public beaches are one of the simplest pleasures in the city, and most visitors do not use them nearly enough. The short answer to where to swim: stick to the official public beaches managed by Dubai Municipality, swim only where lifeguards are on duty and flags show it is safe, and if you want a night swim, use one of the designated night-swimming beaches. This guide covers where to go, how the rules work, how a beach day unfolds and what to pack.

## Dubai's public beaches at a glance

Dubai's coastline mixes private hotel frontage with long stretches of public sand. The public beaches are managed by Dubai Municipality and open to everyone.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="dubai-public-beaches-guide-1" alts="A kitesurfer and kite silhouetted against the setting sun over choppy sea off Kite Beach, with a line of floats marking the swimming area in front" caption="Lines of floats mark the swimming areas, and the sea can be livelier than it looks from the sand."></photo-set>

They are also improving fast. Under a master plan approved in 2023, the length of Dubai's public beaches is set to grow [from 21 km to 105 km by 2040](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/may/25-05/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-master-plan-for-dubai-public-beaches). The same announcement says all of the emirate's public beaches have received Blue Flag certification, and that public beaches will have facilities that meet the needs of People of Determination. Among its short-term plans, it lists retail kiosks on Jumeirah Beach 2 and Umm Suqeim 1, new water sports facilities and smart safe lockers, so what you find on the sand may be better equipped than older guides suggest.

For a visitor, the practical upshot is simple. You do not need a hotel beach club to enjoy the sea in Dubai. The public beaches are open to everyone, many have lifeguards and facilities, and they are where you will see the city relaxing on its days off.

## Where to swim in Dubai

### The Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim beaches

The long run of sand along Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim is the classic choice for visitors. The water is usually calm, the beaches are wide, and many stretches have views towards the city's best-known seafront landmarks.

These are also the beaches where Dubai Municipality has opened [designated night-swimming areas](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1 (more on those below). Dubai Municipality's beach listings describe the [Jumeira Beach Centre](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai-beaches/) as open to the public.

### Al Mamzar, near the Sharjah border

At the northern end of the city, Al Mamzar is a favourite with families. Dubai Municipality lists the Al Mamzar beach park as open [every day from 8:00 to 22:00](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai-beaches/). The master plan also includes the [further development of Al Mamzar](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/may/25-05/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-master-plan-for-dubai-public-beaches), with new public facilities, walking and cycling tracks and mangrove planting along the Corniche section, and a 4,000-metre cycling track among its targets.

### Kite Beach for an active day

If you want more going on, Kite Beach in Jumeirah is the lively option. Visit Dubai sums it up as a place to [choose between watersports, beach bites and fun in the sun](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/places-to-visit/kite-beach). Expect runners, cyclists and kitesurfers, and a busier feel than the quieter stretches. If you swim here, keep well clear of the kites and boards and stay in the areas marked for swimmers.

### JBR, by the Marina

The beach in front of JBR is the busiest and most urban of the lot, backed by towers and a promenade of cafés. The Dubai Tram, which the Roads and Transport Authority says [runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram), makes it one of the easier beaches to reach without a taxi. Our [Dubai Marina and JBR guide](/stories/dubai-marina-and-jbr-guide/) covers it in detail.

### Jebel Ali, the one to watch

Further south, the master plan includes the [development of Jebel Ali Public Beach](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/may/25-05/mohammed-bin-rashid-approves-master-plan-for-dubai-public-beaches) as an eco-tourism destination, with mangrove planting, walking and cycling tracks and turtle enclosures visitors will be able to see. The plan also mentions family spaces, beach camping and a direct public bus route from Jebel Ali Metro Station. Check what has opened before you make the trip.

### Choosing between them

- **First swim, calm water:** the Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim beaches.
- **Families wanting space and facilities:** Al Mamzar beach park.
- **Something to watch while you sit:** Kite Beach.
- **Beach plus dinner without a car:** JBR.
- **A night swim:** one of the three designated night-swimming beaches.

## Lifeguards, flags and staying safe in the water

The Gulf usually looks gentle, which is exactly why people let their guard down.

The UK Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and can sweep even the strongest swimmer out to sea. It advises visitors to:

- check for warning signs, especially red flags, which mean swimming is dangerous
- only swim from approved beaches where there are lifeguards
- look for flags indicating it is safe to swim

A few habits of our own help too:

- **Swim between the markers** and near a lifeguard post.
- **Watch children constantly,** even in shallow water.
- **If you are caught in a current,** do not fight it head on. Swim parallel to the shore until you are out of it, then head back in, and raise an arm to signal for help.
- **Know the emergency number.** The Foreign Office's health advice says to [dial 998 and ask for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health), so save it in your phone before you hit the sand.

### The law on swimming times

This is now a legal matter as well as common sense. Dubai's Law No. (2) of 2026 on public safety, effective from 1 June 2026, requires the public to [observe permitted swimming times at beaches](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/march/06-03/mohammed-bin-rashid-issues-law-on-public-safety) and to avoid restricted areas reserved for staff or other unauthorised zones. The same announcement says the law covers safety standards for swimming pools and beaches, and that the public must follow safety instructions and cooperate with safety supervisors. In practice that means reading the signs at your beach and swimming only when and where they allow.

## Night swimming in Dubai

On a hot evening, a swim after dark is one of the nicest things you can do in Dubai, but only in the right place.

Dubai Municipality has opened three [night-swimming beaches](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/) in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1. Each is 800 metres long, with lighting, electronic screens showing safety information and qualified lifeguards with rescue equipment. Night swimming there runs from sunset to sunrise.

The Municipality's advice is clear: swim at night only in the designated zones, do not go into the sea from other areas, keep a constant eye on children and follow the lifeguards' instructions. It also asks beachgoers to keep the beaches clean.

## Beach rules visitors should know

Dubai's beaches are relaxed, but UAE law does not stop at the sand. The Foreign Office is direct on several points.

### Dress

It advises that [swimming attire should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Bring something to cover up for the walk to a café, a shop or a taxi. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more detail.

### Photos

Do not photograph people without their permission. The Foreign Office notes that [men have been arrested for photographing women on beaches](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Frame your shots around the sea and the skyline.

### Behaviour

Showing affection in public is frowned upon, and the Foreign Office says [you can be arrested for kissing in public](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It is also illegal to drink or be under the influence of alcohol in a public place anywhere in the UAE, and a public beach counts. During Ramadan, it adds, eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime and playing loud music may cause offence, so keep beach picnics and speakers for after sunset.

### Family beaches on holidays

During busy holidays some beaches may be reserved for families. During Eid Al Adha in 2025, for example, Dubai Municipality designated [Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3, Umm Suqeim 1 and Umm Suqeim 2](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/june/04-06/dubai-municipality-designates-four-beaches-for-families-during-eid-al-adha-holiday) exclusively for families, to manage visitor numbers when beaches are at their busiest. The same announcement says the Municipality deployed a safety and rescue team of 126 people and 100 inspectors for the holiday. If you are travelling over a holiday, check for announcements before you set off, and expect more crowds and more staff on the sand.

## How a beach day unfolds

A good Dubai beach day follows the sun rather than the clock.

**Early morning** is when locals walk, run and swim before work. The sand is cool, the light is soft and the water is usually at its calmest. If you are jet-lagged and awake early anyway, this is the best time of day to be on the beach.

**Late morning to mid-afternoon** is when the sun is fiercest. Even in the cooler months there is little shade on open sand. Many people retreat to a café, a mall or their hotel and come back later.

**Late afternoon and sunset** bring the crowds back. Families arrive with picnics, the light turns gold and the skyline starts to glow. Remember that ordinary daytime beaches have permitted swimming times, so check the signs before you go in as the light fades.

**After dark**, if you want to keep swimming, move to one of the designated night-swimming beaches.

### What to pack

- Hat, high-factor sun cream and sunglasses.
- More water than you think you need.
- A cover-up or shirt for leaving the sand.
- Water shoes if your feet are sensitive to hot sand.
- A bag for your rubbish, so you leave the beach as clean as you found it.
- Your phone with the emergency number saved, kept somewhere dry and out of the sun.

## Beaches with children

Dubai's public beaches are generally easy with children: the water along most of the coast is calm, the sand is soft and families are everywhere. The risks are the ordinary ones, and they are easy to underestimate on holiday.

- **One adult on water duty.** Agree who is watching the children in the water at any moment, and swap rather than both looking away. The Municipality's night-swimming advice stresses constant monitoring of children, and the same applies by day.
- **Shade first.** Set up your spot near shade or bring your own, and plan regular breaks out of the sun.
- **Stay near the lifeguards.** If a child wanders, a lifeguard post is a natural meeting point, so point it out when you arrive.
- **Choose the beach to suit the age.** Toddlers are happiest on a quiet stretch with gentle water. Older children may prefer the bustle of Kite Beach or JBR, where there is more to watch.

During public holidays, family-designated beaches can be a calmer choice if they are announced for your dates.

## Accessible beach days

The master plan commits to facilities that meet the needs of People of Determination across Dubai's public beaches, but provision still varies from one beach to the next. If anyone in your group uses a wheelchair or has limited mobility, ask your hotel or operator which beaches have step-free access, accessible toilets and paths across the sand, and check again close to your trip, since facilities are being added as the plan rolls out.

## Timing your beach day

The sun is the biggest hazard on a Dubai beach. Even in the cooler months, there is little shade on open sand, so bring a hat, high-factor sun cream and more water than you think you need.

- **Cooler months:** beach days can run from morning to sunset.
- **Warmer months:** go early, or come back in the evening for a night swim.
- **Weekends and holidays:** arrive early for space and parking.

## Quick answers

**Are Dubai's public beaches open to tourists?** Yes. Dubai Municipality's public beaches are open to everyone, though beach parks such as Al Mamzar have set opening hours, from 8:00 to 22:00 every day.

**Can you swim at night in Dubai?** Yes, but only at designated night-swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1, where swimming runs from sunset to sunrise with lifeguards on duty.

**What does a red flag mean on a Dubai beach?** The UK Foreign Office says red flags mean swimming is dangerous. Only swim at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags showing it is safe.

**Can I wear a bikini in Dubai?** Swimwear is fine on beaches and at pools, but the Foreign Office advises wearing it only there, so cover up when you leave the sand.

**Is it illegal to swim outside permitted times?** Dubai's 2026 public safety law requires the public to observe permitted swimming times at beaches and to keep out of restricted areas.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which public beach would suit our group best, and is it lifeguarded at the times we plan to swim?
- Will any beaches be reserved for families or closed for works on our dates?
- If we book a water sport, what safety briefing, life jackets and supervision are included?
- Can you plan our beach time around the heat, with shade and water available?
- Where is the nearest designated night-swimming beach to our hotel?
