A giraffe leans down over the rail, a small hand holds out a leaf, and for a second nobody in the family is thinking about the flight home. Dubai has a reputation as a city of malls and skyscrapers, but explore Dubai with children and you find animals, fountains, abras, sand and long evenings outdoors.

Dubai with children works well, as long as you plan around two things: the heat and the distances. The short answer is to base yourselves somewhere easy, keep outdoor time to mornings and evenings, and mix big days out with pool or beach time. This guide covers the places families enjoy most, a sample day that works with young children, how to get around with little ones, and the safety and health checks parents should make before they fly.

## Planning Dubai with children: the golden rules

- **Pace the day around the sun.** The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) and says to check on children in hot weather, as they are more at risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke.
- **Watch for the signs.** The NHS lists symptoms of heat exhaustion including tiredness, headache, dizziness and being very thirsty, and says they are often the same in adults and children. Cool them down and give fluids.
- **Dress them for it.** The same NHS page advises light, loose clothing and more cold drinks in hot weather. Hats and a refillable bottle for each child save a lot of complaints.
- **Choose one big outing a day.** Distances are long, and a taxi ride across the city can take a chunk of the afternoon.
- **Pick your season.** Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) shows when days are cooler and more comfortable for outdoor time.

## Animals, play and learning

### Dubai Safari Park

The UAE government portal lists [Dubai Safari Park and Children's City](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) among the activities to try with children in Dubai. The safari park, in Al Warqa, says it is [open every day from 9am to 6pm, with last entry at 5pm](https://www.dubaisafari.ae/), and offers free entry for children under 3 and for people of determination.

Its six zones run from African and Asian villages to an Arabian Desert Safari, where you can see native wildlife such as the Arabian oryx and sand gazelle. For smaller children, the Kids Farm is the highlight: the park describes it as a place where children can meet, pet and feed friendly animals. Go at opening time, before the heat builds, and save the drive-through section for when legs get tired.

<photo-set layout="full" keys="dubai-with-kids-1" alts="A gibbon swinging from a rope above the rocks of its enclosure at Dubai Safari Park" caption="Dubai Safari Park in Al Warqa is laid out in six themed zones."></photo-set>

The park is big. Dubai Municipality describes it as spanning [119 hectares and home to approximately 3,000 animals](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/), so do not try to see everything with young children. Pick two or three zones and the farm, and use the shuttle and drive-through journeys to rest little legs.

The live presentations are worth building the visit around. The park says there are [five live shows every open day, free with a park ticket](https://www.dubaisafari.ae/), each running for about 30 minutes, and suggests taking your seat 10 minutes early. Times can change on the day, so check the board at the entrance when you arrive. Hands-on encounters, such as feeding a giraffe or a gentle pony walk for little riders, have limited spots, and the park advises adding them when you book.

### Children's City

Dubai Municipality describes Children's City as an educational city [devoted to children between two and fifteen years old](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/), where they can investigate, explore and play. It is an indoor option for the middle of a hot day, and it works well for children who like to touch and try things rather than look.

### The Dubai Frame

Children usually love a glass lift and a view, and the Dubai Frame delivers both. Its visitor rules are worth knowing before you go: [children under 12 must be accompanied by an adult](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit) at all times, infants under 3 enter free, and large bags and strollers must be left at the Gate 4 counter. Food and drink are not allowed inside, toys are not allowed either, and you should not sit children on the railing. It stands in Zabeel Park, reached through Al Jafiliya Metro Station, so you can follow the visit with a run around on the grass.

## Parks, flamingos and open space

### Public parks

When everyone needs to run around, head to a park. Dubai Municipality says there are [more than 190 public parks in Dubai](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/), with facilities including sports fields, playgrounds for children and people of determination, and barbecue areas. Late afternoon is the best time, when the grass is shaded and families come out. Bring a picnic blanket, snacks and water, and you have an easy few hours that cost almost nothing.

### Flamingos at Ras Al Khor

For a quieter outing, Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary is close to the city centre. Dubai Municipality's flamingo hide promises [the pink of thousands of flamingoes all year round](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/book-a-visit/), and says families and individuals can walk in at any time during opening hours, with walk-in registration required. The hides are quiet zones with [no phones, food or drinks](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/plan-your-visit/), and only 15 people are allowed inside a hide at a time. The municipality also asks visitors not to sit on the window plank, not to rush or push, and to give priority to people of determination.

This suits children who can whisper. Bring binoculars and talk through what to look for before you go in, so the visit feels like a spotting game rather than a test of patience. The municipality's motto for the site is a good one to share with children: take nothing but memories and leave nothing but footprints.

## Beach days with little ones

Dubai's public beaches are a big part of a family trip, and a few rules keep them safe. The UK Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and checking for red flags, which mean swimming is dangerous. It also says swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools, so pack a cover-up for the walk back.

If daytime heat makes the beach hard work, try swimming after dark. Dubai Municipality has opened [night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting and qualified lifeguards, and stresses the constant monitoring of children and following lifeguards' instructions. It says night swimming runs from sunset to sunrise, and asks swimmers to stay in the designated zones. Our guide to [where to stay in Dubai](/stories/where-to-stay-in-dubai/) shows which areas sit closest to these beaches.

## A sample day with young children

Here is a pattern many families find works. Start early with the big outing, such as the safari park at opening time or the Dubai Frame. Have an early lunch somewhere air-conditioned, then head back for a nap or pool time through the hottest part of the day. In the late afternoon, go to a park or the beach, when the sun is lower and other families are out. Eat dinner early, and if everyone still has energy, finish with a short evening walk by the water or a night swim at one of the lit beaches.

The point is to avoid stacking two big sights back to back. Children cope well with one busy morning and one gentle evening. They cope badly with long taxi rides between attractions in the afternoon heat.

### What to pack for the day

A small day bag makes a big difference. Pack a refillable bottle for each child, sun hats, a light long-sleeved layer for air-conditioned buses, trains and malls, which can feel cold after the heat outside, and a cover-up for the walk back from the beach. Snacks are useful, but remember that some places do not allow food, including the bird hides at Ras Al Khor and the inside of the Dubai Frame, and that eating is not allowed where prohibited on the metro. A copy of each child's passport is handy on boat days, and a printed note of your hotel's name and address helps if a child needs to show it to someone. For pushchair users, a compact, foldable model is easier on abras and in taxis, and remember that the Frame asks for strollers to be left at its counter.

## Getting around with children

### Metro and tram

The metro is air-conditioned and easy with older children. RTA has [cabins reserved for women and children](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), which can feel calmer at busy times. Its rules say children under 8 cannot use public transport without an adult, and children aged 8 to 11 need a permit from their parents. Eating and drinking are not allowed where prohibited, so keep snacks for the platform exit. With a pushchair, it helps that RTA lists lift access to all metro station levels.

### Boats

An abra across the Creek is a hit with most children. If you have a pushchair, RTA describes its Dubai Ferry as [stroller-friendly, with restrooms](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), which makes it a comfortable way to see the coast.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="dubai-with-kids-7" alts="A small water bus travelling along Dubai Creek beside the waterfront" caption="Boat trips on the Creek are a favourite with children."></photo-set>

The boats have their own rules for children. RTA says children under 12 [cannot use its marine transport without an adult](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), that children under 12 travelling with an adult must wear a life jacket on board, and that a valid ID for the child must be shown at the ticketing office at marine stations. Carry a copy of each child's passport on boat days.

### Taxis

The UAE government portal says there are [special taxis for women and children, and for families](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport), which can be hailed or booked in advance. If you need child seats, ask when you book, or arrange transfers through your operator. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) covers nol cards and the rest of the network.

## Health, medicines and hotel rules

### Medicines

Pack children's medicines in their original packaging, and check the rules first. The UK Foreign Office says some UK prescribed and over-the-counter medicines are considered controlled substances in the UAE, and that you must [get approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) to bring them. It says applications can be made online and that the UAE authorities advise they are processed within 5 working days, so do this well before you fly.

### Insurance and emergencies

The Foreign Office stresses having [appropriate travel insurance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) for local treatment, and says to dial 998 for an ambulance. It lists [999 for the police](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/getting-help) as well. Write these numbers down for older children, and agree a meeting point at busy places in case anyone gets separated.

### Teenagers and hotels

If you have older teenagers, note that the Foreign Office says you [cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It also says the legal age for buying and drinking alcohol is 21 in Dubai, and that it is illegal to photograph people without their permission, which is worth mentioning to teenagers who film everything on their phones.

## Ramadan and family days

If you travel during Ramadan, the Foreign Office notes that eating or drinking in public in the daytime [may cause offence](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and suggests checking the opening hours of shops and restaurants. With children, it is polite to hand out snacks and drinks discreetly, away from busy public spaces. Evenings are lively and family-friendly, and the change of rhythm can be an interesting thing for children to see.

## Adding the desert and the mountains

A desert trip can be a family highlight, but dune driving does not suit everyone. ## Quick answers

**Is Dubai good for young children?** Yes, with planning. Parks, the safari park, Children's City and night swimming beaches all suit families, but keep outdoor time out of the hottest hours.

**Can children travel alone on the Dubai Metro?** RTA says children under 8 cannot use public transport without an adult, and those aged 8 to 11 need a permit from their parents.

**Do children need life jackets on Dubai's boats?** RTA says children under 12 must wear a life jacket on its marine transport, and cannot travel on it without an adult.

**Can I take a pushchair to the Dubai Frame?** Strollers and large bags must be left at the Gate 4 counter, and children under 12 must be accompanied by an adult.

**Do I need approval for children's medicines?** Possibly. The UK Foreign Office says some medicines are controlled in the UAE and need advance approval from the Ministry of Health.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you plan each day around naps, meals and the hottest hours?
- Do your vehicles have child seats suitable for our children's ages?
- Which activities have minimum ages or height limits, and what are the alternatives?
- Which beaches near our hotel have lifeguards on duty when we plan to swim?
- Is a desert trip suitable for our youngest child, and can the dune driving be skipped?
- Where can we rest indoors near each stop if the children need a break?
