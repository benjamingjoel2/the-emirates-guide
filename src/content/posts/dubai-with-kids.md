A giraffe leans down over the rail, a small hand holds out a leaf, and for a second nobody in the family is thinking about the flight home. Dubai has a reputation as a city of malls and skyscrapers, but explore Dubai with children and you find animals, fountains, abras, sand and long evenings outdoors.

Dubai with children works well, as long as you plan around two things: the heat and the distances. The short answer is to base yourselves somewhere easy, keep outdoor time to mornings and evenings, and mix big days out with pool or beach time. This guide covers the places families enjoy most, how to get around with young ones, and the safety and health checks parents should make before they fly.

## Planning Dubai with children: the golden rules

- **Pace the day around the sun.** The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) and says to check on children in hot weather, as they are more at risk of heat exhaustion and heatstroke.
- **Watch for the signs.** The NHS lists symptoms of heat exhaustion including tiredness, headache, dizziness and being very thirsty, and says they are often the same in adults and children. Cool them down and give fluids.
- **Choose one big outing a day.** Distances are long, and a taxi ride across the city can take a chunk of the afternoon.
- **Pick your season.** Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) shows when days are cooler and more comfortable for outdoor time.

## Animals, play and learning

### Dubai Safari Park

The UAE government portal lists [Dubai Safari Park and Children's City](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) among the activities to try with children in Dubai. The safari park, in Al Warqa, says it is [open every day from 9am to 6pm, with last entry at 5pm](https://www.dubaisafari.ae/), and offers free entry for children under 3.

Its six zones run from African and Asian villages to an Arabian Desert Safari, where you can see native wildlife such as the Arabian oryx and sand gazelle. For smaller children, the Kids Farm is the highlight: the park describes it as a place where children can meet, pet and feed friendly animals. Go at opening time, before the heat builds, and save the drive-through section for when legs get tired.

### Children's City

Dubai Municipality describes Children's City as an educational city [devoted to children between two and fifteen years old](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/), where they can investigate, explore and play. It is an indoor option for the middle of a hot day.

### The Dubai Frame

Children usually love a glass lift and a view, and the Dubai Frame delivers both. Its visitor rules are worth knowing before you go: [children under 12 must be accompanied by an adult](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit) at all times, infants under 3 enter free, and large bags and strollers must be left at the Gate 4 counter. Food and drink are not allowed inside, and you should not sit children on the railing. It stands in Zabeel Park, reached through Al Jafiliya Metro Station.

## Parks, flamingos and open space

### Public parks

When everyone needs to run around, head to a park. Dubai Municipality says there are [more than 190 public parks in Dubai](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/), with facilities including sports fields, playgrounds for children and people of determination, and barbecue areas. Late afternoon is the best time, when the grass is shaded and families come out.

### Flamingos at Ras Al Khor

For a quieter outing, Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary is close to the city centre. Dubai Municipality's flamingo hide promises [the pink of thousands of flamingoes all year round](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/book-a-visit/). The hides are quiet zones with [no phones, food or drinks](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/plan-your-visit/), so this suits children who can whisper. Bring binoculars and talk through what to look for before you go in.

## Beach days with little ones

Dubai's public beaches are a big part of a family trip, and a few rules keep them safe. The UK Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and checking for red flags, which mean swimming is dangerous.

If daytime heat makes the beach hard work, try swimming after dark. Dubai Municipality has opened [night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting and qualified lifeguards, and stresses the constant monitoring of children and following lifeguards' instructions. Our guide to [where to stay in Dubai](/stories/where-to-stay-in-dubai/) shows which areas sit closest to these beaches.

## Getting around with children

### Metro and tram

The metro is air-conditioned and easy with older children. RTA has [cabins reserved for women and children](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), which can feel calmer at busy times. Its rules say children under 8 cannot use public transport without an adult, and children aged 8 to 11 need a permit from their parents. Eating and drinking are not allowed where prohibited, so keep snacks for the platform exit.

### Boats

An abra across the Creek is a hit with most children. If you have a pushchair, RTA describes its Dubai Ferry as [stroller-friendly, with restrooms](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), which makes it a comfortable way to see the coast.

### Taxis

The UAE government portal says there are [special taxis for women and children, and for families](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport), which can be hailed or booked in advance. If you need child seats, ask when you book, or arrange transfers through your operator.

## Health, medicines and hotel rules

### Medicines

Pack children's medicines in their original packaging, and check the rules first. The UK Foreign Office says some UK prescribed and over-the-counter medicines are considered controlled substances in the UAE, and that you must [get approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) to bring them.

### Insurance and emergencies

The Foreign Office stresses having [appropriate travel insurance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) for local treatment, and says to dial 998 for an ambulance.

### Teenagers and hotels

If you have older teenagers, note that the Foreign Office says you [cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

## Quick answers

**Is Dubai good for young children?** Yes, with planning. Parks, the safari park, Children's City and night swimming beaches all suit families, but keep outdoor time out of the hottest hours.

**Can children travel alone on the Dubai Metro?** RTA says children under 8 cannot use public transport without an adult, and those aged 8 to 11 need a permit from their parents.

**Can I take a pushchair to the Dubai Frame?** Strollers and large bags must be left at the Gate 4 counter, and children under 12 must be accompanied by an adult.

**Do I need approval for children's medicines?** Possibly. The UK Foreign Office says some medicines are controlled in the UAE and need advance approval from the Ministry of Health.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you plan each day around naps, meals and the hottest hours?
- Do your vehicles have child seats suitable for our children's ages?
- Which activities have minimum ages or height limits, and what are the alternatives?
- Which beaches near our hotel have lifeguards on duty when we plan to swim?
- Is a desert trip suitable for our youngest child, and can the dune driving be skipped?
- Where can we rest indoors near each stop if the children need a break?
