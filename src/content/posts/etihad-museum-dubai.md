On 2 December 1971, a group of rulers gathered in a modest guest palace in Dubai. There they signed a constitution, declared a new country and raised its flag for the first time. The building still stands. Beside it, a museum tells the story of how it happened.

Etihad Museum is the place to understand the UAE's founding. "Etihad" means union, and the museum sits next to Union House, where the declaration was made. It is not the flashiest attraction in Dubai, and that is part of its appeal: it is calm, thoughtful and full of the human detail behind a nation that is still young. If you want to know why there are seven emirates, how they came together and who brought them together, this is where to start. Here is the story, what to see, and how to plan a visit.

## Union House: a small building with a large story

Union House began life as the Guest Palace. According to the UAE Ministry of Culture, as reported by the Dubai Media Office, it was commissioned by Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, then Ruler of Dubai, and [completed in 1965](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/july/16-07/ministry-of-culture-unveils-union-house-plaque). In the 1960s and 1970s it hosted Gulf leaders and dignitaries, and it was the venue for meetings that helped shape the Union. On 2 December 1971, it hosted the declaration of the Union and the signing of the UAE Constitution.

The architecture is simple and of its time: two interlocking structures supported by cylindrical concrete columns, with glass and aluminium façades. In July 2026, the Ministry of Culture made Union House the first listed site on the UAE's new National Register of Modern Architectural Heritage, and the same report notes that it is now part of the museum, where it preserves and presents the story of the founding.

The flag outside carries its own weight. The Dubai Media Office describes Union House as the place where the founding fathers [signed the declaration of the Union in 1971 and raised the UAE flag for the first time](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/december/03-12/al-etihad-parade-brings-together-citizens-and-residents-in-a-moving).

## How the UAE came together

The museum's story is a story of negotiation, and it helps to know the outline before you go in.

- **February 1968.** The UAE government says that on [18 February 1968, Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan and Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum concluded an agreement on a federation between Abu Dhabi and Dubai](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/founders-of-the-union). Days later, the rulers of nine states held a [constitutional conference in Dubai from 25 to 27 February 1968](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/history).
- **July 1971.** On 18 July 1971, the rulers of six emirates decided to form a union, according to the same history page.
- **December 1971.** The UAE [formally emerged on the international stage on 2 December 1971](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/founders-of-the-union), and the rulers unanimously elected Sheikh Zayed as its first President.
- **February 1972.** Ras Al Khaimah acceded to the new federation on 10 February 1972, completing the seven emirates you know today.

Etihad Museum's own focus matches this timeline. The Dubai Media Office says its archive concentrates [mainly on the period from 1968 to 1974](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/october/26-10/etihad-museum-research-grant-programme), and that the museum preserves the founding fathers' legacy and their role in forming the Union. For an overview of the seven emirates themselves, see our [first-timer's guide to the UAE](/stories/uae-first-timers-guide-seven-emirates/).

## Inside Etihad Museum

The museum building is worth noticing before you go in. Dubai Culture has described it as having [a unique architectural aesthetic that distinguishes it from any other touristic or heritage landmark in the UAE](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2020/may/03-05/dubai-culture-provides-guided-tours-and-virtual-workshops-at-etihad-museum-for-school-students). The same release refers to interactive exhibits, films and photographs across its sections, alongside historical collections and buildings.

Expect a museum of photographs, archive material and film rather than grand objects. The pleasure lies in the detail: the faces of the rulers in old photographs, the record of their meetings, and the sense of a few short years in which a great deal was decided. Give yourself time to read and watch, and do not rush the films.

### A visit that works for most travellers

- **History lovers** will get the most from it, especially with a guided tour.
- **Families with older children** can use it to explain why the UAE has seven emirates and a shared flag.
- **Anyone short on time** can pair it with a walk to see Union House and the flag outside.

## Planning your Etihad Museum visit

The museum is run by Dubai Culture. Opening hours, ticketing and tour times can change, so check Dubai Culture's official website or call ahead before you set out. We have not listed hours or prices here because we could not confirm them on an official page at the time of writing.

A few practical points:

- **Getting there.** Confirm the address and the best entrance when you check opening times. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) covers the options.
- **Dress.** This is a national landmark. The UK Foreign Office's general guidance is to [dress modestly in public areas, with women covering shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.
- **Photography.** Follow signs inside about where photography is allowed, and remember the Foreign Office advice not to [photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).
- **Pair it well.** Etihad Museum makes a natural half-day with Old Dubai, where the city's own story began beside the Creek. The UAE government notes that Dubai was established in [1833, when members of the Bani Yas tribe led by the Maktoum family settled by the creek](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai). Our [Old Dubai walking guide](/stories/old-dubai-walking-guide/) picks up that thread.

## Visiting around National Day

The UAE's National Day marks the founding on 2 December 1971, and it is the most meaningful time of year to see Union House. The UAE government lists [National Day as a two-day public holiday on 2 and 3 December](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays).

Expect flags on buildings and cars, celebrations across the country and a strong sense of national pride. Around Union House, it can be especially busy. In December 2025, for example, the Dubai Media Office reported an [Al Etihad Parade that started at Union House and ended at Burj Al Arab](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/december/03-12/al-etihad-parade-brings-together-citizens-and-residents-in-a-moving). If you are in Dubai at this time, check the museum's plans in advance and allow extra travel time. Our guide to [UAE National Day for visitors](/stories/uae-national-day-for-visitors/) explains what to expect, and our [food and culture stories](/food-culture/) cover more of the traditions you may see.

## Quick answers

**What is Etihad Museum?**
A Dubai museum about the founding of the UAE, built beside Union House, where the Union was declared and the constitution signed on 2 December 1971. Its archive focuses mainly on 1968 to 1974.

**What is Union House?**
Originally the Guest Palace, completed in 1965, it is where the declaration of the Union and the signing of the UAE Constitution took place, and where the UAE flag was raised for the first time.

**When was the UAE founded?**
On 2 December 1971. Ras Al Khaimah joined on 10 February 1972, bringing the federation to seven emirates.

**Is National Day a good time to visit?**
It is the most meaningful time, with a public holiday on 2 and 3 December, but expect crowds and check the museum's plans in advance.

**What are the opening hours?**
Check Dubai Culture's official website before you go, as hours and tour times can change.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you include a guided tour of Etihad Museum, and does it also cover Union House and the flag outside?
- How long should we allow inside, and is the visit suitable for the ages of the children in our group?
- What else would you pair with the museum on the same day, and in which order?
- How will National Day celebrations affect access, traffic and timings if we visit in early December?
- Is the museum fully accessible for anyone using a wheelchair or with limited mobility?
