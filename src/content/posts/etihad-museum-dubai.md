On 2 December 1971, a group of rulers gathered in a modest guest palace in Dubai. There they signed a constitution, declared a new country and raised its flag for the first time. The building still stands. Beside it, a museum tells the story of how it happened.

Etihad Museum is the place to understand the UAE's founding. "Etihad" means union, and the museum sits next to Union House, where the declaration was made. It is not the flashiest attraction in Dubai, and that is part of its appeal: it is calm, thoughtful and full of the human detail behind a nation that is still young. If you want to know why there are seven emirates, how they came together and who brought them together, this is where to start. Here is the story, what to look for in the building and the galleries, and how to plan a visit that suits your group.

## Union House: a small building with a large story

Union House began life as the Guest Palace. According to the UAE Ministry of Culture, as reported by the Dubai Media Office, it was commissioned by Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, then Ruler of Dubai, and [completed in 1965](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/july/16-07/ministry-of-culture-unveils-union-house-plaque). In the 1960s and 1970s it hosted Gulf leaders and dignitaries, and it was the venue for meetings that helped shape the Union. On 2 December 1971, it hosted the declaration of the Union and the signing of the UAE Constitution.

The architecture is simple and of its time: two interlocking structures supported by cylindrical concrete columns, with glass and aluminium façades. In July 2026, the Ministry of Culture made Union House the first listed site on the UAE's new National Register of Modern Architectural Heritage, and the same report notes that it is now part of the museum, where it preserves and presents the story of the founding.

The flag outside carries its own weight. The Dubai Media Office describes Union House as the place where the founding fathers [signed the declaration of the Union in 1971 and raised the UAE flag for the first time](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/december/03-12/al-etihad-parade-brings-together-citizens-and-residents-in-a-moving).

## How the UAE came together

The museum's story is a story of negotiation, and it helps to know the outline before you go in.

- **January 1968.** The UAE government says that on [16 January 1968, after the announcement of British withdrawal from east of Suez and the Gulf](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/founders-of-the-union), Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan went to Dubai to discuss the next steps with Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum.
- **February 1968.** On 18 February 1968, the two concluded an agreement on a federation between Abu Dhabi and Dubai, and called for a wider federation that would include the seven emirates of the Trucial States as well as Qatar and Bahrain. The rulers of these nine states held a [constitutional conference in Dubai from 25 to 27 February 1968](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/history), which produced an 11-point agreement as the basis for a federation.
- **July 1971.** On 18 July 1971, the rulers of six emirates decided to form a union, according to the same history page.
- **December 1971.** The UAE formally emerged on the international stage on 2 December 1971, and the rulers unanimously elected Sheikh Zayed as its first President.
- **February 1972.** Ras Al Khaimah acceded to the new federation on 10 February 1972, completing the seven emirates you know today.

The deadline mattered. The same history page explains that in early 1968 the British declared their intention to withdraw from the Gulf by the end of 1971, which set a deadline for the rulers to agree on how they would govern together. Much of what you see in the museum, from meeting records to photographs of rulers deep in conversation, comes from that pressured stretch of time. Keep it in mind and the calm of the galleries feels all the more striking.

Etihad Museum's own focus matches this timeline. The Dubai Media Office says its archive concentrates [mainly on the period from 1968 to 1974](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/october/26-10/etihad-museum-research-grant-programme), and that the museum preserves the founding fathers' legacy and their role in forming the Union. For an overview of the seven emirates themselves, see our [first-timer's guide to the UAE](/stories/uae-first-timers-guide-seven-emirates/).

## Reading the building

Before you go inside, spend a few minutes outside. When the museum opened, The National reported that its location is marked by [a huge UAE flag atop a 123-metre golden pole on Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/etihad-museum-a-glimpse-into-the-foundation-of-a-nation-1.70179), and that the building is designed in the shape of a manuscript, inspired by the Unification Agreement, with seven leaning columns representing the pens that signed the declaration of the Constitution in 1971. The same report quoted the museum's director as saying that a body of water near the flag was created to match the shape of the original shoreline at the time the Union was conceived.

The grounds hold three main buildings: Union House, known in Arabic as Dar Al Etihad; the Guest Palace, where the rulers met before the signing; and the Visitors Pavilion, which houses the permanent exhibitions. The National described the restored Union House as oval-shaped, and said the museum's logo is inspired by its shape. Inside the Guest Palace, it reported, you can see Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum's majlis, his private office and a large dining room, although permission was needed to enter at the time. Ask at the desk what is open on the day you visit.

The report also said the museum was a collaboration between the Roads and Transport Authority, which handled design, development and construction, and Dubai Culture, which manages the site. Knowing the layout changes how you walk through it. You move from the modern pavilion into the story, then out to the low, quiet buildings where it actually happened.

## Inside Etihad Museum

The museum building is worth noticing from the inside too. Dubai Culture has described it as having [a unique architectural aesthetic that distinguishes it from any other touristic or heritage landmark in the UAE](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2020/may/03-05/dubai-culture-provides-guided-tours-and-virtual-workshops-at-etihad-museum-for-school-students). The same release refers to interactive exhibits, films and photographs across its sections, alongside historical collections and buildings.

Expect a museum of photographs, archive material and film rather than grand objects. The pleasure lies in the detail: the faces of the rulers in old photographs, the record of their meetings, and the sense of a few short years in which a great deal was decided. Give yourself time to read and watch, and do not rush the films.

At opening, the permanent exhibitions in the Visitors Pavilion spanned ten halls, with names such as the Founding Fathers, the Interactive Timeline Hall, the Seeds of Unity and the Constitution. The National described rare photographs and films, personal belongings of the founding fathers, military uniforms and medals, and reproductions from historical newspapers, alongside interactive technology throughout. Galleries can be rearranged over time, so treat this as a guide to the kind of material you will see rather than a fixed list.

### What to look out for

- **Personal objects.** Glasses, rings and other belongings of the rulers bring the founding fathers down to human scale. Children often find these the most memorable part.
- **The timeline.** Use it early in your visit to fix the key dates in your head, then come back to it at the end.
- **Newspapers and photographs.** Old front pages show how the founding looked to people at the time, before anyone knew how the story would turn out.
- **Quotations on the walls.** Words of the founders appear throughout the galleries, and they are a good way to understand the values the museum wants to pass on.

The museum also runs temporary exhibitions, so the experience can change from one visit to the next. In 2026, for example, Dubai Culture showed the retrospective [Observers of Change at Etihad Museum until 30 June 2026](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/january/19-01/dubai-culture-launches-dubai-art-season-2026). Check what is on before you go.

### A visit that works for most travellers

- **History lovers** will get the most from it, especially with a guided tour.
- **Families with older children** can use it to explain why the UAE has seven emirates and a shared flag.
- **Anyone short on time** can pair it with a walk to see Union House and the flag outside.

## How a visit unfolds

Most visits follow the shape of the building. You arrive on the plaza, take in the pavilion, the flag and the low white Union House, then head down into the galleries. The first part sets the scene with the founders and the period before the Union. The story then moves through the negotiations of 1968 to 1971 and on to the early years of the new country.

Allow plenty of time for a thoughtful visit, and more if you want to watch every film or join a guided tour. Finish outside at Union House, which feels very different once you know what happened inside. If the weather is kind, linger on the plaza before you leave.

## Planning your Etihad Museum visit

The museum is run by Dubai Culture. Opening hours, ticketing and tour times can change, so check Dubai Culture's official website or call ahead before you set out. We have not listed hours or prices here because we could not confirm them on an official page at the time of writing.

A few practical points:

- **Getting there.** Confirm the address and the best entrance when you check opening times. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) covers the options.
- **Dress.** This is a national landmark. The UK Foreign Office's general guidance is to [dress modestly in public areas, with women covering shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.
- **Photography.** Follow signs inside about where photography is allowed, and remember the Foreign Office advice not to [photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).
- **Ramadan.** The same Foreign Office page notes that eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime may cause offence during the holy month, and suggests checking opening hours, which may differ.
- **Pair it well.** Etihad Museum makes a natural half-day with Old Dubai, where the city's own story began beside the Creek. The UAE government notes that Dubai was established in [1833, when members of the Bani Yas tribe led by the Maktoum family settled by the creek](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai), and that Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, who commissioned Union House, ruled Dubai from 1958 to 1990. Our [Old Dubai walking guide](/stories/old-dubai-walking-guide/) picks up that thread.

## Families and accessibility

For children, the museum works best with a little preparation. Before you go, explain that the UAE is made of seven emirates that agreed to join together, and ask them to find each emirate's ruler in the photographs or each emirate's name on the maps. Interactive screens help hold attention, but younger children may tire of a museum built around documents and film, so plan a short visit with a break on the plaza.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="etihad-museum-dubai-5" alts="A quotation displayed in large lettering on a wall inside Etihad Museum" caption="Words of the founders appear on the gallery walls throughout the museum."></photo-set>

Because so much of the museum is below ground, ask about lifts and step-free routes when you check opening times if anyone in your group uses a wheelchair or has limited mobility. The plaza is open to the sun, so bring hats and water in the warmer months.

## Visiting around National Day

The UAE's National Day marks the founding on 2 December 1971, and it is the most meaningful time of year to see Union House. The UAE government lists [National Day as a two-day public holiday on 2 and 3 December](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays).

Expect flags on buildings and cars, celebrations across the country and a strong sense of national pride. Around Union House, it can be especially busy. In December 2025, for example, the Dubai Media Office reported an [Al Etihad Parade that started at Union House and ended at Burj Al Arab](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/december/03-12/al-etihad-parade-brings-together-citizens-and-residents-in-a-moving). If you are in Dubai at this time, check the museum's plans in advance and allow extra travel time. Our guide to [UAE National Day for visitors](/stories/uae-national-day-for-visitors/) explains what to expect, and our [food and culture stories](/food-culture/) cover more of the traditions you may see.

## Quick answers

**What is Etihad Museum?**
A Dubai museum about the founding of the UAE, built beside Union House, where the Union was declared and the constitution signed on 2 December 1971. Its archive focuses mainly on 1968 to 1974.

**What is Union House?**
Originally the Guest Palace, completed in 1965, it is where the declaration of the Union and the signing of the UAE Constitution took place, and where the UAE flag was raised for the first time.

**When was the UAE founded?**
On 2 December 1971. Ras Al Khaimah joined on 10 February 1972, bringing the federation to seven emirates.

**What do the columns on the museum building mean?**
The seven leaning columns represent the pens that signed the declaration of the Constitution in 1971, and the building is shaped like a manuscript.

**What are the opening hours?**
Check Dubai Culture's official website before you go, as hours and tour times can change.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you include a guided tour of Etihad Museum, and does it also cover Union House, the Guest House and the flag outside?
- How long should we allow inside, and is the visit suitable for the ages of the children in our group?
- What else would you pair with the museum on the same day, and in which order?
- How will National Day celebrations affect access, traffic and timings if we visit in early December?
- Is the museum fully accessible for anyone using a wheelchair or with limited mobility, including the underground galleries?
