The first morning usually starts the same way. You wake early, still on another time zone, and the light over the Gulf is already bright and white. By the time you have found coffee, the streets are warming up, and by late morning you understand why everyone here seems to move from shade to shade like it is a reflex.

Five days is enough to see the two big cities properly, spend an evening in the desert and reach an oasis older than most of the skyline around it. It is not enough to do everything, and the plans that go wrong usually try to. The UAE rewards an early start, a long, unhurried middle of the day indoors, and a second wind in the evening when the heat eases and the cities come alive.

This itinerary splits the time between Dubai and Abu Dhabi, with two nights in Dubai and the rest in Abu Dhabi, so that no day is spent shuttling back and forth. It works best in the cooler months, and we flag where a Friday or a Monday changes the plan.

## Before you go

- **Check the travel advice.** Read the current [UK government travel advice for the UAE](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates) before you book and again before you fly. It is being updated as the regional situation changes.
- **Entry rules.** For British citizens, the UK government says a visitor visa is [issued free on arrival and your passport must be valid for at least 6 months after you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). Other nationalities have different rules, and rules change, so check with official sources for your passport before you travel.
- **Know the week.** The UAE federal government works a [four and a half day week, with a half day on Friday and Saturday and Sunday as the official weekend](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/working-in-uae-government-sector/working-hours-in-the-public-sector). Friday is also the main prayer day, so expect some sights to pause around midday.
- **Watch the weather.** Check forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/) in the days before and during your trip, especially if you plan to be outdoors.

## How to pace each day

The rhythm that works for almost everyone looks like this:

- **Early morning:** outdoor sightseeing, walking, markets.
- **Late morning to mid-afternoon:** museums, a long lunch, a rest or a swim.
- **Late afternoon into evening:** the second round, as the light softens and the heat drops.

Carry water everywhere, wear a hat, and do not plan more than one big outdoor activity a day. If you are travelling in the hotter months, shift everything even earlier and later, and accept that the middle of the day belongs to air conditioning.

## The itinerary

### Day 1: Old Dubai, the creek and the souks

Start in Al Fahidi, the restored old quarter on the Bur Dubai side of the creek, while the lanes are still cool and quiet. Wind towers rise above sand-coloured walls, and small courtyards open off narrow alleys. Walk slowly and look up.

From the creek-side, take one of the wooden abras across the water. The crossing is short, noisy and one of the best ways to see the old trading city at work, with dhows loading and unloading along the banks. On the far side is Deira, which Visit Dubai describes as [a neighbourhood with centuries-old history and spectacular souks](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/explore-dubai/dubai-neighbourhoods/deira). Wander the spice souk, where open sacks of dried limes, saffron and frankincense crowd the alleys, then the gold souk next door.

By late morning, retreat indoors for lunch and a long rest. In the evening, head to Downtown to see the Burj Khalifa lit up and the fountain shows.

A note on getting about: the UK government advice recommends [using only licensed taxis or recognised ride-hailing apps](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and reminds pedestrians that crossing outside designated crossings is illegal.

### Day 2: A slow morning, then the desert

Keep the morning easy. Swim, sleep in, or visit a museum. You will be out late tonight.

In the mid-afternoon, head into the desert. Most trips leave the city as the heat begins to ease, so you reach the dunes for the golden light and stay for sunset and dinner under the stars. If wildlife and conservation matter to you, look at trips run in and around the [Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve](https://www.ddcr.org/), which describes itself as the UAE's first national park and offers activities such as falconry, camel rides and desert walks. Our guide to [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) explains what really happens on one and how to choose.

The UK government advice notes that desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and suggests going with a reputable company with proper safety procedures. Do not try dune driving yourself.

**Swap option:** If you prefer mountains to sand, spend the day in Hatta instead. Visit Dubai promotes Hatta Dam as [a spot for kayaking and picnics](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/places-to-visit/hatta-dam-2). Leave early, be on the water before the heat builds and return to the city by late afternoon.

### Day 3: To Abu Dhabi and the old heart of the capital

Check out after breakfast and transfer to Abu Dhabi. Give the move most of the morning: by the time you have packed, travelled and checked in, it is lunchtime. Our guide to [choosing between Dubai and Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) explains why sleeping in the capital makes the next two days far easier.

Once you have rested, visit Qasr Al Hosn, which calls itself [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit). Its official site lists opening hours of 9:00 AM to 8:00 PM from Saturday to Thursday and 2:00 PM to 8:00 PM on Fridays, so on a Friday this is an afternoon visit only. Check before you go, as hours change for holidays and events.

As the sun drops, walk part of the Corniche, the long waterfront promenade. Families come out in the evening, children cycle along the path, and the breeze off the water makes it the most pleasant hour of the day.

### Day 4: The Louvre and the Grand Mosque

Spend the morning on Saadiyat Island at Louvre Abu Dhabi. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that the museum is [generally open from 10 AM and closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi). Under its great dome, light filters through in shifting patterns that the architect called a "rain of light". If Day 4 lands on a Monday, swap it with Day 5.

Rest through the middle of the afternoon, then head to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque for the late afternoon and into dusk, when the white marble turns gold, then soft blue.

- **Book ahead.** The mosque asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/individual-booking) before arriving.
- **Know the hours.** The official [visiting hours](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours) are 9:00 AM to 8:30 PM, with last entry at 8:00 PM, every day except Friday. On Fridays visitors are welcome from 9:00 AM to 11:30 AM and from 2:30 PM to 8:30 PM, with a midday closure for prayers. Hours are different during Ramadan.
- **Dress for it.** The mosque's [dress code](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details) asks women to wear loose clothing to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists and full hair coverage, and men to avoid sleeveless, tight or transparent clothing, preferably with legs fully covered. For broader advice, see [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/).

### Day 5: Al Ain and the oasis

Leave early for Al Ain, which Visit Abu Dhabi puts at [a 90-minute drive from Abu Dhabi city](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/essential-things-to-do-in-al-ain). The city's oasis and nearby archaeological sites at Hili, Jebel Hafit and Bida bint Saud are part of a [UNESCO World Heritage listing](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/unesco-sites).

Al Ain Oasis is the gentlest way to finish a trip. Visit Abu Dhabi describes [shaded pathways and ancient falaj channels](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis) that still carry water to the palms, and notes that entry to the oasis is free. Walk under the canopy, listen to the water moving through the channels, and notice how much cooler it feels in the shade.

If you have time, add Al Jahili Fort or the drive up Jebel Hafit before heading back. If you are flying out today, keep Al Ain to a morning and allow plenty of time to reach the airport.

**Swap option:** If you would rather end with a quieter day, stay in Abu Dhabi, revisit Saadiyat's beach in the morning and leave Al Ain for another trip.

## If your dates fall differently

- **A Friday:** Put Qasr Al Hosn and the Grand Mosque in the afternoon, and use the morning for the Louvre, the oasis or the beach.
- **A Monday:** Keep the Louvre off that day.
- **Ramadan:** Hours change and the evenings take on a very different feel. Read [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) before you finalise anything.
- **The hotter months:** The plan still works, but start earlier, rest longer and swap the desert evening for something indoors if the forecast is extreme. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) will help you choose your dates.

If you have extra days, add one of the northern emirates. Our guide to [the quieter emirates](/stories/beyond-dubai-quieter-emirates/) suggests where to go, and you can find more plans under [itineraries](/itineraries/).

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which of my five days fall on a Friday or a Monday, and how would you reorder the plan around them?
- Can you book a Grand Mosque access pass for the late afternoon, and what dress should I bring?
- Which desert trip do you recommend for a small group, and what safety equipment do the vehicles carry?
- How long should I allow for the Dubai to Abu Dhabi transfer, and for getting from Al Ain to the airport on my departure day?
- If the weather turns very hot or very wet, what indoor alternatives would you suggest for each day?
- Does anything in the current government travel advice affect any part of this route?
