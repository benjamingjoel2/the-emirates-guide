The water jets rise, the music swells, and the crowd along the waterfront goes quiet at the same moment. Nobody has paid a thing to be there, and that is the secret of the best free things to do in Dubai: for all the city's reputation for luxury, some of its finest moments cost nothing at all.

So, what are the best free things to do in Dubai? Walk the lanes of Al Fahidi and browse the old souks, watch the Creek from its banks, spend a day on a public beach, catch the fountain shows in Downtown, and relax in a park or a bird hide at Ras Al Khor. Add a few low-cost extras, such as an abra ride or the metro, and you can fill several days without spending much beyond meals. Here is how to make the most of each, when to go, the rules that matter, and how to string them into a full free day.

## Old Dubai: the best free things to do in Dubai start here

### The lanes of Al Fahidi

Early morning in the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood is the city at its gentlest. Wind towers rise over sand-coloured walls, doorways open onto shaded courtyards, and the lanes are almost empty. The UAE government portal recommends [a visit to the historic Al Fahidi district](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) to complete any Dubai trip, and wandering here costs nothing.

Take your time. Look up at the wind towers, peer into the small galleries and courtyards that are open, and follow the lanes down towards the water. Dubai Municipality runs a heritage architecture preservation project that aims to [safeguard areas, sites and buildings which hold significant historical relevance](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/) and tell the story of Dubai's past, and walking these lanes is the easiest way to see why that matters.

The neighbourhood is also home to the Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding, at House 26 on Al Musallah Street, which runs [cultural meals, heritage tours and mosque visits](https://cultures.ae/) under the motto "Open Doors. Open Minds." Its programmes are paid, but if you have one paid experience on an otherwise free trip, a cultural meal here is a good candidate, because it answers the questions that a free wander raises.

### Browsing the souks

Across the Creek in Deira, the spice and gold souks are free to wander. The government portal suggests [a bargain deal in the traditional souqs](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), but you do not have to buy anything to enjoy the sacks of dried limes and frankincense, or the windows glittering with gold. Browse politely, smile and say no thanks if you are not interested.

Ask before you photograph people. The UK Foreign Office advises that you should [not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that applies to stallholders as much as anyone. Our guide to [the gold and spice souks](/stories/dubai-gold-and-spice-souks/) explains how to browse and bargain politely.

### Watching the Creek

Some of the best entertainment in Old Dubai is simply sitting by the water. Dhows load cargo along the banks, and abras shuttle back and forth between [Al Fahidi, Al Seef, Baniyas, Al Sabkha and the old souks in Deira](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine).

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="free-things-to-do-in-dubai-3" alts="Passengers seated in a wooden abra crossing Dubai Creek from Bur Dubai towards Deira" caption="Watching the abras is free, and the crossing itself is short and simple."></photo-set>

Watching is free; if you decide to cross, the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash, and the ride is short and simple. It is one of the few paid extras we would never skip, because the view of both banks from the water explains the old city better than any map. Pick a bench in the shade, and watch how the boatmen manoeuvre in and out of the busy landing stages.

The Al Seef waterfront on the Bur Dubai side is a pleasant place for a stroll at dusk, as lights come on across the water.

## Beaches: sand and sea for nothing

Dubai's public beaches are free, and they are one of the city's great levellers: families, joggers, tourists and residents side by side. The government portal mentions [the long stretch of clean beaches](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) among the things to do in Dubai. The beaches of Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim are the classic choice, and the beach at JBR, next to Dubai Marina, combines sand with a long promenade for an evening walk.

### Swim safely

The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only from approved beaches where there are lifeguards and flags. Red flags mean swimming is dangerous. It also notes that swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools, so cover up when you leave the sand.

### Swimming after dark

On hot days the evening is the best time for the beach. Dubai Municipality has opened [night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting, safety screens and qualified lifeguards. It says each stretch is 800 metres long, that night swimming runs from sunset to sunrise, and that electronic screens show safety information for beachgoers. It recommends night swimming only in the designated zones.

### Beach etiquette

The beach is a shared space, so a little care goes a long way. The Foreign Office notes that [men have been arrested for photographing women on beaches](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), so keep your camera pointed at the sea and your own group. Take your rubbish with you, keep music to yourself, and bring your own shade, as it can be scarce in the middle of the day. If you are visiting with children, keep them within sight at all times, and choose a stretch with lifeguards on duty even if it means walking a little further along the sand. Arriving early also gives you the pick of the space, before the beach fills up in the late afternoon.

## Downtown after dark: the fountain

The Burj Khalifa itself costs money to climb, but standing at its foot costs nothing. In the evening, the lake below the tower becomes a stage for the fountain shows, and the waterfront fills with people watching the jets rise in time with the music. Arrive a little early for a spot by the railing, then stay for a few shows as the tower lights up above you.

Each show is short, so you can watch one, wander along the waterfront, and come back for the next from a different angle. The view from the far side of the lake, looking back at the tower, is often less crowded than the main viewing areas. Downtown is on the metro's [Red Line](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), which makes it easy to reach and leave without a taxi.

## The Marina and JBR promenade

At the other end of the city, the waterfront at Dubai Marina makes a fine free evening. The promenade runs past the boats and the towers, and as the light fades the buildings start to glow and the water reflects them back. Walk a loop of the marina, then cut through to the beach at JBR to finish by the sea. The tram [runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram/about-tram) and links to the metro, so you can arrive without a taxi.

The promenade gets busy on weekend evenings, which is part of the fun: families out for a stroll, joggers, people watching the boats. If you prefer it quieter, come early in the morning, when the air is cooler and the waterfront belongs to walkers and runners. Our guide to [Dubai Marina and JBR](/stories/dubai-marina-and-jbr-guide/) has more on the area.

## Free views from the outside

Some of the city's paid landmarks are almost as good from the outside. The Dubai Frame stands in [Zabeel Park, reached through Al Jafiliya Metro Station](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), and the park around it is a pleasant place to see the structure from below. Jumeirah Mosque is a fine sight from the street, especially in the soft light of late afternoon. If you want to go inside, the Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a [guided visit every day except Friday](https://cultures.ae/program/jumeirah-mosque-visit/), which is paid but answers a great many questions in a short time.

## Parks and nature

### Public parks

Dubai Municipality says there are [more than 190 public parks](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/) across the city, with sports fields, playgrounds for children and people of determination, and barbecue areas. Late afternoon and evening are the liveliest times, when families arrive with picnics and the grass is in shade. The same page says the municipality protects eight nature reserves covering a total of 31% of Dubai's area, a reminder that there is more open space here than the skyline suggests.

### Flamingos at Ras Al Khor

Just inland from the city centre, Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary feels a world away from the towers. Dubai Municipality lists opening hours of [7.30am to 5.30pm in winter, from October to March](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/plan-your-visit/), and 6am to 6pm in summer, from April to September. Its flamingo hide promises [the pink of thousands of flamingoes all year round](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/book-a-visit/), while the Lagoon Hide looks out over the creek for waders and raptors. The Mangrove Hide was listed as temporarily closed when we checked. The municipality says families and individuals can walk in at any time during opening hours, with walk-in registration required.

<photo-set layout="full" keys="free-things-to-do-in-dubai-5" alts="The Ras Al Khor flamingo hide viewing area seen across the wetland, with the Dubai skyline in the background" caption="The flamingo hide at Ras Al Khor sits on a wetland with the city skyline beyond."></photo-set>

The rules are simple: inside the hides it is a quiet zone, with no phones, food, drinks or smoking, and you must not go beyond the "No Entry" signs. Only 15 people are allowed inside a hide at a time, so you may need to wait your turn at busy times. Bring binoculars, and a bird guide if you have one, as the municipality suggests. The municipality's pages do not mention an admission charge, but check the official site for current arrangements before you set off.

## Making a free day work

### Beat the heat

Free days in Dubai mean time outdoors, so plan around the sun. The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, wearing light, loose clothing and drinking more cold drinks. Carry a refillable bottle, and use the hottest hours for a long lunch or a rest. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) shows when outdoor days are most comfortable.

### Respect the rules

A few rules are worth knowing when you are out and about. The Foreign Office says it is [illegal to cross roads anywhere other than designated pedestrian crossings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), that you should dress modestly in public areas such as shopping malls, and that showing affection in public is frowned upon. It also warns that it is illegal to photograph some government buildings and military installations. During Ramadan, it notes that eating, drinking or smoking in public in the daytime may cause offence. Our guide on [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) covers dress in more detail.

### Getting between free sights cheaply

The cheapest way to link these sights is public transport. Dubai Metro runs on the Red and Green lines, and RTA's nol Red Ticket is a paper ticket [loadable with up to 10 single trips](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/Nol-Home), while the Silver Card suits longer stays. The metro itself is a sightseeing ride, with long stretches of elevated track. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) explains the options.

### Accessibility

Many of these free sights are easy for visitors with limited mobility. The parks include playgrounds for people of determination, the Ras Al Khor hides ask visitors to give priority to people of determination, and RTA lists [dedicated wheelchair spaces on all metro trains and lift access to all station levels](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro). The lanes of Al Fahidi are flat but uneven in places, and the beaches are harder going on soft sand.

### Put it together

A simple free day might be Al Fahidi and the souks in the morning, a long lunch, a late afternoon at the beach and the fountain after dark. On a second day, try Ras Al Khor early, a park at lunchtime in the shade, and the Marina promenade or a night swim in the evening. For a fuller plan, see [three days in Dubai](/stories/three-days-in-dubai/). Find more advice in our [travel tips](/travel-tips/).

## Quick answers

**What can you do in Dubai for free?** Walk Al Fahidi, browse the souks, watch the Creek, swim at public beaches, see the fountain shows in Downtown, and relax in the city's public parks.

**Are Dubai's beaches free?** Public beaches are free. Swim only where there are lifeguards and flags, and stay out when a red flag is flying.

**Can you swim at night in Dubai?** Yes, at the night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1, which have lighting and lifeguards and open from sunset to sunrise. Stay in the designated zones.

**When can I see flamingos in Dubai?** Dubai Municipality says flamingos can be seen all year round from the flamingo hide at Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which free sights can we combine in one day without long taxi rides between them?
- What time of day is best for each, given the heat and crowds in our travel month?
- Would a paid guide in Al Fahidi or the souks add enough to be worth it for us?
- Which public beaches near our hotel have lifeguards on duty when we plan to swim?
- Is Ras Al Khor open on the day we want to go, and which hides are currently open?
