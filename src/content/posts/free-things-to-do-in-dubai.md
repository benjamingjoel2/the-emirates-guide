The water jets rise, the music swells, and the crowd along the waterfront goes quiet at the same moment. Nobody has paid a thing to be there, and that is the secret of the best free things to do in Dubai: for all the city's reputation for luxury, some of its finest moments cost nothing at all.

So, what are the best free things to do in Dubai? Walk the lanes of Al Fahidi and browse the old souks, watch the Creek from its banks, spend a day on a public beach, catch the fountain shows in Downtown, and relax in a park or a bird hide at Ras Al Khor. Add a few low-cost extras, such as an abra ride or the metro, and you can fill several days without spending much beyond meals. Here is how to make the most of each.

## Old Dubai: the best free things to do in Dubai start here

### The lanes of Al Fahidi

Early morning in the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood is the city at its gentlest. Wind towers rise over sand-coloured walls, doorways open onto shaded courtyards, and the lanes are almost empty. The UAE government portal recommends [a visit to the historic Al Fahidi district](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) to complete any Dubai trip, and wandering here costs nothing.

Take your time. Look up at the wind towers, peer into the small galleries and courtyards that are open, and follow the lanes down towards the water.

### Browsing the souks

Across the Creek in Deira, the spice and gold souks are free to wander. The government portal suggests [a bargain deal in the traditional souqs](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), but you do not have to buy anything to enjoy the sacks of dried limes and frankincense, or the windows glittering with gold. Browse politely, smile and say no thanks if you are not interested.

Ask before you photograph people. The UK Foreign Office advises that you should [not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that applies to stallholders as much as anyone.

### Watching the Creek

Some of the best entertainment in Old Dubai is simply sitting by the water. Dhows load cargo along the banks, and abras shuttle back and forth between [Al Fahidi, Al Seef, Baniyas, Al Sabkha and the old souks in Deira](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine). Watching is free; if you decide to cross, the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash, and the ride is short and simple.

The Al Seef waterfront on the Bur Dubai side is a pleasant place for a stroll at dusk, as lights come on across the water.

## Beaches: sand and sea for nothing

Dubai's public beaches are free, and they are one of the city's great levellers: families, joggers, tourists and residents side by side. The government portal mentions [the long stretch of clean beaches](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) among the things to do in Dubai.

### Swim safely

The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only from approved beaches where there are lifeguards and flags. Red flags mean swimming is dangerous. It also notes that swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools, so cover up when you leave the sand.

### Swimming after dark

On hot days the evening is the best time for the beach. Dubai Municipality has opened [night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting, safety screens and qualified lifeguards. It recommends night swimming only in the designated zones.

## Downtown after dark: the fountain

The Burj Khalifa itself costs money to climb, but standing at its foot costs nothing. In the evening, the lake below the tower becomes a stage for the fountain shows, and the waterfront fills with people watching the jets rise in time with the music. Arrive a little early for a spot by the railing, then stay for a few shows as the tower lights up above you.

## Parks and nature

### Public parks

Dubai Municipality says there are [more than 190 public parks](https://www.dm.gov.ae/discover-dubai/) across the city, with sports fields, children's playgrounds and barbecue areas. Late afternoon and evening are the liveliest times, when families arrive with picnics and the grass is in shade.

### Flamingos at Ras Al Khor

Just inland from the city centre, Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary feels a world away from the towers. Dubai Municipality lists opening hours of [7.30am to 5.30pm in winter, from October to March](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/plan-your-visit/), and 6am to 6pm in summer, from April to September. Its flamingo hide promises [the pink of thousands of flamingoes all year round](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/book-a-visit/), while the Lagoon Hide looks out over the creek for waders and raptors. The Mangrove Hide was listed as temporarily closed when we checked.

The rules are simple: inside the hides it is a quiet zone, with no phones, food, drinks or smoking, and you must not go beyond the "No Entry" signs. Bring binoculars. The municipality's pages do not mention an admission charge, but check the official site for current arrangements before you set off.

## Making a free day work

### Beat the heat

Free days in Dubai mean time outdoors, so plan around the sun. The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, wearing light, loose clothing and drinking more cold drinks. Carry a refillable bottle, and use the hottest hours for a long lunch or a rest. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) shows when outdoor days are most comfortable.

### Respect the rules

A few rules are worth knowing when you are out and about. The Foreign Office says it is [illegal to cross roads anywhere other than designated pedestrian crossings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), that you should dress modestly in public areas such as shopping malls, and that showing affection in public is frowned upon. Our guide on [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) covers dress in more detail.

### Put it together

A simple free day might be Al Fahidi and the souks in the morning, a long lunch, a late afternoon at the beach and the fountain after dark. ## Quick answers

**What can you do in Dubai for free?** Walk Al Fahidi, browse the souks, watch the Creek, swim at public beaches, see the fountain shows in Downtown, and relax in the city's public parks.

**Are Dubai's beaches free?** Public beaches are free. Swim only where there are lifeguards and flags, and stay out when a red flag is flying.

**Can you swim at night in Dubai?** Yes, at the night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1, which have lighting and lifeguards. Stay in the designated zones.

**When can I see flamingos in Dubai?** Dubai Municipality says flamingos can be seen all year round from the flamingo hide at Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary.

- Would a paid guide in Al Fahidi or the souks add enough to be worth it for us?
- Which public beaches near our hotel have lifeguards on duty when we plan to swim?
- Is Ras Al Khor open on the day we want to go, and which hides are currently open?
