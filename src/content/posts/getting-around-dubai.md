The train glides out of the station with nobody at the front. No driver, no cab, just a wide window and the city sliding past below: towers, highways, then a flash of the Creek. For many visitors, the Dubai Metro is the first surprise of the trip.

Getting around Dubai is easier than its size suggests. The short answer: use the metro and tram for the long north to south runs, licensed taxis for door-to-door trips and late nights, and the abras for crossing the Creek. Buy a nol card early, because you will use it on almost everything. This guide takes each option in turn, with the rules that catch visitors out.

## Getting around Dubai at a glance

- **Metro:** fast and air-conditioned, best for long distances along the city's spine.
- **Tram:** links the metro with the Marina, JBR and the Palm Monorail.
- **Taxis:** licensed taxis are everywhere and can be hailed or booked.
- **Buses:** useful for gaps in the rail network, and for intercity trips.
- **Abras, ferries and water taxis:** the Creek crossing is a short pleasure; other boats are scenic extras.
- **Walking:** good within neighbourhoods, less so between them, especially in the heat.

## The Dubai Metro: your fastest way across the city

RTA describes Dubai Metro as running on [the Red and Green lines](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), with a Blue Line approved and scheduled for completion in 2029. The UAE government portal calls it a [driverless and fully automated metro rail network](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport).

### Choosing your carriage

Each train has different types of cabin. According to RTA, [Gold Class cabins are for gold class ticket holders only](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), with wider seats, and there are cabins reserved for women and children. Everyone else uses the general cabins. Look for the signs on the platform and on the floor so you board at the right door.

### Luggage and good manners

RTA allows [two suitcases per passenger](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), one large and one small, kept in the luggage area of each cabin. The same page lists fines for eating or drinking where it is prohibited, smoking, carrying alcohol and putting your feet on the seats. The etiquette is simple: let people off before you board, move into the cabin and do not push.

### Children travelling alone

RTA's rules say [children under 8 cannot use public transport without an adult](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), children aged 8 to 11 need a permit from their parents, and those 12 and over can travel alone.

## nol cards: one card for almost everything

Before your first ride, sort out how you will pay. Dubai uses the nol system, and RTA lists [four main options](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/Nol-Home):

- **nol Red Ticket:** a paper ticket for the occasional traveller, sold at ticket vending machines and loadable with up to 10 single trips.
- **nol Silver Card:** described as "the perfect starter card", a smart card with an e-purse available at any ticket office.
- **nol Gold Card:** like the Silver Card, plus access to Gold Class cabins at a premium fare.
- **nol Blue Card:** a personalised smart card.

For most short trips, a Silver Card is the simplest choice. RTA also lists online services for topping up and a journey planner. The government portal notes that you need a nol card or ticket for [water taxis, ferries and abras](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/waterways) as well as the [tram](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport).

## The tram: Marina, JBR and the Palm

Along the coast in the Marina, the tram fills the gap the metro leaves. RTA says it [links Dubai Metro and the Palm Monorail and runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram/about-tram). It also has Gold Class and women and children cabins.

Take care around the tracks. RTA lists fines for crossing tracks outside designated areas and for entering prohibited zones, so use the marked crossings.

## Buses: filling in the gaps

Buses reach the neighbourhoods the rail lines miss. RTA's bus pages include [timetables by route number, a journey planner and a link to book intercity tickets](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/bus/about-bus). The journey planner is the easiest way to see whether a bus beats a taxi for your trip.

## Taxis and ride-hailing: door to door

A taxi is often the easiest way home after dinner, or from the metro to a beach hotel. The UK Foreign Office advises using [only licensed taxis or other recognised forms of public transport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), including recognised ride-hailing apps. It says authorised Dubai taxis are painted cream with different coloured roofs, and that taxis with pink roofs have female drivers. There are also special taxis for women and children.

The UAE government portal says taxis [can be hailed from the roadside or booked in advance](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport), including through RTA, and that booking charges apply and differ by emirate, type of taxi and time of day. The Foreign Office also advises women in particular to take care when travelling alone or at night, and not to accept lifts from strangers.

## Abras, ferries and water taxis

### Crossing the Creek

Nothing beats the old way across Dubai Creek. RTA runs heritage abras on [four Creek routes](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine): Dubai Old Souq to Baniyas, Al Fahidi to Al Sabkha, Al Fahidi to Deira Old Souq, and Al Seef to Baniyas. The motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash. You step down into a low wooden boat, sit on the central bench and are across in minutes.

### Ferries and water taxis

RTA's Dubai Ferry is air-conditioned, [stroller-friendly and has restrooms](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine). In the Marina, the Dubai Water Taxi runs hop-on, hop-off between four stops. There is also an air-conditioned abra between Al Jaddaf and Dubai Festival City, and heritage abras on the Dubai Water Canal. These are as much a sightseeing trip as a way to get somewhere.

## Walking: shorter than it looks, hotter than you think

Within Al Fahidi, the Marina or Downtown, walking is a pleasure. Between them, it is rarely practical. Two things to know:

- The Foreign Office warns that it is [illegal to cross the road anywhere other than designated pedestrian crossings](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that you may face a fine or even imprisonment. It also says vehicles often do not stop at zebra crossings, so take care.
- Heat changes everything. The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm and drinking more cold drinks](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/). Plan longer walks for the morning or evening, and use the air-conditioned metro for the middle of the day.

## Driving yourself

Most visitors to Dubai city do not need a car, but one helps for trips to the mountains or other emirates. The Foreign Office says you can use a [UK photocard driving licence](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) to drive in the UAE, and warns that it is illegal to drink and drive, no matter how small the amount. It notes that driving standards are not always disciplined and speeding is common. If you are in a crash in Dubai, it says to move your vehicle only if it is causing an obstruction, and to call the police in all cases.

## Quick answers

**Is the Dubai Metro easy for tourists?** Yes. It runs on the Red and Green lines, is driverless and air-conditioned, and has Gold Class and women and children cabins. Pay with a nol card or Red Ticket.

**Which nol card should I buy?** For most short trips, the nol Silver Card, sold at ticket offices, is the simplest. The Red Ticket is a paper option loadable with up to 10 single trips.

**Can you eat or drink on the Dubai Metro?** No, not where it is prohibited. RTA lists fines for eating or drinking in prohibited areas, as well as for smoking and carrying alcohol.

**How do I cross Dubai Creek?** Take an abra. RTA runs four heritage abra routes across the Creek, and the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash.

**Are Dubai taxis safe?** The UK Foreign Office advises using only licensed taxis or recognised apps. Licensed Dubai taxis are cream with coloured roofs.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which of our planned sights are easiest by metro or tram, and which need a taxi or transfer?
- Can you arrange a driver for day trips outside the city, and what is included?
- Is a nol card enough for all the transport on our itinerary, or will we need cash for anything?
- How do transfer times change at rush hour or during Ramadan?
- Do your vehicles have child seats if we are travelling with young children?
- If we want to drive ourselves, what licence and insurance checks should we make?
