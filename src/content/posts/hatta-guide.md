The paddle dips, and the only sound is water dripping off the blade. Around the kayak, the lake is an improbable shade of green, and the walls of the basin are bare brown rock, folded and creased like crumpled paper. It is hard to believe you were on a busy Dubai highway this morning.

That contrast is the reason to go to Hatta. This mountain enclave belongs to Dubai but sits apart from the city, up in the Hajar range near the Omani border, and it swaps glass towers for rock, palm groves and stone watchtowers. A Hatta trip suits anyone who wants an active day outdoors without a 4x4 lurching over dunes: you can kayak on the dam, ride or walk the trails, and finish in a restored mud-brick village. It works as a long day trip, and better still as an overnight.

## Why Hatta feels like a different Dubai

The Government of Dubai Media Office describes Hatta as sitting in [the Hajar Mountains](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/December/18-12/Hatta-emerges-as-a-model-for-sustainable-development-and-heritage-preservation), with a history it says dates back more than 3,000 years. Since a development plan launched in 2021, the town has been reshaped for visitors: a new souq selling local products and crafts, a redeveloped heritage village, and attractions such as Hatta Dam, Hatta Hill Park and a honeybee garden and discovery centre, all named in that same report.

Visit Dubai pitches it simply as a place for outdoor lovers, listing [hiking trails, kayaking, mountain biking and camping](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/beyond-dubai/nature-adventures-in-hatta). That is a fair summary. Hatta is not about one headline sight. It is about a day spent moving through a landscape.

## Getting to Hatta from Dubai

Most visitors drive or join an organised trip. If you would rather not drive, Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority launched an express coach in January 2023. According to the RTA announcement, route H02, the Hatta Express, runs from [the Dubai Mall Bus Station to Hatta Bus Station](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/January/04-01/RTA-launches-express-and-tourist-bus) every two hours. A second route, H04, is a circular hop-on hop-off service within Hatta, starting and ending at the bus station and passing Hatta Wadi Hub, Hatta Hill Park, Hatta Dam and the Heritage Village, every 30 minutes.

Bus timetables change, so check the current schedule with the RTA before you rely on it, especially for the last coach back. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) covers the city end of the journey.

## Hatta Dam and the kayaks

The dam is the image most people have of Hatta: still water, steep rock and brightly coloured kayaks drifting across the middle. Paddling here is gentle, flat-water stuff, and it suits beginners and families as much as confident paddlers.

A few things make the experience better:

- **Go early.** The light is softer, the water is calmer and the rock has not yet soaked up the day's heat.
- **Cover up for the sun.** There is little shade on open water. Hats, sunglasses and a long-sleeved top help more than you would expect.
- **Bring a dry bag** for your phone and car keys.
- **Wear the buoyancy aid** you are given, even if you swim well.

The Media Office report also mentions stand-up paddle boarding sessions held in Hatta during the Dubai Fitness Challenge, so ask whether paddle boards are on offer alongside kayaks.

## Trails on two wheels and on foot

For cyclists, Hatta is one of the more serious options in the UAE. The Media Office says the town has [11.5 kilometres of cycling tracks](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/December/18-12/Hatta-emerges-as-a-model-for-sustainable-development-and-heritage-preservation) for getting around, plus a network of 50 kilometres of mountain bike trails across varied terrain, with bikes, e-scooters and mountain bikes available to rent. Those figures date from late 2023, so the network may have grown since.

The trails range from easy gravel to steep, rocky singletrack. If you are not a regular mountain biker, start on the easier loops, rent a helmet as a matter of course and ask staff which routes are suitable before you set off.

Walkers have options too, from short climbs to viewpoints over the dam to longer routes into the hills. Our guide to [hiking in the UAE](/stories/hiking-in-the-uae/) covers seasons, trail habits and what to carry.

## Hatta Heritage Village: stone, palm and falaj

After the water and the trails, the heritage village slows everything down. Dubai Municipality calls it [one of the most important monuments and heritage sites](https://www.dm.gov.ae/historic_item/hatta-heritage-village/) of the Hatta region.

### What you will see

According to Dubai Municipality, the village holds about 30 buildings restored with traditional materials such as clay and straw. Two watchtowers built in 1888 look down from the surrounding hills. Below them stand a restored mosque, described as around 200 years old, and a fort built in 1896 by Sheikh Maktoum bin Hasher Al Maktoum, which served as a place for public meetings. A tower in the fort displays examples of firearms used in the area.

The houses are set up to show daily life: a traditional family home with its majlis, bedrooms, storeroom and animal pens; a house devoted to Nabati poetry, folk songs and drums; and a handicrafts house covering blacksmithing, perfumery and palm-frond work, including how date syrup was made.

### The falaj

Do not miss the water. The village has a channel fed by a falaj, a stream running down from the mountain slopes to water the palm trees, with benches in the shade where you can sit and listen to it. It is the quietest corner of Hatta and a reminder of why people settled here at all.

### Opening hours

Dubai Municipality's page lists the village as [open daily from 07:30 to 20:30, with free entry at present](https://www.dm.gov.ae/historic_item/hatta-heritage-village/). Hours can change around holidays and events, so check before you travel. Dress modestly, as you would at any heritage site.

## When to go, and how to stay safe in the hills

Hatta is at its best in the cooler months. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) will help you pick dates. Outside those months, plan activities for early morning and late afternoon.

### Wadis and rain

The valleys around Hatta are usually dry, but the UAE government notes that in cooler months [when it rains, they flourish with brooks and pools](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/environment-and-energy/Nature-and-wildlife/topography-and-ecosystems). Pretty as that sounds, a dry wadi bed is exactly where water runs when a storm breaks upstream. The UK Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/) and following local advice. If rain is forecast, stay out of wadi beds and do not drive through moving water.

### Heat and sun

Rock reflects heat, and trails offer little shade. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks, wearing light-coloured, loose clothing and avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. Carry more water than you think you need.

### If something goes wrong

The UAE government lists [998 for an ambulance and 999 for police](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies). Tell someone your route before you set off on a longer ride or hike, and keep your phone charged.

## A simple Hatta day plan

- **Morning:** Kayak on the dam while the water is calm.
- **Late morning:** An easy ride or a short walk to a viewpoint, then shade.
- **Lunch:** Around the souq or the Wadi Hub area.
- **Afternoon:** The heritage village as the light softens, ending at the falaj.

## Quick answers

**Is Hatta part of Dubai?** Yes. It belongs to the emirate of Dubai and sits in the Hajar Mountains, away from the city.

**Can you get to Hatta by public transport?** The RTA launched the H02 Hatta Express from the Dubai Mall Bus Station, running every two hours, plus the H04 loop within Hatta. Check current timetables before you go.

**Is Hatta Heritage Village free?** Dubai Municipality says entry is currently free and lists daily opening from 07:30 to 20:30. Confirm before visiting.

**What can you do in Hatta?** Visit Dubai lists hiking, kayaking, mountain biking and camping, and the town also has the heritage village, Hatta Dam and Hatta Hill Park.

**Is it safe to go into the wadis?** Usually, in dry weather. Heavy rain can cause flooding, so check the National Centre of Meteorology forecast and avoid wadi beds when rain is expected.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which kayaking or paddle sessions suit beginners or children, and are buoyancy aids provided in all sizes?
- Which mountain bike or hiking routes match our fitness, and is a guide included or recommended?
- What is the plan if rain or wind is forecast on our day, and who makes the call to change it?
- Is transport from Dubai included, and what time do we leave and return?
- Is there shade, water and a first aid kit at each stop?
- Can the heritage village visit include a guide who explains the fort and the falaj?
