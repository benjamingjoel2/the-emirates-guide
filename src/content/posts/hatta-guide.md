The paddle dips, and the only sound is water dripping off the blade. Around the kayak, the lake is an improbable shade of green, and the walls of the basin are bare brown rock, folded and creased like crumpled paper. It is hard to believe you were on a busy Dubai highway this morning.

That contrast is the reason to go to Hatta. This mountain enclave belongs to Dubai but sits apart from the city, up in the Hajar range near the Omani border, and it swaps glass towers for rock, palm groves and stone watchtowers. A Hatta trip suits anyone who wants an active day outdoors without a 4x4 lurching over dunes: you can kayak on the dam, ride or walk the trails, and finish in a restored mud-brick village. It works as a long day trip, and better still as an overnight.

## Why Hatta feels like a different Dubai

The Government of Dubai Media Office describes Hatta as sitting in [the Hajar Mountains](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/December/18-12/Hatta-emerges-as-a-model-for-sustainable-development-and-heritage-preservation), with a history it says dates back more than 3,000 years. Since a development plan launched in 2021, the town has been reshaped for visitors: a new souq selling local products and crafts, a redeveloped heritage village, and attractions such as Hatta Dam, Hatta Hill Park and a honeybee garden and discovery centre, all named in that same report. The plan was promoted under the name "Hatta, The Highlands of Dubai", and the report adds that around 13,000 indigenous trees have been planted alongside support for local farmers.

The mountains themselves are part of a much larger range. The UAE government notes that the Hajar range runs across the south-eastern Arabian Peninsula, and that in the UAE it extends across Fujairah, Ras Al Khaimah and Sharjah [as well as the Hatta area of Dubai](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/environment-and-energy/Nature-and-wildlife/topography-and-ecosystems). The highest of the Hatta peaks, it says, is Jebel Umm Al Nsur, at around 1,300 metres. You will not climb it on a casual visit, but it explains the skyline: this is proper mountain country, not a few rocky hills.

Visit Dubai pitches Hatta simply as a place for outdoor lovers, listing [hiking trails, kayaking, mountain biking and camping](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/beyond-dubai/nature-adventures-in-hatta). That is a fair summary. Hatta is not about one headline sight. It is about a day spent moving through a landscape.

## Getting to Hatta from Dubai

Most visitors drive or join an organised trip. Dubai Municipality places the heritage village [115 kilometres southeast of Dubai](https://www.dm.gov.ae/historic_item/hatta-heritage-village/), so allow a good part of the morning for the journey and leave early to make the most of the cooler hours. If you drive yourself, check the route the night before and fill up with fuel in the city.

If you would rather not drive, Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority launched an express coach in January 2023. According to the RTA announcement, route H02, the Hatta Express, runs from [the Dubai Mall Bus Station to Hatta Bus Station](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/January/04-01/RTA-launches-express-and-tourist-bus) every two hours. A second route, H04, is a circular hop-on hop-off service within Hatta, starting and ending at the bus station and passing Hatta Wadi Hub, Hatta Hill Park, Hatta Dam and the Heritage Village, every 30 minutes.

That loop matters more than it looks. Hatta's sights are spread out along the valley, and without a car the H04 is what links them. Bus timetables change, so check the current schedule with the RTA before you rely on it, especially for the last coach back to the city. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) covers the city end of the journey.

## Hatta Dam and the kayaks

The dam is the image most people have of Hatta: still water, steep rock and brightly coloured kayaks drifting across the middle. Paddling here is gentle, flat-water stuff, and it suits beginners and families as much as confident paddlers. Keep an eye on the water around you, too. Birds dive and fish along the cliff edges, and on a quiet morning you may share the lake with very few other boats.

A few things make the experience better:

- **Go early.** The light is softer, the water is calmer and the rock has not yet soaked up the day's heat.
- **Cover up for the sun.** There is little shade on open water. Hats, sunglasses and a long-sleeved top help more than you would expect.
- **Bring a dry bag** for your phone and car keys.
- **Wear the buoyancy aid** you are given, even if you swim well.

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="hatta-guide-2" alts="A dark diving bird swimming on calm water at the foot of a sheer, sunlit rock face, with a small kayak far off near the cliff" caption="Wildlife shares the dam with paddlers, and the cliffs drop straight into the water."></photo-set>

The water sits in a deep basin, so the cliffs throw long shadows early and late in the day, and wind can funnel through the gaps in the rock. If the surface starts to ripple, stay closer to the launch area rather than heading for the far end. The Media Office report also says Hatta hosted [stand-up paddle boarding sessions, including a sunset yoga class on boards](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/December/18-12/Hatta-emerges-as-a-model-for-sustainable-development-and-heritage-preservation), during the 2023 Dubai Fitness Challenge, so ask whether paddle boards are on offer alongside kayaks.

The dam is also working infrastructure. The same report describes the Hatta hydroelectric power plant, which it calls the first project of its kind in the GCC, operating in turbine and pumping modes. It also describes plans for a waterfall project near the dam and a cable car from the dam towards the top of Umm Al Nusour. Treat these as plans rather than finished attractions, and check what is open before you build a day around them.

## Trails on two wheels and on foot

For cyclists, Hatta is one of the more serious options in the UAE. The Media Office says the town has [11.5 kilometres of cycling tracks](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2023/December/18-12/Hatta-emerges-as-a-model-for-sustainable-development-and-heritage-preservation) for getting around, plus a network of 50 kilometres of mountain bike trails across varied terrain, with bikes, e-scooters and mountain bikes available to rent. The report notes that the tracks have been linked to the main tourist sites. Those figures date from late 2023, so the network may have grown since.

The trails range from easy gravel to steep, rocky singletrack. If you are not a regular mountain biker, start on the easier loops, rent a helmet as a matter of course and ask staff which routes are suitable before you set off. Loose stones on descents catch out riders used to tarmac, so keep your speed down until you have a feel for the surface.

Walkers have options too, from short climbs to viewpoints over the dam to longer routes into the hills. The rock here is sharp and the ground uneven, so wear proper shoes with grip rather than sandals, even for a short scramble. ## Hatta Heritage Village: stone, palm and falaj

After the water and the trails, the heritage village slows everything down. Dubai Municipality calls it [one of the most important monuments and heritage sites](https://www.dm.gov.ae/historic_item/hatta-heritage-village/) of the Hatta region.

### What you will see

According to Dubai Municipality, the village holds about 30 buildings restored with traditional materials such as clay and straw. Two watchtowers built in 1888 look down from the surrounding hills, and the municipality says the village was once called Al-Hajreen after the two mountains they stand on. Below them stand a restored mosque, described as around 200 years old, and a fort built in 1896 by Sheikh Maktoum bin Hasher Al Maktoum, which served as a place for public meetings. A tower in the fort displays examples of firearms used in the area.

The houses are set up to show daily life: a traditional family home with its majlis, bedrooms, storeroom and animal pens; a house devoted to Nabati poetry, folk songs and drums; a social life house with a majlis hall and pictures of customs and traditions; and a handicrafts house covering blacksmithing, perfumery and palm-frond work. A palm products house shows how date syrup was made, and a restoration house explains how the village itself was rebuilt. Our guide to [the majlis and Emirati hospitality](/stories/majlis-emirati-hospitality/) gives useful background before you step inside those rooms.

### The falaj

Do not miss the water. The village has a channel fed by a falaj, a stream running down from the mountain slopes to water the palm trees, with benches in the shade where you can sit and listen to it. It is the quietest corner of Hatta and a reminder of why people settled here at all.

### Opening hours and practical details

Dubai Municipality's page lists the village as [open daily from 07:30 to 20:30, with free entry at present](https://www.dm.gov.ae/historic_item/hatta-heritage-village/), and notes paved car parks for visitors. It also mentions an old market building with small shops, including stalls selling products made by local families. Hours can change around holidays and events, so check before you travel.

Dress modestly, as you would at any heritage site, with shoulders and knees covered. The lanes are unpaved in places and the climb to the watchtowers is steep, so the village is easier on foot than with a pushchair, and visitors with limited mobility may prefer to stay on the flatter lower lanes around the fort and the falaj.

## How a Hatta day unfolds

A typical day starts on the road before the heat builds. You arrive, park or step off the bus, and head straight for the water while it is calm. By late morning the light flattens and the rock starts to radiate warmth, which is the moment to move to a shaded activity, a short ride on the easier tracks, or lunch.

The middle of the day is the slow part. This is when we would sit somewhere shaded, browse the souq, or simply rest before the afternoon. Things pick up again as the sun drops: the heritage village is at its best in the softer light, and the view from the watchtowers over the palm groves is clearest late in the day.

What catches people out is usually time rather than difficulty. The sights are spread out, the drive back is long, and if you are relying on the express coach, the last departure sets the shape of the whole day. Work backwards from that, rather than forwards from breakfast.

### Hatta with children

Hatta suits families well, with a few adjustments. Kayaking is calm, and buoyancy aids should be provided in children's sizes, though it is worth confirming before you book. Keep walks short, plan the heritage village as a place to explore rather than a museum to read, and build in shade and snacks between activities. The usual family rules for UAE heat apply here too: early starts, long breaks and plenty of water.

## When to go, and how to stay safe in the hills

Hatta is at its best in the cooler months. The Media Office report notes that Hatta features in Dubai's winter campaign for the cooler part of the year, and our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) will help you pick dates. Outside those months, plan activities for early morning and late afternoon.

### Wadis and rain

The valleys around Hatta are usually dry, but the UAE government notes that in cooler months [when it rains, they flourish with brooks and pools](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/environment-and-energy/Nature-and-wildlife/topography-and-ecosystems). Pretty as that sounds, a dry wadi bed is exactly where water runs when a storm breaks upstream. The UK Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/) and following local advice. If rain is forecast, stay out of wadi beds and do not drive through moving water.

### Heat and sun

Rock reflects heat, and trails offer little shade. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks, wearing light-coloured, loose clothing and avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. Carry more water than you think you need.

### If something goes wrong

The UAE government lists [998 for an ambulance and 999 for police](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies). Tell someone your route before you set off on a longer ride or hike, and keep your phone charged. If you join an organised activity, the same FCDO page advises going with a reputable company with proper safety procedures and checking that the activity is covered by your insurance.

## A simple Hatta day plan

- **Morning:** Kayak on the dam while the water is calm.
- **Late morning:** An easy ride or a short walk to a viewpoint, then shade.
- **Lunch:** Around the souq or the Wadi Hub area.
- **Afternoon:** The heritage village as the light softens, ending at the falaj.

## Quick answers

**Is Hatta part of Dubai?** Yes. It belongs to the emirate of Dubai and sits in the Hajar Mountains, away from the city.

**Can you get to Hatta by public transport?** The RTA launched the H02 Hatta Express from the Dubai Mall Bus Station, running every two hours, plus the H04 loop within Hatta. Check current timetables before you go.

**Is Hatta Heritage Village free?** Dubai Municipality says entry is currently free and lists daily opening from 07:30 to 20:30. Confirm before visiting.

**What can you do in Hatta?** Visit Dubai lists hiking, kayaking, mountain biking and camping, and the town also has the heritage village, Hatta Dam and Hatta Hill Park.

**Is it safe to go into the wadis?** Usually, in dry weather. Heavy rain can cause flooding, so check the National Centre of Meteorology forecast and avoid wadi beds when rain is expected.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which kayaking or paddle sessions suit beginners or children, and are buoyancy aids provided in all sizes?
- Which mountain bike or hiking routes match our fitness, and is a guide included or recommended?
- What is the plan if rain or wind is forecast on our day, and who makes the call to change it?
- Is transport from Dubai included, and what time do we leave and return?
- Is there shade, water and a first aid kit at each stop?
- Can the heritage village visit include a guide who explains the fort and the falaj?
