In the dark, the burner roars, and for a second the whole envelope glows orange like a giant lantern. Then the basket shifts under your feet, the ground crew lets go, and the desert simply drops away. No lurch, no engine, no wind in your face, because you are moving with it.

A hot air balloon ride over the UAE desert is the quiet opposite of a dune-bashing safari. You trade adrenaline for altitude, and a crowded camp for a sky that slowly turns from grey to pink to gold over an ocean of dunes. Expect a very early start, a flight shaped entirely by the weather, and a view you cannot get any other way. Here is how the morning unfolds and how to choose a flight that suits you.

## What a desert balloon flight looks like from above

The best-known flights in Dubai launch over and around the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve, which calls itself [the UAE's first national park](https://www.ddcr.org/). The reserve lists a [hot air balloon ride](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) among its activities and describes a leisurely tour over sand dunes and green oases, with a falcon show watched from 3,000 feet in the air and the chance to spot oryx, gazelles and wandering camels in their natural habitat.

That last point is the real reward. From above, the desert's patterns make sense: the long ridges of the dunes, the shadows pooling in the hollows, the tracks of animals crossing the sand. The reserve says it is home to [400 Arabian oryx](https://www.ddcr.org/about/), the largest free-roaming herd of its kind in the UAE, along with Arabian and sand gazelles. Seeing a herd moving across the dunes at first light, with no engine noise to scatter it, is what most people remember.

## The morning, step by step

Every operator runs things slightly differently, but the shape is much the same.

### The pickup

Balloons fly in the calm, cool air around sunrise, so pickups happen in the middle of the night or very early morning. You will usually be collected from your hotel and driven out of the city in the dark. Sleep on the way if you can.

### Inflation and boarding

At the launch site, you watch the crew lay out the envelope, fan cold air into it, and then fire the burners to stand it upright. It is noisy and hot near the basket, and it is one of the best parts of the experience, so keep your camera ready. You climb into the basket, which is divided into compartments, and the pilot gives a safety briefing. Listen carefully, especially to the landing position.

### The flight

Once aloft, the noise stops except for the occasional blast of the burner. Balloons drift with the wind, so the pilot controls height, not direction, and uses different wind layers to steer. The sunrise usually arrives during the flight. Bring a camera strap or wrist cord; nothing you drop is coming back.

### Landing and recovery

The landing site depends on where the wind takes you. A chase crew follows on the ground and meets you wherever you come down. Landings can be gentle or end with a bump and a short drag across the sand, which is why the landing position matters. Afterwards, many trips continue with breakfast or other desert activities before the drive back to the city.

## Weather decides everything

The single most important thing to accept before you book is that a balloon flight may not happen. Wind, poor visibility and unsettled weather can all cancel or delay flights, and pilots make that call on the morning itself. The UK Foreign Office notes that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/), which is also a useful habit in the days before a flight.

Practical ways to protect your plans:

- **Book early in your trip,** so there is time to rebook if the first morning is cancelled.
- **Ask about the cancellation policy** before you pay: what happens to your booking if the flight does not launch.
- **Keep the rest of that day light.** You will be tired, and the whole schedule may slide.

## Who a hot air balloon ride suits, and who should check first

Ballooning is gentle in the air, but it is not effortless. You need to climb in and out of a tall basket, stand for the whole flight and brace for landing.

### Health conditions and pregnancy

If you have back, joint or heart problems, a fear of heights, or recent surgery, ask your doctor before you book and tell the operator. Pregnant travellers should get medical advice first; many operators set their own restrictions. The Foreign Office stresses that [appropriate travel insurance for local treatment or unexpected medical evacuation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) is particularly important if you have a health condition or are pregnant.

### Insurance

Check that your policy covers ballooning. Some policies treat it as an adventure activity with exclusions or extra conditions. For desert driving and quad-biking tours, the Foreign Office advises going with [a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and checking that the activity is covered by your insurance. The same principle is a sensible one for a balloon flight.

### Children

Operators set their own age and height rules for children. Ask before booking, and think about whether a very early start suits younger children.

## What to wear and bring

Desert mornings can be surprisingly cold before sunrise, and warm quickly afterwards. Dress in layers you can peel off. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more on desert clothing.

- **A warm layer** for the pre-dawn wait and the first part of the flight.
- **A hat** that will not blow off; caps can be tricky near the burner, so ask the crew.
- **Closed, flat shoes** for climbing in and out and for landing on sand.
- **Sunglasses and sunscreen** for after sunrise.
- **Water,** because the desert warms up fast. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks, wearing light-coloured, loose clothing and avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather.

Leave large bags in the vehicle. Space in the basket is tight.

## Choosing a flight

Not all balloon trips are equal. A few questions separate them.

### Where does it fly?

Flights over or near a protected area tend to offer more wildlife and more untouched dunes. The DDCR describes its [zoning, limited access for selected operators and strict limits on activities with an environmental impact](https://www.ddcr.org/about/). Ask whether your flight launches in or near the reserve.

### What is included?

Some trips are a straightforward flight. Others bundle breakfast, a falconry display, camel rides or a vintage vehicle drive. Decide whether you want a calm morning or a packed one.

### How many people share the basket?

Larger baskets mean more people and less space at the edge. Smaller or private flights feel very different.

## Quick answers

**Where can you go hot air ballooning in the UAE?** Flights in Dubai's inland desert are the best known, and the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists balloon rides among its activities.

**Will you see wildlife from a balloon?** Often. The reserve describes the chance to spot oryx, gazelles and wandering camels, and says it holds around 400 oryx.

**Can a balloon flight be cancelled?** Yes. Flights depend on the weather on the morning, so book early in your trip and check the cancellation policy.

**Do you need special insurance?** Check that your travel insurance covers ballooning. The Foreign Office advises checking that desert tours are covered and says insurance is particularly important if you are pregnant or have a health condition.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Where do you launch and fly, and is it over or near the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve?
- What time is pickup in the month we are travelling, and how long is the flight itself?
- What happens to our booking if the flight is cancelled for weather?
- How many passengers share the basket, and are there age, height or health restrictions?
- What does the pilot's landing briefing involve, and how do you help anyone with limited mobility?
- What is included after landing, and what time will we be back at the hotel?
