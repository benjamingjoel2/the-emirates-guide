The road up Jebel Hafit leaves the last palms of Al Ain behind and starts to climb almost at once. Bend follows bend, the rock walls close in, and every time the car swings round a hairpin the city drops a little further below, green against the sand.

Near the top, cars pull in at the viewpoints and people step out into a breeze that the plains below never seem to get. That is Jebel Hafit at its simplest: a mountain you can drive up in an afternoon for a view that stretches far across the desert. There is more to it than the road, though. At its foot is a desert park with ancient tombs, trails, campsites, an old fort and some of the UAE's rarest wildlife.

So is it worth the trip? If you are in Al Ain, yes. Go late in the afternoon for the light, and give the desert park at the bottom a few hours of its own.

## Jebel Hafit at a glance

Visit Abu Dhabi describes Jebel Hafit as [Abu Dhabi's highest peak and the UAE's second tallest](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), rising to 1,249 metres and standing on the border with Oman. It says the name translates as "empty mountain", and that you can reach the summit by car, motorbike or, if you have the stamina, bicycle.

The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi (EAD) adds that the mountain sits [on the very edge of Abu Dhabi's southern border with Oman](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/jabal-hafit-national--park), near Al Ain, and that from Abu Dhabi city you head for the E22 Abu Dhabi to Al Ain road.

In practice the mountain has three parts for a visitor: the road and its viewpoints, the desert park along its base, and Green Mubazzarah, the grassy park with hot springs at its foot. You can do the road alone in an hour or two. Add the desert park and you need most of a day.

## Driving the Jebel Hafit mountain road

### The road itself

The Jebel Hafit Mountain Road is described by Visit Abu Dhabi as [an 11.7km fully paved, well-lit highway](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit) that is widely regarded as one of the world's great driving roads. Its separate Al Ain guide puts the drive to the summit at around [20 minutes](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

It is a proper mountain road: steep in places, with tight bends and long drops beyond the barriers. A few habits help:

- Use a lower gear on the way down rather than riding the brakes.
- Pull fully off the road at viewpoints, and watch for cars and cyclists coming round blind bends.
- Take the descent slowly after dark, when headlights from oncoming traffic can dazzle.
- If anyone in the car gets motion sick, give them the front seat and stop at the lay-bys.

### Do you need to walk?

No. Visit Abu Dhabi's family itinerary for Al Ain points out that the road is drivable, so the whole group can take in the views [without needing to walk any of it](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain). That makes Jebel Hafit one of the few UAE mountain viewpoints that works for grandparents, small children and anyone with limited mobility. The viewpoints are simply lay-bys and car parks beside the road, so you can see a great deal from close to the car.

### When to go up

Sunset is the popular choice, and for good reason: the ridges turn gold and then rust, and the lights of Al Ain come on below. Visit Abu Dhabi also recommends the summit for [sunrise and sunset photos](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), and its family itinerary says sunset is [generally considered the best time](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/itineraries/family-day-al-ain) to go.

On a clear winter day, it says, [you can see deep into Oman](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). In summer, go early. Haze and dust can flatten the view at any time of year, so treat a clear horizon as a bonus.

A few timing notes. Sunset is the most popular time, so the best lay-bys can fill early. Aim to be on the road well before the sun goes down rather than racing it. And remember that rain, though rare, is [more common in Al Ain](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) than elsewhere in the emirate because of the nearby Hajar Mountains, according to Visit Abu Dhabi. On a wet day, take the road even more slowly.

## The desert park: tombs, trails and history

### Older than you think

Jebel Hafit Desert Park lies at the foot of the mountain. Visit Abu Dhabi says it occupies [a nine-kilometre stretch](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), where you can hike, mountain bike, or ride a horse or camel, and see remains from the Neolithic period to the Jebel Hafit Tombs.

Abu Dhabi Culture explains that the park is part of [the Hafit component of the UNESCO-listed Cultural Sites of Al Ain](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park). In the Neolithic, it says, the Indian Ocean monsoon moved north, bringing rain and occasionally forming lakes where there are now deserts, and nomadic herders came to the mountain for hard rock that could be shaped into tools and arrowheads. Later, Bronze Age tombs were built around the mountain, the region began exporting copper, and in the Iron Age falaj irrigation developed and people reused the old tombs for burials. Taken together, Abu Dhabi Culture says, the park's remains record human activity in the area over [the past 8,000 years](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park).

### Seeing the tombs

Visit Abu Dhabi says the mountain is home to [more than 500 ancient tombs](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit) and that you can see them on a guided hike through the park. It adds that the 5,000-year-old tombs were excavated in 1959 at the request of Sheikh Zayed.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="jebel-hafit-guide-5" alts="A5" caption="Beehive tombs of stone are scattered across the lower slopes."></photo-set>

They are low, rounded stone structures, often called beehive tombs, scattered across the lower slopes. Reaching them takes [a car and a short scramble, nothing technical](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). From a distance they can look like piles of rubble; up close you start to see the careful coursing of the stones and the small entrances.

Treat them as what they are: graves and protected heritage. Do not climb on them, move stones or take anything away.

### Park practicalities

- Visit Abu Dhabi lists the desert park's day opening hours as [8:00 AM to 6:00 PM](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/jebel-hafit-desert-park), with 24-hour access to your accommodation if you are camping or glamping.
- Activities include hiking, mountain biking, and guided tours by horse, camel or dune buggy.
- Accompanied dogs are allowed on hikes and camping trips, the same page says, but not on private properties, including nearby accommodation.
- There is little shade on the trails. Start early, and carry more water than you expect to need.

## Wildlife on a bare-looking mountain

From the car, Jebel Hafit looks lifeless. It is not. EAD says the national park is home to [some of the world's most threatened wildlife and the UAE's rarest plants](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/jabal-hafit-national--park). It lists the dwarf palm, Blandford's fox, Brandt's hedgehog, the Arabian tahr and the only concentration of the globally threatened Egyptian vulture in the UAE, and notes a recent sighting of an Arabian caracal.

You are unlikely to see the shyer animals on a short visit, but keep an eye on the sky for raptors, and keep your distance from anything you do spot. EAD recommends visiting [during the day](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/jabal-hafit-national--park) to take in the rocky terrain. Binoculars are worth packing, and a slow walk along a wadi bed early in the morning gives you the best chance of seeing birds and lizards.

## Camping under the mountain

For many residents, Jebel Hafit means a night outdoors. Visit Abu Dhabi says overnight camping is allowed [at designated campsites](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain), including near the mountain, and suggests the cooler months from around October to April. The same guide describes the appeal simply: dark skies, cool night air and a silence broken only by the wind. The desert park offers [three camping experiences](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit): basic camping with your own gear, a serviced Bedouin-style camp, and air-conditioned glamping tents.

The camping guide's packing list is sensible: warm layers for the night, sturdy closed-toe shoes, plenty of water, a torch, a first-aid kit, sunscreen and [bags to take all your rubbish home](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain). For families, it suggests a short hike or bike ride on the lower slopes for older children, and a telescope or stargazing app once the sky goes dark. Our guide to [camping overnight in the UAE desert](/stories/overnight-desert-camping-uae/) covers the wider picture, and if you would rather have a bed in town, see [staying overnight in Al Ain](/stories/staying-overnight-in-al-ain/).

## Around the foot of the mountain

### Mezyad Fort

In the south of the park, Abu Dhabi Culture describes Mezyad Fort as the largest of the historic buildings that guarded the approaches to Al Ain, built [around the 1890s](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park). In more recent times it served as a police border post between the UAE and Oman.

Abu Dhabi Culture's page on the building, which it calls Mezyad Castle, gives more detail. It describes a rectangular building of [approximately 2,600 square metres](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/historic-buildings-and-cultural-landscapes/mezyad-castle), with tall cylindrical watchtowers on three corners around a courtyard planted with palms, and more than forty rooms. It was built from sarooj, a water-resistant mortar, with stone and wood. The towers and walls make a handsome stop at the end of a morning in the park, and the palm courtyard shows how even a fort here was organised around water.

### Green Mubazzarah

At the foot of the mountain, Green Mubazzarah has [natural hot springs, grass and picnic areas](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) that fill with Emirati families at weekends. Visit Abu Dhabi calls it a genuine local spot rather than a tourist setup. It is a relaxed place to stop before or after the climb, and a good chance to see how residents use the mountain. Dress modestly here, as you would in any family park.

## Staying safe in the hills

The mountain road is paved, but the land around it is rough. If you plan to leave the tarmac, the UK Foreign Office warns that desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a phone, and leaving your plans with someone. If you join a tour involving desert driving or quad biking, go with a reputable company and check your insurance covers it.

Visit Abu Dhabi's off-road guide lists a route that starts [just south of Al Ain](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/adventure/desert/off-road-desert-driving) towards White Sands, and advises checking tyres and the engine, carrying a first-aid kit, spare tyre, tool kit and plenty of water, and keeping emergency numbers handy, including Abu Dhabi Police on 999. Unless you are an experienced off-road driver in a suitable vehicle, leave this to a guide.

Heat is the bigger risk for walkers. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. In the UAE, [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health). Start walks early, carry more water than you think you need, and turn back if anyone feels unwell.

### Who it suits

Jebel Hafit works for almost everyone, which is unusual for a mountain. Drivers and photographers come for the road and the light. Families can drive up without walking, then let children run about on the grass at Green Mubazzarah. History lovers get the tombs and Mezyad Fort, and walkers and cyclists get the trails of the desert park. The one group it suits less well is anyone hoping for long, shaded hikes: the terrain here is bare rock, and the walking is best kept short and early.

### What catches people out

- **Underestimating the descent.** Brakes overheat on long downhill runs. Lower gears, gentle speeds.
- **Expecting shade.** The trails and tomb sites are exposed. A hat and long sleeves help even in winter.
- **Leaving it too late.** The park keeps day hours, and the viewpoints are at their busiest around sunset.
- **Cold nights.** After a warm day, a night at the campsite can feel surprisingly chilly. Pack a proper layer.

## Quick answers

**Can you drive up Jebel Hafit?** Yes. The mountain road is [an 11.7km fully paved, well-lit highway](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit) to the summit area.

**How long does the drive to the top take?** Visit Abu Dhabi puts it at around [20 minutes](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) from the base.

**Is Jebel Hafit the highest mountain in the UAE?** No. It is Abu Dhabi's highest peak and the UAE's second tallest.

**Can you camp at Jebel Hafit?** Yes, at designated campsites, including in Jebel Hafit Desert Park, which offers basic, serviced and glamping options.

**What are the Jebel Hafit Tombs?** Ancient stone burial tombs on the mountain's lower slopes, part of the UNESCO-listed Cultural Sites of Al Ain. You can see them on a guided hike.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Will the driver stop at the viewpoints on the way up, and how confident are they on the descent after dark?
- Does the desert park visit include a guided walk to the tombs, and how much scrambling is involved?
- Which campsite will we use, is it a designated site, and what bedding, toilets and shade are provided?
- How do you handle heat on walks, and what water and first aid do you carry?
- Will we leave the paved road at any point, and if so, in what vehicle?
