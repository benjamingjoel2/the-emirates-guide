The road up Jebel Hafit leaves the last palms of Al Ain behind and starts to climb almost at once. Bend follows bend, the rock walls close in, and every time the car swings round a hairpin the city drops a little further below, green against the sand.

Near the top, cars pull in at the viewpoints and people step out into a breeze that the plains below never seem to get. That is Jebel Hafit at its simplest: a mountain you can drive up in an afternoon for a view that stretches far across the desert. There is more to it than the road, though. At its foot is a desert park with ancient tombs, trails, campsites and some of the UAE's rarest wildlife.

So is it worth the trip? If you are in Al Ain, yes. Go late in the afternoon for the light, and give the desert park at the bottom a few hours of its own.

## Jebel Hafit at a glance

Visit Abu Dhabi describes Jebel Hafit as [Abu Dhabi's highest peak and the UAE's second tallest](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), standing on the border with Oman. It says the name translates as "empty mountain", and that you can reach the summit by car, motorbike or, if you have the stamina, bicycle.

The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi (EAD) adds that the mountain sits [on the very edge of Abu Dhabi's southern border with Oman](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/jabal-hafit-national--park), near Al Ain, and that from Abu Dhabi city you head for the E22 Abu Dhabi to Al Ain road.

## Driving the Jebel Hafit mountain road

### The road itself

The Jebel Hafit Mountain Road is described by Visit Abu Dhabi as [an 11.7km fully paved, well-lit highway](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit) that is widely regarded as one of the world's great driving roads. Its separate Al Ain guide puts the drive to the summit at around [20 minutes](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

It is a proper mountain road: steep in places, with tight bends and long drops beyond the barriers. A few habits help:

- Use a lower gear on the way down rather than riding the brakes.
- Pull fully off the road at viewpoints, and watch for cars and cyclists coming round blind bends.
- Take the descent slowly after dark, when headlights from oncoming traffic can dazzle.
- If anyone in the car gets motion sick, give them the front seat and stop at the lay-bys.

### When to go up

Sunset is the popular choice, and for good reason: the ridges turn gold and then rust, and the lights of Al Ain come on below. Visit Abu Dhabi also recommends the summit for [sunrise and sunset photos](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit).

On a clear winter day, it says, [you can see deep into Oman](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). In summer, go early. Haze and dust can flatten the view at any time of year, so treat a clear horizon as a bonus.

### Green Mubazzarah at the bottom

At the foot of the mountain, Green Mubazzarah has [natural hot springs, grass and picnic areas](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) that fill with Emirati families at weekends. It is a relaxed place to stop before or after the climb, and a good chance to see how residents use the mountain.

## The desert park: tombs, trails and a fort

### Older than you think

Jebel Hafit Desert Park lies at the foot of the mountain. Visit Abu Dhabi says it occupies [a nine-kilometre stretch](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit), where you can hike, mountain bike, or ride a horse or camel, and see remains from the Neolithic period to the Jebel Hafit Tombs.

Abu Dhabi Culture explains that the park is part of [the Hafit component of the UNESCO-listed Cultural Sites of Al Ain](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park). In the Neolithic, it says, nomadic herders came to the mountain for hard rock that could be shaped into tools and arrowheads. Later, Bronze Age tombs were built around the mountain, and in the Iron Age people reused them for burials.

### Seeing the tombs

Visit Abu Dhabi says the mountain is home to [more than 500 ancient tombs](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit) and that you can see them on a guided hike through the park. They are low, rounded stone structures, often called beehive tombs, scattered across the lower slopes. Reaching them takes [a car and a short scramble, nothing technical](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage).

Treat them as what they are: graves and protected heritage. Do not climb on them, move stones or take anything away.

### Mezyad Fort

In the south of the park, Abu Dhabi Culture describes Mezyad Fort as the largest of the historic buildings that guarded the approaches to Al Ain, built [around the 1890s](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/pre-history-and-paleontology/jebel-hafit-desert-park). In more recent times it served as a police border post between the UAE and Oman. Its towers and walls make a handsome stop at the end of a morning in the park.

## Wildlife on a bare-looking mountain

From the car, Jebel Hafit looks lifeless. It is not. EAD says the national park is home to [some of the world's most threatened wildlife and the UAE's rarest plants](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/jabal-hafit-national--park). It lists the dwarf palm, Blandford's fox, Brandt's hedgehog, the Arabian tahr and the only concentration of the globally threatened Egyptian vulture in the UAE, and notes a recent sighting of an Arabian caracal.

You are unlikely to see the shyer animals on a short visit, but keep an eye on the sky for raptors, and keep your distance from anything you do spot. EAD recommends visiting [during the day](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/experience-green-abu-dhabi/places-to-go/jabal-hafit-national--park) to take in the rocky terrain.

## Camping under the mountain

For many residents, Jebel Hafit means a night outdoors. Visit Abu Dhabi says overnight camping is allowed [at designated campsites](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain), including near the mountain, and suggests the cooler months from around October to April. The desert park offers [three camping experiences](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit): basic camping with your own gear, a serviced Bedouin-style camp, and air-conditioned glamping tents.

The camping guide's packing list is sensible: warm layers for the night, sturdy closed-toe shoes, plenty of water, a torch, a first-aid kit, sunscreen and [bags to take all your rubbish home](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain). ## Staying safe in the hills

The mountain road is paved, but the land around it is rough. If you plan to leave the tarmac, the UK Foreign Office warns that desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a phone, and leaving your plans with someone. If you join a tour involving desert driving or quad biking, go with a reputable company and check your insurance covers it.

Heat is the bigger risk for walkers. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. In the UAE, [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health). Start walks early, carry more water than you think you need, and turn back if anyone feels unwell.

## Quick answers

**Can you drive up Jebel Hafit?** Yes. The mountain road is [an 11.7km fully paved, well-lit highway](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit) to the summit area.

**How long does the drive to the top take?** Visit Abu Dhabi puts it at around [20 minutes](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) from the base.

**Is Jebel Hafit the highest mountain in the UAE?** No. It is Abu Dhabi's highest peak and the UAE's second tallest.

**Can you camp at Jebel Hafit?** Yes, at designated campsites, including in Jebel Hafit Desert Park, which offers basic, serviced and glamping options.

**What are the Jebel Hafit Tombs?** Ancient stone burial tombs on the mountain's lower slopes, part of the UNESCO-listed Cultural Sites of Al Ain. You can see them on a guided hike.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Will the driver stop at the viewpoints on the way up, and how confident are they on the descent after dark?
- Does the desert park visit include a guided walk to the tombs, and how much scrambling is involved?
- Which campsite will we use, is it a designated site, and what bedding, toilets and shade are provided?
- How do you handle heat on walks, and what water and first aid do you carry?
- Will we leave the paved road at any point, and if so, in what vehicle?
