Wind the window down near the top of Jebel Jais and the air that comes in is different. It is thinner and fresher, and in the cooler months it can feel almost sharp, the kind of air you do not expect an hour's drive from a beach. Below you the road you have just climbed lies folded against the mountain like a dropped ribbon.

That change in the air is the main reason people make the trip. This Jebel Jais guide covers the drive, the viewing deck park, the walking trails and the safety basics. The short version: it is a straightforward drive on a good road, the views are wide, and the best way to do it is early in the day, between October and April, with a warm layer and plenty of water.

## What Jebel Jais is

[Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) describes Jebel Jais as the highest mountain in the UAE and part of the Al Hajar range, which runs from Ras Al Khaimah into Oman's Musandam Peninsula. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) gives the height as 1,934 metres.

The tourism board says the temperature on the mountain is usually around 10°C cooler than elsewhere in the emirate. That is not a guarantee of cool weather in summer, but it does make the top a welcome escape from the heat of the plains for much of the year.

### Mountains made of stone and sea

The same [Jebel Jais page](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) says the name Al Hajar means "the stone", and describes a range about 100 km wide and 700 km long, formed by a continental collision more than 70 million years ago. It mentions rare fossils and marine sedimentary rocks among the layers. You do not need to be a geologist to notice it: the cliffs are banded and folded, and the colours shift from grey to ochre to rust as the light moves across them.

Look closely at the road cuttings as you climb and you will see those layers exposed in neat bands. It is one of the few places in the country where the geology is on display right beside the tarmac, and it is worth pausing at a pull-in just to look at the rock.

## The Jebel Jais drive, bend by bend

The drive starts on the coastal plain, among date farms and low villages, and the mountains rise ahead like a fortress wall. Then the road begins to climb. The tourism board's page on the [Jais Viewing Deck Park](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/) describes a route that starts at sea level and passes desert, rocky wadis, boulders, abandoned villages and date palm farms before it reaches the park.

### On the way up

The tourism board describes the road as well constructed, with hairpin bends and [regular pull-ins](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) where you can stop safely to look at the views. Use them. Pulling over is the only safe way to take photos, and it gives the driver a break from the bends.

The road climbs through bare rock in reds, ochres and greys, with deep ravines dropping away below the barriers. On clear mornings you can see down to the plain and the sea.

### Driving it yourself

The road up to the viewing deck park is, according to [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/), accessible by cars, four-wheel drives, motorbikes and cycles. You do not need an off-road vehicle for the main road. The tourism board's [FAQs](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) describe the trip to the top as long but straightforward, on roads that are generally in excellent condition.

You do need patience. The UK Foreign Office notes that [driving standards are not always as disciplined](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) as in the UK and that speeding is common. On a mountain road, that means expecting fast cars behind you and cyclists on the climbs. Keep to your lane on the bends, use lower gears on the way down, and let faster traffic pass at the pull-ins. Our guide to [driving in the UAE as a visitor](/stories/driving-in-the-uae-as-a-visitor/) covers the wider rules.

## The viewing deck park

Near the top, the road reaches the [Jais Viewing Deck Park](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/). The tourism board says it sits 1,250 metres above sea level and almost 30 km into the mountains, and that it spans a ridge with seven viewing decks and 12 binoculars. On one side you look back over the zigzagging road and the peaks; on the other, towards date wadis, villages and the Gulf.

It also says the park is lit by solar power and has places for coffee and snacks. Its listed opening hours are 8am to 8pm from Sunday to Thursday, and 8am to 11pm on Friday and Saturday. The [Jebel Jais page](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) notes that visiting the mountain itself is free but that entry to the viewing deck park and to the adventure attractions is charged.

### Sunset or sunrise?

The tourism board says the sun sets early here all year, [between 17:30 and 18:00](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/), and that you can watch it go down over the Gulf before the night sky comes out over the mountains. Late afternoon brings soft light, and it is when the decks are busiest. Early morning is quieter and cooler, with clearer air before the haze builds. If you plan to walk, go early.

## Walking the Jebel Jais trails

The mountain is more than a viewpoint. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/activities/hiking/) says there are six designated trails with a combined length of 16 km, spread through the upper and lower parts of the mountain and suited to walkers of different levels. It describes the mountains as home to mountain goats, donkeys and the occasional eagle.

The tourism board says climbing experience is not needed, but that you need a head for heights, reasonable fitness and proper hiking shoes. Its safety list is worth following to the letter:

- Choose a route that suits the least experienced person in your group.
- Check the weather forecast, because the mountains can have their own weather, especially in winter.
- Never hike alone, and tell someone where you are going and when you expect to be back.
- Carry a map. The routes are marked, but you should still know where you are.
- Take plenty of water and snacks.
- Wear boots with good grip, never flip-flops or sandals, and dress in layers.
- Carry a first aid kit and know how to use it.
- Take regular breaks, do not push others beyond what they can manage, and note emergency numbers before you set off.

The same page says groups of ten or more must inform the Ras Al Khaimah tourism authority by email before the hike, with details of the route, the participants and the timings.

### What a walk feels like

Expect loose stone underfoot, little shade and steep drops in places. Distances that look short on a map take longer than you think, because you will be picking your footing on rock rather than striding along a path. Start early, turn back at a set time whatever the distance covered, and keep an eye on the slowest walker rather than the fastest. The quiet is striking: once you are a few minutes from the road, the loudest thing you will hear is often your own boots.

### Thrills higher up

The mountain also has paid adventure attractions, including ropes courses and a long zipline, which the tourism board lists on its [Jebel Jais page](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/). It gives the zipline as 2.83 km long, suspended at 1,680 metres, lasting roughly three minutes at speeds of up to 150 km/h, and the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) says it is certified by Guinness World Records as the world's longest. The portal also mentions the UAE's highest restaurant on the mountain. Book attractions directly and check age, weight and health restrictions before you go.

## When to go up Jebel Jais

The tourism board says the best months are [October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/), when the mountain weather suits a full day outdoors. In summer, it says, major landmarks across the emirate reach 35 to 38°C, while the peak averages 31°C in the daytime: cooler, but still hot for walking. Its [FAQs](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) add that occasional rain showers can fall in winter, particularly in the mountains.

Rain is the one thing to take seriously. The Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises checking forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology. The tourism board's page on nearby [Wadi Shawka](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/wadi-shawka/) warns that wadis are prone to flash floods during heavy rain and should be avoided until the weather clears. It also lists the emergency numbers: 999 for the police and 998 for an ambulance.

## How a day on the mountain unfolds

A good day on Jebel Jais starts in the dark. Leave the coast around first light, drive up with the sun rising behind the ridges, and stop at two or three pull-ins on the way. Reach the viewing decks while they are still quiet, then walk one of the shorter trails before the sun gets high.

By late morning the decks start to fill and the light flattens. That is a good time for coffee and then the drive down, in low gear and without hurry. Lunch on the coast, rest through the early afternoon, and you still have time for a short heritage visit before dark. If you would rather catch sunset from the top, reverse the plan: a lazy morning, a mid-afternoon drive up, and the descent in the first hour of darkness, taken slowly.

## What to bring

- A warm layer, even when the coast is hot. Wind on the ridge can make it feel much colder than the number suggests.
- More water than you think you need, plus snacks.
- Shoes with good grip if you plan to step off the decks.
- Sun protection: hat, sunglasses and sunscreen. Shade is scarce.
- A charged phone and the emergency numbers noted down.
- Binoculars, if you have them, for the goats and birds of prey.

## Families and accessibility

Jebel Jais is one of the easier mountain trips in the UAE for families, because the main road takes you most of the way. The tourism board describes the mountain as suitable for families, solo travellers and couples, and its [Jebel Jais page](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) says expert guides are present for booked activities. The bends can unsettle children prone to car sickness, so build in stops.

The tourism board's viewing deck page gives no detail on step-free access, so check directly if anyone in your group uses a wheelchair. The trails are a different matter: rocky, uneven and exposed, they suit older children with proper shoes and a steady head, not toddlers or anyone unsteady on their feet.

## Staying overnight

You do not have to come down the same day. The tourism board's [Jebel Jais page](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) lists overnight options on and around the mountain, including a camp with air-conditioned cabins and a glamping camp it describes as the highest in the UAE. A night up high means sunrise without a pre-dawn drive and the trails to yourself in the first cool hour. ## Making a day of it

Jebel Jais pairs naturally with the rest of the emirate. Come down by lunchtime and you have the afternoon for something gentler. [Dhayah Fort](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/dhayah-fort/) sits on a hill between the date gardens and the Jebel Jais mountains, and its 239 steps are a good short climb once the day starts to cool. Our guide to [climbing to Dhayah Fort](/stories/dhayah-fort-guide/) has the details.

If you want to walk somewhere lower, the tourism board describes [Wadi Shawka](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/wadi-shawka/) as having beginner loops of about 3 to 5 km and longer routes of close to 10 km, with no entry fee, and recommends November to April. ## Quick answers

**How high is Jebel Jais?** The UAE government portal gives its height as 1,934 metres, making it the highest peak in the UAE.

**Is Jebel Jais cooler than the rest of the UAE?** Visit Ras Al Khaimah says the mountain is usually around 10°C cooler than elsewhere in the emirate.

**Do I need a 4x4 to drive up Jebel Jais?** No. The tourism board says the road to the viewing deck park is accessible by cars, four-wheel drives, motorbikes and cycles.

**Is it free to visit Jebel Jais?** The tourism board says visiting the mountain is free, but the viewing deck park and adventure attractions charge for entry.

**When is the best time to visit Jebel Jais?** The tourism board recommends October to April.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is your driver experienced on the Jebel Jais road, and will they stop at the pull-ins for photos?
- Which of the marked trails would suit our group's fitness, and how long will it take us?
- Do you provide a guide, maps and water for the walk, and what is the plan in an emergency?
- What will the temperature and wind be like at the top on our date, and what should we wear?
- If rain is forecast for the mountains, what is the alternative plan?
- If we book an adventure attraction, what age, weight or health restrictions apply?
