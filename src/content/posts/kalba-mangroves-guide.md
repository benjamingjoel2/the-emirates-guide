The paddle dips, the kayak slides forward, and the noise of the coast road simply stops. Ahead, a channel of still water bends between walls of glossy green leaves, roots arching down into the tide like the legs of a crouching animal. Somewhere in the branches a flash of blue and white darts away.

That is Kalba at its best. This small town on Sharjah's east coast sits beside what Visit Sharjah calls [the oldest mangrove forest in the UAE](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/khor-kalba-mangrove-centre/), and the Kalba mangroves are the main reason to make the long drive across the mountains. If you want quiet water, birdlife and a slower slice of the country, it repays the effort. If you want a beach resort buzz, look elsewhere.

## Where Kalba is, and why it feels far away

Kalba is one of Sharjah's enclaves on the Gulf of Oman, separated from Sharjah city by mountains and by other emirates. Visit Sharjah places it [120km east of Sharjah city and around 12km south of Fujairah city](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/kalba/), so in practice you reach it by crossing the Hajar range and then dropping down towards the coast.

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/sharjah) describes Sharjah as the only emirate that lies on both coasts, and says Kalba keeps a historical charm, is known for its old forts, and attracts bird watchers, kayakers and hikers. That mix is exactly what you will find: a modest seaside town, a promenade, a heritage house and, on its edge, a great green tangle of mangroves.

History runs deep here. The government's emirate guide notes that Kalba was [an important settlement 4,500 years ago](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), and that its fort, now in ruins, was mentioned by a Venetian traveller in an account of his journey through the region in 1580.

## Kayaking the Kalba mangroves

Early morning, the water flat and pale, is the time to be on it. The light is gentle, the air is still cool enough to paddle comfortably, and birds are busiest.

Visit Sharjah lists [kayaking among the oldest mangrove forest in Arabia](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/kalba/) as one of the headline things to do in Kalba, and its East Coast guide mentions kayaking alongside scuba diving and snorkelling in the [seaside towns of Khorfakkan and Kalba](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/).

### What a paddle feels like

The open lagoon gives way to narrower channels where the mangroves close in on both sides. You will hear the click and pop of crabs in the mud, smell salt and leaf, and notice how the water changes colour with depth and shade. It is calm work rather than an adventure sport, which is why it suits beginners and families with older children.

### Practical points for paddlers

- **Go guided the first time.** Channels look alike, and a guide will know which ones are passable at the state of the tide on the day.
- **Wear a life jacket.** The Foreign Office advises checking that [life jackets are available for all passengers](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) on tourist boats, and the same common sense applies in a kayak.
- **Cover up against the sun.** A hat, sunglasses and long sleeves matter more than sunscreen alone on reflective water.
- **Keep your distance.** Do not grab branches, step on roots or chase birds. The mangroves are a living nursery for fish and crabs.

## The Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre

If you would rather stay on dry feet, or want to understand what you have just paddled through, start at the visitor centre. Visit Sharjah describes the [Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/khor-kalba-mangrove-centre/) as one of the most recent ecotourism projects in Kalba, set in the middle of an ancient stand of mangroves. It says visitors taking the outdoor tour can see trees up to 8 metres tall.

Inside, the same page says, you can watch shore birds including the rare Arabian collared kingfisher, along with crabs, green turtles, fish and smaller marine life. It is a good stop for children, and for anyone who likes to put names to what they have seen.

## Birdwatching around Kalba

Bring binoculars and patience. The best sightings come to people who find a shady spot and simply wait.

The kingfisher is the bird most people hope for. The government's emirate guide notes that the [Khor Kalba Nature Reserve](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), close to the border with Oman, is home to the rare white-collared kingfisher among other wildlife. Visit Sharjah's Kalba page also highlights [some of the rarest birds in the world](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/kalba/) and the Kalba Bird of Prey Centre, which showcases falconry traditions and a diverse collection of birds.

The same page notes that many visitors come to see the Arabian oryx, the UAE's national animal, before heading to a nearby beach, and lists the Al Hefaiyah Mountain Conservation Centre, where native mountain and desert species are shown across a protected area in the Khor Kalba area. If wildlife is your main reason for travelling, our guide to [birdwatching in the UAE](/stories/birdwatching-in-the-uae/) shows how Kalba fits alongside the country's other wetlands.

## Heritage in town: the old house by the sea

After a morning on the water, the town itself is pleasant to wander. Visit Sharjah points to the [Bait Sheikh Saeed bin Hamed Al Qassimi museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/kalba/), a restored fort and a sleepy seaside promenade. It says the museum was built in 1901 and once served as a royal residence.

The house is a good counterpoint to the mangroves: thick walls, shaded rooms and a sense of how the coast was lived in before the highways arrived. Treat it like any heritage site in the UAE. Dress modestly, keep your voice down and ask before photographing people.

## Beaches and the wider east coast

Kalba is not a big resort town, but it has a quieter shoreline and Visit Sharjah mentions [a selection of scenic beaches](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/kalba/). If you swim, the Foreign Office warns that rip currents can occur at any beach and advises [swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Red flags mean it is dangerous to swim.

Kalba also works well as one stop on a longer coastal route. Visit Sharjah's East Coast guide mentions [guided boat tours to spot dolphins](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/) and water sports further north. Our [Khorfakkan guide](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) covers the next town up the coast, and our [east coast road trip](/stories/uae-east-coast-road-trip/) links Kalba with Fujairah and Dibba.

## How to plan a Kalba day

Because of the distance, Kalba rewards an early start or an overnight stay. A sensible shape looks like this:

1. **Leave early.** Aim to be on the water while the morning is still cool and the birds are active.
2. **Paddle or tour the mangroves first.** Guided kayaking or the visitor centre's outdoor tour.
3. **Late breakfast or early lunch in town.** Simple seafood and local bakeries suit the place.
4. **Heritage in the heat of the day.** The museum house is shaded and cool.
5. **Afternoon on the coast.** A beach walk, or a drive north towards Khorfakkan.

The cooler months make all of this far more comfortable. ## Quick answers

**Where are the Kalba mangroves?** On Sharjah's east coast, beside the town of Kalba. Visit Sharjah places Kalba about 120km east of Sharjah city and around 12km south of Fujairah city.

**Can you kayak in the Kalba mangroves?** Yes. Visit Sharjah lists kayaking in the mangrove forest as one of Kalba's main activities, and a guide is the easiest way to do it the first time.

**What wildlife can you see in Kalba?** Visit Sharjah mentions the rare Arabian collared kingfisher, crabs, green turtles and fish around the Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre, and the Arabian oryx and birds of prey at nearby centres.

**Are the Kalba mangroves really the oldest in the UAE?** That is how Visit Sharjah describes them, with trees up to 8 metres tall seen on the centre's outdoor tour.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What time should we be on the water on our date, given the tide and the heat?
- Is the kayaking guided, and are life jackets and sun protection provided for everyone?
- Can you combine the mangroves with the Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre, the heritage museum or the bird of prey centre in one day?
- How long is the drive from our hotel, and would an overnight on the east coast make the day easier?
- Which nearby beaches have lifeguards and flags if we want to swim afterwards?
