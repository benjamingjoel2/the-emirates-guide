The road climbs through tunnels cut into bare brown mountains, and then, without warning, the rock falls away. Below you is a wide blue bay, a curve of sand, a port full of cranes and a town pressed between the sea and the peaks. You have crossed the country and arrived on a different coast.

That is Khorfakkan, Sharjah's town on the Gulf of Oman. Sharjah is the only emirate on both coasts, says the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/sharjah), and Khorfakkan is the largest town on the east coast, midway between Dibba Al Hisn and Fujairah. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/khorfakkan/) notes that it is surrounded by the emirate of Fujairah, which explains why it feels like an island of Sharjah on the far shore.

What is there to do? A relaxed beach and corniche, a waterfall and amphitheatre facing the bay, watchtowers on the hills with short, rewarding trails, a mountain lake, a wadi and good snorkelling. It works as a long day trip, and better still as a night or two.

## Getting to Khorfakkan

Most visitors drive or come with a driver. The Sharjah to Khorfakkan highway makes the journey quicker than it once was; [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/al-rafisah-dam/) says the new road cut travel time from Sharjah to Khorfakkan to 45 minutes. Allow more if you are coming from Dubai.

The drive is part of the pleasure. Plan a stop at Al Rafisah Dam on the way in (see below) and you will arrive in town with the sea still ahead of you.

## The bay, the beach and the corniche

Khorfakkan Beach is the heart of town life. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/khorfakkan-beach/) describes a 3 km crescent of sand lined with palm trees, with swings and football goalposts, and boat and jet ski rentals near the centre of the corniche. The town page adds [kayaking, sailing, diving and dhow cruises](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/khorfakkan/), and an afternoon by the busy port.

Late afternoon is the best time: the sun drops behind the mountains, the bay falls into shade, and families come out to walk the corniche.

Before you swim, the UK Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises swimming only at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags. Look for red flags, which mean swimming is dangerous. Swimwear belongs on the beach; the same advice asks visitors to [dress modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), so cover up when you walk back into town.

## The amphitheatre and the waterfall

On the waterfront, a Roman-inspired amphitheatre faces the bay, and next to it water pours down a rock face. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/khorfakkan-amphitheater-and-waterfall/) says the waterfall is 45 m high, man-made but carved from natural rock, and often lit at night. Viewing windows at the top are sometimes open to the public, so you can look down as the water falls.

The amphitheatre hosts events, so check what is on. Even without a show, come after dark to see the waterfall lit, then walk along the beach.

## Up to the towers: short climbs with long views

Khorfakkan's hills are dotted with old watchtowers, part of a chain that once guarded the town. They make the best short walks on the coast.

### The Al Rabi Tower trail

If you do one walk, make it this. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/al-rabi-hiking-trail/) describes a well-marked trail that zigzags up the mountain to Al Rabi Tower, built in 1915 as part of a defence network for the town. It is often called a beginner's hike, good for people with little experience and for children, with views of the Gulf of Oman the whole way. The page suggests the round trip takes two to three hours.

Two practical notes from the same page: the few parking spaces at the start fill quickly, and the trail is busy at weekends. Go early, carry plenty of water, and take your rubbish home.

### Al Adwani Tower

Closer to the port, a winding, lit path leads up to [Al Adwani Tower](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/al-adwani-tower/). Visit Sharjah says the original was built in 1623 as a lighthouse for ships entering Khorfakkan port, was destroyed in 1985, and has since been rebuilt in its original location. It is a gentle evening stroll with a view over the harbour.

### The Resistance Monument

At the entrance to town, on a hill above Khorfakkan Square, the [Resistance Monument](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/resistance-monument/) honours those who resisted the Portuguese invasion in 1507. Visit Sharjah says it has a glass hall with views over the town, and a walkway around it for looking out and reflecting on the history.

### Hisn Khorfakkan

To put it all in context, visit [Hisn Khorfakkan](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/hisn-khorfakkan/), a museum inspired by the design of the original fort, which stands nearby. It uses visual displays to tell the town's long history. Our [guide to Sharjah's museums](/stories/sharjah-museums-guide/) covers the city collections too.

## Into the mountains: the dam, a village and a wadi

Turn inland and the town disappears behind rock within minutes.

### Al Rafisah Dam

On the highway into town, [Al Rafisah Dam](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/al-rafisah-dam/) holds a clear lake below the Hajar mountains, with boating, a visitor centre and a rest area. Visit Sharjah says a palm-fringed walkway of about a kilometre runs around the dam, through old plantations and up to the restored Wadi Shea Fortress.

### Najd Al Maqsar

High above the valley, [Najd Al Maqsar](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/najd-al-maqsar-village/) is a restored mountain village with palm frond houses, farms watered by a falaj irrigation system and a fortress Visit Sharjah says is nearly 300 years old. A dedicated trail links it to Al Rafisah Dam, and restored houses now offer places to stay.

### Wadi Shees

[Wadi Shees](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/wadi-shees/) lies in the Hajar mountains near Khorfakkan, with rocky terrain, cliffs and, after rain, streams and pools. Visit Sharjah notes that facilities are limited, some off-road areas may need a four-wheel-drive, and you should bring water and food. For an easy taste, the [Wadi Shees Nature Trail](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/wadi-shees-nature-trail/) is a 1.8 km family-friendly loop beside an old falaj, through terraced farms and palm shade.

Respect the water. The Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises checking forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology. Never drive or linger in a wadi bed when rain is possible.

## Under the water: snorkelling and diving

Just off the public beach, [Sira Khorfakkan](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/sira-khorfakkan/) is a small rocky island that Visit Sharjah says is popular for snorkelling, diving and kayaking, with sea anemones and turtles for snorkellers and moray eels, rays and Arabian angelfish for divers. It recommends families use a nearby diving centre for guides.

Visit Sharjah's [diving page](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/sports/diving-in-khorfakkan/) lists seven dive sites along the bay, from reefs to a sunken boat. It advises that you must be certified to dive, should go with a certified school or operator, and should check you have scuba insurance, since not all travel insurance covers it. ## Quick answers

**How long does it take to drive from Sharjah to Khorfakkan?**
Visit Sharjah says the newer Sharjah to Khorfakkan highway cut the journey to 45 minutes. Allow more from Dubai.

**Is Khorfakkan part of Sharjah or Fujairah?**
It belongs to Sharjah. Visit Sharjah notes it is surrounded by the emirate of Fujairah, and the UAE government portal calls it the largest town on the east coast.

**Is it safe to swim at Khorfakkan Beach?**
The UK Foreign Office says rip currents can occur at any beach, and advises swimming only where there are lifeguards and flags.

**What is the easiest hike in Khorfakkan?**
Visit Sharjah calls the Al Rabi Tower trail a beginner's hike suitable for children, taking two to three hours there and back.

**Can you snorkel in Khorfakkan?**
Yes. Visit Sharjah says Sira Khorfakkan, just off the public beach, is popular for snorkelling, with sea anemones and turtles.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which beaches in Khorfakkan have lifeguards and flags on the days we are visiting?
- Is your dive or snorkel centre certified, and what experience do we need for Sira Khorfakkan or the bay dive sites?
- Is the Al Rabi trail realistic for our group, and when will it be quietest?
- Is anything on at the amphitheatre during our stay, and are the waterfall viewing windows open?
- What is the forecast for the mountains, and is Wadi Shees safe to visit on the day?
- Can we stay a night in Khorfakkan or Najd Al Maqsar to see the bay at sunrise?
