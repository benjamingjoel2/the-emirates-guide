The road south from Abu Dhabi to Liwa runs straight for a long time. Then the dunes start: low at first, then taller, then so tall that the tarmac looks like a thin grey thread laid between walls of orange sand.

By the time you reach Liwa, the city feels very far away. There are palm groves in the hollows, a few towns strung along the edge of the sand, and beyond them the Empty Quarter, one of the largest sand seas on Earth. For a first-timer, Liwa is the place in the UAE where the desert stops being a backdrop to an evening out and becomes the whole point.

Is it worth the drive? If you want scale, silence and the biggest dunes in the country, yes. It suits travellers with a car, a night or two to spare and a healthy respect for the heat. Here is how to plan it.

## Where Liwa is, and what the Empty Quarter means

Liwa lies in Al Dhafra, Abu Dhabi's western region. Visit Abu Dhabi describes Al Dhafra as stretching [from the Liwa Desert to the west coast](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) and offshore islands, including Sir Bani Yas Island, and lists desert experiences in Liwa among its main draws.

The Empty Quarter, the Rub' al Khali, is the vast sand desert that spreads south from here across the Arabian Peninsula. Liwa sits on its northern edge, which is why the dunes here are so much larger than those near Dubai. The oasis itself is a long arc of palm farms and settlements in the hollows between dunes. Visit Abu Dhabi ranks it as the UAE's second-largest oasis, after Al Ain, describing Al Ain Oasis as the largest [followed by Liwa Oasis](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis).

If the name feels familiar, it may be thanks to Wilfred Thesiger. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that he [crossed the Empty Quarter twice in the late 1940s](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) with local companions. His photographs hang at Al Jahili Fort in Al Ain, and they are worth seeing before or after a Liwa trip.

### Why Liwa matters to Abu Dhabi

Liwa is not just scenery. Abu Dhabi Culture explains that the Bani Yas, the most notable tribal confederation of Al Dhafra, controlled [a large territory from the Liwa Oasis to the coast](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-dhafra-fort), and that the alliance was led for more than a generation by the Al Bu Falah, who originated from the Liwa Oasis. In other words, the families who went on to lead Abu Dhabi trace their roots to these dunes and date gardens. Keep that in mind as you drive past the farms: this is old, settled land, not empty wilderness.

## Getting to Liwa and the Empty Quarter

### Driving there

Visit Abu Dhabi says driving from Abu Dhabi city to Al Dhafra [typically takes around two to three hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra), depending on where you are heading, and puts Tel Moreeb at [around two and a half hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/tel-moreeb) south of the city on a straightforward, well-signed drive.

A car is the most convenient way to explore, because the sights are spread out. The same page says [guided tours are available](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) for some experiences, and taxis work best for direct journeys rather than exploring widely.

Leave the city with a full tank, plenty of water and snacks, and a phone charger for the car. The road is good, but long stretches pass through open desert, and it is easier to stop when you want to than to hunt for a service station when you need one.

### Day trip or overnight?

You can technically do it in a day, but it is a lot of driving for a short look. A night lets you see the dunes at sunset and sunrise, when the light is low and the ridges throw long shadows, and spares you a tired drive home in the dark. Visit Abu Dhabi describes the region as [well suited to long weekends and short breaks](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra). Our guide to choosing [a desert stay or a city hotel](/stories/desert-stay-or-city-hotel-uae/) helps you weigh it up.

### How a two-day trip unfolds

A first visit tends to settle into the same shape. Leave Abu Dhabi in the late morning, eat on the way, and arrive in the early afternoon. Rest through the heat, then head to Tel Moreeb in the late afternoon to watch the light change and, if you have the legs, climb part of the way up. Dinner, a look at the night sky, and an early night.

The next morning is the best part. Be on the sand before sunrise, when it is cool underfoot and the dunes are sharp with shadow. After breakfast, drive slowly through the oasis settlements, stop at one of the forts, and set off back to the city before the middle of the day.

## Tel Moreeb: the dune everyone comes to see

### How big it is

Tel Moreeb, also written Moreeb Dune, is the landmark of the region. Visit Abu Dhabi says it rises [more than 300 metres](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/tel-moreeb) from the sands, with a 50-degree incline that makes it one of the world's tallest sand dunes. Local drivers first tackled the slope in 2001, and it has hosted motorsport ever since.

Standing at the bottom, it does not look like a dune so much as a wall.

### Climbing it on foot

You do not need a festival ticket to visit. Visit Abu Dhabi says a [paved road runs to the base](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/tel-moreeb) and you can climb it on foot year-round, but warns that the slope makes for a genuinely strenuous climb and many visitors stop partway.

Tips if you try:

- Go at sunrise or late afternoon, never in the middle of the day.
- Climb barefoot only if the sand is cool; it heats fast.
- Zigzag rather than going straight up, and rest often.
- Come down slowly. The descent is quick, and fun, but easy to tumble on.
- Leave something at the bottom for later: water, a hat, and a bag to keep sand out of your phone.

### Other ways up

Outside the festival, the same page lists [quad bikes, dune buggies and guided 4x4 rides](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/tel-moreeb) to the top, and notes the dune is a popular photography spot at sunrise and sunset. ## The Liwa International Festival

Every winter the desert gets loud. Visit Abu Dhabi says the Liwa International Festival [typically runs for around three weeks](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events/platinum-events/liwa-international-festival) from mid-December into early January, with most attractions and Liwa Village open daily from afternoon until midnight. The festival page lists this season's dates as [11 December 2026 to 2 January 2027](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events/platinum-events/liwa-international-festival), from 4 PM to midnight.

Motorsport at Tel Moreeb is the headline, with drifting, drag racing and dune events. The Moreeb Hill Climb brings four-wheel drives, quad bikes and dirt bikes to compete on the dune, Visit Abu Dhabi says, with [a flat plain at its base](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/tel-moreeb) reserved for competitors and spectators.

There is more to it than engines. Liwa Village has a play park, a petting zoo, a craft souk with workshops in perfume-making, henna, weaving and coffee, a Ferris wheel, live concerts and fireworks. Visit Abu Dhabi says the festival's falconry and pigeon hunting competitions, and traditional sports such as sand wrestling, [celebrate skills passed down through generations](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events/platinum-events/liwa-international-festival), and that the Liwa Forts open for cultural cafes and pop-up shops by local artisans. The festival site also includes prayer rooms, a clinic, and family and individual camping areas.

Entry details vary by attraction and are confirmed closer to each festival, so check the official channels before you go. If you want quiet dunes, avoid these weeks; if you want atmosphere, plan around them, and book somewhere to sleep early.

## Beyond the big dune

Liwa is more than one slope of sand. Visit Abu Dhabi points to the historic Liwa Forts, Liwa Oasis and the Empty Quarter, along with sandboarding and guided dune bashing. Drive slowly along the oasis settlements and you will see date farms tucked into the dips between the dunes, which is the oldest reason anyone lived here at all.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="liwa-empty-quarter-guide-6" alts="A6" caption="Liwa's forts guarded water and the people who depended on it."></photo-set>

Abu Dhabi Culture describes the forts and defensive buildings of Al Dhafra, many from the 19th century or earlier, as forming [a coherent defensive belt](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-sites/museums/al-dhafra-fort) that protected water access and the tribes. Elsewhere in the region, Al Dhafra Fort, built in 2017 in the style of that historic architecture, has an exhibition on the customs and desert way of life of Al Dhafra. Abu Dhabi Culture lists its hours as daily 9 am to 2:30 pm, and 9 am to 12 pm on Fridays, so plan a morning visit.

If you have more time in Al Dhafra, Visit Abu Dhabi suggests [Sir Bani Yas Island, the Arabian Wildlife Park and the mangroves at Al Mugheirah Bay](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/tel-moreeb), along with Mirfa Beach on the coast. Our [Sir Bani Yas Island guide](/stories/sir-bani-yas-island-guide/) shows how a coastal leg can follow the dunes.

For a gentler first taste of the desert closer to the cities, see our guide to [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/).

## Sleeping in the desert

You have three broad choices: a hotel or resort in or near the oasis, a camp, or a festival camping area during the winter festival. Visit Abu Dhabi's camping guide notes you can [pitch a tent among the towering dunes of Al Dhafra](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain), and that overnight camping is allowed at designated campsites in the UAE. If you are new to it, a guided camp with tents already pitched takes away most of the risk.

Nights can be cold in winter, even after a warm day. Pack a proper layer, a torch and bags to take your rubbish home. Our guide to [camping overnight in the UAE desert](/stories/overnight-desert-camping-uae/) has a fuller checklist.

## Staying safe in the desert

This is serious desert, and it deserves serious preparation.

### Driving off the tarmac

The UK Foreign Office warns that desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It advises always travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and leaving a copy of your plans with someone. If you join a tour for desert driving or quad biking, go with a reputable company with proper health and safety procedures, and check your insurance covers it.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="liwa-empty-quarter-guide-7" alts="A7" caption="Soft sand catches vehicles easily, which is why convoys and recovery gear matter."></photo-set>

If you are in an ordinary hire car, stay on the paved roads. Soft sand catches even confident drivers. Visit Abu Dhabi's off-road guide grades its Liwa Crossing route, [109km long](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/adventure/desert/off-road-desert-driving), as one for advanced drivers with camping skills, and suggests planning one and a half to two days for it. The same guide advises checking tyres and the engine, carrying a first-aid kit, spare tyre, tool kit, navigation and plenty of water, and keeping Abu Dhabi Police (999) and the ambulance service (998) to hand.

### Heat and sun

Even in winter the sun on open sand is strong. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, drink more cold drinks and wear light, loose clothing. It lists tiredness, dizziness, headache, nausea, heavy sweating and strong thirst as signs of heat exhaustion, and treats heatstroke as a medical emergency. In the UAE, [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health).

### What catches people out

- **Distance.** It is easy to underestimate how far apart things are. Plan fuel and water stops.
- **Sand temperature.** Cool at dawn, painful by late morning. Keep shoes on hand.
- **Phone signal and battery.** Do not rely on one phone for navigation and emergencies. Carry a power bank.
- **Wind.** Blowing sand gets into cameras, eyes and food. Sunglasses and a scarf help.
- **Festival crowds.** During the festival, roads and accommodation are much busier. Book ahead.

### When to go

Visit Abu Dhabi says [the cooler months are ideal](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) for outdoor activities and desert experiences, while warmer months are quieter and better for coastal stays and indoor attractions. Its climate page adds that [something slightly warmer may be needed](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) in the winter months, which matters on a dune at dawn, and that rain is sporadic and falls mainly between November and March. ## Quick answers

**How far is Liwa from Abu Dhabi?** Visit Abu Dhabi says the drive to Al Dhafra [typically takes around two to three hours](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra), depending on where you are going.

**Can you climb Tel Moreeb?** Yes, on foot and year-round, via a paved road to the base. Expect a strenuous climb.

**When is the Liwa International Festival?** It typically runs for [around three weeks](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events/platinum-events/liwa-international-festival) from mid-December into early January; this season it is listed from 11 December 2026 to 2 January 2027.

**Do I need a 4x4 for Liwa?** Not for the paved roads. For driving on sand, the UK Foreign Office advises a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle, other cars for company, water and a phone.

**Should I stay overnight?** If you can, yes. Sunset and sunrise on the dunes are the best of Liwa, and a night saves a long drive home in the dark.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is your driver experienced in high dunes like those around Liwa, and do you travel with a second vehicle?
- What recovery equipment, water and communications do you carry off-road?
- Can we reach Tel Moreeb for sunrise or sunset, and how much time will we have to climb it?
- Do our dates overlap with the Liwa International Festival, and how does that change the plan?
- Where will we stay or camp, and what bedding, shade and toilets are there?
- Can the dune-driving section be gentler or skipped for anyone with back, neck or pregnancy concerns?
