Somewhere under the dome, the floor is covered in moving spots of light. They drift slowly as clouds pass overhead, and people who came for the paintings find themselves standing still, looking up.

That effect is the first thing most visitors remember about Louvre Abu Dhabi, and it is a good reason to plan your visit with care rather than squeezing it into a spare hour. The essentials: the museum sits in the Saadiyat Cultural District on Saadiyat Island, it is [closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), most visitors spend two to three hours inside, and entry is free for children under 18. Opening hours and ticket categories change, so check the museum's own website before you go.

## What makes Louvre Abu Dhabi different

Visit Abu Dhabi describes Louvre Abu Dhabi as [the first universal museum in the Arab world](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), built around a single idea: that civilisations have always had more in common than in conflict. Instead of separate wings for separate countries, it places objects from different cultures and centuries side by side, so you can see how ideas, materials and forms travelled.

The museum was [developed jointly by the United Arab Emirates and France](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/Abu-Dhabi-Museums/Louvre-Abu-Dhabi), and opened in November 2017, according to Abu Dhabi's culture department. The same page says its 23 permanent galleries present a wide range of cultures, traditions and eras in this side-by-side way, so that visitors can spot shared influences and unexpected connections between them.

That approach changes how you look. In a traditional museum you might walk through a room of Egyptian objects, then a room of Greek ones. Here, a single gallery may set pieces from several regions of the same period next to each other, and the interest lies in comparing them: similar tools, similar gods, similar ways of showing a face or a ruler. Read the wall panels at the entrance to each gallery, because they explain the theme that ties the room together.

## The dome and the rain of light

### Why the building matters

The building is by the architect Jean Nouvel, whom Visit Abu Dhabi notes is a [winner of the Pritzker Prize](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi). Its centrepiece is a vast silvery dome which, according to the same page, is 180 metres wide and weighs 7,500 tons, roughly the same as the Eiffel Tower, and is made up of 7,850 stars arranged in eight layers. It was inspired by the interlocking geometry of Arabic cupolas. As sunlight passes through, it casts shifting patterns on the spaces below, an effect Nouvel called the "rain of light".

Abu Dhabi's culture department adds that the museum is [made up of dozens of buildings linked by waterfront promenades](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/Abu-Dhabi-Museums/Louvre-Abu-Dhabi), with the dome appearing to float above the complex. Walking those promenades between galleries is part of the experience, so build in time for it.

The pattern of light changes with the hour and the season, which is why it is worth spending time under the dome at more than one point in your visit. Around the middle of the day the spots are sharp and bright; later on they soften and stretch. Find a bench near the water, sit for ten minutes and watch the floor.

### Seeing it from the water

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that you can also [kayak around and beneath the dome](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and lists guided kayaking tours among the museum's highlights. It is a very different view of the architecture and worth considering if you are visiting in the cooler months. Book through an operator or the museum, and ask about life jackets, sun protection and what happens if the wind picks up.

## Inside the galleries

### What you will see

Visit Abu Dhabi says the museum covers [9,200 square metres of permanent and temporary galleries](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that loans from the Musée du Louvre, Musée d'Orsay and Centre Pompidou sit alongside the museum's own collection. Temporary exhibitions run through the year.

Among the works Abu Dhabi's culture department picks out are [a standing Bactrian princess from about 2000 BCE](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/Abu-Dhabi-Museums/Louvre-Abu-Dhabi), a 3,000-year-old Middle Eastern gold bracelet with a lion's head, Osman Hamdi Bey's 1878 painting *Young Emir Studying*, Paul Gauguin's *Children Wrestling*, René Magritte's *The Subjugated Reader*, a 1928 Picasso collage, nine canvases by Cy Twombly and the oldest known photographic representation of a veiled woman. Loans rotate, so not every highlight will be on show on the day you visit; if one piece matters to you, check before you go.

### A route that works

The galleries tell a story in roughly chronological order, from early human history to the present, so the simplest approach is to follow them through and resist jumping ahead. If you have limited time, pick a period you care about and slow down there, rather than racing past everything. Leave the last half hour for the dome and the water's edge.

A sensible rhythm for a first visit is to spend the first stretch in the early galleries while you are fresh, take a break under the dome, then continue into the later periods. The galleries are separate buildings, so each time you move between them you step back out under the dome, which gives the visit a natural pace. Do not be surprised if the outdoor stretches feel warm in the middle of the day.

### Tours and audio guides

According to Visit Abu Dhabi, [guided tours and audio guides are available](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi). Schedules and languages change, so check the official website for current options. A guide is especially useful on a first visit, because so much of the meaning lies in the connections between objects rather than in any single piece.

## How a visit unfolds

Most visits follow the same shape. You arrive at the forecourt, pass the museum's name on the long white entrance wall and collect or show your ticket. From there you walk into the first galleries, which deal with the earliest human history, and work forward through time. Between galleries you come out under the dome, where you can sit, look at the water and the sea beyond, and pick up the thread again. Many people end the visit by the water's edge, watching the light, before heading to the café or out to the next stop.

If you are short of time, a focused visit of around an hour can still work: choose two or three galleries, then spend the rest under the dome. If you have a whole morning, slow down, read the panels and leave room for a temporary exhibition.

## Planning your Louvre Abu Dhabi visit

### When it is open

Different sources publish different hours, which is a good reason to check the museum's own site before you set off. Visit Abu Dhabi says the museum is [generally open from 10 AM](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), that gallery closing times vary by day, that the dome stays open later in the evening, and that the museum is closed on Mondays. Timings can also change during holidays and special periods such as Ramadan; our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains why.

### Tickets and free entry

Visit Abu Dhabi says ticket prices vary by category, with reduced rates for selected groups including UAE teachers and military personnel, and that [entry is free for children under 18](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), senior UAE residents, People of Determination and certain accredited card holders. People of Determination can also bring one companion free. A ticket also gives access to the Children's Museum, a separate space designed for young visitors.

If you plan to see more than one museum on the island, Visit Abu Dhabi promotes a [Museum Pass](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district) that combines Louvre Abu Dhabi with Zayed National Museum and the Natural History Museum Abu Dhabi, and lets you pick any two or all three. Our [Saadiyat Cultural District guide](/stories/saadiyat-cultural-district-guide/) shows how to fit them into a day.

### Accessibility

Visit Abu Dhabi describes [step-free access throughout the museum](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), accessible facilities and interpretation tools in the galleries, and says wheelchairs are available on complimentary loan. The promenades between buildings are long, so if anyone in your group tires easily, ask for a wheelchair at the start rather than halfway round.

### Food and breaks

You do not need to leave the museum to eat. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that [a French brasserie sits within the museum](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and there are lighter options too. A break halfway through is worth planning rather than leaving to chance: two to three hours of galleries is tiring, and a sit-down under the dome or over lunch makes the second half of the visit far more rewarding.

### Gallery manners

The usual museum courtesies apply. Keep your voice down, keep a little distance from the cases and from other people's view, and check the signs at the entrance for any rules on photography, bags or particular exhibitions, which can differ for temporary shows. Dress is relaxed compared with the capital's religious sites, but modest, comfortable clothing is a sensible choice for any public building in the UAE.

## Visiting with children

Louvre Abu Dhabi works well for families if you plan around attention spans. Visit Abu Dhabi says the ticket includes the [Children's Museum](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), a dedicated space that runs its own exhibitions for young visitors, so it makes sense to start or finish there. In the main galleries, turn the visit into a game: look for animals, find the oldest object in a room, or compare how different cultures show the same subject. The open space and water under the dome give children room to breathe between galleries.

Visit Abu Dhabi also notes that [under-18s get free entry to most Saadiyat museums](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district) year-round, though policies vary by venue, which makes the district good value for families who want to see more than one museum.

## Getting to Saadiyat Island

Visit Abu Dhabi says Saadiyat Island is about a [15-minute drive from Abu Dhabi city centre](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/saadiyat-island) and around 25 minutes from Zayed International Airport, and is reachable by car, taxi and bus. The same page points to the free Experience Abu Dhabi shuttle bus, which connects the airport and downtown with stops on Saadiyat, including Louvre Abu Dhabi.

The shuttle page says the buses are [free of charge, air-conditioned and wheelchair accessible](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/getting-around-abu-dhabi/shuttle-bus), with Wi-Fi, and run on eight routes linking Yas Island, Jubail Island, Saadiyat Island, the city centre and the Grand Canal area. You hop on at any stop and scan a QR code, and you can download the routes and timetables. If you are coming from Dubai for the day, a car or a booked transfer gives you more control over timing; our comparison of [Dubai or Abu Dhabi as a base](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) can help you decide where to sleep.

## What catches people out

- **Mondays.** The museum is closed, and it is an easy detail to miss when planning a tight itinerary.
- **Closing times.** Galleries and the dome close at different times, and gallery hours vary by day, so check the day you plan to visit.
- **The heat between buildings.** The dome gives shade, but the outdoor promenades can still feel warm in the middle of the day, especially outside the winter months.
- **Trying to do too much.** Visit Abu Dhabi suggests allowing [one to two hours per attraction](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district) if you want to cover the whole district in a day, and recommends two full days to see it properly.

## Making a day of it

The Louvre pairs naturally with the rest of the cultural district. Visit Abu Dhabi lists Zayed National Museum, the Natural History Museum Abu Dhabi and teamLab Phenomena Abu Dhabi as its neighbours, with the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi still to come. Beyond the museums, the wider island is known for its beaches, and Visit Abu Dhabi notes it is [one of the few places in the region where hawksbill turtles nest](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/saadiyat-island) in the wild.

It also works with the city's other big sights. Many visitors combine it with an afternoon at the [Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/), which has its own dress code and booking system, so read up on both before you plan. Our [three days in Abu Dhabi](/stories/three-days-in-abu-dhabi/) itinerary shows one way to fit everything in, and for more ideas around the capital, see our [Abu Dhabi destination page](/destinations/abu-dhabi/).

Bring a light layer: museum interiors are often cool compared with the heat outside, and you may spend longer than you planned. Wear comfortable shoes for the promenades, too, and carry a refillable bottle for the walk between buildings.

## Quick answers

**Is Louvre Abu Dhabi open on Mondays?**
No. Visit Abu Dhabi and the emirate's culture department both say it is closed on Mondays.

**How long do I need at Louvre Abu Dhabi?**
Visit Abu Dhabi says most visitors spend two to three hours, and art lovers may want longer.

**Do children pay to enter Louvre Abu Dhabi?**
Entry is free for children under 18, and a ticket includes the Children's Museum.

**How do I get to Louvre Abu Dhabi without a car?**
By taxi, bus, or the free Experience Abu Dhabi shuttle bus, which has a stop at the museum.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you plan our visit so we are under the dome at the time of day when the light is at its best?
- Do you offer, or can you book, a guided tour or kayak trip around the museum, and in which languages?
- If we want to combine the Louvre with other Saadiyat museums, would a Museum Pass make sense for our group?
- How long is the transfer from our hotel, and where exactly will you drop us and pick us up?
- Are there any closures, holiday hours or special exhibitions on the days we are travelling?
