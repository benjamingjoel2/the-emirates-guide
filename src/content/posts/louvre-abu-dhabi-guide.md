Somewhere under the dome, the floor is covered in moving spots of light. They drift slowly as clouds pass overhead, and people who came for the paintings find themselves standing still, looking up.

That effect is the first thing most visitors remember about Louvre Abu Dhabi, and it is a good reason to plan your visit with care rather than squeezing it into a spare hour. The essentials: the museum sits in the Saadiyat Cultural District on Saadiyat Island, it is [closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), most visitors spend two to three hours inside, and entry is free for children under 18. Opening hours and ticket categories change, so check the museum's own website before you go.

## What makes Louvre Abu Dhabi different

Visit Abu Dhabi describes Louvre Abu Dhabi as [the first universal museum in the Arab world](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), built around a single idea: that civilisations have always had more in common than in conflict. Instead of separate wings for separate countries, it places objects from different cultures and centuries side by side, so you can see how ideas, materials and forms travelled.

The museum was [developed jointly by the United Arab Emirates and France](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/Abu-Dhabi-Museums/Louvre-Abu-Dhabi), and opened in November 2017, according to Abu Dhabi's culture department. The same page says its 23 permanent galleries present a wide range of cultures, traditions and eras in this side-by-side way.

## The dome and the rain of light

### Why the building matters

The building is by the architect Jean Nouvel. Its centrepiece is a vast dome which, according to Visit Abu Dhabi, is [180 metres wide, weighs 7,500 tons](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi) and is made up of 7,850 stars arranged in eight layers. It was inspired by the interlocking geometry of Arabic cupolas. As sunlight passes through, it casts shifting patterns on the spaces below, an effect Nouvel called the "rain of light".

Abu Dhabi's culture department adds that the museum is [made up of dozens of buildings linked by waterfront promenades](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/Abu-Dhabi-Museums/Louvre-Abu-Dhabi), with the dome appearing to float above the complex. Walking those promenades between galleries is part of the experience, so build in time for it.

### Seeing it from the water

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that you can also [kayak around and beneath the dome](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and lists guided kayaking tours among the museum's highlights. It is a very different view of the architecture and worth considering if you are visiting in the cooler months.

## Inside the galleries

### What you will see

Visit Abu Dhabi says the museum covers [9,200 square metres of permanent and temporary galleries](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that loans from the Musée du Louvre, Musée d'Orsay and Centre Pompidou sit alongside the museum's own collection. Temporary exhibitions run through the year.

Among the works Abu Dhabi's culture department picks out are [a standing Bactrian princess from about 2000 BCE](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/Abu-Dhabi-Museums/Louvre-Abu-Dhabi), a 3,000-year-old Middle Eastern gold bracelet with a lion's head, Osman Hamdi Bey's 1878 painting *Young Emir Studying*, Paul Gauguin's *Children Wrestling*, René Magritte's *The Subjugated Reader*, a 1928 Picasso collage and nine canvases by Cy Twombly.

### A route that works

The galleries tell a story in roughly chronological order, from early human history to the present, so the simplest approach is to follow them through and resist jumping ahead. If you have limited time, pick a period you care about and slow down there, rather than racing past everything. Leave the last half hour for the dome and the water's edge.

### Tours and audio guides

According to Visit Abu Dhabi, [guided tours and audio guides are available](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi). Schedules and languages change, so check the official website for current options.

## Planning your Louvre Abu Dhabi visit

### When it is open

Different sources publish different hours, which is a good reason to check the museum's own site before you set off. Visit Abu Dhabi says the museum is [generally open from 10 AM](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), that gallery closing times vary by day, that the dome stays open later in the evening, and that the museum is closed on Mondays. Timings can also change during holidays and special periods such as Ramadan; our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains why.

### Tickets and free entry

Visit Abu Dhabi says ticket prices vary by category, with reduced rates for selected groups, and that [entry is free for children under 18](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), senior UAE residents, People of Determination and certain accredited card holders. People of Determination can also bring one companion free. A ticket also gives access to the Children's Museum, a separate space designed for young visitors.

If you plan to see more than one museum on the island, Visit Abu Dhabi promotes a [Museum Pass](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district) that combines Louvre Abu Dhabi with Zayed National Museum and the Natural History Museum Abu Dhabi. Our [Saadiyat Cultural District guide](/stories/saadiyat-cultural-district-guide/) shows how to fit them into a day.

### Accessibility

Visit Abu Dhabi describes [step-free access throughout the museum](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), accessible facilities and interpretation tools in the galleries, and says wheelchairs are available on complimentary loan.

## Getting to Saadiyat Island

Visit Abu Dhabi says Saadiyat Island is about a [15-minute drive from Abu Dhabi city centre](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/saadiyat-island) and around 25 minutes from Zayed International Airport, and is reachable by car, taxi and bus. The same page points to the free Experience Abu Dhabi shuttle bus, which connects the airport and downtown with stops on Saadiyat, including Louvre Abu Dhabi.

The shuttle page says the buses are [free of charge, air-conditioned and wheelchair accessible](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/getting-around-abu-dhabi/shuttle-bus), and that you can download the routes and timetables. If you are coming from Dubai for the day, a car or a booked transfer gives you more control over timing; our comparison of [Dubai or Abu Dhabi as a base](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) can help you decide where to sleep.

## Making a day of it

The Louvre pairs naturally with the rest of the cultural district, but it also works with the city's other big sights. Many visitors combine it with an afternoon at the [Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/), which has its own dress code and booking system, so read up on both before you plan. For more ideas around the capital, see our [Abu Dhabi destination page](/destinations/abu-dhabi/).

Bring a light layer: museum interiors are often cool compared with the heat outside, and you may spend longer than you planned. Wear comfortable shoes for the promenades, too.

## Quick answers

**Is Louvre Abu Dhabi open on Mondays?**
No. Visit Abu Dhabi and the emirate's culture department both say it is closed on Mondays.

**How long do I need at Louvre Abu Dhabi?**
Visit Abu Dhabi says most visitors spend two to three hours, and art lovers may want longer.

**Do children pay to enter Louvre Abu Dhabi?**
Entry is free for children under 18, and a ticket includes the Children's Museum.

**How do I get to Louvre Abu Dhabi without a car?**
By taxi, bus, or the free Experience Abu Dhabi shuttle bus, which has a stop at the museum.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you plan our visit so we are under the dome at the time of day when the light is at its best?
- Do you offer, or can you book, a guided tour or kayak trip around the museum, and in which languages?
- If we want to combine the Louvre with other Saadiyat museums, would a Museum Pass make sense for our group?
- How long is the transfer from our hotel, and where exactly will you drop us and pick us up?
- Are there any closures, holiday hours or special exhibitions on the days we are travelling?
