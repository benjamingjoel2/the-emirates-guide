Run your hand along the rock and it feels rough, ridged, almost like coral. Look closer and that is because it once was the seabed: the shells of creatures that lived in an ancient ocean, now high and dry in the middle of the Sharjah desert.

That is the strange pull of Mleiha. It is a desert day that is about time as much as sand: fossils from a vanished sea, Bronze Age tombs, Stone Age tools, and at night a sky dark enough to show the stars properly. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) places the Mleiha Archaeological Centre near Jebel Fayah, 65 km south-east of Sharjah city, and describes guided site tours, hiking and evenings in the desert.

Is it worth the trip? If you want the desert with a story attached, very much so. Go in the cooler months, arrive early for the archaeology and hiking, and stay late for sunset and the stars.

## Where Mleiha is, and why it matters

Mleiha lies in Sharjah's Central Region, a landscape of sand and rocky mountains that Visit Sharjah says was once crossed by [ancient caravan routes](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/central-region/) linking east and west. The same page describes it as less than an hour from Sharjah city, home to the country's most important archaeological sites, dunes such as Big Red at Al Badayer, and farming towns such as Al Dhaid. It adds that small villages here still keep strong Bedouin traditions.

The bigger story is very old indeed. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/jebel-faya/) says Jebel Faya, the ridge above Mleiha, is part of the Sharjah Cultural Landscape of the Central Region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It describes more than 210,000 years of human history there, with stone tools that are among the earliest evidence of human life beyond Africa, and finds that support the theory of a southern migration route from Africa into Arabia.

The detail is striking. The same page says the record spans the Middle to Late Stone Age, between 210,000 and 6,000 years ago, with 18 phases of prehistoric occupation along a ridge of roughly 20 km. More than 35,000 stone artefacts have been found across seven excavated sites. When the first people arrived, it says, the land was greener, with water running through the wadis; even in later, drier periods, people kept coming back.

The visitor area is now run as a national park. Visit Sharjah's [Mleiha page](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/central-region/mleiha/) says Mleiha National Park covers 34.2 square kilometres within the Faya landscape, bringing together archaeology, guided desert experiences, astronomy and places to stay.

You will not see a grand ruin. You will see a landscape, and with a guide you learn to read it.

## Morning: the archaeological centre and the tombs

Start at the Mleiha Archaeological Centre, the base for most visits. Its exhibits of ancient artefacts and interactive displays set the scene, and Visit Sharjah says the centre offers [guided tours](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) of the important sites nearby. There is a café, which matters more than it sounds when the nearest alternative is a long drive away.

The highlight is the Umm an-Nar tomb, a circular Bronze Age grave. Standing beside it in the low morning light, the stones arranged in a careful ring, you get a sense of how long people have lived and buried their dead in this place.

What to look for: the shape of the tomb and how the stones were fitted, the way the ridge of Jebel Faya frames the site, and the flat gravel plains between the dunes, where much of the evidence was found. Ask your guide to point out where excavations took place; much of what matters here is below your feet.

If you want to see what was found here, the [Sharjah Archaeology Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-archaeology-museum/) in the city holds a 2,000-year-old golden bridle from Mleiha. Our [guide to Sharjah's museums](/stories/sharjah-museums-guide/) covers it.

## Fossil Rock: a seabed in the sand

You will see Fossil Rock long before you reach it. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/fossil-rock/) says the formation is clearly visible from more than 25 km away and sits about an hour's drive from Sharjah city. Its crevices are dotted with marine fossils.

### Hiking the Fossil Rock trail

For a closer look, there is a hiking route. Visit Sharjah describes the [Mleiha Fossil Rock Hiking Trail](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/mleiha-fossil-rock-hiking-trail/) as a 6 km route over sand dunes and rocky terrain, with steep sections and one short, steep rock-climbing section that may be difficult for some. It suggests it suits intermediate hikers and takes two to three hours. Many of the fossils, it says, are thought to be more than 80 million years old.

The same page has two warnings worth taking seriously: there is no shade on the route, so start early in the morning or late in the afternoon, and carry plenty of water and a small first aid kit. Take all your rubbish home with you.

Leave the fossils where they are. Look, photograph and touch gently, but do not chip or collect anything: this is a protected landscape, and the next visitor deserves to find the same seabed you did.

### Camel Rock and the dunes

The [Mleiha National Park](https://mleihanationalpark.com/) site also points visitors to Camel Rock, a formation it describes as carved over centuries by wind, which is often included on dune buggy routes. The red dunes around it are where the park runs its sandboarding.

## Evening: sunset and stargazing

As the sun drops, the rock formations glow orange and the dunes turn to ripples of shadow. Then the dark comes, and this is where Mleiha comes into its own.

Away from the city lights, the night sky opens up. The park site lists evening stargazing sessions exploring the stars and planets with experienced guides. It also runs special evenings for meteor showers, such as the Geminids in mid-December, so check its listings if your dates line up.

A few tips make the evening better. Let your eyes adjust for a while away from torches and phone screens, use a red light if you have one, and go when the moon is new or a thin crescent if you can choose. Binoculars help with the brighter objects even without a telescope.

Bring a warm layer. Desert nights in the cooler months can feel surprisingly chilly after a warm day.

### Staying the night

To see both sunset and sunrise, stay over. The park site mentions glamping in the desert, and Visit Sharjah's own [Mleiha itinerary](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/trips/itineraries/a-mleiha-adventure/) suggests a night in the desert between a day of archaeology and a day of outdoor activities. Visit Sharjah's Mleiha page describes two options: a restored building from the 1960s now run as a boutique desert retreat, and a glamping site with tents and dome-style rooms. Our guide to [camping overnight in the UAE desert](/stories/overnight-desert-camping-uae/) explains what to pack and what to ask.

## Other ways to explore

Mleiha is not only about walking slowly among ruins. The [park site](https://mleihanationalpark.com/) lists sandboarding, horse riding open to all experience levels, dune buggy rides, and flights over the area, as well as a chance to look for the park's gazelles and desert foxes. Visit Sharjah's itinerary notes that adventure activities are [subject to booking, eligibility and conditions](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/trips/itineraries/a-mleiha-adventure/), including the weather.

If your group is split between quiet and adrenaline, this is an easy place to please both.

### A detour to the buried village

On the way to or from Mleiha, the [Al Madam Buried Village](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/al-madam-buried-village/) is an eerie stop. Visit Sharjah says it was built as public housing after the UAE's formation in 1971 for a Bedouin community, and that within about 20 years its residents had left, most likely because of the harsh conditions, desert winds and long journeys to work. The sand is slowly taking it back. Walk softly, stay out of unstable buildings, and treat it as the empty home it was.

### Buhais Geological Park

If the fossils have caught your imagination, go further back in time. Visit Sharjah says [Buhais Geological Park](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/buhais-geological-park/) takes visitors back 93 million years, in five connected pods that echo the shapes of local fossils. Inside are interactive displays on Sharjah's mountains and dunes, and outside a trail links viewing areas, rock formations and ancient burial grounds. The page says guiding is available in Arabic and English, and that transport is not provided, so you will need a car or a driver.

## How the day unfolds

A typical day starts with an early drive out of the city, so that you are at the archaeological centre as it opens and on the trail before the sun climbs. Late morning is for the exhibits and the tombs, with a long lunch and rest in the shade during the hottest hours. In the late afternoon, head back out for Fossil Rock, the dunes or a buggy ride, and time it so you are somewhere high and open for sunset. After dark, join a stargazing session or settle in at your camp.

With children, shorten the hike or skip it, and lean on the archaeological centre's displays, the buggy ride or horse riding and the stars. Check any age or height limits before you book.

## Combining Mleiha with the wider Central Region

Mleiha sits in a part of Sharjah that most visitors never see, and it is worth looking around. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/sharjah) describes Al Badayer, in the Al Madam area, as one of the most popular desert areas in the emirate for campers and off-road drivers, and Al Dhaid as Sharjah's third largest town and its main producer of fruit and vegetables such as strawberries, dates, limes, guava and mangoes. A stop at a roadside fruit stall on the way back is a pleasant end to a dusty day.

If you have two days, one easy plan is archaeology and stargazing on the first, then dunes, Buhais or the buried village on the second. Our guide to [desert stays and city hotels](/stories/desert-stay-or-city-hotel-uae/) helps if you are weighing a night out here against a return to the city.

## What to pack

Visit Sharjah's own hiking advice for the emirate's trails is a sensible base: [plenty of water, sturdy shoes, a hat and sunblock](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/al-rafisah-hiking-trail/), comfortable clothes and snacks, plus a small medical kit with plasters and insect bite cream. For Mleiha, add a warm layer for the evening, a head torch with a red setting for stargazing, sunglasses against the glare off the sand, and a bag for your rubbish.

## Respecting the place and the rules

This is a protected heritage landscape, and Sharjah's laws apply out here just as they do in the city.

- **No alcohol.** The UK Foreign Office says [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). That includes desert camps, so leave it behind.
- **Dress sensibly and modestly.** Long, loose layers protect you from the sun and suit local expectations.
- **Ask before photographing people.** The same advice says not to photograph people without their permission, and that some hobbies can be misunderstood near sensitive sites.
- **Stay on marked routes.** Off-road driving on fragile ground damages both archaeology and wildlife habitat. Go with a licensed operator who knows where vehicles are allowed.

## Staying safe in the desert

The desert is beautiful, and it punishes poor preparation.

- **Go with someone who knows the ground.** The UK Foreign Office warns that [desert excursions can be dangerous](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive. It advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and leaving your plans with someone.
- **Choose operators carefully.** For desert driving or quad biking, the same advice says to use a reputable company with proper health and safety procedures, and to check that your insurance covers the activity. Our guide to [dune sports safety](/stories/dune-sports-safety-uae/) has more.
- **Respect the heat.** Hike early or late, never at midday, and carry more water than you think you need.
- **Know the emergency number.** The Foreign Office says to [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) in the UAE.

## Getting there and when to go

Mleiha is easiest with a car or a driver. If you do not have your own transport, Visit Sharjah says [return transfers from Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) are available. Fill up with fuel and buy snacks before you leave the main roads.

The cooler months are the time to go, since hiking and long evenings outside are far more pleasant then. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you pick dates. For more desert ideas, see our [desert adventure page](/desert-adventure/).

## Quick answers

**Where is Mleiha?**
In Sharjah's Central Region, near Jebel Fayah, 65 km south-east of Sharjah city according to Visit Sharjah.

**Is Mleiha a UNESCO World Heritage Site?**
Visit Sharjah says Jebel Faya is part of the Sharjah Cultural Landscape of the Central Region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with evidence of more than 210,000 years of human history.

**Can you go stargazing at Mleiha?**
Yes. The Mleiha National Park site lists evening stargazing sessions with experienced guides, plus special meteor shower evenings.

**How hard is the Fossil Rock hike?**
Visit Sharjah describes a 6 km trail with steep sections and no shade, suited to intermediate hikers and taking two to three hours.

**Can I visit Mleiha without a car?**
Visit Sharjah says return transfers from Sharjah are available.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you book a guided tour from the Mleiha Archaeological Centre that includes the Umm an-Nar tomb and Fossil Rock?
- Which stargazing evenings fall on our dates, and how late do they finish?
- Is the Fossil Rock trail suitable for our group, and can you provide a guide and water?
- If we camp overnight, what are the bedding, toilets and night-time temperatures like in our month?
- Are your desert drivers trained, and do your vehicles carry recovery gear and first aid?
- Which adventure activities have age, weight or health restrictions?
