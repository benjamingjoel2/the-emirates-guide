Run your hand along the rock and it feels rough, ridged, almost like coral. Look closer and that is because it once was the seabed: the shells of creatures that lived in an ancient ocean, now high and dry in the middle of the Sharjah desert.

That is the strange pull of Mleiha. It is a desert day that is about time as much as sand: fossils from a vanished sea, Bronze Age tombs, Stone Age tools, and at night a sky dark enough to show the stars properly. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) places the Mleiha Archaeological Centre near Jebel Fayah, 65 km south-east of Sharjah city, and describes guided site tours, hiking and evenings in the desert.

Is it worth the trip? If you want the desert with a story attached, very much so. Go in the cooler months, arrive early for the archaeology and hiking, and stay late for sunset and the stars.

## Where Mleiha is, and why it matters

Mleiha lies in Sharjah's Central Region, a landscape of sand and rocky mountains that Visit Sharjah says was once crossed by [ancient caravan routes](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/central-region/) linking east and west. The same page describes it as less than an hour from Sharjah city, home to the country's most important archaeological sites, dunes such as Big Red at Al Badayer, and farming towns such as Al Dhaid.

The bigger story is very old indeed. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/jebel-faya/) says Jebel Faya, the ridge above Mleiha, is part of the Sharjah Cultural Landscape of the Central Region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It describes more than 210,000 years of human history there, with stone tools that are among the earliest evidence of human life beyond Africa, and finds that support the theory of a southern migration route from Africa into Arabia.

You will not see a grand ruin. You will see a landscape, and with a guide you learn to read it.

## Morning: the archaeological centre and the tombs

Start at the Mleiha Archaeological Centre, the base for most visits. Its exhibits of ancient artefacts and interactive displays set the scene, and Visit Sharjah says the centre offers [guided tours](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) of the important sites nearby.

The highlight is the Umm an-Nar tomb, a circular Bronze Age grave. Standing beside it in the low morning light, the stones arranged in a careful ring, you get a sense of how long people have lived and buried their dead in this place.

If you want to see what was found here, the [Sharjah Archaeology Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-archaeology-museum/) in the city holds a 2,000-year-old golden bridle from Mleiha. Our [guide to Sharjah's museums](/stories/sharjah-museums-guide/) covers it.

## Fossil Rock: a seabed in the sand

You will see Fossil Rock long before you reach it. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/fossil-rock/) says the formation is clearly visible from more than 25 km away and sits about an hour's drive from Sharjah city. Its crevices are dotted with marine fossils.

### Hiking the Fossil Rock trail

For a closer look, there is a hiking route. Visit Sharjah describes the [Mleiha Fossil Rock Hiking Trail](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/mleiha-fossil-rock-hiking-trail/) as a 6 km route over sand dunes and rocky terrain, with steep sections and one short, steep rock-climbing section that may be difficult for some. It suggests it suits intermediate hikers and takes two to three hours. Many of the fossils, it says, are thought to be more than 80 million years old.

The same page has two warnings worth taking seriously: there is no shade on the route, so start early in the morning or late in the afternoon, and carry plenty of water and a small first aid kit. Take all your rubbish home with you.

## Evening: sunset and stargazing

As the sun drops, the rock formations glow orange and the dunes turn to ripples of shadow. Then the dark comes, and this is where Mleiha comes into its own.

Away from the city lights, the night sky opens up. The [Mleiha National Park](https://mleihanationalpark.com/) site lists evening stargazing sessions exploring the stars and planets with experienced guides. It also runs special evenings for meteor showers, so check its listings if your dates line up.

Bring a warm layer. Desert nights in the cooler months can feel surprisingly chilly after a warm day.

### Staying the night

To see both sunset and sunrise, stay over. The park site mentions [glamping in the desert](https://mleihanationalpark.com/), and Visit Sharjah's own [Mleiha itinerary](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/trips/itineraries/a-mleiha-adventure/) suggests a night in the desert between a day of archaeology and a day of outdoor activities. Our guide to [camping overnight in the UAE desert](/stories/overnight-desert-camping-uae/) explains what to pack and what to ask.

## Other ways to explore

Mleiha is not only about walking slowly among ruins. The [park site](https://mleihanationalpark.com/) lists sandboarding, horse riding open to all experience levels, dune buggy rides, and flights over the area, as well as a chance to look for the park's gazelles and desert foxes. Visit Sharjah's itinerary notes that adventure activities are [subject to booking, eligibility and conditions](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/trips/itineraries/a-mleiha-adventure/), including the weather.

If your group is split between quiet and adrenaline, this is an easy place to please both.

### A detour to the buried village

On the way to or from Mleiha, the [Al Madam Buried Village](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/al-madam-buried-village/) is an eerie stop. Visit Sharjah says it was built as public housing after the UAE's formation in 1971 for a Bedouin community, and that within about 20 years its residents had left. The sand is slowly taking it back. Walk softly, stay out of unstable buildings, and treat it as the empty home it was.

## Staying safe in the desert

The desert is beautiful, and it punishes poor preparation.

- **Go with someone who knows the ground.** The UK Foreign Office warns that [desert excursions can be dangerous](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive. It advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and leaving your plans with someone.
- **Choose operators carefully.** For desert driving or quad biking, the same advice says to use a reputable company with proper health and safety procedures, and to check that your insurance covers the activity. Our guide to [dune sports safety](/stories/dune-sports-safety-uae/) has more.
- **Respect the heat.** Hike early or late, never at midday, and carry more water than you think you need.
- **Know the emergency number.** The Foreign Office says to [dial 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) in the UAE.

## Getting there and when to go

Mleiha is easiest with a car or a driver. If you do not have your own transport, Visit Sharjah says [return transfers from Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) are available.

The cooler months are the time to go, since hiking and long evenings outside are far more pleasant then. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you pick dates. For more desert ideas, see our [desert adventure page](/desert-adventure/).

## Quick answers

**Where is Mleiha?**
In Sharjah's Central Region, near Jebel Fayah, 65 km south-east of Sharjah city according to Visit Sharjah.

**Is Mleiha a UNESCO World Heritage Site?**
Visit Sharjah says Jebel Faya is part of the Sharjah Cultural Landscape of the Central Region, a UNESCO World Heritage Site with evidence of more than 210,000 years of human history.

**Can you go stargazing at Mleiha?**
Yes. The Mleiha National Park site lists evening stargazing sessions with experienced guides.

**How hard is the Fossil Rock hike?**
Visit Sharjah describes a 6 km trail with steep sections and no shade, suited to intermediate hikers and taking two to three hours.

**Can I visit Mleiha without a car?**
Visit Sharjah says return transfers from Sharjah are available.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you book a guided tour from the Mleiha Archaeological Centre that includes the Umm an-Nar tomb and Fossil Rock?
- Which stargazing evenings fall on our dates, and how late do they finish?
- Is the Fossil Rock trail suitable for our group, and can you provide a guide and water?
- If we camp overnight, what are the bedding, toilets and night-time temperatures like in our month?
- Are your desert drivers trained, and do your vehicles carry recovery gear and first aid?
- Which adventure activities have age, weight or health restrictions?
