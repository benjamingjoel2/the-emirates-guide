Stand at the foot of the green mound on Sheikh Zayed Road and look up. A silver ring curves above you, its skin carved with flowing Arabic script, and through the empty oval at its centre you can see the sky and the towers behind.

That view is still yours to enjoy. What has changed is what happens inside. If you are planning a trip and the Museum of the Future is on your list, the most important thing to know is this: the museum is temporarily closed while it is completely reimagined, and its official help page says it is [set to reopen in the first quarter of 2027](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help). Here is what that means for your plans, what will be different when the doors open again, and how to make the most of the building in the meantime.

## Is the Museum of the Future open?

Not for its main exhibitions. According to the museum, it [temporarily closed its doors to visitors at the beginning of September 2026](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help) to prepare an entirely new experience. The homepage notes that while the exhibitions are unavailable, the [online retail store and selected experiences remain open](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en).

The help page adds that some spaces [may remain accessible for selected activities and services](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help), and that selected events and programmes may continue during the closure. It also says the Auditorium remains open for event bookings while Level 7 is unavailable, which is mainly useful if you are organising a conference or private event rather than visiting as a tourist.

The museum's programmes page lists [current and past programmes](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/programs), including an education programme running through 2026. If you see an event advertised, check the museum's official channels to confirm what is on and whether it is open to the public.

The closure is not permanent. The museum describes it as lasting [only a few months](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help), timed so that the reopening coincides with its fifth anniversary. It first opened in 2022, so the renewal comes after its first few years of welcoming visitors.

### What if your trip is before reopening?

Then treat the Museum of the Future as an architectural stop rather than a museum visit. Allow time to walk around the building, see the calligraphy up close and take photographs from across the road. It sits well alongside other Downtown and Trade Centre sights, and you can fill the rest of the day with places that are open.

### What if your trip is after reopening?

Keep an eye on the official site. The museum says tickets for the renewed experience will be released [closer to the reopening](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help). Expect strong demand in the first weeks, and book as soon as you can once dates are confirmed.

### What if your dates fall right around reopening?

This is the trickiest case. Build your plans so that the museum is a bonus rather than the anchor of a day: pick a morning or afternoon when you would be happy simply to see the exterior, and upgrade it to a full visit if tickets go on sale in time. Avoid booking non-refundable extras around a reopening date that has not been formally announced.

## What will be different when it reopens

The renewal is not a light refresh. The museum says the closure is a chance to [reimagine it from the ground up](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help), with a new narrative, new exhibitions, new spaces and a redesigned visitor journey. It also says it is enhancing its technologies and interactive experiences during the closure.

A few points stand out from the official answers:

- **The old exhibitions will not return.** The museum says the current exhibitions and experiences [will not come back in their previous form](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help). If you saw it before, the next visit will be a different journey.
- **More interaction.** The renewed museum aims to make visitors [active participants](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help), exploring ideas and shaping their own route, with a more immersive story, new exhibitions and labs.
- **The building stays the same.** The museum confirms the [exterior will not change](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help); the work focuses on what happens inside.
- **Accessibility remains a priority.** The museum says the renewed space will include [spaces, services and experiences](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help) designed for a comfortable visit for People of Determination.

### What kind of museum is it?

Do not expect glass cases and ancient relics. The museum describes itself as a place that combines [exhibition, immersive theatre and themed attraction](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/about-us), with each floor of its first edition designed like a film set from a possible future. Its stated aim is to be hopeful about the future while staying honest about the problems of the present. It is an initiative of the [Dubai Future Foundation](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/about-us), which the museum says was formed in 2016 to plan and shape the emirate's future.

That gives a sense of the tone, even before the new exhibitions are revealed: interactive, theatrical and built around ideas rather than objects. If your group includes people who prefer history and heritage, balance the day with something older; if you are travelling with curious teenagers, this is likely to be one of the places they remember.

## Reading the building from the outside

Even with the doors closed to exhibition visitors, the Museum of the Future rewards a slow look. Few buildings in Dubai say so much on their surface.

### The shape

The building has three parts, each with a meaning. The museum explains that the [green hill represents the earth](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help), the torus-shaped building represents humankind's ability to innovate, and the void at the centre represents the unknown. Walk around it and you will notice how the void frames different slices of the city depending on where you stand. The museum's building page says the form was meant to open [a new path away from the high-rise towers](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/the-building) that dominate skylines everywhere, and that contrast is clear when you see it against its neighbours.

### The calligraphy

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="museum-of-the-future-guide-4" alts="An illuminated panel inside the museum showing three sayings in gold Arabic calligraphy with English translations, beside a UAE flag" caption="Inside, a panel sets out the sayings on the facade in Arabic with English translations."></photo-set>

The facade is far more than decoration. The museum says the building ["speaks Arabic"](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/the-building): its surface is a canvas for sayings of the Ruler of Dubai on the future, innovation and legacy, rendered in Arabic calligraphy. The help page gives English translations of the sayings, which deal with imagining and building the future, innovation as the secret of renewal, and leaving a creative legacy that outlasts us. Look closely and the letters seem to shift as the light moves across the steel through the day.

If you do not read Arabic, it is worth looking up the translations on the official help page before you go. Knowing what the lines say changes how you look at them.

### The numbers behind the shine

The facade is made of stainless steel in [1,024 pieces](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/the-building), produced with a robot-assisted process and covering a surface area of 17,600 square metres. The building stands 77 metres tall and the museum gives its overall area as 30,548 square metres. The museum also says it receives solar energy from [arrays mounted nearby](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en/get-help).

### Photographing it

Photographers tend to favour two moments: soft early light, when the steel turns warm, and dusk, when the city lights come on around it. Step back across the road for the whole ring, and move in close for the texture of the letters. Keep your photography to the building and the street. The UK Foreign Office warns visitors [not to photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that applies here as much as anywhere.

The same advice notes that it is illegal to photograph some government buildings and military installations. The museum is a public landmark that people photograph all the time, but stay alert to signs if your walk takes you past other official buildings along the road.

## Planning a visit once it reopens

Nothing about the renewed museum's ticketing has been published yet, so the best preparation is a few sensible habits rather than a fixed plan.

- **Use the official channels.** Tickets will be released closer to the reopening, according to the museum. Buy from the official site or an operator you trust, and be wary of anyone selling tickets for the renewed museum before the official release.
- **Give it an unhurried slot.** The museum promises more interaction, labs and a route visitors shape themselves. Experiences like that reward time, so avoid squeezing it between two other timed bookings.
- **Ask about access in advance.** If anyone in your group has mobility, sensory or other access needs, contact the museum before you book. It has said the renewed space will include services designed for People of Determination, and it is easier to plan around them ahead of time than on arrival.
- **Expect early crowds.** New exhibitions in a building this well known tend to draw residents as well as visitors in the first weeks. If your dates are flexible, a weekday is likely to feel calmer than a weekend.

### If you visited before

The museum is clear that the exhibitions you saw will not return in the same form, so a second visit after reopening should feel like a new place inside a familiar shell. That makes it a reasonable choice even for repeat visitors to Dubai, who might otherwise skip attractions they have already seen.

### If you are short on time

If you only have a day or two in Dubai, do not build your plans around the museum until reopening dates and tickets are confirmed. A quick look at the exterior on the way between other sights gives you the building without the risk.

## Getting there

The museum stands beside Sheikh Zayed Road, the main artery through this part of the city, so it is easy to reach by taxi and by metro. If you use the metro, the Roads and Transport Authority notes [lift access to all metro station levels](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro) and dedicated spaces for wheelchair users on all trains. Trains also have cabins reserved for women and children and a Gold Class cabin, so check the signs on the platform before you board. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) explains the network, fares and tickets.

Expect wide roads and busy junctions around the building. Use marked crossings and bridges rather than trying to cross the traffic, even if the building looks close.

## How to fit it into a Dubai day

Because a visit to the exterior takes far less time than a full museum visit, it pairs easily with other sights.

### A futures and views day

Start with the Museum of the Future exterior in the morning, then head to Downtown for the observation decks. Our guide to [visiting the Burj Khalifa](/stories/burj-khalifa-visit-guide/) covers which deck to pick and when to book. Finish at the Dubai Frame, which tells the story of the city's growth from fishing village to modern metropolis; see our [Dubai Frame guide](/stories/dubai-frame-guide/).

### A past and future day

For contrast, pair the building's vision of tomorrow with the city's past. A morning in the wind-tower lanes of the old town, covered in our [Old Dubai walking guide](/stories/old-dubai-walking-guide/), followed by a late afternoon at the Museum of the Future makes the distance Dubai has travelled feel real.

### With children

Children who are disappointed that the inside is closed often still enjoy spotting letters in the facade and walking around the mound. Our [Dubai with children guide](/stories/dubai-with-kids/) has more family ideas that work while the museum is being renewed. For the wider city, start at our [Dubai destination page](/destinations/dubai/).

### Practical notes

- **Dress:** the building sits in a business district, and modest, comfortable clothes are the easiest choice. The Foreign Office advises visitors to [dress modestly in public areas like shopping malls](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), with women covering shoulders and knees.
- **Heat:** you will be outdoors the whole time, so go early or late in the warmer months and carry water.
- **Ramadan:** if you visit during the holy month, the Foreign Office notes that eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime may cause offence, so keep your water bottle discreet.
- **Check before you go:** closures, events and reopening plans can change, so look at the official site shortly before your visit.

## Quick answers

**Is the Museum of the Future closed?** Yes, temporarily. Its main exhibitions closed at the beginning of September 2026 while the museum is completely reimagined.

**When does the Museum of the Future reopen?** The museum says it is set to reopen in the first quarter of 2027, coinciding with its fifth anniversary.

**Can I buy tickets now?** Not yet for the renewed museum. Tickets will be available closer to the reopening.

**Will the exhibitions be the same as before?** No. The museum says the previous exhibitions will not return in their old form and will be replaced by new ones.

**Can I still see the building?** Yes. The exterior is not changing, and the online store and selected experiences remain open during the closure.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Has the Museum of the Future announced a firm reopening date or ticket release since we booked?
- Are any events or spaces at the museum open to visitors during our dates?
- When is the best time of day on our dates to see and photograph the building?
- Can you plan a route that combines the museum exterior with open attractions nearby without long waits in the heat?
- If the museum reopens during our trip, can you help us get timed tickets as soon as they are released?
