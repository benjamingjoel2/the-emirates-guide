The diesel engine coughs, the boatman pushes off from the wooden quay, and for a few minutes the city goes quiet. Behind you are the wind towers of Bur Dubai. Ahead, on the Deira bank, a wall of shopfronts, cargo dhows and the faint smell of cardamom drifting from the spice stalls.

This is Old Dubai, and it is best seen on foot. An Old Dubai walking route links three things most visitors want to see in one easy loop: the lanes of Al Fahidi, a crossing of Dubai Creek by abra, and the spice and gold souks of Deira. You need comfortable shoes, a few hours, some small cash for the boat and a cool part of the day. You do not need a guide, although a good one adds a great deal. Here is how to plan it.

## Why the Creek is where Dubai began

Every story of the city starts at the water. The UAE government explains that Dubai was established in [1833, when members of the Bani Yas tribe led by the Maktoum family settled by the creek](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai), and that the creek was a natural harbour that made Dubai a centre for fishing, pearling and sea trade. The same page notes that the creek still divides the city, with Deira to the north and Bur Dubai to the south.

Visit Dubai calls the Creek [the historic heart of the city](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf), and walking its two banks is the clearest way to see how a trading port grew into the city you flew into.

## Your Old Dubai walking route at a glance

The loop below starts and ends on the Bur Dubai side, so you can return to your taxi or the metro without retracing every step.

1. **Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood**: wind towers, quiet lanes and small museums.
2. **The Bur Dubai waterfront**: watch the boats and find the abra station.
3. **Across the Creek by abra** to the Deira Old Souq station.
4. **The Spice Souk**: sacks, baskets and the scent of the old trade.
5. **The Gold Souk**: shop windows full of jewellery.
6. **Back across by abra**, with an optional detour to the Al Shindagha area at the mouth of the Creek.

Allow a morning or a late afternoon for the main loop. If you like to linger in museums or stop for coffee, give it most of a day.

## Start in the lanes of Al Fahidi

Early morning is the best time to arrive. The lanes are cool and shaded, the light is soft on the sand-coloured walls, and you may have whole alleys to yourself.

Visit Dubai says most of the buildings here [date back to the late 19th century and now house museums, galleries and cafes](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf). Dubai's Media Office describes the neighbourhood as showing [everyday life in Dubai from around the 1800s until the 1970s](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/july/04-07/trip-adviser). Look up as you walk: the square wind towers that rise above the rooftops were the old way of catching a breeze and sending it down into the rooms below.

### A guided start, if you want one

The Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding is based in the neighbourhood and runs a [Heritage Tour](https://cultures.ae/program/heritage-tour-al-farooq-mosque/) that begins with Arabic coffee and dates in a wind tower house, includes a question and answer session with a cultural presenter, then walks the narrow lanes, known as sikkas, and ends at a mosque. The centre's page lists the tour at 1.5 hours and gives its weekly schedule, so check it and book ahead. It is a gentle way to start, because you can ask the questions you might feel shy about asking elsewhere.

For a deeper look at the neighbourhood itself, see our guide to [Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood](/stories/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood/).

## Crossing the water

From Al Fahidi, head for the creekside and the Bur Dubai abra station. The abra is the small wooden water taxi that has carried people across the creek for generations, and it is the highlight of the walk for many visitors.

The Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) lists the traditional route from [Bur Dubai Abra Station to Deira Old Souq Abra Station running from 6:00 am to midnight](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/promotion/new-marine-routes-abra), with fares paid in cash only. A second traditional route runs between Dubai Old Souq and Al Sabkha around the clock. The RTA also lists [heritage abra routes that include Al Fahidi to Deira Old Souq and Al Seef to Baniyas](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), so there is more than one way across.

If you are travelling with children, note the RTA rules on the same page: children under 12 are not allowed on marine transport unaccompanied, and children under 12 travelling with an adult must wear a life jacket. Our [beginner's guide to crossing Dubai Creek by abra](/stories/dubai-creek-abra-guide/) covers the crossing step by step.

## Deira's souks, nose first

Step off the boat on the Deira side and you will probably smell the Spice Souk before you see it. Visit Dubai describes it as a traditional market [where aromatic herbs and spices spill from large baskets at every turn](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-hungry-foodie.pdf). Look for dried limes, saffron, frankincense and rose petals, and do not be surprised when a stallholder offers you a sniff of something before you have even stopped walking.

A short walk on, the lanes change character. The Gold Souk is all glass and glitter, window after window of necklaces, bangles and rings. Visit Dubai points to the souks of Deira and Bur Dubai for [gold jewellery, handwoven fabrics, traditional perfumes and souvenirs](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf), and its shopping guide reminds you to [haggle, because it is expected and welcomed](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-shopping-lover.pdf). Keep it friendly, smile, and walk away politely if the price is not right. Our guide to [Dubai's gold and spice souks](/stories/dubai-gold-and-spice-souks/) has more on browsing and bargaining.

## The return, and a detour to Al Shindagha

Cross back to the Bur Dubai side by abra. If you still have energy, walk along the water towards the mouth of the Creek and the Al Shindagha area. Visit Dubai describes the Al Shindagha Museum as offering [a glimpse of how the city developed around its historic creek](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf), and the Dubai Media Office says it is spread across [22 pavilions in more than 80 historical houses](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/july/04-07/trip-adviser). It is a good place to finish, because it puts everything you have just seen into context.

## Dress, photos and manners in the old quarter

Old Dubai is a living neighbourhood, with mosques, homes and workplaces alongside the sights. Visit Dubai advises that [it is advisable to dress conservatively when visiting historical areas and cultural sites](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf). The UK Foreign Office's general guidance is to [dress modestly in public areas, with women covering shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Loose, light layers are the comfortable answer for everyone. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more detail.

The same Foreign Office page is clear on photography: [do not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Boatmen, stallholders and shoppers are going about their day. Ask first, or frame your shots around buildings, boats and baskets instead.

## Beating the heat on an Old Dubai walk

Most of this route is outdoors, with stretches of open quayside and very little shade on the water. Plan around the sun. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm, wear light-coloured, loose clothing and drink more cold drinks](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and lists tiredness, dizziness, headache and feeling sick among the signs of heat exhaustion.

In practice, that means starting early, ducking into the covered souks and the museums when the sun is high, and saving the second crossing for the late afternoon, when the light turns gold on the water. In the hotter months, a late afternoon start that runs into the evening is often the more comfortable choice. ## Quick answers

**How long does an Old Dubai walk take?**
Allow a morning or a late afternoon for the main loop of Al Fahidi, the abra crossing and the Deira souks, and most of a day if you add the Al Shindagha Museum and a long lunch.

**Do I need cash for the abra?**
Yes. The RTA lists the traditional Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq route as cash only, so carry small notes or coins.

**What time does the abra run?**
The RTA lists the Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq route from 6:00 am to midnight, and the Dubai Old Souq to Al Sabkha route around the clock.

**Is bargaining allowed in the souks?**
Yes. Visit Dubai says haggling in the souks is expected and welcomed. Keep it polite and good humoured.

**What should I wear in Old Dubai?**
Visit Dubai advises dressing conservatively in historical areas and cultural sites. Covering shoulders and knees is a sensible rule for everyone.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which order of stops works best for the time of year and day we are visiting, and where can we shelter from the midday sun?
- Does your walk include the abra crossing, and how do you handle it with young children or anyone with limited mobility?
- Can the tour include a cultural meal or a coffee and dates stop, and is that run by a cultural centre or a private venue?
- Will the guide explain the history of the Creek, the wind towers and the souks, or is the walk mainly for shopping?
- Do you include the Al Shindagha Museum, and are entry tickets part of the arrangement?
- How large are your walking groups, and what happens if it is too hot on the day?
