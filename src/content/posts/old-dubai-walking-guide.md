The diesel engine coughs, the boatman pushes off from the wooden quay, and for a few minutes the city goes quiet. Behind you are the wind towers of Bur Dubai. Ahead, on the Deira bank, a wall of shopfronts, cargo dhows and the faint smell of cardamom drifting from the spice stalls.

This is Old Dubai, and it is best seen on foot. An Old Dubai walking route links three things most visitors want to see in one easy loop: the lanes of Al Fahidi, a crossing of Dubai Creek by abra, and the spice and gold souks of Deira. You need comfortable shoes, a few hours, some small cash for the boat and a cool part of the day. You do not need a guide, although a good one adds a great deal. Here is how to plan it, how the walk actually unfolds, and what tends to catch first-timers out.

## Why the Creek is where Dubai began

Every story of the city starts at the water. The UAE government explains that Dubai was established in [1833, when members of the Bani Yas tribe led by the Maktoum family settled by the creek](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai), and that the creek was a natural harbour that made Dubai a centre for fishing, pearling and sea trade. The same page notes that the creek still divides the city, with Deira to the north and Bur Dubai to the south, and that Dubai's first oil exports in 1969 were followed by a period of rapid development. Almost everything you will see on this walk comes from the century and more before that change.

Visit Dubai calls the Creek [the historic heart of the city](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf), and walking its two banks is the clearest way to see how a trading port grew into the city you flew into. The Creek is still a working waterway, too. In 2025 Dubai Municipality reported finishing work on the [two-kilometre Deira wharf](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/may/18-05/dubai-municipality-completes-development-of-dubai-creek), which added a new public promenade alongside the cargo handling areas, with work on the Bur Dubai side under way at the time.

## Your Old Dubai walking route at a glance

The loop below starts and ends on the Bur Dubai side, so you can return to your taxi or the metro without retracing every step.

1. **Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood**: wind towers, quiet lanes and small museums.
2. **The Bur Dubai waterfront**: watch the boats and find the abra station.
3. **Across the Creek by abra** to the Deira Old Souq station.
4. **The Spice Souk**: sacks, baskets and the scent of the old trade.
5. **The Gold Souk**: shop windows full of jewellery.
6. **Back across by abra**, with an optional detour to the Al Shindagha area at the mouth of the Creek.

Allow a morning or a late afternoon for the main loop. If you like to linger in museums or stop for coffee, give it most of a day.

## Start in the lanes of Al Fahidi

Early morning is the best time to arrive. The lanes are cool and shaded, the light is soft on the sand-coloured walls, and you may have whole alleys to yourself.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="old-dubai-walking-guide-1" alts="A narrow sand-coloured lane in Al Bastakiya with a wind tower rising above the houses" caption="Al Fahidi's narrow lanes are shaded for much of the morning, which makes them the natural place to start."></photo-set>

Visit Dubai says most of the buildings here [date back to the late 19th century and now house museums, galleries and cafes](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf). Dubai's Media Office describes the neighbourhood as showing [everyday life in Dubai from around the 1800s until the 1970s](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/july/04-07/trip-adviser). Look up as you walk: the square wind towers that rise above the rooftops were the old way of catching a breeze and sending it down into the rooms below.

The neighbourhood is protected. Dubai Municipality's heritage architecture project lists [Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, the Al Shindagha Heritage District and the Naif area](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/may/12-05/hamdan-bin-mohammed-approves-2nd-phase-of-dubai-heritage-architectur) among the places covered by its first phase, which set out to document and protect buildings from Dubai's earlier history. In other words, you are walking through a deliberately preserved district, not a theme park, and that shapes how you should behave in it.

### A guided start, if you want one

The Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding is based in the neighbourhood and runs a [Heritage Tour](https://cultures.ae/program/heritage-tour-al-farooq-mosque/) that begins with Arabic coffee and dates in a wind tower house, includes a question and answer session with a cultural presenter, then walks the narrow lanes, known as sikkas, and ends at a mosque. The centre's page lists the tour at 1.5 hours and gives its weekly schedule, so check it and book ahead. It is a gentle way to start, because you can ask the questions you might feel shy about asking elsewhere.

For a deeper look at the neighbourhood itself, see our guide to [Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood](/stories/al-fahidi-historical-neighbourhood/).

## Crossing the water

From Al Fahidi, head for the creekside and the Bur Dubai abra station. The abra is the small wooden water taxi that has carried people across the creek for generations, and it is the highlight of the walk for many visitors.

The Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) lists the traditional route from [Bur Dubai Abra Station to Deira Old Souq Abra Station running from 6:00 am to midnight](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/home/promotion/new-marine-routes-abra), with fares paid in cash only. A second traditional route runs between Dubai Old Souq and Al Sabkha around the clock. The RTA also lists [heritage abra routes that include Al Fahidi to Deira Old Souq and Al Seef to Baniyas](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), so there is more than one way across.

The stations themselves have been rebuilt in recent years. The RTA said in 2024 that the upgraded [Deira Old Souq station serves the Old Baladiya Road area and the Gold Souq](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/february/20-02/rta-opens-deira-old-souq-marine-transport-station), with more sheltered waiting space, and that work kept the cultural identity of the station in mind. That is the station you want for the souks.

If you are travelling with children, note the RTA rules on its marine transport page: children under 12 are not allowed on marine transport unaccompanied, and children under 12 travelling with an adult must wear a life jacket. Our [beginner's guide to crossing Dubai Creek by abra](/stories/dubai-creek-abra-guide/) covers the crossing step by step.

## Deira's souks, nose first

Step off the boat on the Deira side and you will probably smell the Spice Souk before you see it. Visit Dubai describes it as a traditional market [where aromatic herbs and spices spill from large baskets at every turn](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-hungry-foodie.pdf). Look for dried limes, saffron, frankincense and rose petals, and do not be surprised when a stallholder offers you a sniff of something before you have even stopped walking.

<photo-set layout="trio" keys="old-dubai-walking-guide-2,old-dubai-walking-guide-3,old-dubai-walking-guide-4" alts="Open sacks of frankincense, dried lemons, cinnamon and other spices at a shop in Deira's Spice Souk|Rows of gold necklaces and bangles in a lit shop window in Deira's Gold Souk|Colourful spices and dried goods piled in baskets outside a stall in the Deira spice souk" caption="Spices and gold sit a few minutes' walk apart on the Deira side of the Creek."></photo-set>

A short walk on, the lanes change character. The Gold Souk is all glass and glitter, window after window of necklaces, bangles and rings. Visit Dubai points to the souks of Deira and Bur Dubai for [gold jewellery, handwoven fabrics, traditional perfumes and souvenirs](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf), and its shopping guide reminds you to [haggle, because it is expected and welcomed](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-shopping-lover.pdf). Keep it friendly, smile, and walk away politely if the price is not right. Our guide to [Dubai's gold and spice souks](/stories/dubai-gold-and-spice-souks/) has more on browsing and bargaining.

### Following the heritage trails

Deira's old market district is easier to navigate than it used to be. In 2025 Dubai Municipality completed [three heritage tourist trails](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/august/24-08/dubai-municipality-completes-landmark-project): the Gold Souq Trail, the Al Ahmadiya School Heritage Axis and the Spice Souq Axis. Together they connect seven traditional markets, including the Herbs Market, Spice Souq, Utensils Souq, Perfume Market, Carpets Market, Textiles Souq and Household Market, as well as the Gold Souq and the Al Ahmadiya area. The same announcement mentions new signboards, floor markings, fabric shading and three public squares with seating, and says traditional materials such as sarooj, a lime-based plaster, were used to restore façades.

In practice, this means you can wander beyond the two famous souks without fear of losing your way. Follow the markings, glance down side lanes of pots and pans or bolts of fabric, and use the squares as places to pause, drink water and regroup.

## How a morning on the route actually unfolds

It helps to picture the walk before you do it, because the pace changes a great deal from one stretch to the next.

The first hour in Al Fahidi is slow and quiet. You will stop often, peer into courtyards, read panels and take photographs of doors and towers. Then the walk to the water opens out onto the creekside, with more traffic, more people and more sun. The abra station is a short, practical interlude: queue, step on, sit down, hand over your fare and watch the water.

The Deira side is the opposite of Al Fahidi. It is busy, loud and commercial, with stallholders calling out, porters wheeling goods through the lanes and shoppers comparing prices. Many visitors find this the most memorable part of the walk, but it is also where people get tired, overheated or overwhelmed. Plan a break here rather than pushing on. A cold drink in the shade of one of the new squares, or a sit-down on the return abra, resets the morning.

The crossing back is the reward. Late in the morning or towards sunset, the light on the Creek changes the look of both banks, and the short ride is a good moment to take stock of what you have seen before you decide whether to carry on to Al Shindagha or call it a day.

## The return, and a detour to Al Shindagha

Cross back to Bur Dubai by abra. With energy to spare, walk along the water towards the mouth of the Creek and Al Shindagha. Visit Dubai describes the Al Shindagha Museum as offering [a glimpse of how the city developed around its historic creek](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf), and the Dubai Media Office says it is spread across [22 pavilions in more than 80 historical houses](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2024/july/04-07/trip-adviser). It puts everything you have just seen into context.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="old-dubai-walking-guide-5" alts="Restored sand-coloured buildings of the Al Shindagha historic area with wind towers and wooden details under a clear sky" caption="The Al Shindagha area at the mouth of the Creek is a natural extension of the walk for anyone with energy left."></photo-set>

Al Shindagha also hosts cultural events. Dubai Culture held the 2026 edition of its Sikka Art and Design Festival at [Al Shindagha Historic Neighbourhood from 23 January to 1 February 2026](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/january/19-01/dubai-culture-launches-dubai-art-season-2026), so if you visit in the cooler months, check what is on before you go. If you would rather stop at one museum and leave the rest, Al Shindagha is the one that ties the whole walk together.

### Adding Etihad Museum

If you have a full day, the story of the city leads naturally into the story of the country. Etihad Museum, beside Union House where the UAE was declared in 1971, is a taxi ride away along the coast in Jumeirah. Our guide to [Etihad Museum](/stories/etihad-museum-dubai/) explains how to fit it in.

## Dress, photos and manners in the old quarter

Old Dubai is a living neighbourhood, with mosques, homes and workplaces alongside the sights. Visit Dubai advises that [it is advisable to dress conservatively when visiting historical areas and cultural sites](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/-/media/Files/guides/dubai-mini-guide-04-cultural-explorer.pdf). The UK Foreign Office's general guidance is to [dress modestly in public areas, with women covering shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Loose, light layers are the comfortable answer for everyone. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more detail.

The same Foreign Office page is clear on photography: [do not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Boatmen, stallholders and shoppers are going about their day. Ask first, or frame your shots around buildings, boats and baskets instead.

### Walking the route during Ramadan

The walk is still possible during the holy month, but it feels different. The Foreign Office says it [may cause offence to eat, drink, smoke or chew gum in public in the daytime](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) during Ramadan, and advises checking the opening hours of shops and restaurants. On a hot walk, that means planning where you can drink discreetly, and expecting quieter lanes by day and busier souks after sunset. Our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) covers the rest.

## Beating the heat on an Old Dubai walk

Most of this route is outdoors, with stretches of open quayside and very little shade on the water. Plan around the sun. The NHS advises people to [avoid the sun between 11am and 3pm, wear light-coloured, loose clothing and drink more cold drinks](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and lists tiredness, dizziness, headache and feeling sick among the signs of heat exhaustion.

In practice, start early, duck into the covered souks and museums when the sun is high, and save the second crossing for late afternoon. In the hotter months, a late afternoon start running into the evening is often more comfortable. If someone in your group does feel unwell, stop, find shade and get help. The UAE government lists the emergency numbers as 999 for police and 998 for an ambulance on its [Dubai page](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai).

## Access, children and what catches people out

This is a walk with uneven surfaces, steps at the quayside and a boat to board, so it pays to think ahead if anyone in your group has limited mobility or is travelling with a pushchair.

- **The abras are improving.** The RTA says its fourth-generation traditional abras [follow the Dubai Universal Design Code](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/february/17-02/rta-unveils-fourth-generation-traditional-abras-to-enhance-marine-transport-network), with improved flooring and dedicated spaces for people of determination. You may not get one of the newer boats on every crossing, so ask at the station.
- **The stations are better too.** The RTA's 2025 upgrade of Old Dubai Souq and Al Sabkha stations [expanded shaded waiting areas by 50%](https://www.mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2025/september/01-09/rta-completes-final-development-phase-of-dubai-creek-stations) and was designed to meet Dubai's code for people of determination.
- **Cash catches people out.** The traditional crossing is cash only, so carry small notes before you leave Al Fahidi.
- **Sharp corners and busy lanes.** Deira's souks are narrow and crowded, with goods stacked at child height. Keep little ones close and agree a meeting point.
- **Too much shopping, too early.** Bags of spices and souvenirs get heavy on a hot day. Browse on the way out and buy on the way back if your route allows it.

## Quick answers

**How long does an Old Dubai walk take?**
Allow a morning or a late afternoon for the main loop of Al Fahidi, the abra crossing and the Deira souks, and most of a day if you add the Al Shindagha Museum and a long lunch.

**Do I need cash for the abra?**
Yes. The RTA lists the traditional Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq route as cash only, so carry small notes or coins.

**What time does the abra run?**
The RTA lists the Bur Dubai to Deira Old Souq route from 6:00 am to midnight, and the Dubai Old Souq to Al Sabkha route around the clock.

**Is bargaining allowed in the souks?**
Yes. Visit Dubai says haggling in the souks is expected and welcomed. Keep it polite and good humoured.

**What should I wear in Old Dubai?**
Visit Dubai advises dressing conservatively in historical areas and cultural sites. Covering shoulders and knees is a sensible rule for everyone.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which order of stops works best for the time of year and day we are visiting, and where can we shelter from the midday sun?
- Does your walk include the abra crossing, and how do you handle it with young children or anyone with limited mobility?
- Can the tour include a cultural meal or a coffee and dates stop, and is that run by a cultural centre or a private venue?
- Will the guide explain the history of the Creek, the wind towers and the souks, or is the walk mainly for shopping?
- Do you include the Al Shindagha Museum, and are entry tickets part of the arrangement?
- How large are your walking groups, and what happens if it is too hot on the day?
