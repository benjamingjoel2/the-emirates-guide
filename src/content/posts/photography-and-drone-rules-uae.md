The light is perfect. The sun is dropping behind the mountains, the sky is turning apricot, and somewhere in your luggage is a drone that would turn this into the shot of the trip. Before you unpack it, stop.

Photography and drone rules in the UAE are two very different stories. With an ordinary camera or phone, you can photograph almost anything you see as a visitor, as long as you follow a few clear rules about people and sensitive sites. Drones are another matter. Under the current federal regulation, recreational drone flying is limited to registered UAE residents, which in practice means most tourists cannot fly one at all.

Here is how both work, based on official UAE and UK government sources. Rules change, especially for drones, so check the linked pages before you travel.

## Your camera: what is fine and what is not

Skylines, souks, desert dunes, museum courtyards, plates of food: point your camera at all of them. Visit Abu Dhabi says [normal tourist photography is acceptable](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs). The rules that matter are about who and what is in the frame.

### Photographing people

This is the big one. The UK Foreign Office says plainly: [do not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It adds that men have been arrested for photographing women on beaches.

Visit Abu Dhabi gives the same advice in gentler terms, saying it is polite to [ask permission before taking photos of people](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs), particularly women. It also notes that cameras may be banned in public areas set aside for women and children only.

In practice:

- Ask before you photograph anyone, and accept a no gracefully.
- Take extra care at beaches, pools, parks and family areas.
- Frame wide shots of busy places so individuals are not the subject.
- Never photograph children who are not with you.
- Delete a photo if someone asks you to.

Privacy also matters after you press the shutter. The UAE government says its media rules include [respecting privacy rules and individuals' private lives](https://u.ae/en/media/media-in-the-uae/media-regulation), and the country's cybercrime law covers what you share online. Think twice before posting identifiable photos of strangers.

### Government, military and airport sites

The Foreign Office says it is [illegal to take photos of some government buildings and military installations](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that hobbies like bird watching and plane spotting may be misunderstood near military sites, government buildings and airports. Visit Abu Dhabi adds [ports and airports](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs) to the list of places not to photograph.

Landmarks that welcome visitors, such as palaces and mosques that run public tours, usually set out their own photography rules. Follow the signs and any instructions from staff. Our guides to the [Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/) and [Qasr Al Watan](/stories/qasr-al-watan-visit/) cover what to expect.

## Big lenses, content creators and media permits

If you are travelling with serious kit, or planning to make content, read this part closely.

The Foreign Office warns that you [may need a licence to use equipment](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) like satellite phones, recording devices, radio transmitters, powerful cameras or binoculars, and suggests getting advice from the UAE embassy before you travel.

It also says you must apply for the appropriate permissions to carry out media activity, including the production and distribution of printed, digital, audio, video or visual information, and that failing to do so could lead to imprisonment and a substantial fine. The UAE government defines a media activity as the production, circulation, printing or publishing of media content, and says the activity [may be at a cost or free of charge](https://u.ae/en/media/media-in-the-uae/media-regulation).

If you are a professional photographer, a filmmaker or an influencer working with brands, check what permits apply to you before you arrive. Holiday snaps for your own album are a different matter, but the line can blur once content is being made for an audience.

## Drones: why most visitors should leave theirs at home

Picture the scene from the start of this piece again. The answer to "can I fly my drone here?" is, for most tourists, no.

### What the current regulation says

The General Civil Aviation Authority's regulation for recreational drone flights, [Issue 04 dated 3 September 2025](https://www.gcaa.gov.ae/en/epublication/EPublications/Civil%20Aviation%20Regulations%20%28CARs%29/EMERGING%20TECHNOLOGIES/UNMANNED%20AIR%20SYSTEMS%20%28UAS%29/CAR-UAR%20-%20UNMANNED%20AIRCRAFT%20UA%20RECREATIONAL%20FLIGHTS%20-%20ISSUE%2004.pdf), sets out who may fly for fun and how. Under it, a recreational pilot must:

- Register on the UAE Drones platform via UAE Pass.
- Be at least 18 years old.
- Be a UAE resident.
- Hold a training certificate from an approved training organisation before flying.
- Register the drone, which must have a remote identification system.
- Obtain flight approval before each flight and use the UAE Drones app during it.

The same regulation sets operating limits: daylight only, within visual line of sight, at no more than 400 feet above ground, with a drone weighing no more than 5 kilograms, away from airports and over permitted areas only. It also prohibits fitting a recreational drone with a camera or any device that can film or record.

That last point is worth reading twice. Even for residents who qualify, the recreational category is not about aerial photography.

### The platform

The [UAE Drones platform](https://drones.gov.ae/) is where pilots sign up, request flight approvals and get live airspace updates. Operators log in with UAE Pass, the national digital identity.

### Older guidance you may find online

Search for UAE drone rules and you will find older pages describing a simpler system. The UAE government portal, for example, still lists rules such as [registering with the GCAA](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/aviation-safety) and flying in green zones shown in an app. The GCAA's own registration page states that [all recreational drones, regardless of weight](https://www.gcaa.gov.ae/en/pages/UASRegistration.aspx/), must be registered, and lists penalties including imprisonment and fines for flying without authorisation or over prohibited areas. Where older pages and the latest regulation differ, the regulation's own header says to check the GCAA website for the current version.

### Commercial and professional filming

Aerial filming for work is a separate category. The government portal says organisations need an [Unmanned Aircraft Operator Authorisation](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/aviation-safety) and must apply for security clearance if a flight involves a camera or other recording device. If you want aerial footage for a wedding, a business or a campaign, the realistic route is a licensed local company rather than your own drone.

## Great shots without breaking a rule

None of this should put you off bringing a camera. The UAE is wonderfully photogenic, and the best images rarely need a drone.

- Go early or late. The soft light at sunrise and in the hour before sunset flatters dunes, mountains and old stone alike. Our [Jebel Jais guide](/stories/jebel-jais-guide/) is a good start for mountain light.
- Find height legally. Viewing decks and observation points give you the aerial feel without a drone.
- Shoot the details: wind towers, carved doors, spice sacks and coffee pots.
- Ask, smile and show people the picture. A friendly exchange often leads to better portraits than a sneaky snap.

## Quick answers

**Can I fly a drone in the UAE as a tourist?** In practice, generally not. The current GCAA regulation requires recreational pilots to be UAE residents aged 18 or over, registered on the UAE Drones platform.

**Can I take photos of people in the UAE?** Only with their permission. The Foreign Office warns that photographing people without consent can lead to arrest.

**Where is photography not allowed in the UAE?** Some government buildings, military installations, ports and airports should not be photographed, and cameras may be banned in areas reserved for women and children.

**Do I need a permit to film content in the UAE?** Media activity, including producing and distributing video or visual content, requires the appropriate permissions, according to the Foreign Office.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Are there stops on our tour where photography is restricted, or where we should ask before taking pictures?
- What time of day gives the best light at each place on our itinerary?
- If we want aerial footage, can you recommend a licensed provider rather than us flying our own drone?
- Is it acceptable to photograph the guides, falconers or performers we meet on tours?
- Do any venues on our itinerary charge or require permission for professional camera equipment?
