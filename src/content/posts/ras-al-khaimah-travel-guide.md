The motorway north from Dubai runs flat and fast for a long time. Then, somewhere past the last of the warehouses, a dark wall of mountains starts to fill the right-hand window, and the sea keeps pace on the left. That is the moment Ras Al Khaimah announces itself: an emirate where the mountains come down almost to the shore.

This first-timer's guide to Ras Al Khaimah covers what most visitors come for, and what most of them miss. The short answer to "is it worth the trip?" is yes, if you want variety. In one emirate you get the UAE's highest mountain, the country's only surviving hill fort, an abandoned pearling village, quiet public beaches and an old town with a museum inside a fort. It works as a long day from Dubai, but it rewards two or three nights far more.

## Why Ras Al Khaimah feels different

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) describes an emirate near the northernmost point of the country, bordering Umm Al Quwain, Fujairah, Sharjah and Oman. It lists a landscape of rolling dunes, coastal strips, salt flats, mangroves and the Hajar mountains, and a history traced back some 7,000 years. The same page says the emirate has about 1,000 archaeological sites.

That mix is the point. You can spend a morning on a mountain trail, an afternoon walking through coral-stone ruins and an evening on a seafront promenade, and barely spend an hour in the car between them.

### Getting there and getting around

According to [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), the emirate is about 45 minutes by car from Dubai International Airport, and Ras Al Khaimah International Airport has some regional and seasonal flights. The tourism board says getting around is easiest by car, that taxis are metered and that the public bus network run by the RAK Transport Authority is more limited than in the bigger cities. For the mountains and desert, it recommends a four-wheel-drive vehicle.

In practice, most first-timers either hire a car or book a driver for the day. If you are new to driving in the region, read up on local road rules before you collect the keys.

## The old capital of the sea traders

Before the resorts, before the mountain road, this was a trading coast. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) recalls Julfar, a port that became one of the most prominent trading posts in the Arabian Gulf and was known for its pearls. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/about-ras-al-khaimah/history-culture/) adds that the area has been known through the ages as Julfar, Majan or Al Seer, and that eighteen historic fortifications, castles and towers remain from a turbulent past.

It also mentions Al Wahabiyya, a traditional dance it describes as unique to Ras Al Khaimah, performed by rows of dancers and drummers at weddings and celebrations. If you happen to see it, stop and watch.

## Five places to start in Ras Al Khaimah

### 1. Jebel Jais and the mountain road

The road up Jebel Jais is the emirate's signature drive. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) calls it the highest mountain in the UAE, part of the Al Hajar range, and says the temperature on the mountain is usually around 10°C cooler than elsewhere in the emirate. The road has regular pull-ins where you can stop safely for the views.

Near the top, the [Jais Viewing Deck Park](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/) spans a ridge with seven viewing decks. Our full [Jebel Jais guide](/stories/jebel-jais-guide/) covers the drive, the trails and how to time it.

### 2. Dhayah Fort

On a lone hill above the date gardens sits a twin-peaked mud-brick fort. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/dhayah-fort/) describes it as the only hill fort remaining in the UAE, reached by 239 zigzagging steps, with views across palms to Oman and down to the sea. The climb has no lighting and is not accessible to prams or wheelchairs, so go in daylight. See our guide to [climbing to Dhayah Fort](/stories/dhayah-fort-guide/).

### 3. Al Jazeera Al Hamra

South of the city, a village of coral-stone houses stands empty. The tourism board calls [Al Jazeera Al Hamra](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-jazeera-al-hamra/) the only remaining historical pearling village in the Gulf region, and says its residents left between 1968 and 1971. The site is open-air and unmanned, and visitors are asked not to climb on or enter the buildings. Our [guide to the pearling village](/stories/al-jazirat-al-hamra-guide/) walks you through it.

### 4. The old town and the National Museum

In the older part of the city, a fort built of coral blocks and stone now holds the [National Museum of Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/national-museum-of-ras-al-khaimah/). The tourism board says it was the residence of the ruling Al Qawasim family until 1964, later served as the police headquarters and prison, and became a museum in the 1980s. It lists opening hours of 8am to 6pm from Tuesday to Sunday, closed on Mondays and the first day of Eid, and warns hours may change during national holidays and Ramadan.

Afterwards, walk part of [Corniche Al Qawasim](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-qawasim-corniche/), a waterfront promenade along the mangroves that, the tourism board says, comes alive at night.

### 5. The beaches

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) mentions 64 km of beaches. For an easy public option, [Flamingo Beach](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/flamingo-beach/) lies between the city centre and Al Hamra Village, with walkways suited to prams and wheelchairs, lifeguards and calm, shallow water. Further north, [Al Rams Beach](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-rams-beach/) and Saraya Island feel wilder, but the tourism board notes you need a four-wheel drive, or a guide to drive you, to reach the island.

Swim only where it is safe. The UK Foreign Office advises checking for warning signs and red flags and [swimming only from approved beaches](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) with lifeguards.

## When to go

Visit Ras Al Khaimah says the best time to visit is [from October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), and that summer, which it puts at June to September, can be extremely hot, especially inland. It adds that occasional winter showers can fall, particularly in the mountains.

Rain matters here more than you might expect, because the mountains channel it into wadis. The Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises checking forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology.

## Dress, manners and entry

Ras Al Khaimah is relaxed, but it is still a Muslim society. The tourism board says there are [no official rules for visitors' attire](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/plan-your-trip/cultural-awareness/) but that people are expected to dress modestly out of respect. The Foreign Office is more specific for public areas such as malls: women should [cover shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and swimwear belongs on beaches and at pools. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.

Entry rules are set for the UAE as a whole. For British citizens, the Foreign Office says a [visitor visa is issued free on arrival](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), and that passports need at least six months' validity from the date you arrive. Other nationalities should check with the UAE authorities before travelling.

## How long to stay

A day trip from Dubai can cover the mountain road and one heritage site, but it is a long day. With two nights you can do Jebel Jais early one morning, Dhayah Fort and the museum the next, and still have an afternoon on the beach. If you are deciding where to base yourself, our guide to [where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah](/stories/where-to-stay-in-ras-al-khaimah/) compares beach, mountain and town. For more on the emirate, see our [Ras Al Khaimah page](/destinations/ras-al-khaimah/).

## Quick answers

**How far is Ras Al Khaimah from Dubai?** Visit Ras Al Khaimah says it is about 45 minutes by car from Dubai International Airport.

**Do I need a car in Ras Al Khaimah?** The tourism board says getting around is easiest by car. Taxis are metered and there is a public bus network, but it is more limited than in the larger cities.

**What is the best time to visit Ras Al Khaimah?** The tourism board recommends October to April, and warns that summer can be extremely hot.

**Is Jebel Jais cooler than the coast?** Yes. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says it is usually around 10°C cooler than elsewhere in the emirate.

**What should I wear in Ras Al Khaimah?** Dress modestly in public. The Foreign Office advises women to cover shoulders and knees in public areas, with swimwear kept to beaches and pools.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can we do the Jebel Jais drive early in the day and still fit in Dhayah Fort or the pearling village without rushing?
- Is your driver experienced on mountain roads, and do you need a four-wheel drive for anything on our plan?
- Can you arrange a knowledgeable guide for Al Jazeera Al Hamra or the National Museum?
- What happens to the plan if rain is forecast for the mountains or wadis?
- Which public beach would you suggest for our group, and is it lifeguarded?
- Are any heritage sites or the museum likely to have different hours on our dates because of a holiday or Ramadan?
