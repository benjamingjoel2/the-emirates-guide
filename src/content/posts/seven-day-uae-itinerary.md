Somewhere on the road up Jebel Jais, the air coming through the car window changes. It loses the soft, salty weight of the coast and turns thin and dry, the rock outside glows copper, and the city you left that morning feels much further away than the map says.

That is the point of stretching a UAE trip to a week. Five days covers the two big cities. Seven gives you room for the mountains of Ras Al Khaimah as well, so the trip has three distinct moods: Dubai's creek and souks, the cool ridges of the north, and the calm, cultural capital in Abu Dhabi.

This seven-day UAE itinerary spends three nights in Dubai, two in Ras Al Khaimah and two in Abu Dhabi. It keeps the middle of every day slow and flags where a Friday or a Monday changes the plan. If you would rather move hotels less often, our [five-day itinerary](/stories/five-day-uae-itinerary/) is the shorter version of the same idea.

## Before you go: checks for a seven-day UAE itinerary

- **Read the current travel advice.** The UK government warns that the regional situation remains unpredictable and that travellers should be [prepared for possible flight cancellations, airspace closures and travel disruptions](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance). Check it before you book and again before you fly.
- **Check your entry rules.** For British citizens, the UK government says a visitor visa is [issued free of charge on arrival, and your passport must be valid for at least 6 months after the date you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). Other passports have other rules.
- **Know the shape of the week.** The UAE federal government works [a four and a half day week, with Saturday and Sunday as the official weekend](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/working-in-uae-government-sector/working-hours-in-the-public-sector) and a half day on Friday. Friday is also the main prayer day, which matters on Day 6.
- **Sort insurance and medicines early.** The UK government advises having [travel insurance for local treatment or unexpected medical evacuation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health), and says some UK prescribed and over-the-counter medicines need approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health. The emergency number for an ambulance is 998.

## How each day is shaped

Every day on this route follows the same rhythm, and it is worth understanding before you look at the detail. Mornings are for walking and anything outdoors. The middle of the day is for a long lunch, the hotel pool or an air-conditioned museum. The late afternoon and evening are for the big views, because that is when the light is softest and the heat has eased.

It sounds slow on paper, and it is meant to. The most common mistake on a first week in the UAE is treating it like a European city break, with sightseeing from nine to six. By the third day that pace leaves people tired, sunburnt and short-tempered in traffic. Plan for two proper things a day and you will usually manage a third without trying.

## Days 1 and 2: Dubai's old heart and the dunes

### Day 1: Abras, spices and the view from the Frame

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="seven-day-uae-itinerary-1" alts="Open sacks of dried red rosebuds, dried limes, safflower, frankincense and other spices piled up for sale in the Deira spice souk" caption="Dried flowers, limes and resins in the spice souk, best seen early before the lanes warm up."></photo-set>

Start early on the Bur Dubai side of the creek, in the shaded lanes of Al Fahidi, then cross the water by abra. Deira, on the far bank, is described by Visit Dubai as [a neighbourhood teeming with character, centuries-old history and spectacular souks](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/explore-dubai/dubai-neighbourhoods/deira). Wander the spice souk while the air is still cool, then retreat indoors for a long lunch and a rest.

Souk etiquette is simple. Browse freely, ask before you handle anything delicate, and ask before you photograph a trader. The UK government's advice is blunt: [do not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Sacks of spices, lanterns and doorways are fair game.

In the late afternoon, head to the Dubai Frame. Its [official site](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en) describes an Old Dubai Gallery charting the city's change from fishing village to metropolis, and a sky deck with views of "Old Dubai" to the north and "New Dubai" to the south. Visitors also pass through a swirl tunnel with audio and visual effects. Seen on your first evening, it gives you a mental map of the city for the rest of the week.

### Day 2: A lazy morning, then sunset on the sand

Keep the morning empty; you will be out late. In the afternoon, head out for the golden hour on the dunes and dinner at camp. If you want the calmer, conservation-led version, the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists [wildlife drives, nature walks, falconry and dune dinners](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) among its activities.

The reserve calls itself the UAE's first national park, and it works with a set of approved operators. Its approved tours focus on nature or culture, use guides certified by the reserve, and follow rules on environmental impact and waste. On a wildlife drive, the guide explains the desert ecology while looking out for free-roaming herds, and you can record what you see for the reserve's own records. It is a gentler evening than the louder dune-bashing camps, and the sunset is the same.

The UK government notes that desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises going with a reputable company. Our guide to [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) explains how to choose a trip that suits you.

## Getting between the three bases

You have two long transfers on this route: Dubai to Ras Al Khaimah on Day 3 and Ras Al Khaimah to Abu Dhabi on Day 5. Inside Dubai and Abu Dhabi, taxis and ride-hailing apps are easy. The UK government recommends [only licensed taxis or recognised apps](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and notes that Dubai's authorised taxis are cream with coloured roofs while Abu Dhabi's are silver or grey.

For the north, Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board says [a car is the easiest way to get around](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), with metered taxis and the main ride-hailing apps also available, and a four-wheel drive recommended for mountain and desert areas. It adds that the emirate's bus network links the city centre with Dubai, but is more limited than in the bigger cities.

If you drive yourself, know the basics first. The UK government says British visitors can use a UK photocard licence, that [it is illegal to drink and drive no matter how small the amount](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that flashing headlights can mean a driver is coming through rather than giving way. Many visitors find the simplest answer is a driver for the two transfer days and taxis everywhere else. Our guide to [driving in the UAE as a visitor](/stories/driving-in-the-uae-as-a-visitor/) covers licences and hire cars in detail.

## Days 3 and 4: Ras Al Khaimah's coast and mountains

### Day 3: North to Al Jazeera Al Hamra

Check out after breakfast and drive north. The tourism board puts Ras Al Khaimah [about 45 minutes by car from Dubai International Airport](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), and says getting around is easiest by car, so this is a good stretch of the trip to hire one or book a driver.

Spend the late afternoon at Al Jazeera Al Hamra, which the tourism board calls [the only remaining historical pearling village in the entire Gulf region](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-jazeera-al-hamra/). Coral-stone houses, a fort, watchtowers and a wind tower residence stand empty in the low light. The site is free to enter but unmanned, so do not climb on or go inside the buildings, and leave before dark, as there is limited lighting.

Look closely at the walls as you walk. Many are built from coral and beach stone, and where the plaster has fallen away you can see the rough pieces set in rows. Wear closed shoes, carry water, and keep children close, as there is loose rubble in places.

### Day 4: Up Jebel Jais and Dhayah Fort

This is the day that makes the week. Jebel Jais is the [highest mountain in the UAE, at 1,934 metres above sea level](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/), and the tourism board says its temperature is usually 10°C cooler than other parts of the emirate. The same page describes well-built roads with hairpin bends and regular pull-ins where you can stop safely for the view, and six marked trails for walkers of all levels.

Near the top, Jais Viewing Deck Park has a run of viewing decks along a ridge. Its listed hours are [8:00am to 8:00pm from Sunday to Thursday, and 8:00am to 11:00pm on Friday and Saturday](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/), and the page notes that the sun tends to set between 17.30 and 18.00 through the year. Our [Jebel Jais guide](/stories/jebel-jais-guide/) has more on the drive and the trails.

Pack a warm layer even if the coast is hot. Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board describes winters, from November to March, as mild at [about 15°C to 28°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), with occasional brief showers mainly in the mountains, while summer temperatures can exceed 45°C, especially inland. After dark on the ridge it can feel properly cold. Drive down slowly, in a low gear, and give cyclists plenty of room on the bends.

<photo-set layout="full" keys="seven-day-uae-itinerary-2" alts="The square mud-brick tower of Dhayah Fort on its walled hilltop in evening light, with palm groves, houses and bare mountains below and behind" caption="Dhayah Fort on its hilltop, reached by a rocky stairway that is best climbed in daylight."></photo-set>

If you have energy left before the mountain, Dhayah Fort is a morning climb. Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board describes it as [the only hill fort remaining in the UAE](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/dhayah-fort/), reached by 239 zigzagging steps. The climb is uneven and rocky, not suitable for prams or wheelchairs, and unlit, so go in daylight. For more ideas in the emirate, see our [Ras Al Khaimah guide](/stories/ras-al-khaimah-travel-guide/).

## Days 5 to 7: Abu Dhabi and the oasis

### Day 5: The long transfer and Qasr Al Hosn

This is the longest drive of the week, south past Dubai to the capital, so leave early and plan nothing demanding before check-in.

Late in the afternoon, visit Qasr Al Hosn, which describes itself as [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit). Its listed hours are 9:00 AM to 8:00 PM from Saturday to Thursday and 2:00 PM to 8:00 PM on Friday. The same page says general admission to the House of Artisans and the landscaped grounds is complimentary, with a ticket needed for the fort itself, and that tickets can be bought online or on site. The House of Artisans is a good short stop if you want to see traditional crafts after a day in the car. Finish with an evening walk on the Corniche.

### Day 6: A rain of light, then marble at dusk

Spend the morning at Louvre Abu Dhabi on Saadiyat Island. Visit Abu Dhabi says the museum is [generally open from 10 AM and closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that most visitors spend two to three hours inside. If Day 6 is a Monday, swap it with Day 7.

The building is half the visit. The same page describes a dome 180 metres wide, made up of 7,850 stars arranged in eight layers, which scatters sunlight across the galleries below in what the architect called the "rain of light". Entry is free for children under 18, the museum has step-free access throughout, and wheelchairs are available on complimentary loan. Guided kayaking tours around the dome are another way to see it if you have time.

<photo-set layout="pair" keys="seven-day-uae-itinerary-3,seven-day-uae-itinerary-4" alts="Flat stone platforms set in shallow water beneath the latticed dome of Louvre Abu Dhabi, with the open sea beyond|White gallery blocks and steps around a pool of still water beneath the latticed dome of Louvre Abu Dhabi, with the dome reflected in the water" caption="Under the Louvre Abu Dhabi dome, where light falls through layers of geometric stars onto the water."></photo-set>

Rest through the afternoon, then go to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque for the late light. The mosque asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). Its listed hours are 9:00 AM to 8:30 PM, with last entry at 8:00 PM, every day except Friday, when it opens 9:00 AM to 11:30 AM and 2:30 PM to 8:30 PM. Hours differ during Ramadan.

The [mosque's dress code](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details) asks women to wear loose clothing to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists and full hair coverage, and asks men to avoid sleeveless, tight or transparent clothing, preferably with legs fully covered. The same rules ask visitors to avoid excessive jewellery, heavy make-up and vibrant colours, to keep phones on silent, to take photographs only at the designated photo stops, and to bring no food or drink other than water. The marble floors can be slippery, so watch your step. Our [Grand Mosque visitor guide](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/) covers the visit in detail.

### Day 7: Palm shade in Al Ain, or a quiet last morning

If your flight is late, drive to Al Ain. Visit Abu Dhabi describes Al Ain Oasis as [a 90-minute drive from the capital](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), with shaded pathways, ancient falaj channels and free entry. The oasis and nearby sites at Hili, Jebel Hafit and Bida bint Saud make up [Al Ain's UNESCO World Heritage listing](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/unesco-sites).

The oasis covers about 1,200 hectares and holds some 147,000 date palms, according to the same tourism page. Its two central falaj systems are called Al Aini and Dawood, and the page warns that the water can disappear and reappear with the season. You can walk, rent a bicycle or take a golf buggy, and the free Eco-Centre explains the farming that keeps the palms alive. Allow up to two hours. If you have longer, the Hili Archaeological Site holds what Visit Abu Dhabi describes as [the earliest known evidence of an agricultural village in the UAE](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/unesco-sites).

Flying early? Keep it to a last swim and plenty of time for the airport.

## What to pack and wear for the week

Your bag has to cover a souk, a desert camp, a mountain ridge, a museum and a mosque. Light, loose layers do most of the work. The UK government advises [dressing modestly in public areas like shopping malls](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), with women covering shoulders and knees, and wearing swimwear only on beaches or at pools. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests [a jacket or sweater for cold air conditioning](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) in hotels and cinemas, and recommends dressing more conservatively during Ramadan.

Add a warm top for Jebel Jais, closed shoes for Dhayah Fort and Al Jazeera Al Hamra, a hat and sunscreen for the desert, and one outfit that meets the Grand Mosque dress code without improvising.

## Making the week work for you

- **Fridays:** Put Qasr Al Hosn and the Grand Mosque in the afternoon.
- **Mondays:** Keep the Louvre off that day.
- **The hotter months:** Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board names [October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) as the best time for the outdoors. Outside that window, start earlier and rest longer.
- **Ramadan:** The UK government says eating, drinking or smoking in public in the daytime [may cause offence during Ramadan](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that driving can be erratic around dusk. Check opening hours and keep the transfers to the morning.
- **Travelling with children:** Dhayah Fort is not suitable for prams, and Al Jazeera Al Hamra is unlit, so plan both for daylight. The Louvre and the Al Ain oasis are easier, with free entry for children at the museum and shaded paths in the oasis.

## Quick answers

**Is seven days enough for the UAE?** It is enough for Dubai, Abu Dhabi and one mountain region at a comfortable pace, with a day spare for Al Ain or the desert. It is not enough to see all seven emirates without rushing.

**How far is Ras Al Khaimah from Dubai?** The emirate's tourism board puts it [about 45 minutes by car](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) from Dubai International Airport.

**Do I need to book the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque?** Yes. The mosque asks visitors to pre-book and get an online access pass before arriving.

**Is Louvre Abu Dhabi open every day?** No. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [closed on Mondays and generally opens from 10 AM](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi).

**When is the best time for Jebel Jais?** Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board recommends [October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) for outdoor activities.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which of my seven days fall on a Friday or a Monday, and how would you reorder the Abu Dhabi days around them?
- Can you arrange the transfers from Dubai to Ras Al Khaimah and on to Abu Dhabi, and how long should we really allow for each in traffic?
- Is a hire car or a driver better for the Jebel Jais and Dhayah Fort day, and is the mountain road open on our dates?
- Can you book a Grand Mosque access pass for the late afternoon on Day 6?
- Which desert trip do you recommend for Day 2, and what safety equipment do the vehicles carry?
- If the forecast turns wet or very hot, what indoor alternatives would you suggest in each emirate?
