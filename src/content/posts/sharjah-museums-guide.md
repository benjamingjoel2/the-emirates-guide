Under the central dome of a museum on the Sharjah waterfront, a mosaic of the zodiac constellations glows in gold and blue. Most people walk straight past it. Look up, and you have found the first of many quiet surprises in a city that takes its museums very seriously.

Sharjah's museums are the reason many visitors come at all. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/sharjah) says the emirate is home to nearly a quarter of all the UAE's museums. The Sharjah Museums Authority describes a range that covers [Islamic art and culture, archaeology, heritage, science and marine life](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-US/Visit/MuseumsEnthusiasts), and the history of Sharjah and the wider region.

That choice can feel overwhelming. Here is the short answer: if you see only one, make it the Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization. If you have a whole day, pair it with the heritage houses of the old quarter. Then pick a third by interest: archaeology, calligraphy or the sea.

## If you only see one Sharjah museum

### The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization

The building alone is worth the trip, a pale landmark on the Corniche just north of the old quarter. Inside, [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/sharjah-museum-of-islamic-civilization/) says there are more than 5,000 artefacts across seven galleries on two levels.

The ground floor deals with the Islamic faith and the work of Arab scientists and astronomers: astrolabes, manuscripts and instruments that show how far ahead the scholars of the time were. Upstairs, the galleries move through Islamic arts and crafts in order, from ceramics and coins to textiles and jewellery.

Two things not to miss: the gold-embroidered curtain made for the door of the Holy Ka'ba, and that zodiac mosaic inside the central dome. Allow a long morning or afternoon. It is the most rewarding place in the city to sit out the midday heat.

## For deep history: the Archaeology Museum

Long before pearls and souks, people lived and traded across this land, and the [Sharjah Archaeology Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-archaeology-museum/) tells that story from the Stone Age to the Islamic era. Visit Sharjah lists pottery, jewellery and weapons, a 2,000-year-old golden bridle from Mleiha, and finds from a Stone Age graveyard.

It sits in the Halwan district, away from the old quarter, so take a taxi. There is a children's area with interactive displays and films, which makes it a good family choice.

If the Mleiha finds catch your imagination, you can see where they came from. Visit Sharjah says the [Mleiha Archaeological Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) in the desert offers guided tours of the sites; our [Mleiha guide](/stories/mleiha-sharjah-desert-guide/) explains how to plan the trip.

## For everyday life: the heritage houses

Walk into the restored old quarter and the museums become homes. This is where to understand how people lived in Sharjah before air conditioning and oil.

### Sharjah Heritage Museum

Set in a restored pearl merchant's house, the [Sharjah Heritage Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-heritage-museum/) has six galleries on the landscape, lifestyle, celebrations, livelihoods, traditional knowledge and oral traditions of the emirate. Labels are in English and Arabic. Look for the aftakh, a golden toe ring worn by Bedouin women. Children tend to enjoy the folk tales, proverbs and puzzles.

### Bait Al Naboodah

A few steps away, [Bait Al Naboodah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/bait-al-naboodah-museum/) was built in 1845 around a sunlit courtyard, with coral walls, carved decoration and wind catchers. Walking its rooms, you follow the daily routines of the pearling family who lived here. It is a short visit, but it makes the rest of the district make sense.

### Majlis Al Midfa

Just round the corner, [Majlis Al Midfa](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/majlis-al-midfa/) has what Visit Sharjah calls the UAE's only round wind tower. It was a meeting place for intellectuals and merchants, named after the man who started the country's first newspaper in 1927. The tower holds antiques donated by the family, including a carved pearl chest and a silver dagger.

For a walking route that links these houses with the souks, see our guide to [the Heart of Sharjah](/stories/heart-of-sharjah-guide/).

## For art lovers: calligraphy and contemporary work

### Sharjah Calligraphy Museum

On Calligraphy Square in the old quarter, the [Sharjah Calligraphy Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/sharjah-calligraphy-museum/) is, according to Visit Sharjah, the only museum in the Arab world devoted entirely to calligraphy. It is set in a historic house around a covered, light-filled courtyard, and traces the art from early Kufic script to more fluid styles, with works on canvas, wood, paper and ceramic. Ask about workshops if you want to try a pen yourself.

### Bait Al Serkal and the art houses

Several heritage houses in the district now host exhibitions under the Sharjah Art Foundation. [Bait Al Serkal](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/arts/bait-al-serkal-and-bait-obaid-al-shamsi/) has a long history: Visit Sharjah says it once served as headquarters for the British delegate in the Gulf and later as a hospital, and it now hosts exhibitions, workshops and the Sharjah Biennial. Nearby Bait Obaid Al Shamsi, built around 1845, is another restored house with wind towers. What is on changes, so check before you go.

## For the sea: maritime museum and aquarium

Sharjah grew from the water, and the [Sharjah Maritime Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/family/sharjah-maritime-museum/) explains how. Displays cover pearling, fishing and trade, with wooden dhows including a full-size pearling boat, and diving tools. Visit Sharjah says it also holds a pearl found by archaeologists in a coastal cemetery in Sharjah, believed to be one of the oldest known pearls in the world.

Next door, the [Sharjah Aquarium](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/sharjah-aquarium/) recreates local habitats from coral reefs and rock pools to lagoons and mangroves. Visit Sharjah says one ticket covers both, which makes this pair the easiest museum outing for families.

## Beyond the city

Sharjah's museums do not stop at the city limits. On the east coast, [Hisn Khorfakkan](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/hisn-khorfakkan/) is a museum inspired by the design of the town's original fort, which still stands nearby. If you are heading over the mountains, our [Khorfakkan guide](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) shows how to fit it in.

## How to plan a museum day

- **Group by area.** The Heritage Museum, Bait Al Naboodah, Majlis Al Midfa and the Calligraphy Museum are all within a short walk of each other. The Museum of Islamic Civilization is close by on the Corniche. The Archaeology Museum, the Maritime Museum and the aquarium need a taxi.
- **Check the days.** Opening times vary by museum and season, and some close on certain days. Check each museum's official listing before you set out, rather than relying on old guidebooks.
- **Use the heat.** Walk the old quarter early, then move into the larger museums as the day warms up.
- **Dress for it.** Public places in the UAE call for modest dress; the UK Foreign Office advises women to [cover shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Our guide to [what to wear](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.
- **Ask before photos of people.** The same advice says not to [photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Rules on photographing exhibits vary, so look for signs.

## Quick answers

**Which Sharjah museum is best for first-time visitors?**
The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization, with more than 5,000 artefacts across seven galleries, gives the broadest introduction.

**Are Sharjah's museums good for children?**
Yes. The Archaeology Museum has a children's area with interactive displays, and the Heritage Museum has folk tales and puzzles. The aquarium and Maritime Museum share one ticket, according to Visit Sharjah.

**How many museums does Sharjah have?**
The UAE government portal says Sharjah is home to nearly a quarter of all the UAE's museums.

**Can I see several museums on foot?**
Yes. The Heritage Museum, Bait Al Naboodah, Majlis Al Midfa and the Calligraphy Museum are all in the Heart of Sharjah heritage district.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which museums are open on our dates, and do any close on particular days of the week?
- Can you arrange a guide who specialises in Islamic art, archaeology or Emirati heritage?
- Is there a combined ticket or pass that makes sense for the museums we want to see?
- Are any temporary exhibitions or Sharjah Art Foundation shows on while we are there?
- Can you add the Mleiha Archaeological Centre or Hisn Khorfakkan to a longer trip?
