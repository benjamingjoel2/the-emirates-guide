Under the central dome of a museum on the Sharjah waterfront, a mosaic of the zodiac constellations glows in gold and blue. Most people walk straight past it. Look up, and you have found the first of many quiet surprises in a city that takes its museums very seriously.

Sharjah's museums are the reason many visitors come at all. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/sharjah) says the emirate is home to nearly a quarter of all the UAE's museums. The Sharjah Museums Authority describes a range that covers [Islamic art and culture, archaeology, heritage, science and marine life](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-US/Visit/MuseumsEnthusiasts), and the history of Sharjah and the wider region. Its own [list of museums](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums) runs from an old airport and a 19th-century fort to a science museum, an aquarium and a monument on the east coast.

That choice can feel overwhelming. Here is the short answer: if you see only one, make it the Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization. If you have a whole day, pair it with the heritage houses of the old quarter. Then pick a third by interest: archaeology, calligraphy, aviation or the sea.

## If you only see one Sharjah museum

### The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization

The building alone is worth the trip, a pale landmark on the Corniche just north of the old quarter. Inside, [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/sharjah-museum-of-islamic-civilization/) says there are more than 5,000 artefacts across seven galleries on two levels.

The building has a history of its own. The Sharjah Museums Authority says it [opened as Souq Al Majarrah in 1987](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/sharjah-museum-of-islamic-civilization) and reopened as the Museum of Islamic Civilization in 2008, with a collection spanning the first century AH (the 7th century AD) to the 14th century AH (the 20th century AD). Early Islamic coins, including Umayyad and Abbasid dinars and dirhams, are shown in the atrium, and a separate gallery hosts temporary exhibitions twice a year.

The ground floor deals with the Islamic faith and the work of Arab scientists and astronomers: astrolabes, manuscripts and instruments that show how far ahead the scholars of the time were. Upstairs, the galleries move through Islamic arts and crafts in order, from ceramics and coins to textiles and jewellery.

Two things not to miss: the gold-embroidered curtain made for the door of the Holy Ka'ba, and that zodiac mosaic inside the central dome. The authority describes the curtain as black silk satin embroidered with gold and silver thread, and the most prominent piece in the gallery of Islamic faith. Allow a long morning or afternoon. It is the most rewarding place in the city to sit out the midday heat.

## For deep history: the Archaeology Museum

Long before pearls and souks, people lived and traded across this land, and the [Sharjah Archaeology Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-archaeology-museum/) tells that story from the Stone Age to the Islamic era. Visit Sharjah lists pottery, jewellery and weapons, a 2,000-year-old golden bridle from Mleiha, and finds from a Stone Age graveyard with the skeletons of 500 nomadic herders.

It sits in the Halwan district, away from the old quarter, next to the Sharjah Science Museum, so take a taxi. There is a children's area with interactive displays and films, which makes it a good family choice.

If the Mleiha finds catch your imagination, you can see where they came from. Visit Sharjah says the [Mleiha Archaeological Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) in the desert offers guided tours of the sites; our [Mleiha guide](/stories/mleiha-sharjah-desert-guide/) explains how to plan the trip.

## For everyday life: the heritage houses

Walk into the restored old quarter and the museums become homes. This is where to understand how people lived in Sharjah before air conditioning and oil.

### Sharjah Heritage Museum

Set in a restored pearl merchant's house, the [Sharjah Heritage Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-heritage-museum/) has six galleries on the landscape, lifestyle, celebrations, livelihoods, traditional knowledge and oral traditions of the emirate. Labels are in English and Arabic. Look for the aftakh, a golden toe ring worn by Bedouin women. Children tend to enjoy the folk tales, proverbs and puzzles.

### Bait Al Naboodah

A few steps away, [Bait Al Naboodah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/bait-al-naboodah-museum/) was built in 1845 around a sunlit courtyard, with coral walls, carved decoration and wind catchers. Walking its rooms, you follow the daily routines of the pearling family who lived here. It is a short visit, but it makes the rest of the district make sense. The museums authority adds that the house uses [teak columns and wind scoops built into the walls](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/bait-al-naboodah) rather than the more familiar wind towers, and runs drop-in workshops for children and their families.

### Sharjah Fort (Al Hisn)

At the centre of the quarter stands the building that once ran it. The Sharjah Museums Authority says [Al Hisn was built in 1823](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/sharjah-fort-%28al-hisn%29) as the seat of government and home of the ruling Al Qasimi family, from coral stones collected from the sea, with teak doors and ceilings of mangrove trunks. Its rooms cover the ruling family, old defence strategies and an early jail, and show how date syrup was once made. It sits behind Souq Al Shanasiyah.

### Al Qasimia School

The newest of the heritage museums tells a story most visitors never think about: how the emirate learned to read and write. The authority says [Al Qasimia School opened as a museum in January 2025](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/al-qasimia-school) in Sharjah's first formal school, with restored classrooms, original books and documents, and six galleries tracing how the curriculum grew from the Quran to grammar, calligraphy, mathematics, geography and English.

### Majlis Al Midfa

Just round the corner, [Majlis Al Midfa](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/majlis-al-midfa/) has what Visit Sharjah calls the UAE's only round wind tower. It was a meeting place for intellectuals and merchants, named after the man who started the country's first newspaper in 1927. The tower holds antiques donated by the family, including a carved pearl chest and a silver dagger.

The authority sells a [heritage joint ticket](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-US/Visit/Heritage-Joint-Ticket) covering Bait Al Naboodah, the Heritage Museum, the Calligraphy Museum, the fort and Al Qasimia School, which suits anyone planning a full morning here. For a walking route that links these houses with the souks, see our guide to [the Heart of Sharjah](/stories/heart-of-sharjah-guide/).

## For art lovers: calligraphy and contemporary work

### Sharjah Calligraphy Museum

On Calligraphy Square in the old quarter, the [Sharjah Calligraphy Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/sharjah-calligraphy-museum/) is, according to Visit Sharjah, the only museum in the Arab world devoted entirely to calligraphy. It is set in a historic house around a covered, light-filled courtyard, and traces the art from early Kufic script to more fluid styles, with works on canvas, wood, paper and ceramic. Ask about workshops if you want to try a pen yourself.

### Sharjah Art Museum

For painting and sculpture, the [Sharjah Art Museum](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/sharjah-art-museum) has a permanent collection of modern and contemporary Arab art and a programme of temporary exhibitions through the year. The authority says its art library holds over 4,000 titles in Arabic, English and other languages.

### Bait Al Serkal and the art houses

Several heritage houses in the district now host exhibitions under the Sharjah Art Foundation. [Bait Al Serkal](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/arts/bait-al-serkal-and-bait-obaid-al-shamsi/) has a long history: Visit Sharjah says it once served as headquarters for the British delegate in the Gulf and later as a hospital, and it now hosts exhibitions, workshops and the Sharjah Biennial. Nearby Bait Obaid Al Shamsi, built around 1845, is another restored house with wind towers. What is on changes, so check before you go.

## For flight and science: Al Mahatta and the Science Museum

Aviation fans should not skip [Al Mahatta Museum](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/al-mahatta-museum). The authority says it occupies the first airport in the region, built in 1932 and used in the 1930s as a staging post on commercial routes from Britain to India. Restored propeller aircraft stand in the hangar beside the original refuelling tanker, and a film shows daily life at the airport and in Sharjah town at the time.

With younger visitors, the [Sharjah Science Museum](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/museums/sharjah-science-museum) has more than 50 interactive exhibits on physics, biology and astronomy, with demonstrations and workshops. For small children, Visit Sharjah describes the [Sharjah Discovery Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/family/sharjah-discovery-centre/) as an edutainment centre for ages three to 12, with seven themed areas, on the airport road.

## For the sea: maritime museum and aquarium

Sharjah grew from the water, and the [Sharjah Maritime Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/family/sharjah-maritime-museum/) explains how. Displays cover pearling, fishing and trade, with wooden dhows including a full-size pearling boat, and diving tools. Visit Sharjah says it also holds a pearl found by archaeologists in a coastal cemetery in Sharjah, believed to be one of the oldest known pearls in the world.

Next door, the [Sharjah Aquarium](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/sharjah-aquarium/) recreates local habitats from coral reefs and rock pools to lagoons and mangroves. Visit Sharjah says one ticket covers both, which makes this pair the easiest museum outing for families. The museums authority's [family route](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/visit/family-time-at-the-museums) says the aquarium shows around 100 marine species native to the region's waters.

## Beyond the city

Sharjah's museums do not stop at the city limits. On the east coast, [Hisn Khorfakkan](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/hisn-khorfakkan/) is a museum inspired by the design of the town's original fort, which still stands nearby. If you are heading over the mountains, our [Khorfakkan guide](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) shows how to fit it in.

Further south in Kalba, Visit Sharjah says [Bait Sheikh Saeed Bin Hamad Al Qasimi](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/bait-sheikh-saeed-bin-hamad-al-qasimi/) is a late 19th-century beachside house with carved wooden doors, wind towers and a central courtyard, opened as a museum in 1999. Inland, the [Natural History and Botanical Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/family/natural-history-botanical-museum/) in Sharjah Desert Park has fossils, meteorites and desert ecosystems, with a garden of nearly 100 native plant species.

## How long each museum takes

The Sharjah Museums Authority publishes suggested routes with rough tour times, which help when you are building a day. On its [history route](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/visit/exploring-sharjah-s-history), it suggests about 60 minutes each for the Archaeology Museum and the fort, 40 minutes each for Al Mahatta, the Heritage Museum and the Maritime Museum, and 30 minutes for Bait Al Naboodah. Its [art route](https://www.sharjahmuseums.ae/en-us/visit/an-art-lover-s-haven) allows 40 minutes for the Art Museum, 30 for the Calligraphy Museum and 60 for the Museum of Islamic Civilization. Treat these as minimums: if a gallery grabs you, stay.

## How to plan a museum day

- **Group by area.** The Heritage Museum, Bait Al Naboodah, Majlis Al Midfa, the fort and the Calligraphy Museum are all within a short walk of each other. The Museum of Islamic Civilization is close by on the Corniche. The Archaeology Museum, the Maritime Museum and the aquarium need a taxi.
- **Check the days.** Opening times vary by museum and season, and some close on certain days. The fort's page, for example, lists closures on the first days of Eid and separate Ramadan hours. Check each museum's official listing before you set out, rather than relying on old guidebooks.
- **Use the heat.** Walk the old quarter early, then move into the larger museums as the day warms up.
- **Plan for access.** Several authority museums, including the fort and the Museum of Islamic Civilization, list a lift, wheelchair entrance and baby changing on their pages. The older heritage houses have courtyards and stairs, so ask ahead if access matters.
- **Dress for it.** Public places in the UAE call for modest dress; the UK Foreign Office advises women to [cover shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Our guide to [what to wear](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more.
- **Ask before photos of people.** The same advice says not to [photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Rules on photographing exhibits vary, so look for signs.

### Three sample days

- **First visit:** the heritage houses and fort in the morning, the Museum of Islamic Civilization through midday, and a walk on the Corniche at the end of the afternoon.
- **History deep dive:** the Archaeology Museum first thing, then Al Mahatta, then the fort and Bait Al Naboodah once the heat eases.
- **With children:** the Science Museum or Discovery Centre in the morning, lunch, then the aquarium and Maritime Museum, finishing on the sand nearby.

## Quick answers

**Which Sharjah museum is best for first-time visitors?**
The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization, with more than 5,000 artefacts across seven galleries, gives the broadest introduction.

**Are Sharjah's museums good for children?**
Yes. The Archaeology Museum has a children's area with interactive displays, and the Science Museum has more than 50 hands-on exhibits. The aquarium and Maritime Museum share one ticket, according to Visit Sharjah.

**How many museums does Sharjah have?**
The UAE government portal says Sharjah is home to nearly a quarter of all the UAE's museums.

**Can I see several museums on foot?**
Yes. The Heritage Museum, Bait Al Naboodah, Majlis Al Midfa, the fort and the Calligraphy Museum are all in the Heart of Sharjah heritage district.

**Is there a combined ticket?**
The Sharjah Museums Authority sells a heritage joint ticket for five museums in the Heart of Sharjah, available at those museums.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which museums are open on our dates, and do any close on particular days or holidays?
- Can you arrange a guide who specialises in Islamic art, archaeology or Emirati heritage?
- Does the heritage joint ticket make sense for the museums we want to see?
- Are any temporary exhibitions or Sharjah Art Foundation shows on while we are there?
- Which museums have step-free access if someone in our group uses a wheelchair or pushchair?
- Can you add the Mleiha Archaeological Centre or Hisn Khorfakkan to a longer trip?
