You step out of the heat and onto the marble, and the noise of the road simply stops. Ahead, a courtyard so white it makes you squint stretches towards the prayer hall, its edges lined with columns inlaid with flowers.

Visiting the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is, for many travellers, the moment Abu Dhabi stops being a stopover and starts being a place. It is also a working mosque, and that shapes everything about your visit. The short answer to what most people want to know: yes, non-Muslim visitors are welcome; you need to [pre-book an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours) before you go; and there is a strict, clearly published [dress code](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details) that applies to everyone. Get those three things right and the rest of the day takes care of itself.

## A mosque first, a landmark second

It helps to know what you are walking into. The mosque is named after Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the founder of the UAE. According to the mosque centre, construction began on [5 November 1996](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/about/the-mosque-and-the-founder), the inner prayer halls first opened for Eid Al Adha worship in 2007, and the building can hold approximately 50,000 people. The same page says more than 3,000 workers and 38 contracting companies took part in building it, drawing on artisans and natural materials from many countries.

The centre describes the mosque as a place meant to reflect Islamic moderation and to encourage tolerance and exchange between cultures. That is the spirit in which visitors are received. You are a guest in a place of prayer, and the rules below exist so that worshippers and visitors can share the space comfortably.

## How to book your visit

### The access pass

Worshippers can walk straight in without a ticket. Visitors cannot. The official visitors page asks you to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://szgmc.gov.ae/en/visiting-the-mosque/visitors) through the "Book Your Visit" link on the mosque centre's website. Do this before you leave your hotel, and keep the pass on your phone.

On the question of cost, information online varies, so rely on the source. The mosque centre's own [FAQ](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-faqs) sets out entry fees by visitor category, and states that worshippers enter free. Check the booking page for the current position when you reserve.

### Visiting hours

The mosque centre publishes its [visiting hours](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). For visitors, outside Ramadan:

- **Saturday to Thursday:** 9:00 AM to 8:30 PM, with last entry at 8:00 PM.
- **Friday:** 9:00 AM to 11:30 AM (last entry 11:00 AM), then 2:30 PM to 8:30 PM (last entry 8:00 PM).

The Friday gap is for Friday prayers, so do not plan a late Friday morning visit. During Ramadan the hours change completely, with separate daytime and late-night windows for the first 20 days and shorter hours in the last ten. If your trip overlaps the holy month, read our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) and check the official page again in the week you travel.

## The Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque dress code, explained

This is where visits most often go wrong, so it is worth being precise. The mosque centre's [mosque manners page](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details) sets out the rules.

### For women

- Clothing should be loose-fitting down to the ankles.
- Sleeves should reach all the way to the wrists.
- Hair must be fully covered.
- Avoid transparent, tight-fitting or revealing clothing.

The mosque centre's FAQ sums it up as [long, loose, ankle-length clothing, with a headscarf for women](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-faqs).

### For men

Men have more latitude, but not unlimited. The manners page says covering the entire legs is preferred, and that transparent, tight-fitting or sleeveless shirts should be avoided. In practice, long trousers and a T-shirt or shirt with sleeves are the safe choice.

### For everyone

The same page asks all visitors to avoid excessive jewellery, heavy make-up and bright colours, and says clothing with slogans or inappropriate drawings is not permitted. A plain, loose outfit in a muted colour is ideal. Pack a light scarf even if you think you will not need it; it is the simplest insurance against being turned away. For a wider view of what to pack for the UAE, see our guide to [what to wear and local etiquette](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/).

## Inside: how to behave and what to leave behind

Once inside, the atmosphere is calm and the expectations are clear. The [mosque manners](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details) guidance asks you to:

- Keep your voice down and set your phone to silent.
- Avoid public displays of affection.
- Not smoke, sleep or lie down anywhere on the grounds.
- Not bring food, water or other drinks inside.
- Take care on the marble floors, which can be slippery.

The ban on water surprises people. The [FAQ](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-faqs) explains that it protects the delicate marble and carpets. Drink before you go in, and leave bottles in the car.

### Items you cannot bring

The mosque centre lists a range of prohibited items, including e-cigarettes, lighters, sharp objects, whistles, laser pointers, loudspeakers, dolls and stuffed animals, masks, flags and slogans. That last group catches families out, so leave the soft toys in the hotel.

### Photography

Personal photos are welcome, but only at [designated photo stops](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details), and you should not lie down or behave inappropriately to get a shot. Commercial or media photography and professional equipment need a permit from the centre, and devices used in breach of the rules may be confiscated.

## Free cultural tours

You can explore on your own, but a guide adds a great deal. The mosque centre runs [free cultural tours](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-cultural-tours) daily, with start times of 11:00, 13:00 and 17:00, and no 13:00 tour on Fridays. During Ramadan, tours run Saturday to Thursday at 11:00 and 13:00 only. The tour page says a tour lasts between 30 and 45 minutes. According to the [FAQ](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-faqs), tours are offered in English and Arabic, start on the east side of the mosque, and you should arrive about 15 minutes early.

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that guides point out details many visitors miss, such as the [gold-leaf calligraphy around the mosque's 82 domes](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque).

## When to go for the best light

Visit Abu Dhabi suggests that [dusk is particularly special](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque), when the fading sun warms the white facade, and the building is lit up after sunset. Morning visits tend to feel quieter and cooler underfoot. If you can, arrive in late afternoon, take the 17:00 tour, and stay on as the lights come up.

Remember that Friday is the main day of congregational prayer. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that the [main prayer hall is closed to visitors at Friday prayer time](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque), which matches the gap in the official Friday hours.

## Getting there and practical details

The mosque centre's [FAQ](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-faqs) says it does not provide transport, so you will arrive by taxi, bus or your own car. There are parking areas on the southern side, marked by signs near the entrances, and restrooms for men and women. Wheelchairs can be requested from security, and buggies carry visitors between the car parks and the tour starting point. Children are welcome but must stay under their parents' supervision at all times.

## Quick answers

**Do I need to book to visit the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque?**
Yes. Visitors must pre-book and obtain an online access pass through the mosque centre's website. Worshippers can enter without a ticket.

**What is the dress code for women?**
Loose clothing down to the ankles, sleeves to the wrists, and hair fully covered. Avoid transparent, tight or revealing clothes.

**Can men wear shorts?**
The mosque centre prefers men to cover their entire legs and asks them to avoid sleeveless shirts, so long trousers are the safe choice.

**Can I take photos inside?**
Personal photos are allowed at designated photo stops. Professional equipment and commercial filming need a permit.

**Is the mosque open on Fridays?**
Yes, but visiting is split into a morning window and an afternoon window, with a gap for Friday prayers.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Will you book our access passes, or should each of us book individually through the mosque centre's website?
- Can you time our visit to catch one of the free cultural tours, or the light at dusk?
- How will you handle Friday prayer times or Ramadan hours if they fall on our day?
- Can you check our outfits against the dress code before we set off, so nobody is turned away?
- Where will you drop us, and how far is it from the car park to the tour starting point for anyone with limited mobility?
- How long will we have at the mosque before moving on to the next stop?
