The speedboat slows, the engine drops to a murmur, and a low island takes shape across the water. A little later, from the back of a 4x4, you watch a giraffe lower its head to look at you, with the Gulf glittering behind it. It is not the picture most people have of Abu Dhabi.

Sir Bani Yas Island is the emirate's wildlife island, a nature reserve off the Al Dhafra coast where Arabian oryx, gazelles and giraffes roam free, and where the ruins of a church more than a thousand years old have been uncovered. It takes effort to reach, and that is part of its appeal.

The short answer to "is it worth it?": yes, if you want wildlife, space and quiet, and you are willing to plan ahead. The island is a long drive and a boat ride from the city, day visits to the wildlife park must be booked well in advance, and most visitors stay at least one night.

## What makes Sir Bani Yas Island different

Visit Abu Dhabi explains that Sir Bani Yas was [established in 1971 by the late founder of the UAE, Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, as a Royal Nature Reserve](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/guide-to-sir-bani-yas-island). It is one of eight islands in Al Dhafra, Abu Dhabi's western region, and the same guide lists 87 square kilometres of nature, wildlife, forty-two historical sites and adventure activities.

It also helps to know that Sir Bani Yas is one of very few islands you can simply visit. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that Abu Dhabi is [an archipelago of more than 200 islands](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands), but only a select number are open for tourism; others are private, military zones or conservation sanctuaries.

What sets it apart is the balance. This is not a resort island with a few animals. Visit Abu Dhabi says the Arabian Wildlife Park [takes up more than half the island](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/arabian-wildlife-park), and that over half of Sir Bani Yas is [dedicated to wildlife conservation](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/sir-bani-yas-island). The region around it sets the mood: Visit Abu Dhabi describes Al Dhafra as a place of [open desert, quiet shores and landscapes shaped by nature and tradition](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra).

## The Arabian Wildlife Park

Early morning is the time to be out. The light is soft, the air is still cool, and the animals are moving before the heat sends them into the shade.

Visit Abu Dhabi says the park is home to [more than 17,000 free-roaming animals](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/arabian-wildlife-park), and that guided tours show free-roaming Arabian oryx, gazelles and giraffes, along with hyenas and cheetahs. Guests of the island's resorts can join 4x4 nature and wildlife drives with expert staff in specially made vehicles.

One of the more memorable moments is face to face. The park has purpose-built viewing platforms, made from recycled materials, where you stand at eye level with the resident giraffes.

### What to look for on a drive

The animals are free-roaming, so no two drives are the same. Arabian oryx, with their long straight horns and pale coats, are the animal many visitors most want to see. Gazelles are smaller, quicker and easy to miss until they move. Giraffes are hard to miss at all. Predators such as cheetahs and hyenas are less predictable, so treat a sighting as a bonus rather than a promise.

A few habits make a drive better for you and for the wildlife: stay in the vehicle unless your guide says otherwise, keep voices low when animals are close, and do not ask the driver to push closer than they judge safe. Bring binoculars, and a camera with a zoom lens if you have one. If you are curious about the oryx's story across the country, our guide on [where to see the Arabian oryx](/stories/where-to-see-arabian-oryx/) covers other places to see it.

### Booking a wildlife drive as a day visitor

You do not have to stay on the island to see the park, but you do need to plan. Visit Abu Dhabi says day passes can be booked through the island's resort activities team without being a hotel guest, depending on availability, and that [day visits have to be booked a month in advance](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/arabian-wildlife-park). The day passes include the boat transfer from Jebel Dhanna. Prices are seasonal, so ask for a quote when you book. The island page also confirms there is [no general entry fee for the island](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/sir-bani-yas-island); what you pay depends on the activities, stays and excursions you book.

## The church and monastery

The island also holds something few visitors expect. Visit Abu Dhabi describes a church and monastery, [discovered in the early 1990s](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/church-and-monastery-sir-bani-yas-island), which is open to the public and is the UAE's only known ancient Christian site. A church, a monastery and courtyard houses dating to the 7th and 8th centuries CE were found there. The church was identified by its plaster crosses and its plan, which resembles ancient churches known elsewhere in the Gulf.

Artefacts found at the site, the same page says, show the people there fished, kept cattle, sheep and goats, and traded across the Gulf and into the Indian Ocean. Visit Abu Dhabi notes the ruins can be explored freely at [set daily times, 10am and 4pm](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/church-and-monastery-sir-bani-yas-island), or on a guided culture and history tour. Check the schedule on arrival, because boat timings shape what you can fit in.

These are excavated ruins rather than a restored building, so much of the interest lies in imagining the community that lived here. A guide makes a real difference.

## On and under the water

The sea around Sir Bani Yas is part of the attraction. Visit Abu Dhabi describes a watersports centre on the island offering sailing lessons, sunset cruises, diving and kayaking, with routes through [a non-fishing zone that stretches 8km from the shores](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/sports-and-recreation/sir-bani-yas-island-desert-islands-watersports-centre). The same page says the surrounding waters are home to a wide variety of marine life, much of it unique to the region, and that the centre runs dive trips and PADI certification. The island page adds coral reefs to the list and mentions horse riding over the island's dunes, so a stay can easily mix land and sea.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="sir-bani-yas-island-guide-4" alts="A pale sandy beach on Sir Bani Yas Island with calm, clear shallow water" caption="The island's beaches face calm, protected water, which suits kayaking and snorkelling."></photo-set>

Other islands nearby are off limits without permission. Visit Abu Dhabi explains that marine protected areas in the emirate are [strictly regulated, and public access, boating, diving or fishing requires official permits or authorised eco-tour arrangements](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands) through the Environment Agency Abu Dhabi. Stick to the activities run on the island.

On any boat trip, the UK government advice says to [make sure life jackets are available for all passengers](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

## Getting to Sir Bani Yas

Most visitors drive west to Jebel Dhanna and cross by boat. Visit Abu Dhabi's island page gives the route: take the E11 towards Tarif and Mirfa, exit after Al Ruwais for Sir Bani Yas, then [park at Jebel Dhanna Jetty and take the free speedboat](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/islands/sir-bani-yas-island). The same page estimates the full journey from Abu Dhabi city at around three to three and a half hours, including the boat. There is also a seaplane option and a private air taxi.

To put the drive in perspective, Visit Abu Dhabi's island guide places the Jebel Dhanna jetty [approximately 250km west of Abu Dhabi and 370km southwest of Dubai](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/guide-to-sir-bani-yas-island), and says the seaplane journey takes around forty-five minutes. The wildlife park page adds that the boat crossing itself takes 20 to 25 minutes. Without a car, the island page notes you can use Abu Dhabi's bus network to reach the ferry terminal.

If you are staying on the island, the island page says hotel guests have access to a complimentary water taxi that runs several times a day and should be booked 24 hours in advance and confirmed with the hotel. Missing the last crossing back is the classic mistake, so ask for the boat times as soon as you arrive.

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that a car is [the most convenient way to explore Al Dhafra](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra), since attractions are spread across a wide area. Start early, fill up before you leave the city, and do not try to squeeze Sir Bani Yas and Liwa into one day. Our [Liwa and Empty Quarter guide](/stories/liwa-empty-quarter-guide/) covers the desert side of the region if you are planning a longer loop.

### Driving safely in the west

The E11 west is a fast highway, and long stretches pass through open desert. The UK government advice notes that [speeding is common despite widespread speed cameras](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that it is illegal to drink and drive in any amount. Take breaks, carry water, and leave the city early so you are not driving the last stretch tired or in the dark.

## Day trip or overnight?

A day trip is possible, but it is a long day. In practice it means leaving the city before dawn, making the boat crossing, joining a wildlife drive or tour, and then driving back the same evening. You see the animals, but you see them on a timetable.

An overnight stay changes the experience. You can be out on an early drive when the light is soft and the animals are active, spend the heat of the day by the water or in the shade, and visit the church ruins at one of the set times. A second morning gives you another chance at the wildlife you missed. If you have the time, one or two nights is the more relaxed way to do it.

## Making a trip of Al Dhafra

Sir Bani Yas sits in a region that rewards a slower road trip. Visit Abu Dhabi says Al Dhafra stretches [from the Liwa Desert to the west coast and offshore islands](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra), and that driving from Abu Dhabi city to the region typically takes around two to three hours depending on where you are going. It also notes that each December the Liwa International Festival runs for three weeks of desert motorsports, concerts and fireworks.

On the way west you pass Al Mirfa, a quiet coastal town. Visit Abu Dhabi says its restored [early 20th-century fort, a former watchtower, is now a museum](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/al-mirfa), that overnight camping is allowed on Al Mirfa Beach, and that the town hosts the Al Dhafra Water Sports Festival in February. It makes a good break on the drive.

Al Dhannah, the town around the jetty, has a public beach popular with families. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that ferries to Dalma Island also leave from [the nearby harbour, and booking in advance is highly recommended](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/al-dhannah), especially with a vehicle. The same page suggests an overnight stay if you want to explore the local beaches and take the Dalma ferry.

## When to go

Visit Abu Dhabi says the best time to visit Sir Bani Yas Island is [during the winter season of November to March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/wildlife/arabian-wildlife-park). Wildlife drives are far more comfortable then, and the animals are easier to spot when they are not sheltering from the heat. Visit Abu Dhabi's Al Dhafra page makes the same point for the wider region: the cooler months suit outdoor and desert activities, while the warmer months are quieter and better suited to coastal stays and indoor attractions. Our [month-by-month guide to the UAE](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) explains how the seasons shift.

Whenever you go, check the latest [UK government travel advice for the UAE](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance), which warns of possible flight cancellations and travel disruption in the region, and make sure your insurance covers your itinerary and activities.

## What to pack

- **Layers.** Early drives in winter can feel cool in an open vehicle, and the middle of the day is warm.
- **Sun protection.** A hat, sunglasses and sunscreen, even in the cooler months.
- **Modest cover-ups.** Swimwear is for the beach and pool; cover up elsewhere on the island.
- **Binoculars and a zoom lens** for the wildlife, and spare batteries, as you will be far from the shops.
- **Your booking confirmations** for the boat, the wildlife drive and any tours, ideally on paper as well as on your phone.

## Who will love it

- **Wildlife lovers and photographers** who would rather see oryx at dawn than a city skyline.
- **Couples** looking for a quiet, remote break.
- **Families with older children** who will enjoy the drives and the giraffe platforms.
- **History buffs** curious about the region's early Christian past.

## Quick answers

**How do you get to Sir Bani Yas Island?**
Most visitors drive to Jebel Dhanna Jetty and take the free speedboat across. Visit Abu Dhabi estimates around three to three and a half hours from Abu Dhabi city, including the boat.

**Can you visit Sir Bani Yas Island as a day trip?**
Yes, but day passes for the Arabian Wildlife Park are booked through the resort activities team, subject to availability, and Visit Abu Dhabi says they must be booked a month in advance.

**What animals can you see on Sir Bani Yas?**
Tours show free-roaming Arabian oryx, gazelles and giraffes, as well as hyenas and cheetahs.

**When is the best time to visit Sir Bani Yas Island?**
Visit Abu Dhabi recommends the winter season, from November to March.

**Is there an entry fee for Sir Bani Yas Island?**
There is no general entry fee. You pay for the activities, stays and excursions you book.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you secure a wildlife park day pass for our date, and how far ahead do we need to confirm it?
- What time do the boats leave Jebel Dhanna, and what happens if we miss the return crossing?
- Do the wildlife drives run in the early morning, and how long are we in the vehicle?
- Can we visit the church and monastery ruins on the same day as a wildlife drive?
- Are life jackets and a safety briefing provided on every boat and kayak trip?
- Does our travel insurance need to cover anything specific for remote island activities?
