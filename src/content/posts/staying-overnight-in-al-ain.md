At first light in Al Ain, the camel market is already loud. Trucks back up to the pens, buyers lean on the rails, and young camels complain at the tops of their voices. By the time most day-trippers have left Dubai or Abu Dhabi, the best of the trading is over.

That is the case for staying overnight in Al Ain in one scene. The Garden City is often squeezed into a single day trip, and it works as one. But a night here changes the rhythm completely: you get the cool, quiet hours at both ends of the day, which is exactly when Al Ain is at its best.

So is it worth it? Yes, if you want more than the highlights. Visit Abu Dhabi says you can see the main sites in a day, but that to go deeper, or add more places, you should stay one night. Base yourself in the city if you want easy access to the oasis and forts, or near Jebel Hafit for mountain views, sunrise and the option of camping.

## Why staying overnight in Al Ain is worth it

Al Ain sits inland, against the Hajar Mountains, and its draw is history and greenery rather than beaches and towers. Visit Abu Dhabi says the city is [around 90 minutes' drive from Abu Dhabi city](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and that drives from Abu Dhabi or Dubai take approximately 1.5 to 2 hours. Doing that there and back in one day, with sightseeing in between, makes for a long day.

Its own Al Ain itinerary is clear on the trade-off. A day is possible if you are there early, but [to go deeper or add Qasr Al Muwaiji, stay one night](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). Staying also means you are not driving back on the highway after dark at the end of a busy day.

If you only have one day, our [Al Ain day trip](/stories/al-ain-day-trip/) guide shows how to fit in the essentials.

## Evening: the mountain at sunset

Arrive in the afternoon, check in, and head for the mountain. Visit Abu Dhabi recommends the drive up Jebel Hafit for [sunset views across the desert](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), and calls the peak Abu Dhabi's highest and the UAE's second tallest. You can reach the summit by car on [a fully paved, well-lit road](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit).

At the mountain's base, Green Mubazzarah has [natural hot springs, grass and picnic areas](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage) that fill with Emirati families at weekends. It is a good place to see local life on a cool evening.

Our [Jebel Hafit guide](/stories/jebel-hafit-guide/) covers the mountain in more detail.

## Morning: the hours day-trippers miss

This is where an overnight stay really pays off.

### The camel market at dawn

Visit Abu Dhabi says the serious trading at Al Ain Camel Market [happens before 9 AM](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), and suggests arriving around 7 to see camels being assessed and traded. It is a working market, not a show, so ask before photographing people. Unless you leave Dubai or Abu Dhabi very early, this is hard to reach on a day trip.

### The oasis before the heat

Next, walk into Al Ain Oasis, part of the city's UNESCO World Heritage Site. Visit Abu Dhabi recommends going [in the morning for cooler air and quieter paths](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage). The oasis page notes that [entry is free](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), and that you can explore on foot, by rented bicycle or by golf buggy. Look for the falaj, the ancient channels that still carry water through the palms, though the tourism board warns the water can come and go with the seasons.

### Forts and palaces

Then move on to Al Ain's mudbrick heritage. Al Jahili Fort was [built in the 1890s](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/al-jahili-fort) and is one of the largest forts in the UAE. Visit Abu Dhabi highlights its permanent exhibition of photographs by the explorer Wilfred Thesiger, and suggests pairing it with [Qasr Al Muwaiji](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/the-ancient-world-of-al-ain-abu-dhabis-unesco-heritage), a restored mudbrick palace whose exhibition covers the region and royal family life before oil.

For the full story of the UNESCO sites, see our guide to [Al Ain Oasis and the UNESCO sites](/stories/al-ain-oasis-unesco-sites/).

## Where to base yourself in Al Ain

Al Ain does not have the vast hotel choice of Dubai or Abu Dhabi, so it pays to think about what kind of night you want.

### In the city

A hotel in or near the centre puts you closest to the oasis, the forts and the camel market for an early start. It suits travellers without a car, families, and anyone who wants to walk out for dinner.

### Near Jebel Hafit

Staying towards the mountain puts you nearer to Jebel Hafit, Green Mubazzarah and the desert park, with quieter surroundings and easier sunrise and sunset drives. You will need a car or taxis to reach the heritage sites in town.

### Under canvas

For something different, camp. Visit Abu Dhabi confirms that [overnight camping at designated campsites in Al Ain is allowed](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/camping-guide-to-alain), and calls Jebel Hafit the best place to pitch your tent. Jebel Hafit Desert Park offers [three camping experiences](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/jebel-hafit): basic camping with your own gear, a serviced camp with Bedouin-style tents and breakfast, and air-conditioned glamping tents. Our guide to [overnight desert camping](/stories/overnight-desert-camping-uae/) explains what to expect from a night on the sand.

## Getting there and getting around

Visit Abu Dhabi says Al Ain is reached by road on the [E22 or E66 highways](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) from Abu Dhabi or Dubai. Once there, the city has a public bus service, but many visitors hire a car for convenience, and guided tours are another option.

If you drive, follow the local rules carefully. The UK Foreign Office says it is [illegal to drink and drive, no matter how small the amount](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and warns that driving standards are not always as disciplined as in the UK. At check-in, the same advice notes it is normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport.

## When to go

Visit Abu Dhabi calls Al Ain a year-round destination, but says the cooler days [from October to March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) are best for outdoor adventures. In the warmer months, shade under the palms, early starts and museums help. Its itinerary adds that in summer you should drive up Jebel Hafit early.

## Quick answers

**Is it worth staying overnight in Al Ain?** Yes, if you want more than the highlights. Visit Abu Dhabi says a day trip can cover the main sites, but to go deeper or add Qasr Al Muwaiji you should stay one night.

**How far is Al Ain from Abu Dhabi and Dubai?** Visit Abu Dhabi says the drive from either city takes approximately 1.5 to 2 hours.

**Can you camp overnight in Al Ain?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says overnight camping is allowed at designated campsites, including near Jebel Hafit.

**When is the best time to visit Al Ain?** Visit Abu Dhabi recommends October to March, when the cooler days suit outdoor activities.

**Is Al Ain Oasis free to visit?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says entry is free, with bicycles and golf buggies available at extra cost.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Should we stay in the city or near Jebel Hafit, given the sites we want to see?
- Can you arrange an early start for the camel market and the oasis on our second morning?
- If we camp near Jebel Hafit, what is provided, and how cold does it get at night in the month we are travelling?
- Can you include Qasr Al Muwaiji and Al Jahili Fort in a guided morning, and how long should we allow?
- What are the transfer times from our hotel in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, and can we be dropped at the mountain for sunset?
