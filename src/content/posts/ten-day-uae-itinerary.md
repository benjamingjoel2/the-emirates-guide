On the morning you cross from the Gulf coast to the east coast, the mountains arrive before you expect them: bare, folded rock closing in on both sides of the road, then opening again onto a different sea, with fishing boats pulled up on the sand and a small, ancient mosque sitting low against the hills.

Most visitors never see that side of the country. They fly into Dubai, perhaps add Abu Dhabi, and leave believing the UAE is a story of two skylines. Ten days is enough to tell the whole story. This ten-day UAE itinerary visits all seven emirates without turning the trip into a relay race, using five bases so that you unpack four times rather than nine.

The shape is simple: start with the capital, move to Dubai and use it to reach Sharjah, then head north through Ajman and Umm Al Quwain to Ras Al Khaimah, and finish on the Fujairah coast. It works best in the cooler months and with a hire car or driver from Day 6 onwards. New to the country? Our [first-timer's guide to the seven emirates](/stories/uae-first-timers-guide-seven-emirates/) explains how they differ.

## Before you plan ten days in the UAE

- **Check the travel advice.** The UK government says travellers in the region should be [prepared for possible flight cancellations, airspace closures and travel disruptions](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance). Read it before you book and again before you fly.
- **Check your entry rules.** For British citizens, the UK government says a visitor visa is [issued free on arrival, and your passport must be valid for at least 6 months after the date you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). Check the rules for your own passport.
- **Know that rules differ by emirate.** The clearest example: the UK government says [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and it is illegal to drink or be under the influence in a public place anywhere in the UAE.
- **Get insurance that covers the whole route.** The UK government advises [insurance for local treatment or unexpected medical evacuation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) and notes that some medicines need approval from the UAE Ministry of Health before you travel. For an ambulance, dial 998.

## The five bases, and why they work

Ten days across seven emirates sounds like a lot of packing. In practice the route uses five bases: Abu Dhabi for three nights, Dubai for three, Ras Al Khaimah for two, and the east coast for the last two, with the final night wherever suits your flight. Sharjah is a day trip from Dubai, and Ajman and Umm Al Quwain are stops on the drive north.

That arrangement keeps the long drives to four days out of ten, and each one ends somewhere you can rest. It also means the busiest cities come first, while you are fresh, and the quieter coast comes last, when you are ready to slow down. If you need to shorten the trip, drop Al Ain on Day 3 or merge the two east coast days into one long day.

## Days 1 to 3: Abu Dhabi and the oasis

### Day 1: Arrive and walk the Corniche

Land, check in and keep the first day light. In the evening, walk part of the Corniche as the heat leaves the day.

### Day 2: The Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Hosn

Spend the morning at the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, which asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). On a Friday, it closes to visitors between 11:30 AM and 2:30 PM.

Dress for it before you leave the hotel. The mosque's [visitor rules](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details) ask women for loose clothing to the ankles, sleeves to the wrists and full hair coverage, and ask men to avoid sleeveless, tight or transparent clothing. They also ask visitors to keep voices low and phones on silent, to take photographs only at the designated photo stops, and to bring no food or drink apart from water.

In the late afternoon, visit Qasr Al Hosn, [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit), which opens only from 2:00 PM on Fridays. The same page says general admission to the House of Artisans and the landscaped grounds is complimentary, with tickets needed for the fort itself.

### Day 3: Al Ain Oasis

Drive inland to Al Ain, which Visit Abu Dhabi puts [a 90-minute drive from the capital](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis). The oasis is free to enter, and its falaj channels still carry water to the palms. Return to Abu Dhabi for the night.

The same tourism page describes an oasis of about 1,200 hectares with some 147,000 date palms, watered by two central falaj systems called Al Aini and Dawood. It warns that the water can disappear and reappear with the season, so do not be disappointed by a dry channel. You can walk, rent a bicycle or take a golf buggy, and the free Eco-Centre explains how the farming works. The oasis is part of a wider UNESCO listing that includes the 5,000-year-old [Jebel Hafit tombs and the Hili Archaeological Site](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/unesco-sites), which Visit Abu Dhabi says holds the earliest known evidence of an agricultural village in the country.

## Days 4 to 6: Dubai, with a day in Sharjah

### Day 4: To Dubai, and the old creek

Transfer to Dubai in the morning. In the late afternoon, cross the creek by abra to Deira, [a neighbourhood teeming with character, centuries-old history and spectacular souks](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/explore-dubai/dubai-neighbourhoods/deira), and lose an hour among the spice sacks.

### Day 5: The desert

Rest in the morning, then head out for sunset on the dunes. The Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists [wildlife drives, nature walks and dune dinners](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) among its activities, a calmer option than the busiest camps. The reserve describes itself as the UAE's first national park, and its approved tours use guides it has certified to explain the flora, fauna, geology and culture of the dunes.

### Day 6: Sharjah, the cultural neighbour

Sharjah sits right next to Dubai, but it feels very different. Start in the Heart of Sharjah heritage district at the Sharjah Heritage Museum, set in [a restored 18th-century pearl merchant's house](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/heritage/sharjah-heritage-museum/) with galleries on Emirati life, celebrations and oral traditions. Visit Sharjah says there are six galleries in all, with labels in English and Arabic, and points out an aftakh, a golden toe ring once worn by Bedouin women. Children tend to like the folk tales, proverbs and puzzles. The museum sits opposite Souk Al Arsa, so you can browse the old covered market straight afterwards.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="ten-day-uae-itinerary-2" alts="ALT2" caption="CAP2"></photo-set>

Then walk to the Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization on the Corniche, where Visit Sharjah singles out [the gold-embroidered curtain for the door of the Holy Ka'ba and the zodiac mosaic inside the central dome](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/culture/sharjah-museum-of-islamic-civilization/). Its seven galleries are spread over two levels: the ground floor covers the Islamic faith and the work of Arab scientists and astronomers, while the upper floors trace Islamic arts and crafts in order.

Sharjah's government works a different week: its official weekend is [Friday, Saturday and Sunday](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/jobs/Sector-of-employment/working-in-uae-government-sector/working-hours-in-the-public-sector). Dress conservatively and remember the alcohol law. Our guide to [visiting Sharjah's rules](/stories/sharjah-rules-for-visitors/) explains what that means in practice.

## Driving the northern and eastern days

From Day 7 the trip changes character. The big-city taxis are behind you and the sights are spread out, so you need either a hire car or a driver. Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board says [a car is the easiest way to get around](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) and recommends a four-wheel drive for mountain and desert areas.

If you drive, read the rules first. The UK government says British visitors can use a UK photocard licence, that driving standards [are not always as disciplined as in the UK](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that it is illegal to drink and drive no matter how small the amount. It also explains that the rules after an accident differ between Abu Dhabi, Dubai and the other emirates, which matters on a route that crosses all of them. In every case, call the police and do not leave the scene before they arrive.

Keep the driving days short and finish in daylight. The mountain roads in Ras Al Khaimah and across to the east coast are well built, but they are slower than the map suggests, and you will want to stop.

## Days 7 and 8: Ajman, Umm Al Quwain and Ras Al Khaimah

### Day 7: Two small emirates on the way north

Check out of Dubai and drive north, stopping in Ajman and Umm Al Quwain. Both are small and low-key. In Ajman, the museum inside the old fort and an early walk on the corniche are enough for a short stop. In Umm Al Quwain, the quiet old town, its fort and the lagoon and mangrove shoreline show a slower side of the coast. Neither needs a full day, which is why they work well as stops on the drive.

Arrive in Ras Al Khaimah for the late afternoon and visit Al Jazeera Al Hamra, which the tourism board calls [the only remaining historical pearling village in the entire Gulf region](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-jazeera-al-hamra/). It is unmanned, so do not climb on the buildings, and leave before dark.

### Day 8: Jebel Jais and Dhayah Fort

Climb Dhayah Fort in the morning, [the only hill fort remaining in the UAE](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/dhayah-fort/), by a rocky, unlit path that is best done in daylight. Then drive up Jebel Jais, [the highest mountain in the UAE](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/), where the tourism board says it is usually 10°C cooler than other parts of the emirate. Stay for sunset.

Bring a warm layer. The tourism board describes Ras Al Khaimah's winters, from November to March, as [mild at about 15°C to 28°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), with occasional brief showers mainly in the mountains, and the ridge feels colder again once the sun has gone.

## Days 9 and 10: Fujairah and the east coast

### Day 9: Across the mountains to the other sea

Drive across to the east coast. The road crosses the Hajar Mountains, which the Fujairah tourism authority describes as [the highest mountain range in the eastern Arabian Peninsula](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-NaturalSites.html), running through both the UAE and Oman. The same page notes that Wadi Ham, the largest and longest wadi in the UAE, stays dry for most of the year but turns into a rushing stream after rain, so never drive into a flooded wadi.

Stop at Al Bidya Mosque, which the Fujairah tourism authority says lies [35 km north of the city of Fujairah on the road to Dibba](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-PlacesOfWorship.html) and describes as the oldest mosque in the UAE, built of local stone and burnt clay. Look up when you are inside: no timber was used for the roof, and a single central column carries its four domes. Dress modestly, remove your shoes and keep your voice down, as it is still a place of worship.

<photo-set layout="pair" keys="ten-day-uae-itinerary-3,ten-day-uae-itinerary-4" alts="ALT3|ALT4" caption="CAP3"></photo-set>

Then continue south to Khorfakkan, a part of Sharjah on the east coast. Its beach is [a crescent-shaped stretch](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/beaches/khorfakkan-beach/) with options for swimming, kayaking and snorkelling near Shark Island. Visit Sharjah says the beach runs for 3 kilometres and that Khorfakkan lies about halfway between Dibba and Fujairah. In the evening, the amphitheatre and its man-made waterfall, [often illuminated at night](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/culture/khorfakkan-amphitheater-and-waterfall/), face the bay. The waterfall is 45 metres high and was carved out of natural rock. Swim only from approved beaches with lifeguards, and watch for red flags, which the UK government says [mean swimming is dangerous](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

### Day 10: Fujairah Fort, then back across

On your last morning, visit Fujairah Fort, which stands [on a high hill in the old Fujairah area](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-MuseumAndForts.html) and was built from local stone, gravel and clay. Its plan is unusual: three round towers and one square tower, linked by walls around a central hall, shaped by the uneven rock it sits on.

If you have time, go a little further south to the Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre, set in what Visit Sharjah calls [the oldest mangrove forest in the Emirates](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/nature-and-wildlife/khor-kalba-mangrove-centre/). The same page mentions mangrove trees up to 8 metres tall and birds such as the Arabian Collared Kingfisher. Then drive back west for your flight, leaving plenty of time.

## Families, accessibility and pace

- **Children:** The Sharjah Heritage Museum's folk tales and puzzles, the beach at Khorfakkan and the shaded paths of Al Ain Oasis suit younger travellers. Dhayah Fort and Al Jazeera Al Hamra are rough underfoot and unlit, so keep them to daylight and hold small hands.
- **Limited mobility:** Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board says the climb to Dhayah Fort [is not accessible to prams, wheelchairs and other mobility aids](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/dhayah-fort/). The oasis offers golf buggies, and the big Abu Dhabi and Sharjah museums are the easier stops.
- **Heat:** Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board says summer temperatures [can exceed 45°C, especially inland](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), which is why this route is built for the cooler months.

## Making ten days feel unhurried

- **Swap Day 3 for a rest day** if the first days feel full. Al Ain can be dropped without breaking the route.
- **Keep Friday mornings flexible.** Mosques and some sights pause around midday prayers.
- **Watch the season.** Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board recommends [October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) for outdoor activities. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) covers the rest of the year.
- **Pack for every stop.** The UK government advises [dressing modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), with women covering shoulders and knees, and wearing swimwear only at beaches and pools. Sharjah, the mosques and the east coast villages all reward a conservative wardrobe.

## Quick answers

**Can you visit all seven emirates in ten days?** Yes, if you use a few bases and treat Ajman and Umm Al Quwain as stops on the drive north rather than full days.

**Do I need a car?** Not for Abu Dhabi and Dubai, but a hire car or driver makes the northern and eastern days far easier.

**Can I drink alcohol in Sharjah?** No. The UK government says [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

**What is the oldest mosque in the UAE?** The Fujairah tourism authority describes [Al Bidya Mosque](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-PlacesOfWorship.html) as the oldest mosque in the UAE.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Would you base us anywhere differently to cut down on driving across the ten days?
- Can you arrange a driver from Dubai northwards, or is a hire car better for our group?
- Which of our days fall on a Friday, and how would that change the Grand Mosque, Qasr Al Hosn and Sharjah days?
- Is the Jebel Jais road open on our dates, and how long should we allow to cross from Ras Al Khaimah to the east coast?
- Which east coast beaches have lifeguards, and where is the snorkelling calmest in our month?
- If the weather turns, which indoor sights would you swap in for each emirate?
