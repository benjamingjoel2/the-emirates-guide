By the third evening, the city has a shape in your head. You know where the old fort stands among the towers, how the mosque's marble shifts colour as the sun goes down, and how daylight falls through the Louvre's dome. Three days is enough to understand Abu Dhabi, as long as you do not try to see it all at once.

This three days in Abu Dhabi itinerary is built for a first visit. Day one is the old city and the Corniche, day two is the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and the presidential palace, and day three is Saadiyat Island's museums and beach. Each day keeps the outdoor parts for the cooler hours and the indoor sights for the middle of the day.

Two dates matter more than any others: the Grand Mosque closes to visitors around Friday midday prayers, and Louvre Abu Dhabi is closed on Mondays. Arrange your days around those, and everything else falls into place. Before you go, check the latest [UK government travel advice for the UAE](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance), which warns of possible flight cancellations and airspace closures in the region.

## Before you start: getting around

Abu Dhabi is spread across islands, so you will use taxis on most days. There is also a free option: the Experience Abu Dhabi Shuttle Bus runs [eight routes connecting Yas Island, Jubail Island, Saadiyat Island, the city centre and the Grand Canal area](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/getting-around-abu-dhabi/shuttle-bus). Base yourself centrally or on Saadiyat and none of the days below needs a hire car. Our guide to [where to stay in Abu Dhabi](/stories/where-to-stay-in-abu-dhabi/) compares the areas.

When you do take a cab, the UK advice notes that [Abu Dhabi's licensed taxis are painted silver/grey](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and that apps such as Careem, Uber and Abu Dhabi Taxi count as recognised transport. If you plan to walk between nearby sights, cross only at designated pedestrian crossings, which the same advice says is the law.

### How to pace the three days

The pattern for each day is the same: outdoors early, indoors in the middle, outdoors again late. Even in winter, the open spaces around the mosque, the Corniche and the Saadiyat waterfront offer little shade at midday. Build in a proper break in the afternoon, ideally back at your hotel, and you will enjoy the evenings far more. Visit Abu Dhabi names [November to May as the best time to visit the UAE](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/best-time-to-travel) and June to August as the hottest months, when the outdoor parts of each day shrink to the first and last hours of light.

## Day 1: the old city and the Corniche

### Morning: the port and the fort

Start where Abu Dhabi started, by the sea. At Mina Zayed, Visit Abu Dhabi describes [a historic dhow harbour where wooden boats still line the water](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/mina-zayed), and markets selling fresh seafood and dates. Come early, before the heat builds. The tourism board says the fish market is still open alongside the fruit and vegetable, plant and meat markets, and that Zayed Port Heritage Village recreates Emirati life in the 1960s.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="three-days-in-abu-dhabi-1" alts="The low white walls and square tower of Qasr Al Hosn surrounded by gardens and the glass towers of central Abu Dhabi" caption="Qasr Al Hosn, the fort where Abu Dhabi's story begins, now ringed by towers."></photo-set>

Then head to Qasr Al Hosn, which calls itself [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit). Its official page lists hours of 9am to 8pm from Saturday to Thursday and 2pm to 8pm on Friday, so on a Friday swap the morning around. Admission to the House of Artisans and the grounds is complimentary, while the fort itself is ticketed. Allow a couple of hours for the fort, its galleries and the craft workshops next door, and note that the site says tickets are non-refundable.

### Afternoon: rest, then the beach

Go back to your hotel for the hottest part of the day, or stay indoors in a museum. When the light softens, walk down to the Corniche. Visit Abu Dhabi calls it [one of the city's most walkable and accessible areas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/abu-dhabi-corniche), with broad promenades, cycling paths and a public beach. Visit Abu Dhabi's list of free things to do describes Corniche Beach as [two kilometres of white sand](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/things-to-do-for-free-in-abu-dhabi) with a special family zone. A swim and a slow walk west is the ideal reset after a morning of history. Our [Abu Dhabi Corniche guide](/stories/abu-dhabi-corniche-guide/) explains how the beach sections work.

### Evening: Heritage Village and the Founder's Memorial

At the western end, on the Corniche Breakwater, Heritage Village [recreates life in the UAE before oil was discovered](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/heritage-village), with craft demonstrations and a small museum about pearl diving. Entrance is free. Finish at the Founder's Memorial, which Visit Abu Dhabi lists as [open seven days a week from 9am to 10pm](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/the-founders-memorial). Its centrepiece, The Constellation, is a three-dimensional portrait of Sheikh Zayed, and it is at its most striking after dark. The tourism board says individuals and small groups do not need to book.

## Day 2: the Grand Mosque and the palace

### Morning: Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

<photo-set layout="full" keys="three-days-in-abu-dhabi-2" alts="The silhouette of the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque domes and minarets against an orange sunset sky, seen across water" caption="The Grand Mosque at sunset, worth seeing again from a distance at the end of day two."></photo-set>

The Grand Mosque is the visit most people remember, and it rewards a little preparation. Visit Abu Dhabi says it can [house up to 55,000 worshippers and visitors](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/abu-dhabi-city) and welcomes people of all beliefs, and its free list notes that entry and the [cultural tours led by Emirati guides are free of charge](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/things-to-do-for-free-in-abu-dhabi). The mosque's official page asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). It lists visiting hours of 9am to 8:30pm every day except Friday, when visits run from 9am to 11:30am and again from 2:30pm to 8:30pm. Hours change during Ramadan, so check the same page if your trip falls then.

Dress is strict. The mosque's rules say women's clothing should be [loose-fitting to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists, and full hair coverage](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details), avoiding anything transparent or tight. Men should avoid transparent, tight or sleeveless shirts, and clothing covering the legs is preferred. Photography is only allowed at designated photo stops. Our [Grand Mosque visitor guide](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/) covers booking and etiquette in detail.

A practical tip: pack your mosque clothes rather than wearing a thin layer you might be asked to change. Comfortable shoes that slip on and off easily help too, and a guided tour is the best way to understand what you are looking at.

### Afternoon: Qasr Al Watan

After lunch, move on to Qasr Al Watan, the presidential palace. Check the dress code before you go: the palace asks visitors to [wear clothes that cover both knees and shoulders](https://www.qasralwatan.ae/en/plan-your-visit/opening-hours), and says shorts, sleeveless tops, see-through clothing and flip-flops are not allowed. Expect airport-style security. The same page says there are no lockers and that bags above a set size will be refused, so leave luggage at your hotel. The palace specifies that [bags larger than 500 by 350 millimetres will not be allowed through security](https://www.qasralwatan.ae/en/plan-your-visit/opening-hours), and suggests that anyone visiting on the way to or from the airport uses luggage storage at the airport or their hotel. A free shuttle runs between the visitor centre and the palace entrance, parking at the visitor centre is free, and there is no Wi-Fi on the palace grounds, so download anything you need beforehand.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="three-days-in-abu-dhabi-3" alts="The white facade and central dome of Qasr Al Watan, the presidential palace in Abu Dhabi, behind landscaped gardens" caption="Qasr Al Watan is a working palace, so expect security checks and a strict dress code."></photo-set>

The palace also runs an evening light show, Palace In Motion, and the same page notes last entry is 30 minutes before it starts. If you would rather stay for the show than go to Wahat Al Karama, check the schedule when you book.

### Evening: the Honour Guard at sunset

Back near the mosque, Wahat Al Karama is a memorial to the country's martyrs. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests you [arrive just before sunset to watch the daily Honour Guard march](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/things-to-do-for-free-in-abu-dhabi). It is a quiet, moving way to end the day, and the mosque glows across the road as night falls.

On a Friday, flip the order. The mosque closes to visitors around midday prayers, so either go in the early morning session or move the mosque to the late afternoon and do Qasr Al Watan in the morning instead.

## Day 3: Saadiyat's museums and a mangrove evening

### Morning: Louvre Abu Dhabi

Spend the morning under the dome. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that Louvre Abu Dhabi is [generally open from 10am and closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that most visitors spend two to three hours there. Aim to arrive near opening so the walk in is done before the midday heat. Children under 18 go free, according to the same page. Our [Louvre Abu Dhabi guide](/stories/louvre-abu-dhabi-guide/) helps you plan the route.

Look up as well as around. The tourism board describes the dome as [180 metres wide and made up of 7,850 stars in eight layers](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), casting the shifting patterns of light that architect Jean Nouvel called the "rain of light". The dome area stays open later than the galleries, and the same page says the museum has step-free access throughout, with wheelchairs available on free loan. A Louvre ticket also covers the children's museum, which is useful if you are travelling with younger visitors.

### Afternoon: more museums, or the beach

Saadiyat Cultural District is compact. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [compact enough to explore mostly on foot](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district), and suggests allowing one to two hours per attraction. Choose one more museum, or swap culture for a swim on Saadiyat's beach.

### Evening: the Eastern Mangroves

End among the mangroves. Visit Abu Dhabi notes you can [walk the public boardwalk at Mangrove National Park for free](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/eastern-mangroves), and that you may spot flamingos and herons. Guided kayak tours are also available if you want to get on the water. The board notes the mangroves are natural and managed by the Environment Agency Abu Dhabi, that the promenade has cafes and a children's playground nearby, and that the drive from downtown takes less than 15 minutes. If you paddle, book a guided tour, wear a hat even in the evening and ask about the tide, which changes how much of the channels you can reach.

## What to book before you fly

Most of this itinerary is walk-up, but a few things are worth sorting in advance:

- **Grand Mosque access pass.** The mosque asks visitors to pre-book online. Choose your slot once you know which day is day two.
- **Qasr Al Hosn tickets.** You can book online or on site, but tickets are non-refundable, so buy them once your dates are fixed.
- **Louvre Abu Dhabi.** Booking ahead is not essential on a quiet weekday, but it saves queuing in peak season and on public holidays.
- **A mangrove kayak.** Guided tours have limited places, so book once you know which evening is free.
- **A driver for day two.** The mosque, the palace and Wahat Al Karama are spread out, and a driver for the day can be simpler than a string of taxis.

Keep confirmations on your phone and, where possible, a screenshot in case you lose signal.

## If you have a fourth day

Visit Abu Dhabi recommends [a minimum of 3 to 5 days](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/best-time-to-travel) for the emirate, so a fourth day is never wasted. The two most popular additions are an Al Ain day trip for oases and forts, and a day on Yas Island for the circuit, marina and beach. Yas works well even without a park ticket: its marina, beach and boardwalk are free to walk.

## Swaps and variations

- **Travelling with children?** Swap the day 3 afternoon for a few hours on Yas Island, with its waterfront promenades and theme parks. Children under 18 go free at the Louvre, according to Visit Abu Dhabi.
- **Short on energy?** Drop the Mina Zayed start on day 1 and begin at Qasr Al Hosn, and skip Wahat Al Karama on day 2.
- **Want more history?** Replace day 3 with [an Al Ain day trip](/stories/al-ain-day-trip/) to the oasis city, and save Saadiyat for another visit.
- **Visiting in Ramadan?** The UK advice says to [check opening hours of shops and restaurants](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and follow local dress codes closely. Read [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) first.

## Quick answers

**Is three days enough for Abu Dhabi?**
Three days is enough for the main sights on a first visit: the old city and Corniche, the Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Watan, and the Saadiyat museums.

**Do you need to book the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque?**
Yes. The mosque's official site asks visitors to pre-book and obtain an online access pass. On Fridays it closes to visitors between the morning and afternoon sessions.

**Which day is Louvre Abu Dhabi closed?**
Louvre Abu Dhabi is closed on Mondays, so do not plan your Saadiyat day for a Monday.

**What should I wear to the Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Watan?**
At the mosque, women need loose clothing to the ankles, sleeves to the wrists and a head covering. Qasr Al Watan asks all visitors to cover knees and shoulders and not to wear flip-flops.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which order of days works best for our dates, given Friday prayers at the mosque and the Louvre's Monday closure?
- Can you book our Grand Mosque access passes and confirm the time slot?
- Is a driver for one or two days better value for us than taxis, given where we are staying?
- How long should we allow between the Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Watan, including security?
- Can you arrange a guided kayak in the mangroves at the right tide for our last evening?
- Are there any public holidays or events during our stay that will change opening hours?
