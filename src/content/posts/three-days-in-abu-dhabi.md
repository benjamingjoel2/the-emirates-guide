By the third evening, the city has a shape in your head. You know where the old fort stands among the towers, how the mosque's marble shifts colour as the sun goes down, and how daylight falls through the Louvre's dome. Three days is enough to understand Abu Dhabi, as long as you do not try to see it all at once.

This three days in Abu Dhabi itinerary is built for a first visit. Day one is the old city and the Corniche, day two is the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and the presidential palace, and day three is Saadiyat Island's museums and beach. Each day keeps the outdoor parts for the cooler hours and the indoor sights for the middle of the day.

Two dates matter more than any others: the Grand Mosque closes to visitors around Friday midday prayers, and Louvre Abu Dhabi is closed on Mondays. Arrange your days around those, and everything else falls into place. Before you go, check the latest [UK government travel advice for the UAE](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance), which warns of possible flight cancellations and airspace closures in the region.

## Before you start: getting around

Abu Dhabi is spread across islands, so you will use taxis on most days. There is also a free option: the Experience Abu Dhabi Shuttle Bus runs [eight routes connecting Yas Island, Jubail Island, Saadiyat Island, the city centre and the Grand Canal area](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/getting-around-abu-dhabi/shuttle-bus). Base yourself centrally or on Saadiyat and none of the days below needs a hire car. Our guide to [where to stay in Abu Dhabi](/stories/where-to-stay-in-abu-dhabi/) compares the areas.

## Day 1: the old city and the Corniche

### Morning: the port and the fort

Start where Abu Dhabi started, by the sea. At Mina Zayed, Visit Abu Dhabi describes [a historic dhow harbour where wooden boats still line the water](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/mina-zayed), and markets selling fresh seafood and dates. Come early, before the heat builds.

Then head to Qasr Al Hosn, which calls itself [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit). Its official page lists hours of 9am to 8pm from Saturday to Thursday and 2pm to 8pm on Friday, so on a Friday swap the morning around. Admission to the House of Artisans and the grounds is complimentary, while the fort itself is ticketed.

### Afternoon: rest, then the beach

Go back to your hotel for the hottest part of the day, or stay indoors in a museum. When the light softens, walk down to the Corniche. Visit Abu Dhabi calls it [one of the city's most walkable and accessible areas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/abu-dhabi-corniche), with broad promenades, cycling paths and a public beach.

### Evening: Heritage Village and the Founder's Memorial

At the western end, on the Corniche Breakwater, Heritage Village [recreates life in the UAE before oil was discovered](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/heritage-village), with craft demonstrations and a small museum about pearl diving. Entrance is free. Finish at the Founder's Memorial, which Visit Abu Dhabi lists as [open seven days a week from 9am to 10pm](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/heritage/the-founders-memorial).

## Day 2: the Grand Mosque and the palace

### Morning: Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

The Grand Mosque is the visit most people remember, and it rewards a little preparation. The mosque's official page asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). It lists visiting hours of 9am to 8:30pm every day except Friday, when visits run from 9am to 11:30am and again from 2:30pm to 8:30pm. Hours change during Ramadan, so check the same page if your trip falls then.

Dress is strict. The mosque's rules say women's clothing should be [loose-fitting to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists, and full hair coverage](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details), avoiding anything transparent or tight. Men should avoid transparent, tight or sleeveless shirts, and clothing covering the legs is preferred. Photography is only allowed at designated photo stops. Our [Grand Mosque visitor guide](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/) covers booking and etiquette in detail.

### Afternoon: Qasr Al Watan

After lunch, move on to Qasr Al Watan, the presidential palace. Check the dress code before you go: the palace asks visitors to [wear clothes that cover both knees and shoulders](https://www.qasralwatan.ae/en/plan-your-visit/opening-hours), and says shorts, sleeveless tops, see-through clothing and flip-flops are not allowed. Expect airport-style security. The same page says there are no lockers and that bags above a set size will be refused, so leave luggage at your hotel.

### Evening: the Honour Guard at sunset

Back near the mosque, Wahat Al Karama is a memorial to the country's martyrs. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests you [arrive just before sunset to watch the daily Honour Guard march](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/things-to-do-for-free-in-abu-dhabi). It is a quiet, moving way to end the day, and the mosque glows across the road as night falls.

## Day 3: Saadiyat's museums and a mangrove evening

### Morning: Louvre Abu Dhabi

Spend the morning under the dome. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that Louvre Abu Dhabi is [generally open from 10am and closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that most visitors spend two to three hours there. Aim to arrive near opening so the walk in is done before the midday heat. Children under 18 go free, according to the same page. Our [Louvre Abu Dhabi guide](/stories/louvre-abu-dhabi-guide/) helps you plan the route.

### Afternoon: more museums, or the beach

Saadiyat Cultural District is compact. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [compact enough to explore mostly on foot](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district), and suggests allowing one to two hours per attraction. Choose one more museum, or swap culture for a swim on Saadiyat's beach.

### Evening: the Eastern Mangroves

End among the mangroves. Visit Abu Dhabi notes you can [walk the public boardwalk at Mangrove National Park for free](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marinas-waterfronts/eastern-mangroves), and that you may spot flamingos and herons. Guided kayak tours are also available if you want to get on the water.

## Swaps and variations

- **Travelling with children?** Swap the day 3 afternoon for a few hours on Yas Island, with its waterfront promenades and theme parks.
- **Want more history?** Replace day 3 with [an Al Ain day trip](/stories/al-ain-day-trip/) to the oasis city, and save Saadiyat for another visit.
- **Visiting in Ramadan?** The UK advice says to [check opening hours of shops and restaurants](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and follow local dress codes closely. Read [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) first.

## Quick answers

**Is three days enough for Abu Dhabi?**
Three days is enough for the main sights on a first visit: the old city and Corniche, the Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Watan, and the Saadiyat museums.

**Do you need to book the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque?**
Yes. The mosque's official site asks visitors to pre-book and obtain an online access pass. On Fridays it closes to visitors between the morning and afternoon sessions.

**Which day is Louvre Abu Dhabi closed?**
Louvre Abu Dhabi is closed on Mondays, so do not plan your Saadiyat day for a Monday.

**What should I wear to the Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Watan?**
At the mosque, women need loose clothing to the ankles, sleeves to the wrists and a head covering. Qasr Al Watan asks all visitors to cover knees and shoulders and not to wear flip-flops.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which order of days works best for our dates, given Friday prayers at the mosque and the Louvre's Monday closure?
- Can you book our Grand Mosque access passes and confirm the time slot?
- Is a driver for one or two days better value for us than taxis, given where we are staying?
- How long should we allow between the Grand Mosque and Qasr Al Watan, including security?
- Can you arrange a guided kayak in the mangroves at the right tide for our last evening?
- Are there any public holidays or events during our stay that will change opening hours?
