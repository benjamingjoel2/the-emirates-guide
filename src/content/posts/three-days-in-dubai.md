Morning on Dubai Creek: a wooden abra noses away from the bank, the boatman collects coins, and on the far side the spice sellers are already rolling up their shutters. By evening the same city has become a forest of glass towers lit up against the sky. Three days in Dubai is enough to see both.

This three days in Dubai itinerary is built for first-timers. Day one is Old Dubai: Al Fahidi, the Creek and the souks. Day two takes you through the city's story at the Dubai Frame, then into Downtown and the skyline. Day three slows down with a mosque visit, a beach and an evening by the Marina. Each day keeps the outdoor parts for the cooler hours and leaves room for rest, and we finish with swaps for heat, rain, children and the days when sights close.

## Before you start: how this three days in Dubai plan works

- **Base:** anywhere near a metro station works. Our guide to [where to stay in Dubai](/stories/where-to-stay-in-dubai/) compares the areas.
- **Transport:** get a nol card on day one and use the metro, tram and abras. See [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/).
- **Season:** in the hotter months, shrink the outdoor sections and stretch the indoor ones.
- **Dress:** pack layers that cover shoulders and knees for the mosque and souks. Our guide on [what to wear](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has the details.

### Which days go where

The order matters more than it looks. Old Dubai comes first because it is best on foot early in the morning, when you are freshest. The Dubai Frame comes second because it makes far more sense once you have walked the Creek and can pick out the places you saw. The mosque and beach day comes last because it is the gentlest, which suits a final day when you may be packing or heading to the airport later.

One fixed point to plan around is Friday. The Jumeirah Mosque visit on day three runs every day except Friday, so if your third day falls on a Friday, swap days two and three. If you arrive in the evening before day one, keep that first night simple: a walk near your hotel, an early dinner and a full night's sleep.

### What catches first-timers out

The most common mistake is trying to fit too much into each day. Distances between Old Dubai, Downtown and the Marina are longer than they look on a map, and every journey adds a little waiting, walking and queuing. Two or three main stops a day, with a proper break in between, is a pace most people enjoy. The second mistake is underestimating the sun on the walking parts of the day, even outside high summer. The third is forgetting that some sights have their own rules on dress, bags and food, so read each stop's notes below before you set out, and carry a light layer and a refillable water bottle every day.

## Day one: Old Dubai, the Creek and the souks

### Morning: the lanes of Al Fahidi

Start early in the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, where wind towers rise above quiet lanes of restored houses. The UAE government portal suggests completing a Dubai trip with [a visit to the historic Al Fahidi district, an abra ride across the creek and a bargain in the traditional souqs](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), and that is exactly what today is.

If you want context as well as atmosphere, the Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding, based in Al Fahidi, runs [cultural meals, including an Emirati breakfast with a host](https://cultures.ae/) who talks through local traditions and answers your questions. Its site describes the breakfast as lasting roughly 90 minutes, served in a courtyard with seating on floor cushions, with Arabic coffee and dates. It is a relaxed first conversation with the culture you have come to see, and the questions you ask there will make the rest of the trip richer. Book ahead through the centre's website.

After breakfast, wander without a fixed route. The lanes are short and it is hard to get lost for long, because the Creek is always close by. Look up at the wind towers, step into any open courtyard galleries, and keep your camera ready for the doorways.

### Late morning: across the water

Walk down to the Creek and take an abra. RTA's heritage abras run between [Al Fahidi and Deira Old Souq, Al Fahidi and Al Sabkha, and Al Seef and Baniyas](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), among other routes, and the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash. Sit low, keep your hands inside the boat and enjoy the view of dhows loading cargo along the bank.

### Afternoon: souks, then rest

On the Deira side, wander the spice and gold souks. Browse without pressure and bargain politely if you decide to buy. Ask before you photograph stallholders: the UK Foreign Office advises that you should [not photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

Then head back to your hotel for the hottest part of the day. The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, and in Dubai that is sound planning, not just health advice. A long lunch, a swim or a nap now means you have energy for the evening.

### Evening: Al Seef

As the light softens, stroll along the waterfront at Al Seef on the Bur Dubai side, where the Creek glows at dusk and the abras keep crossing until late. Dinner by the water is the natural end to the day.

## Day two: the Dubai Frame and Downtown

### Morning: old and new Dubai in one view

Take the metro to Al Jafiliya and walk into Zabeel Park, where the Dubai Frame stands at Gate 4. The Frame says it is [open all year round, including weekends and public holidays, from 8am to 9pm](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), though timings may vary during Ramadan and holidays. Food and drink are not allowed inside, toys and sharp tools are not allowed either, and large bags and strollers must be left at the Gate 4 counter. Its rules also ask visitors to wear respectful clothing.

Inside, the story unfolds in stages. An Old Dubai gallery tells of the city's [transformation from a fishing village to a thriving modern metropolis](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en), then the sky deck shows "Old Dubai" to the north and "New Dubai" to the south. It is the best primer for the rest of your trip: you will look out and recognise yesterday's Creek. Going in the morning has another advantage, as the park around it is pleasant for a short walk before the heat builds.


### Afternoon: Downtown and the tallest tower

Spend the afternoon in Downtown, around the Burj Khalifa, which the UAE government lists as [the tallest building in the world](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-). The same page links to booking a visit to At the Top, the tower's viewing experience. If you want to go up, book ahead and choose a time slot that suits the light you want.

A note on the Museum of the Future: at the time of writing, its official site says it is [temporarily closed](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en) as it prepares for its next chapter. Check before you plan around it.

If you are not going up the tower, the afternoon is a good time to rest indoors, then come back out as the light drops. Downtown is built for evenings.

### Evening: the fountain and the lake

Stay for the evening, when the lake below the tower comes alive with the fountain shows and the crowds gather along the waterfront. RTA also runs an electric traditional abra serving [Dubai Mall, Burj Plaza and The Palace](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), a gentle way to see the lake from the water. Eat nearby and take the metro or a taxi back.

## Day three: a mosque, the beach and the Marina

### Morning: Jumeirah Mosque

Begin with one of the most rewarding hours in the city. The Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a [guided visit to Jumeirah Mosque every day except Friday, at 10am and 2pm](https://cultures.ae/program/jumeirah-mosque-visit/), lasting 1 hour 15 minutes, with refreshments and time for questions. Its page says prior booking is not required and that registration starts 30 minutes before the programme, so arrive in good time. The centre describes the mosque as one of the few in Dubai open to non-Muslim visitors, and the session is a chance to ask the questions you may have been saving up.

Dress modestly: the UK Foreign Office advises that in public areas [women should cover shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that some venues and buildings have their own dress codes. Bringing a light scarf is sensible for women.

### Afternoon: the beach

Head to the public beaches of Jumeirah or Umm Suqeim. The Foreign Office advises [swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and checking for red flags, which mean swimming is dangerous. It also notes that swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools, so cover up when you leave the sand.

If you would rather swim after dark, Dubai Municipality has opened [night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting and lifeguards, and asks swimmers to stay in the designated zones. It says night swimming runs from sunset to sunrise.

### Evening: Dubai Marina and JBR

Finish by the water at Dubai Marina and JBR. The tram [runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram/about-tram) and links to the metro, so it is easy to reach. Walk the promenade as the towers light up, then find somewhere for a final dinner. If your legs are tired, the Dubai Water Taxi runs [hop-on, hop-off between four stops](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine) inside the Marina.

## Adjusting the plan

### In the heat

From late spring to early autumn, treat each day as two halves with a long break in between. Do the walking parts as early as you can, keep the middle of the day for indoor sights, lunch and rest, and come out again from late afternoon. The NHS also advises [wearing light, loose clothing and drinking more cold drinks](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, and lists tiredness, headache, dizziness and being very thirsty among the signs of heat exhaustion.

### With children

The plan works for families with small changes. Shorten the souk visit, keep the abra crossing, and give the Frame plenty of time, as children usually enjoy the views from the top. The Frame says [children under 12 must be accompanied by an adult](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit) at all times. On boats, RTA says children under 12 [must wear a life jacket](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine) when travelling with an adult. More ideas are in our guide to [Dubai with children](/stories/dubai-with-kids/).

### With limited mobility

The metro is the easiest way to link these days if anyone in your group uses a wheelchair: RTA lists [dedicated wheelchair spaces on all trains and lift access to all station levels](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro). The Frame offers tickets for people of determination with up to two companions. The lanes of Al Fahidi are flat but uneven in places, and stepping down into an abra is awkward, so consider the air-conditioned ferry for a view of the water instead.

### During Ramadan

If your dates fall during Ramadan, the Foreign Office advises that eating, drinking or smoking in public in the daytime [may cause offence](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and suggests checking the opening hours of shops and restaurants. The Frame notes that its timings may vary during the holy month. Evenings take on a lively, social feel, especially around sunset.

## Swaps and add-ons

- **For nature lovers:** swap a beach afternoon for Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary, where Dubai Municipality lists [winter opening from 7.30am to 5.30pm](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/plan-your-visit/) between October and March. Bring binoculars and keep quiet in the bird hides, where only 15 people are allowed at a time.
- **For the mountains:** the government portal suggests a [1.5 hour drive to Hatta](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) for its natural water pools. It works best as a fourth day rather than squeezed into these three.
- **For the desert:** add an evening desert safari on day two or three. Read [is a desert safari worth it?](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) before you book.
- **For a longer trip:** extend into Abu Dhabi or the northern emirates with our [five-day UAE itinerary](/stories/five-day-uae-itinerary/). Browse more ideas in our [itineraries](/itineraries/).

## Quick answers

**Is three days enough for Dubai?** Three days is enough for a first look: Old Dubai and the Creek, the Dubai Frame and Downtown, and a beach and Marina day. Add more time if you want a desert trip, Hatta or other emirates.

**What are the Dubai Frame opening hours?** The Dubai Frame lists opening hours of 8am to 9pm, all year round including weekends and public holidays, though timings may vary during Ramadan and holidays.

**Is the Museum of the Future open?** At the time of writing, its official site says it is temporarily closed, so check before you go.

**Can tourists visit Jumeirah Mosque?** Yes. The Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a guided visit every day except Friday, at 10am and 2pm, and prior booking is not required.

- Can you adjust this three-day plan around our arrival and departure times?

- How would you change the plan for the heat in the month we are travelling?
- Can you fit in a desert evening without making day two or three too long?
- What is the easiest way to get between each day's stops with children or older travellers?
