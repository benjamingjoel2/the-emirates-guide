Morning on Dubai Creek: a wooden abra noses away from the bank, the boatman collects coins, and on the far side the spice sellers are already rolling up their shutters. By evening the same city has become a forest of glass towers lit up against the sky. Three days in Dubai is enough to see both.

This three days in Dubai itinerary is built for first-timers. Day one is Old Dubai: Al Fahidi, the Creek and the souks. Day two takes you through the city's story at the Dubai Frame, then into Downtown and the skyline. Day three slows down with a mosque visit, a beach and an evening by the Marina. Each day keeps the outdoor parts for the cooler hours and leaves room for rest.

## Before you start: how this three days in Dubai plan works

- **Base:** anywhere near a metro station works. Our guide to [where to stay in Dubai](/stories/where-to-stay-in-dubai/) compares the areas.
- **Transport:** get a nol card on day one and use the metro, tram and abras. See [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/).
- **Season:** in the hotter months, shrink the outdoor sections and stretch the indoor ones.
- **Dress:** pack layers that cover shoulders and knees for the mosque and souks. Our guide on [what to wear](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has the details.

## Day one: Old Dubai, the Creek and the souks

### Morning: the lanes of Al Fahidi

Start early in the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, where wind towers rise above quiet lanes of restored houses. The UAE government portal suggests completing a Dubai trip with [a visit to the historic Al Fahidi district, an abra ride across the creek and a bargain in the traditional souqs](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), and that is exactly what today is.

If you want context as well as atmosphere, the Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding, based in Al Fahidi, runs [cultural meals, including an Emirati breakfast with a host](https://cultures.ae/) who talks through local traditions and answers your questions. It is a relaxed first conversation with the culture you have come to see.

### Late morning: across the water

Walk down to the Creek and take an abra. RTA's heritage abras run between [Al Fahidi and Deira Old Souq, Al Fahidi and Al Sabkha, and Al Seef and Baniyas](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), among other routes, and the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash. Sit low, keep your hands inside the boat and enjoy the view of dhows loading cargo along the bank.

### Afternoon: souks, then rest

On the Deira side, wander the spice and gold souks. Browse without pressure, ask before you photograph stallholders, and bargain politely if you decide to buy. Then head back to your hotel for the hottest part of the day. The NHS advises [avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) in hot weather, and in Dubai that is sound planning, not just health advice.

### Evening: Al Seef

As the light softens, stroll along the waterfront at Al Seef on the Bur Dubai side, where the Creek glows at dusk and the abras keep crossing until late.

## Day two: the Dubai Frame and Downtown

### Morning: old and new Dubai in one view

Take the metro to Al Jafiliya and walk into Zabeel Park, where the Dubai Frame stands at Gate 4. The Frame says it is [open all year round, including weekends and public holidays, from 8am to 9pm](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), though timings may vary during Ramadan and holidays. Food and drink are not allowed inside, and large bags and strollers must be left at the Gate 4 counter.

Inside, the story unfolds in stages. An Old Dubai gallery tells of the city's [transformation from a fishing village to a thriving modern metropolis](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en), then the sky deck shows "Old Dubai" to the north and "New Dubai" to the south. It is the best primer for the rest of your trip: you will look out and recognise yesterday's Creek.

### Afternoon: Downtown and the tallest tower

Spend the afternoon in Downtown, around the Burj Khalifa, which the UAE government lists as [the tallest building in the world](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-). The same page links to booking a visit to At the Top, the tower's viewing experience. If you want to go up, book ahead and choose a time slot that suits the light you want.

A note on the Museum of the Future: at the time of writing, its official site says it is [temporarily closed](https://museumofthefuture.ae/en) as it prepares for its next chapter. Check before you plan around it.

### Evening: the fountain and the lake

Stay for the evening, when the lake below the tower comes alive with the fountain shows and the crowds gather along the waterfront. Eat nearby and take the metro or a taxi back.

## Day three: a mosque, the beach and the Marina

### Morning: Jumeirah Mosque

Begin with one of the most rewarding hours in the city. The Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a [guided visit to Jumeirah Mosque every day except Friday, at 10am and 2pm](https://cultures.ae/program/jumeirah-mosque-visit/), lasting 1 hour 15 minutes, with refreshments and time for questions. Its page says registration starts 30 minutes before the programme. Dress modestly: the UK Foreign Office advises that in public areas [women should cover shoulders and knees](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that some venues and buildings have their own dress codes.

### Afternoon: the beach

Head to the public beaches of Jumeirah or Umm Suqeim. The Foreign Office advises [swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and checking for red flags, which mean swimming is dangerous. If you would rather swim after dark, Dubai Municipality has opened [night swimming beaches in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting and lifeguards, and asks swimmers to stay in the designated zones.

### Evening: Dubai Marina and JBR

Finish by the water at Dubai Marina and JBR. The tram [runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram/about-tram) and links to the metro, so it is easy to reach. Walk the promenade as the towers light up, then find somewhere for a final dinner.

## Swaps and add-ons

- **For nature lovers:** swap a beach afternoon for Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary, where Dubai Municipality lists [winter opening from 7.30am to 5.30pm](https://www.dm.gov.ae/ras-al-khor/plan-your-visit/) between October and March. Bring binoculars and keep quiet in the bird hides.
- **For the desert:** add an evening desert safari on day two or three. Read [is a desert safari worth it?](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/) before you book.
- **For a longer trip:** extend into Abu Dhabi or the northern emirates with our [five-day UAE itinerary](/stories/five-day-uae-itinerary/). Browse more ideas in our [itineraries](/itineraries/).

## Quick answers

**Is three days enough for Dubai?** Three days is enough for a first look: Old Dubai and the Creek, the Dubai Frame and Downtown, and a beach and Marina day. Add more time if you want a desert trip or other emirates.

**What are the Dubai Frame opening hours?** The Dubai Frame lists opening hours of 8am to 9pm, all year round including weekends and public holidays, though timings may vary during Ramadan and holidays.

**Is the Museum of the Future open?** At the time of writing, its official site says it is temporarily closed, so check before you go.

**Can tourists visit Jumeirah Mosque?** Yes. The Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a guided visit every day except Friday, at 10am and 2pm.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you adjust this three-day plan around our arrival and departure times?
- Would a guided walk in Al Fahidi and the souks add value for us, and how long does it take?
- Which sights should we book ahead on our dates, and are any closed or running reduced hours?
- How would you change the plan for the heat in the month we are travelling?
- Can you fit in a desert evening without making day two or three too long?
- What is the easiest way to get between each day's stops with children or older travellers?
