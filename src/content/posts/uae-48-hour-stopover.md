The doors slide open, and after hours of recycled cabin air the warmth meets you like a hand on the shoulder. It is the middle of the night, or the middle of the afternoon, your body has no idea which, and you have two days before the next flight.

A 48-hour UAE stopover is one of the easiest ways to see the country, and one of the easiest to waste. The trick is to accept that you will see one city, not two, and to plan around jet lag rather than against it. Pick Dubai if you want the creek, the souks and a night in the desert. Pick Abu Dhabi if you want the Grand Mosque, the Louvre and a calmer pace. Both work. Trying to do both in two days usually means spending your stopover in a car.

Before any of that, though, there is one question to answer at home: can you actually leave the airport?

## The paperwork for a 48-hour UAE stopover

### Visa on arrival, visa-free or transit visa

Whether you need anything in advance depends on your passport. For British citizens, the UK government says a visitor visa is [issued free of charge when you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), and you do not need to apply before travel. The same page says that visa is valid for up to 90 days over a 180-day period, which is far more than a stopover needs, but worth knowing if you pass through the UAE several times in a year.

If you are not eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry and want to step out of the airport, the UAE government says you need [a pre-arranged transit visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/transit-visa). It is issued for either 48 or 96 hours, is single-entry, cannot be extended, and is applied for through the UAE-based airline you are flying with, a travel agency or an airport hotel. The same page says transit visas are sponsored by UAE-based airlines and must be approved before you enter the country, so this is not something to sort out at the immigration desk. Dubai's residency authority lists the requirements as [a recent passport-style photo, a copy of a passport valid for at least six months, and a ticket confirming onward travel](https://gdrfad.gov.ae/en/services/ee043e3c-5c61-11ea-0320-0050569629e8).

Flying through Abu Dhabi? Visit Abu Dhabi says that if you are flying with Etihad Airways, [a transit visa may be arranged for you on request](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/travelling-to-abu-dhabi/getting-a-visa). It adds that GCC citizens do not need a visa, that some nationalities can get one on arrival, and that everyone else needs to apply in advance. Our [UAE tourist visa guide](/stories/uae-tourist-visa-guide/) explains how to check your own situation.

### Passport validity

For British citizens entering the UAE, the UK government says your passport must have [an expiry date at least 6 months after the date you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). If you are only transiting and not passing through immigration, the requirement is at least 3 months after the date of transit.

### Medicines

This catches people out. The UK government says some medicines need [approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), but you do not need to complete that process if you are only changing planes and not passing through immigration. Leave the airport, and the rules apply in full. The UK government's health page adds that [applications are processed within 5 working days](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health), so a stopover booked at the last minute may not leave enough time. Our guide to [bringing medicines into the UAE](/stories/bringing-medicines-into-uae/) has the details.

### Disruption and insurance

The UK government currently advises travellers in the region to be [prepared for possible flight cancellations, airspace closures and travel disruptions](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance). On a tight stopover, that matters: build in slack before your onward flight and keep your airline's app close. Check that your travel insurance covers the stopover days as well as the main trip, including local medical treatment. In an emergency, the number for an ambulance is 998.

## Choosing your city

Our guide to [choosing between Dubai and Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) goes into this properly, but for two days the choice is simple. Stay in the city your flight uses. Stopover time spent on a transfer is time you do not get back.

Whichever you choose, use licensed taxis or recognised ride-hailing apps. The UK government recommends [using only licensed taxis or other recognised public transport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It notes that Dubai's authorised taxis are cream with different coloured roofs, with pink-roofed taxis driven by women, and that Abu Dhabi's taxis are silver or grey.

## Your first three hours on the ground

What you do straight after landing shapes the whole stopover. Most hotels take a copy of your passport at check-in; the UK government says this is [normal practice](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). If your room is not ready, leave your bags, find breakfast or a shaded pool, and resist the urge to head straight out into the midday sun.

A short sleep, a shower and a proper meal are worth more than an extra sight. Then decide which of the two days carries the fixed booking, such as the mosque pass or a desert evening, and build the rest around it.

## Two days in Dubai

### Day 1: Sleep, then the creek at golden hour

Do not fight the jet lag. Sleep, swim, eat, and save the city for the late afternoon. Then head to the creek, take an abra across the water and walk into Deira, which Visit Dubai describes as [a neighbourhood teeming with character, centuries-old history and spectacular souks](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/explore-dubai/dubai-neighbourhoods/deira).

The spice souk is at its best as the light softens. Ask before photographing traders: the UK government advises [not photographing people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). In the evening, go to Downtown for the Burj Khalifa lit up above the fountain shows. Cross roads only at marked crossings, as the same advice says crossing elsewhere is illegal and that vehicles often do not stop at zebra crossings.

### Day 2: Two views of the city, then the desert

In the morning, visit the Dubai Frame. Its [official site](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en) describes an Old Dubai Gallery on the city's change from fishing village to metropolis, then a sky deck with "Old Dubai" to the north and "New Dubai" to the south. It is a quick way to make sense of the city.

If the Museum of the Future was on your list, check first: its own site currently says it is [temporarily closed](https://www.museumofthefuture.ae/) while it prepares for its next chapter.

Rest after lunch, then head out for an evening in the desert if your flight allows. The Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists [wildlife drives, falconry and dune dinners](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) among its activities, and describes itself as the UAE's first national park. If you fly that night, skip it; a desert evening runs late.

## Two days in Abu Dhabi

### Day 1: Marble at dusk

Rest through the day, then go to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque for the late afternoon light. The mosque asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). Its listed hours are 9:00 AM to 8:30 PM, with last entry at 8:00 PM, every day except Friday, when visits run 9:00 AM to 11:30 AM and 2:30 PM to 8:30 PM.

Pack for the dress code before you land. The mosque asks women to wear [loose clothing to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists and full hair coverage](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details), and men to avoid sleeveless, tight or transparent clothing. Its rules also ask visitors to keep phones on silent, to take photographs only at the designated photo stops, and to bring no food or drink other than water. If your suitcase is checked through to your final destination, pack a suitable outfit in your hand luggage.

### Day 2: The Louvre and the old fort

Spend the morning at Louvre Abu Dhabi. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [generally open from 10 AM and closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that most visitors spend two to three hours there. The same page describes the dome as 180 metres wide, made of 7,850 stars in eight layers, and says entry is free for children under 18, with step-free access throughout and wheelchairs available on loan.

If your second day is a Monday, swap in Qasr Al Hosn instead, which describes itself as [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit) and is open from 9:00 AM Saturday to Thursday. General admission to its House of Artisans and grounds is complimentary, according to the same page, with a ticket needed for the fort.

Getting between sights is easier than you might expect. Visit Abu Dhabi runs a free [Experience Abu Dhabi Shuttle Bus](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/getting-around-abu-dhabi/shuttle-bus) on eight routes linking Saadiyat Island, Yas Island, the city centre and more. The buses are air-conditioned and wheelchair accessible; you hop on at a stop and scan a QR code.

## The last day: luggage, timing and the airport

Plan the final day backwards. Decide first when you must leave for the airport, add generous slack for traffic and for the current risk of disruption, and only then fill the hours before it. If you check out in the morning, ask the hotel to hold your bags, and keep a small day bag with water, a layer and your passport.

A late flight is the best case for a slow last day. Spend the hottest hours in a museum or by the pool, eat early, and head to the airport rested. If your flight leaves in the small hours, consider keeping the room for a late check-out so you can shower before the journey.

## Stopovers in Ramadan, with children, or in summer

- **Ramadan:** The UK government says it may cause offence to [eat, drink or smoke in public in the daytime](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) during Ramadan, including in your car, and that driving can be erratic around dusk. Check opening hours before you plan the day.
- **Children:** The same advice notes that under-18s cannot stay in a hotel unless accompanied by an adult. Louvre Abu Dhabi is free for children under 18, and a pool afternoon is often the best jet lag cure for the whole family.
- **Summer:** Visit Abu Dhabi recommends [a jacket or sweater for cold air conditioning](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). In the hot months, push everything outdoors to the evening and spend the day indoors.

## Packing your hand luggage

On a short stopover, your main suitcase may be checked through to your final destination, so think about your hand luggage as the bag for the city. Visit Abu Dhabi says that while attitudes to clothing are fairly liberal, [short or tight clothing will attract attention](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), and that it is best to cover shoulders and legs in government buildings. Swimwear, including bikinis, is fine on the beach.

A practical stopover bag holds one set of loose, covering clothes that work for the Grand Mosque or the souks, a light layer for the air conditioning, swimwear, any medicines in their original packaging with the paperwork you need, and a phone charger. Keep your passport with you rather than in the hotel safe on the day you fly.

One more thing on the way out: settle every hotel bill before you leave. The UK government warns that [non-payment of bills, including hotel bills](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), can result in a prison sentence, a fine or both.

## Small things that make a stopover work

- **Leave a bag at your hotel** and travel light around the city.
- **Carry a layer.** Air conditioning is fierce, and you will need covered shoulders and knees for some sights.
- **Plan backwards from your flight.** Decide when you must leave for the airport first, then fill the time before it.
- **Keep one thing optional.** If the jet lag wins, you lose a nice extra, not the trip.

## Quick answers

**Can I leave the airport on a UAE layover?** Yes, if your passport is eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry, or if you hold a pre-arranged transit visa.

**How long is a UAE transit visa?** The UAE government says it is issued for [either 48 or 96 hours](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/transit-visa), is single-entry and cannot be extended.

**Is 48 hours enough to see Dubai and Abu Dhabi?** Not comfortably. Pick the city your flight uses and see it properly.

**Do I need to book the Grand Mosque?** Yes. The mosque asks visitors to [pre-book and get an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours).

## Questions to ask your operator

- Based on my passport and airline, do I need a transit visa, and who should arrange it?
- Can you meet me at the airport and plan the first day around my arrival time?
- How much time should I leave to get back to the airport for my onward flight?
- Can you book a Grand Mosque access pass that fits my arrival day?
- Is a desert evening realistic on the night before my flight, or should I swap it?
- Where can I safely leave my luggage if I check out before an evening flight?
