The doors slide open, and after hours of recycled cabin air the warmth meets you like a hand on the shoulder. It is the middle of the night, or the middle of the afternoon, your body has no idea which, and you have two days before the next flight.

A 48-hour UAE stopover is one of the easiest ways to see the country, and one of the easiest to waste. The trick is to accept that you will see one city, not two, and to plan around jet lag rather than against it. Pick Dubai if you want the creek, the souks and a night in the desert. Pick Abu Dhabi if you want the Grand Mosque, the Louvre and a calmer pace. Both work. Trying to do both in two days usually means spending your stopover in a car.

Before any of that, though, there is one question to answer at home: can you actually leave the airport?

## The paperwork for a 48-hour UAE stopover

### Visa on arrival, visa-free or transit visa

Whether you need anything in advance depends on your passport. For British citizens, the UK government says a visitor visa is [issued free of charge when you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), and you do not need to apply before travel.

If you are not eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry and want to step out of the airport, the UAE government says you need [a pre-arranged transit visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/transit-visa). It is issued for either 48 or 96 hours, is single-entry, cannot be extended, and is applied for through the UAE-based airline you are flying with, a travel agency or an airport hotel. Dubai's residency authority lists the requirements as [a recent passport-style photo, a copy of a passport valid for at least six months, and a ticket confirming onward travel](https://gdrfad.gov.ae/en/services/ee043e3c-5c61-11ea-0320-0050569629e8).

Flying through Abu Dhabi? Visit Abu Dhabi says that if you are flying with Etihad Airways, [a transit visa may be arranged for you on request](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/travelling-to-abu-dhabi/getting-a-visa). Our [UAE tourist visa guide](/stories/uae-tourist-visa-guide/) explains how to check your own situation.

### Passport validity

For British citizens entering the UAE, the UK government says your passport must have [an expiry date at least 6 months after the date you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). If you are only transiting and not passing through immigration, the requirement is at least 3 months after the date of transit.

### Medicines

This catches people out. The UK government says some medicines need [approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), but you do not need to complete that process if you are only changing planes and not passing through immigration. Leave the airport, and the rules apply in full. Our guide to [bringing medicines into the UAE](/stories/bringing-medicines-into-uae/) has the details.

### Disruption

The UK government currently advises travellers in the region to be [prepared for possible flight cancellations, airspace closures and travel disruptions](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance). On a tight stopover, that matters: build in slack before your onward flight and keep your airline's app close.

## Choosing your city

Our guide to [choosing between Dubai and Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) goes into this properly, but for two days the choice is simple. Stay in the city your flight uses. Stopover time spent on a transfer is time you do not get back.

Whichever you choose, use licensed taxis or recognised ride-hailing apps. The UK government recommends [using only licensed taxis or other recognised public transport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

## Two days in Dubai

### Day 1: Sleep, then the creek at golden hour

Do not fight the jet lag. Sleep, swim, eat, and save the city for the late afternoon. Then head to the creek, take an abra across the water and walk into Deira, which Visit Dubai describes as [a neighbourhood teeming with character, centuries-old history and spectacular souks](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/explore-dubai/dubai-neighbourhoods/deira). The spice souk is at its best as the light softens. In the evening, go to Downtown for the Burj Khalifa lit up above the fountain shows.

### Day 2: Two views of the city, then the desert

In the morning, visit the Dubai Frame. Its [official site](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en) describes an Old Dubai Gallery on the city's change from fishing village to metropolis, then a sky deck with "Old Dubai" to the north and "New Dubai" to the south. It is a quick way to make sense of the city.

If the Museum of the Future was on your list, check first: its own site currently says it is [temporarily closed](https://www.museumofthefuture.ae/) while it prepares for its next chapter.

Rest after lunch, then head out for an evening in the desert if your flight allows. The Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists [wildlife drives, falconry and dune dinners](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/) among its activities. If you fly that night, skip it; a desert evening runs late.

## Two days in Abu Dhabi

### Day 1: Marble at dusk

Rest through the day, then go to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque for the late afternoon light. The mosque asks visitors to [pre-book the visit and obtain an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). Its listed hours are 9:00 AM to 8:30 PM, with last entry at 8:00 PM, every day except Friday, when visits run 9:00 AM to 11:30 AM and 2:30 PM to 8:30 PM.

Pack for the dress code before you land. The mosque asks women to wear [loose clothing to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists and full hair coverage](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details), and men to avoid sleeveless, tight or transparent clothing.

### Day 2: The Louvre and the old fort

Spend the morning at Louvre Abu Dhabi. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [generally open from 10 AM and closed on Mondays](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi), and that most visitors spend two to three hours there. If your second day is a Monday, swap in Qasr Al Hosn instead, which describes itself as [Abu Dhabi's oldest and most significant building](https://www.qasralhosn.ae/en/plan-your-visit) and is open from 9:00 AM Saturday to Thursday.

Getting between sights is easier than you might expect. Visit Abu Dhabi runs a free [Experience Abu Dhabi Shuttle Bus](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/getting-around-abu-dhabi/shuttle-bus) on eight routes linking Saadiyat Island, Yas Island, the city centre and more.

## Small things that make a stopover work

- **Leave a bag at your hotel** and travel light around the city.
- **Carry a layer.** Air conditioning is fierce, and you will need covered shoulders and knees for some sights.
- **Plan backwards from your flight.** Decide when you must leave for the airport first, then fill the time before it.
- **Keep one thing optional.** If the jet lag wins, you lose a nice extra, not the trip.

## Quick answers

**Can I leave the airport on a UAE layover?** Yes, if your passport is eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry, or if you hold a pre-arranged transit visa.

**How long is a UAE transit visa?** The UAE government says it is issued for [either 48 or 96 hours](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/transit-visa), is single-entry and cannot be extended.

**Is 48 hours enough to see Dubai and Abu Dhabi?** Not comfortably. Pick the city your flight uses and see it properly.

**Do I need to book the Grand Mosque?** Yes. The mosque asks visitors to [pre-book and get an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours).

## Questions to ask your operator

- Based on my passport and airline, do I need a transit visa, and who should arrange it?
- Can you meet me at the airport and plan the first day around my arrival time?
- How much time should I leave to get back to the airport for my onward flight?
- Can you book a Grand Mosque access pass that fits my arrival day?
- Is a desert evening realistic on the night before my flight, or should I swap it?
- Where can I safely leave my luggage if I check out before an evening flight?
