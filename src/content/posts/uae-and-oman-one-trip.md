Somewhere past the last petrol station, the landscape starts to change. The flat, sandy coastal plain of the northern emirates gives way to bare, folded mountains in shades of brown and grey, the road starts to climb, and you pull up at a border post with your passport ready. Not long after, you might be on a wooden dhow sliding between cliffs that drop straight into the sea, or wandering through a mud-brick fort in a palm-shaded town.

The UAE and Oman share a long border, and for many travellers adding a few days in Oman is the most rewarding way to stretch a UAE trip. The pace is slower and the contrast with the towers of Dubai and Abu Dhabi is part of the pleasure.

It does take more planning than a day trip to another emirate. You are crossing an international border, often in a hire car, and the rules on entry, insurance and behaviour change on the other side. Here is how to do it well. One note before you start: the UK Foreign Office's advice for both countries currently includes a [warning about regional tensions](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/warnings-and-insurance), updated on 27 October 2026, and advises travellers in the region to be prepared for flight cancellations, airspace closures and travel disruption. Read the latest advice before you book and again before you go.

## Three ways to add Oman

Most UAE and Oman trips use one or more of three gateways.

### Musandam from Ras Al Khaimah

Musandam is the Omani peninsula at the very northern tip of the Arabian Peninsula, separated from the rest of Oman by UAE territory. Oman's official tourism site describes it as a place of [steep mountains and calm seas](https://experienceoman.om/destinations/musandam) looking out over the Strait of Hormuz, with four main wilayats (districts): Khasab, Bukha, Dibba and Madha. It lists mountain treks, boat tours and diving among the main activities, and says Musandam is accessible by land, sea and air.

From the UAE side, the usual approach is by road from Ras Al Khaimah, which makes Musandam an easy add-on to a stay in the northern emirates. The draw for most visitors is a day on the water among the khors (inlets), where the mountains rise straight from the sea. On land, [Khasab Fort](https://experienceoman.om/destinations/musandam/khasab-fort) overlooks the Strait of Hormuz; Experience Oman says it was built at the beginning of the 17th century, gives opening hours of 8am to 6pm daily, and suggests October to April as the best time to visit. Check hours before you go, as they can change, particularly during Ramadan.

For more on the UAE side of this corner, see our guide to [Ras Al Khaimah and the quieter emirates](/stories/beyond-dubai-quieter-emirates/) and the [Ras Al Khaimah destination page](/destinations/ras-al-khaimah/).

### Al Ain and the Buraimi side

Inland, the oasis city of Al Ain sits right against the Omani border, with the Omani town of Al Buraimi on the other side. Visit Abu Dhabi describes Al Ain as [around 90 minutes' drive from Abu Dhabi city](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain), says the drive from Abu Dhabi or Dubai takes approximately 1.5 to 2 hours, and highlights its UNESCO World Heritage Site, the palm groves of Al Ain Oasis watered by ancient falaj channels, and the road up Jebel Hafit.

Across the border, Experience Oman points to the forts of the [Al Buraimi governorate](https://experienceoman.om/destinations/al-buraimi), including Al Hillah Fort and the 19th-century Al Khandak Fort with its defensive trench, along with old falaj irrigation systems and archaeological sites. It also notes that summers here are scorching and winters temperate.

Do not assume you can simply walk or drive between the two towns. Which crossing a foreign visitor can use, and what it requires, depends on your nationality and on current rules, so check with the Omani authorities before you plan a day around it.

### Muscat

Oman's capital is the fullest taste of the country, and the natural end point of a longer trip. Experience Oman lists the [Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque, Mutrah Souq, the National Museum and the Royal Opera House](https://experienceoman.om/destinations/muscat) among its attractions, along with forts overlooking the bay and seafront promenades. You can fly between the UAE and Muscat, or drive, but it is a long road trip by any measure, so many travellers fly in one direction and drive or take a guided tour in the other.

## Visas and entry: check before you travel

Entry rules depend on your passport and they change, so confirm everything with the official sources before you book.

### The UAE side

For people travelling on a full British citizen passport, the Foreign Office says you can get a [visitor visa free of charge on arrival in the UAE](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), valid for up to 90 days over a 180-day period from your first entry. The stay can be continuous or split across several visits, as long as the total does not exceed 90 days within that window. That matters for this trip: if you leave for Oman and come back, your days in the UAE still count towards the same total. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from the date you arrive.

### The Oman side

For British citizens, the Foreign Office says you can [visit Oman for up to 14 days without a visa](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/entry-requirements). To stay longer, you need a visa; the Foreign Office recommends applying online before you travel, choosing the "unsponsored visit visa" option as a tourist, and notes that you can also get a visit visa on arrival at an airport in Oman. It also says that at Omani border control you must be able to show:

- a ticket for return or onward travel
- proof of accommodation, such as a hotel booking
- proof of travel health insurance

Your passport must have at least 6 months' validity from the date you arrive in Oman.

Visa applications are made through the [Royal Oman Police eVisa portal](https://evisa.rop.gov.om/), where you register, apply and track your application. Oman's tourism site points out that [entry procedures differ by country of origin](https://experienceoman.om/visa) and recommends getting a visa before travel for a smoother experience, with links to the Royal Oman Police's eligibility tools. If you hold another passport, start there, or contact the Omani embassy in your country.

### Medicines

Both countries are strict about medication. The UAE requires [advance approval from the Ministry of Health](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) for medicines it classes as controlled, and the Foreign Office says some UK medicines are [illegal in Oman](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/entry-requirements) and advises carrying a copy of your prescription and checking with Oman's Ministry of Health well before you travel.

## Driving across the border

A road trip is flexible, and also the part most likely to catch you out.

### Hire car insurance

Do not assume a car hired in the UAE can be taken into Oman. Ask the rental company before you book whether cross-border travel is allowed, whether the insurance is valid in Oman, and what paperwork you need to carry at the border. Get the answer in writing. The Foreign Office lists [uninsured vehicles](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/safety-and-security) among the situations that count as a serious accident in Oman, where you must stay with the vehicle and call the Royal Oman Police on 9999.

### Licences

The Foreign Office says you can use a [UK photocard driving licence](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/safety-and-security) to drive a rental car in Oman for the first 6 weeks after you arrive, and that hire companies often have stricter requirements, such as a higher minimum age, a year of driving experience or an international driving permit. In the UAE, it also says a [UK photocard licence can be used](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), though older paper licences may need updating or an international driving permit.

### On the road in Oman

The Foreign Office warns that [driving can be dangerous outside Muscat](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/safety-and-security), with a risk of hitting wandering camels and goats, and that wadis are prone to flash floods after heavy rain from October to March. It advises checking the forecast before visiting a wadi and avoiding them where heavy rain is expected and for at least a day afterwards. All passengers must wear seatbelts, and mobile phone use while driving is illegal. For desert and mountain trips it recommends a well-equipped 4-wheel-drive vehicle, travelling in convoy if possible, carrying water and a phone, and making sure you are insured for that kind of travel.

## What changes when you cross

Some rules differ, and the Foreign Office lists several that visitors should know about in Oman:

- **Dress.** Dress modestly in public, including in shopping malls, with [clothing that covers the tops of your arms and your legs to below the knee](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/oman/safety-and-security). Swimwear is for tourist beaches and pools only.
- **Alcohol.** Visitors can drink in licensed venues, but it is illegal to drink in public, be drunk in public or drink-drive.
- **Vapes.** It is illegal to import or use e-cigarettes or vape products in Oman. Leave them behind before you cross.
- **Drones and photography.** Flying a drone without a valid licence is illegal, and photographing some government and military sites is not allowed. Ask before photographing people.
- **Ramadan.** During the holy month, do not eat, drink or smoke in public in daytime, including in your car, and expect changed opening hours.

## A sample route

This loop suits a trip of around ten days in the cooler months.

1. **Dubai or Abu Dhabi (two to three nights).** Our guide to [choosing a base](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) will help you decide.
2. **Ras Al Khaimah (two nights).** Mountains, forts and a slower pace. Use one day for Musandam: cross into Oman, spend the day on the water around Khasab, and return to Ras Al Khaimah in the evening.
3. **Al Ain (two nights).** Walk the oasis, visit the heritage sites and drive up Jebel Hafit for sunset. If your passport and the current rules allow, look across to the Buraimi forts.
4. **Muscat (three nights).** Fly from the UAE, or join a guided overland trip. Spend your days between the Grand Mosque, Mutrah Souq and the seafront, and fly home from Muscat if an open-jaw ticket works for you.

If you have less time, a single Musandam day from Ras Al Khaimah gives a real taste of Oman without moving bases. ## Questions to ask your operator

- Which border crossing will we use, and what entry requirements apply to our passports there?
- Do we need to apply for an Omani visa on the eVisa portal before we travel, or is our nationality covered by visa-free entry for the length of our stay?
- If we hire a car, is it permitted to cross into Oman, and is the insurance valid there? What documents do we need at the border?
- On a Musandam boat trip, are life jackets provided for every passenger, and what happens if the sea is rough?
- How would you adjust the route if the regional travel advice changes, or flights are disrupted while we are away?
- Will any part of our trip fall during Ramadan or a public holiday, and how might that affect border queues and opening hours?
