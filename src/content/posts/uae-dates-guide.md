Walk into a date shop in the UAE and the first thing you notice is the colour range. Amber, chestnut, near-black, honey-gold; some glossy and wrinkled, some firm and matt, some so soft they slump in the tray. Then the seller offers you one to try, and then another, and you realise you have never really tasted dates before. In the UAE, they are a whole culture of their own.

Dates in the UAE are not a dried fruit in a plastic box. They are a welcome, a staple, a festival, a harvest and the first thing eaten at sunset in Ramadan. For visitors, they are also one of the easiest ways to taste local culture: no booking, no reservation, just a market stall and a little curiosity.

This guide covers the varieties you are likely to meet, where to buy and taste dates, the Liwa Dates Festival, the traditions around them, and how to choose a box to take home.

## Why dates matter so much here

The date palm made life possible in the oases. The UAE government portal describes dates as [a boon in the desert](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/culture/food) and a vital source of nutrition, and notes that more than 40 varieties are grown in the country, each with its own colour and flavour. It also records an older use: dates were once fermented in the sun to act as a raising agent for bread.

The tree itself was just as useful. UNESCO, in its listing of date palm knowledge and traditions, which the UAE coordinated, describes how communities use [its leaves, fronds and fibres for traditional crafts and social rituals](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/date-palm-knowledge-skills-traditions-and-practices-01902), and how the palm appears in poetry and songs. Look up in a heritage village or old fort and you will often see palm-frond roofing and screens, the same material put to work.

You can see this oasis life for yourself in Al Ain, where shaded paths run between palm gardens fed by the old falaj irrigation channels. Hospitality is the third strand. Visit Abu Dhabi calls dates [a symbol of hospitality, heritage and nourishment](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/culture-and-traditions/coffee-and-dates), traditionally served with Arabic coffee. ## The date varieties worth knowing

You do not need to memorise dozens of names. A handful will get you a long way in any shop. The Abu Dhabi Media Office lists the varieties judged in the Liwa Dates Festival's fresh date competitions as [Dabbas, Khalas, Fard, Khneizi, Bumaan, Shishi and Zamli](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/arts-culture/22nd-liwa-dates-festival-attracts-record-breaking-participation-and-visitor-numbers/), which gives you a good list of local names to look for.

Market traders also stock varieties grown across the wider region. Speaking to The National at Abu Dhabi's date market, traders named [Ajwa as the most popular type for Ramadan](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/heritage/abu-dhabi-s-date-market-bustles-as-traders-enjoy-ramadan-boost-1.1203105), with medjool, mabroom and khalas selling more on ordinary days.

### How to taste like a buyer

- **Ask for a taste.** Sellers expect it, and comparing two or three side by side is the only way to learn what you like.
- **Notice the texture.** Some dates are soft and syrupy, others firm and almost chewy. Neither is better; it is personal.
- **Notice the finish.** Some taste of caramel, some of honey, some are drier and less sweet. A drier date often pairs better with coffee.
- **Ask what is in season.** Fresh, semi-ripe dates are a summer treat, and the seller will tell you what is new.
- **Taste plain before filled.** Stuffed and coated dates are delicious, but they hide the fruit. Try the plain version of a variety first.
- **Have water or coffee between tastes.** Several sweet dates in a row blur together quickly. A sip of water or a small cup of Arabic coffee resets your palate.

### Etiquette at the stall

Date shops run on hospitality as much as trade, so a few habits help. Take dates with your right hand, one at a time, and keep a tissue handy for the stones rather than putting them back in the tray. If a seller offers you a taste, it is polite to accept, and there is no obligation to buy just because you have tasted. Ask before photographing the seller or other customers, although photographing the displays is usually fine.

## Rutab: the fresh-date season

Most visitors only meet dried dates. In summer, there is another stage: rutab, the semi-ripe date, softer and fresher in flavour. The Abu Dhabi Media Office describes the Liwa Dates Festival as being held [annually during the UAE's rutab harvesting season](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/arts-culture/22nd-liwa-dates-festival-attracts-record-breaking-participation-and-visitor-numbers/).

If you are in the country during the harvest, look out for rutab in markets and on hospitality trays. The texture is part of the appeal: somewhere between fresh fruit and the soft dried dates most people know. Because rutab is fresh, it is best eaten soon after you buy it, and it is not the date to pack in a suitcase.

## The Liwa Dates Festival

Out in the Al Dhafra region, at the edge of the Empty Quarter, the date harvest is celebrated every summer. Visit Abu Dhabi says that [each July the Liwa Date Festival in Al Dhafra](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/culture-and-traditions/coffee-and-dates) celebrates the fruit, with local farmers competing for the best dates, alongside prizes, auctions and family activities.

According to the Abu Dhabi Media Office, the 22nd edition, organised by the Abu Dhabi Heritage Authority, ran [from 14 July to 23 July in Liwa City](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/arts-culture/22nd-liwa-dates-festival-attracts-record-breaking-participation-and-visitor-numbers/). Beyond the competitions, it included a Rutab Market selling fresh dates and palm offshoots, a Heritage Souq, a Handicrafts Pavilion with products made from date palm materials, folk performances and a Children's Village.

The scale gives a sense of how seriously the harvest is taken. The Media Office reports that the 22nd edition brought together [more than 465 Emirati farmers across 23 categories](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/arts-culture/22nd-liwa-dates-festival-attracts-record-breaking-participation-and-visitor-numbers/) over 10 days, and attracted approximately 80,000 visitors from across the UAE and the Gulf. The Rutab Market had 97 retail outlets and the Heritage Souq 68 stalls, while the Handicrafts Pavilion had 14 sections.

### What happens at the festival

The competitions are the heart of the event. The Media Office lists 11 Rutab Mazayna competitions for named varieties, plus categories such as Largest Date Bunch and Kharayef Al Bayt (Home Harvest), along with fruit competitions for lemons, mangoes and figs, and awards for model farms. Visit Abu Dhabi adds that the festival includes [a live date auction](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/what-to-buy-and-souvenirs/dates-chocolates-and-sweets), and that prizes go to the top dates in each category.

For a visitor, the most rewarding parts tend to be the Rutab Market, where you can taste fresh dates, and the handicrafts, where you can see palm fronds turned into baskets, mats and other objects. The Media Office also mentions educational lectures, workshops and seminars, with a particular focus on date palm cultivation, so it is a good chance to ask questions.

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that [travellers from out of town are welcome](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/what-to-buy-and-souvenirs/dates-chocolates-and-sweets) at the family-friendly event. Two honest caveats: July is the height of summer, so plan around the heat, and dates are confirmed each year, so check the organisers' announcements before you build a trip around it. Our [Liwa and the Empty Quarter guide](/stories/liwa-empty-quarter-guide/) helps with the wider journey.

If you go, start early, drink plenty of water, wear a hat and loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees, and plan your time around the shaded and indoor areas. Families with young children should treat it as a short visit rather than a full day out in the heat.

## Where to buy dates

### Abu Dhabi's date market

In the Mina Zayed area of Abu Dhabi, a cluster of shops forms the city's date market. Visit Abu Dhabi lists the [Abu Dhabi Dates Market](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/what-to-buy-and-souvenirs) alongside souks and supermarkets as places offering a range of high-quality options. The National reported that on the first day of Ramadan, the market at Mina Zayed was [busier than usual](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/heritage/abu-dhabi-s-date-market-bustles-as-traders-enjoy-ramadan-boost-1.1203105), as the holy month is peak season for date sales.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="uae-dates-guide-6" alts="A date shop at the Abu Dhabi vegetable market with trays and boxes of different dates stacked on shelves and counters" caption="A date shop in Abu Dhabi's market area, with varieties laid out for tasting."></photo-set>

Go in the cooler part of the day, take your time, and taste before you buy. Visit Abu Dhabi also points food lovers to the nearby [Mina Market and Mina Fruit and Vegetable Souk](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/culture-and-traditions/local-cuisine), so you can make a morning of it.

### Souks and supermarkets

You do not need a specialist market. Souks across the country sell dates loose and boxed, and Visit Abu Dhabi notes that [supermarkets also offer a range](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/what-to-buy-and-souvenirs). Supermarkets are a good place to compare packaged varieties and check labels.

### A morning at the date market

A visit to a date market does not need much planning. Arrive in the morning, when it is cooler and the shops are calm, and walk the whole row before you stop. Shops tend to specialise, so you will see some piled high with loose fruit by the kilo and others laid out like confectioners, with dates in trays and gift boxes.

Pick two or three shops, taste a few varieties in each and note the names you like. Ask the seller which dates are local and which are imported, and which are best for eating with coffee, for cooking or for gifts. When you have decided, buy loose for eating during your trip and boxed for taking home. The whole visit can be as short or as long as you like, and it combines well with the nearby fish and produce markets.

### Dates with children

A date market is one of the easier food outings with children. Tasting is part of the visit, the colours and shapes are interesting to compare, and dates are naturally sweet without being sticky in the way many sweets are. Remove stones before handing dates to small children, and steer them towards plain dates rather than nut-filled ones if allergies are a concern. Older children often enjoy keeping a list of the names they like best.

## Dates in Ramadan and at the table

At sunset in Ramadan, the date comes first. The UAE government portal explains that the fast is [broken with dates and laban (buttermilk)](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan), that in the UAE and other Gulf countries dates are considered the bread of the desert, and that alqurs, a bread-like crumble with dates and cardamom, is a popular Emirati sweet in Ramadan. It also describes how organisations distribute boxes of meals, dates and water at mosques and malls shortly before iftar. Our guide on [how visitors can join an iftar](/stories/how-visitors-can-join-iftar/) covers the evening meal.

Dates also turn up in the kitchen as syrup. The UAE portal describes dibs, or date syrup, as [a dip for both savoury and sweet dishes](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/culture/food), and Visit Ras Al Khaimah notes that luqaimat, the fried dumplings served at the end of many Emirati meals, are [drizzled with date syrup](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/emirati-flavors-traditional-food-uae/) and sprinkled with sesame.

Look out, too, for dates on breakfast tables, where date syrup is drizzled over bread and pancakes. Our guide to [an Emirati breakfast](/stories/emirati-breakfast-guide/) explains the dishes. In shops you will also find date jams, date-based sweets and, increasingly, desserts and drinks that use the fruit in modern ways.

## Choosing dates to take home

Dates are one of the most popular edible souvenirs. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests looking for [gourmet dates stuffed with almonds or pistachios, or coated in camel milk chocolate](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/what-to-buy-and-souvenirs), and the UAE portal mentions [dates stuffed with almonds or candied orange peel](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/culture/food) as luxury gifts.

Visit Abu Dhabi also describes camel milk chocolate, made by [blending camel milk with vanilla, honey and cocoa beans](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/what-to-buy-and-souvenirs/dates-chocolates-and-sweets) and sold in bars, pralines and camel-shaped figurines, which makes a natural companion to a box of dates.

A few tips:

- **Mix plain and filled.** Plain dates show off the variety; filled ones make a better gift for people new to them.
- **Buy sealed boxes for travel.** Loose dates are lovely to taste but travel less well.
- **Check your home country's rules** on bringing food back, especially fresh rutab.
- **Keep them cool.** Dates are sturdy, but chocolate-coated ones will not survive a hot car or a day in a bag in summer. Buy them on your last day if you can.
- **Think about the recipient.** Firm, less sweet dates suit people who drink black coffee; soft, caramel-like ones suit a sweet tooth.

## Quick answers

**How many varieties of dates grow in the UAE?** The UAE government portal says more than 40 varieties are grown in the country, each with its own colour and flavour.

**When is the Liwa Dates Festival?** It is held each July in the Al Dhafra region during the rutab harvest. The 22nd edition ran from 14 to 23 July in Liwa City; check official announcements for the next one.

**Where can I buy dates in Abu Dhabi?** Visit Abu Dhabi lists the Abu Dhabi Dates Market, souks and supermarkets.

**Why are dates eaten at iftar?** The UAE government portal says the fast is broken with dates and laban, following the example of the Prophet Muhammad.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you include a date market visit with a guide who can explain the varieties as we taste?
- If we travel in July, is a visit to the Liwa Dates Festival realistic, and how would you manage the heat and the distance?
- Can you arrange a visit to a farm or oasis where date palms are grown?
- Which dates and date products travel best for taking home?
- If we visit during Ramadan, where can we see the date markets at their busiest without getting in the way?
