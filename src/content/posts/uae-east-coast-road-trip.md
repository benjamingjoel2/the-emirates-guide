Somewhere in the mountains the road starts to fall instead of climb, the rock walls open up, and a strip of blue appears ahead. Wind the window down and the air smells different: salt, warm stone, a hint of fish from a harbour you cannot see yet.

That is the start of an east coast road trip, and it is one of the easiest ways to see a completely different UAE in a short time. In two or three days you can link Fujairah's fort and wadis, Khorfakkan's bay and hilltop watchtower, the country's oldest mosque, and the harbour town of Dibba, sleeping by the sea along the way. If you only have one day, you can still drive over, see Fujairah or Khorfakkan and come back, but the coast rewards a night or two.

## Why an east coast road trip works

The east coast is a narrow strip between the mountains and the sea, and it belongs to more than one emirate. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/) describes Sharjah's east coast as a place where rugged mountains meet beaches and mangroves, with seaside towns such as Khorfakkan and Kalba. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/fujairah) describes Fujairah as the only emirate located completely on the east coast, with a shoreline running from Fujairah city north to Dibba.

So you drive in and out of two emirates as you go, sometimes within a few minutes. One road links most of the stops, the distances between them are short, and the scenery changes constantly. It is ideal for a self-drive trip or a few days with a driver.

### Two ways over the mountains

There are two main ways in, and using one to arrive and the other to leave turns the trip into a loop. The southern route climbs through Masafi and down Wadi Ham to Fujairah city. The northern route is the newer Sharjah to Khorfakkan road: Visit Sharjah describes how it opens up a view of [Al Rafisah Dam](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/al-rafisah-dam/), first built in the 1980s and since upgraded with a visitor centre, a rest area, a lake with boating and a walkway through the plantations to a restored fort.

Close to the same road, [Najd Al Maqsar](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/najd-al-maqsar-village/) is a restored mountain village with a fort that Visit Sharjah describes as nearly 300 years old, 13 old houses, and rocks engraved with camels and horses. It makes a good first or last stop if you come in through Khorfakkan.

## Before you set off

### When to go

Fujairah's tourism authority describes summer, from [May to September](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-Overview.html), as hot and sometimes humid, and October to April as very pleasant. The cooler months are best for walking up to forts and towers. In summer, plan outdoor stops for early morning and late afternoon. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you choose.

### Driving

The UK Foreign Office says you can drive in the UAE on a [UK photocard driving licence](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), warns that speeding is common, and points out that flashing headlights can mean a driver is coming through rather than giving way. Mountain roads have long descents, so use lower gears and take your time. Read our guide to [driving in the UAE as a visitor](/stories/driving-in-the-uae-as-a-visitor/) before you pick up a car.

### Weather on the road

The same Foreign Office page warns that [heavy rain can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). If rain is forecast, stay out of wadi beds and never drive through flowing water. Check forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/) before you set off each day.

### Rules that change as you drive

Because the route moves between emirates, some rules change along the way. The Foreign Office notes that drinking or possessing alcohol is [illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), which includes Khorfakkan, Kalba and Dibba Al Hisn. During Ramadan it also warns that driving may be erratic around dusk, when people are trying to get home. Save the emergency numbers before you go: the Foreign Office lists [999 for police and 998 for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/getting-help).

### What to pack in the car

- Plenty of water and snacks, more than you think you need.
- Hats, sunscreen and walking shoes with grip for forts and trails.
- A scarf and clothing that covers shoulders and knees for the mosque.
- Swimwear and a cover-up for the walk from beach to car.
- A small first aid kit, as Visit Sharjah suggests for its trails.

## Day one: over the mountains to Fujairah

### Wadi Ham and Al Bithna

The drive in is part of the day. Fujairah's tourism authority describes [Wadi Ham](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-NaturalSites.html) as the largest and longest wadi in the UAE, with much of it running alongside a main road, dry for most of the year but rushing after rain. The authority gives its length as 30 km, from Masafi to the dam. Above the valley, [Al Bithna fort](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-MuseumAndForts.html) stands close to the main road that links Fujairah to the rest of the UAE, so it is an easy first stop to stretch your legs. The authority dates it to 1735 and places it 13 km from Fujairah city.

### Fujairah Fort and the old town

In Fujairah city, head for the fort on its hill in the old town, about three kilometres from the coast. The authority describes its three round towers and one square tower, built from local stone, gravel, clay, hay and gypsum, and the museum nearby has halls on traditional crafts, weapons, household objects and archaeological finds. Late afternoon is a good time for photos of the walls.

### Optional: south to Kalba

If you have time, Kalba lies south of Fujairah city. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/kalba/) describes its mangroves as the oldest in the UAE and mentions kayaking, wildlife spotting and the Kalba Bird of Prey Centre. Visit Sharjah places Kalba about 12 km south of Fujairah city. On the seafront, the [Bait Sheikh Saeed Bin Hamad Al Qasimi](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/bait-sheikh-saeed-bin-hamad-al-qasimi/) is a late 19th century house restored and opened as a museum in 1999, with carved wooden doors, wind towers and a central courtyard, opposite Kalba's fort. Our guide to [Kalba and its mangroves](/stories/kalba-mangroves-guide/) has more. Otherwise, spend the evening on the Fujairah Corniche.

## Day two: Khorfakkan, bay and tower

### Morning: the climb to Al Rabi Tower

Khorfakkan is a Sharjah town on a bay, surrounded by Fujairah. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/khorfakkan/) describes a long beach set against rock formations and the Hajar mountains, with a corniche, water sports and dhow cruises.

Start early with the walk to Al Rabi Tower. Visit Sharjah describes it as a watchtower [built in 1915](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/al-rabi-hiking-trail/) as part of Khorfakkan's defence network, reached by a well-marked trail with rest stops and historical signs, often recommended for beginners and children. The same page notes that parking at the start fills quickly and that it gets busy at weekends. Its checklist is sensible: plenty of water, sturdy shoes, a hat and sunblock, and snacks.

If you would rather not climb, Khorfakkan has other towers and viewpoints. Visit Sharjah describes [Al Adwani Tower](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/al-adwani-tower/), originally built in 1623 as a lighthouse to guide ships into the port and since reconstructed on its original site, reached by a winding, lit path. At the entrance to town, the [Resistance Monument](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/resistance-monument/) sits on a hill above Khorfakkan Square and honours those who resisted the Portuguese invasion in 1507, with a walkway that looks over the town.

### Afternoon: beach and water

Spend the middle of the day by the water. Visit Sharjah lists sailing, kayaking, scuba diving and dhow cruises in Khorfakkan, and its [Khorfakkan Beach page](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/khorfakkan-beach/) describes a 3 km crescent-shaped beach, with snorkelling near Shark Island a short boat ride away. The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags.

### Evening: the amphitheatre and waterfall

Finish at the seafront amphitheatre. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/khorfakkan-amphitheater-and-waterfall/) describes a Roman-inspired amphitheatre facing Khorfakkan's bay, beside a man-made waterfall carved out of natural rock and often lit up at night. Stay until after dark if you can. Our [Khorfakkan guide](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) covers the town in more depth.

### A detour to Wadi Shees

If you have an hour spare, the [Wadi Shees Nature Trail](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/hiking-trails/wadi-shees-nature-trail/) is one of the gentlest walks on the coast. Visit Sharjah describes a 1.8 km loop beside an old falaj water channel, through palm groves and past terraced farms, taking 30 to 40 minutes and suitable for all ages. There are no major rest spots, so bring water and food.

## Day three: north to Al Bidya and Dibba

### Al Bidya Mosque

Head north along the coast road, with the mountains on your left and the sea on your right. Your main stop is Al Bidya Mosque, which the Fujairah Tourism and Antiquities Authority describes as [the oldest mosque in the UAE](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-PlacesOfWorship.html). The authority places it about 35 km north of Fujairah city. It is small and built from stone and burnt clay, with four domes resting on a single central column. It is still a place of worship: dress modestly, bring a scarf, take off your shoes and avoid prayer times.

### Dibba, where the road runs out

At the northern end of the coast is Dibba. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/dibba-al-hisn/) explains that Dibba Al Hisn belongs to Sharjah, Dibba Al Baya to the north is part of Oman, and Dibba Muhallab to the south belongs to Fujairah. It describes a marina, a fort, mosques, produce markets and a souk, plus boat trips for snorkelling, fishing and spotting dolphins. Do not cross into Oman without checking the entry rules first; our guide to [combining the UAE and Oman](/stories/uae-and-oman-one-trip/) explains the options.

From Dibba, drive back inland through the mountains, or spend a final night by the sea. Visit Sharjah describes Dibba Al Hisn as an easy 90-minute drive from Sharjah city, which makes it a natural place to finish the loop.

## How a day on the road actually feels

The pattern quickly becomes familiar. Set off early, when the light is soft and the roads quiet, and do anything on foot first: a fort, a tower, a short trail. By late morning, head for the coast, a museum or a long lunch of grilled fish. The middle of the day is for shade and a swim from a beach with lifeguards. From mid-afternoon the light warms up again, and the seafronts fill with families as the day cools.

Distances on the coast are short, but traffic slows in the towns, and you will want to stop more often than you expect. Build in slack rather than squeezing in one more sight.

## What catches people out

- **Over-planning.** Trying to see everything leaves you in the car all day. Pick one or two main stops for each day.
- **The Oman border at Dibba.** Dibba Al Baya is Omani. Know where the border is before you follow a coast road north.
- **Weekend crowds.** Visit Sharjah notes that the Al Rabi trail is busy at weekends and parking at the start fills quickly. Go early.
- **Prayer times.** Mosques, including Al Bidya, are busiest at prayer times. Plan visits between them.
- **Evening driving.** If you are not used to mountain roads, plan to cross the Hajar in daylight.

## Making the loop your own

- **One day only?** Choose either Fujairah city and Al Bithna, or Khorfakkan. Trying to do the whole coast in a day means more time in the car than out of it.
- **With children?** Khorfakkan's beach and the Al Rabi trail, which Visit Sharjah describes as suitable for children, make a good pairing.
- **Into heritage?** Spend longer at Fujairah Fort, the museum and Al Bidya, and ask a guide to join you.
- **Into the water?** Add a snorkelling boat trip from Dibba or Khorfakkan on a calm day.
- **Short on time?** Drive in on the Sharjah to Khorfakkan road, spend a night in Khorfakkan, and return via Fujairah and Wadi Ham.
- **Limited mobility?** Al Bithna, Fujairah Fort and Al Bidya Mosque can all be appreciated from near the road, and the Khorfakkan seafront is flat and easy.

Wherever you stop, dress modestly away from the beach, and follow the Foreign Office advice [not to photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). ## Quick answers

**How many days do you need for the UAE's east coast?** Two or three days lets you see Fujairah, Khorfakkan, Al Bidya Mosque and Dibba without rushing; a single day works if you focus on one area.

**Is Khorfakkan in Fujairah?** No. Khorfakkan belongs to Sharjah, though Visit Sharjah notes that it is surrounded by the emirate of Fujairah.

**Can you drive to the east coast on a UK licence?** The UK Foreign Office says you can use a UK photocard driving licence to drive in the UAE.

**What is the best time for an east coast road trip?** The cooler months from October to April, which Fujairah's tourism authority describes as very pleasant.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you plan the driving so that walks and fort visits fall in the cooler parts of the day?
- Would a driver be better than self-drive for the mountain roads on our dates?
- Which beaches on our route have lifeguards, and which boat trips are best for our group?
- Can you arrange a guide for Fujairah Fort and Al Bidya Mosque?
- If rain is forecast, how will you change the route?
- Where should we stay so that the loop has as little backtracking as possible?
