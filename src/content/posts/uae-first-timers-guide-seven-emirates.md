Somewhere behind the towers you see from the plane window is a mangrove lagoon so still you can hear herons stepping through the shallows. Further on, a mountain road climbs in switchbacks into cooler air, and beyond the mountains lies a different sea altogether.

Most first-timers never reach any of it, because they assume the UAE is one city. It is a federation of seven emirates, each with its own ruler, its own character and its own pace. Some glitter; others feel like small coastal towns where the loudest sound at dusk is the call to prayer. You can cross from the Arabian Gulf to the Gulf of Oman in a morning, and swap a mall for a mud-brick fort before lunch.

## A map in your head before you land

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates) lists the seven as Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah, Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah. Abu Dhabi is the largest emirate and the federal capital; Ajman is the smallest. Most line up along the Arabian Gulf coast, with the Hajar Mountains rising behind the northern ones and Fujairah alone on the eastern shore.

For planning, think in groups:

- **The two big cities**: Abu Dhabi and Dubai, where most people stay and most flights land.
- **The culture-first neighbour**: Sharjah, right next door to Dubai.
- **The small coastal emirates**: Ajman and Umm Al Quwain, quieter and more local.
- **The mountains and the east coast**: Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah, where the landscape changes completely.

Many visitors base themselves in one city and take day trips. Still choosing a base? See our comparison of [Dubai and Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/).

One piece of admin first: entry rules depend on your passport. The UK government's travel advice, for example, says British visitors can get a visit visa on arrival valid for up to 90 days in a 180-day period, and that passports should be valid for at least six months from arrival ([FCDO entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements)). Rules differ by nationality and change, so check the official advice for your own passport before you book.

## Abu Dhabi: a capital that lets you breathe

Late afternoon on the corniche, the light goes soft and gold and the sea sits flat beside you. Abu Dhabi city sits on an island and feels calmer and more spread out than Dubai. According to the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi), the emirate's coastline includes more than 200 natural islands, and it stretches inland to Al Ain and west into the region of Al Dhafra.

The landmark most people come for is the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, a sweep of white domes and marble courtyards that glows as the sun drops. It is open to visitors of all faiths, and it has a clear dress code: the [mosque's own visitor code](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/mosque-manner) asks women to wear loose clothing to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists and to cover their hair, and suggests men cover their legs and avoid sleeveless or tight tops. Check the official site before you go, and pack the right clothes rather than improvising at the gate.

On Saadiyat Island, the [Saadiyat Cultural District](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/landmarks-and-architecture/saadiyat-cultural-district) gathers Louvre Abu Dhabi, Zayed National Museum and teamLab Phenomena within walking distance of one another, a good way to spend the hottest hours indoors. Inland, [Al Ain](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-ain) is known as the Garden City, with shaded palm groves still watered by ancient falaj channels and a road that climbs Jebel Hafit for sunset over the desert. Far to the south-west, [Al Dhafra](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) runs from the Liwa dunes to the west coast and its islands.

**Who it suits:** families, culture lovers, people who want beaches without a party scene, and anyone who prefers a slower city. Explore more on our [Abu Dhabi page](/destinations/abu-dhabi/).

## Dubai: the skyline, and the creek that came first

Step off a wooden abra on the Deira side of the creek into spice sacks, gold shop windows and the smell of cardamom and diesel. This is the Dubai that came before the towers. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/dubai) describes how the historic creek divides the old city into Deira to the north and Bur Dubai to the south, and how that natural harbour made Dubai a centre for fishing, pearling and trade.

On the Bur Dubai side, the Al Fahidi neighbourhood keeps its wind towers and sand-coloured alleys, cool and quiet at mid-morning. Go early, while the shade still reaches across the lanes. Then a short metro ride takes you to the Burj Khalifa, the marina and the malls.

Dubai also stretches into the mountains. [Hatta](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/beyond-dubai/nature-adventures-in-hatta), in the Hajar range, is promoted for hiking trails, kayaking, mountain biking and camping, and it makes a good day out when the city starts to feel like too much.

**Who it suits:** first-timers who want everything in one place, shoppers, food lovers, nightlife seekers and anyone on a short stopover. See our [Dubai page](/destinations/dubai/), and our [five-day itinerary](/stories/five-day-uae-itinerary/) if you want to combine it with Abu Dhabi.

## Sharjah: cross an invisible line, change the rules

On a busy road you may not notice Dubai becoming Sharjah. Step out, and you will. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/sharjah) notes that UNESCO named Sharjah the Cultural Capital of the Arab World in 1998, and that it is the only emirate with land on both coasts, including the east-coast town of Khorfakkan.

It is also a dry emirate. The UK government's advice states that drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah ([FCDO safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security)), and dress expectations are generally more conservative than in the beach districts of Dubai. Crossing over for the day? Dress for Sharjah when you leave your hotel. Our [etiquette guide](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has the detail.

In return, you get a city of museums and souks. [Visit Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/) highlights the Central Souk (often called the Blue Souk), Souk Al Arsah and the restored Heart of Sharjah, where coral-stone houses and shaded courtyards show how the town looked before oil.

**Who it suits:** travellers interested in Islamic art, archaeology and heritage, families, and anyone staying in Dubai who wants a calmer cultural day. Start with our [Sharjah page](/destinations/sharjah/).

## Ajman: an evening on the corniche with the locals

As the heat eases, families spread out along Ajman's beachfront and the working waterfront winds down. Ajman is the [smallest of the seven](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ajman), a short stretch of Gulf coast squeezed between Sharjah and Umm Al Quwain. Its compact centre is anchored by Ajman Fort, which the government portal describes as an 18th-century landmark restored and turned into a museum.

Ajman offers ordinary life at an easy pace. You will usually see more residents than tourists on the corniche, and the sand is rarely crowded on a weekday afternoon. The emirate also has inland areas, Manama and Masfout, known for farming and mountain valleys.

**Who it suits:** budget-conscious travellers, beach days without the resort scene, and visitors who want a glimpse of everyday Emirati coastal life. More on our [Ajman page](/destinations/ajman/).

## Umm Al Quwain: the art of doing very little

Mornings on the lagoon are glassy and still. Herons pick along the mangrove edges. Umm Al Quwain is the second smallest and least populated emirate, according to the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/umm-al-quwain). The old town sits on a narrow peninsula, the coast is fringed with mangroves, and a scatter of islands lies just offshore. The largest of them, Al Seniah, is home to Arabian gazelles, falcons and turtles.

Fishing has long shaped the town, and it still feels like a place where people know one another.

**Who it suits:** birdwatchers, paddlers, slow travellers, and anyone who wants a quiet night between busier stops. See our [Umm Al Quwain page](/destinations/umm-al-quwain/).

## Ras Al Khaimah: where the mountains take over

Drive north and the Hajar Mountains start filling the windscreen. Ras Al Khaimah reaches up towards the UAE's northern tip, and the landscape changes sharply. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) describes a history going back some 7,000 years, from the medieval trading port of Julfar to around 1,000 archaeological sites, and a terrain that runs from the Hajar Mountains to dunes and beaches.

The headline is Jebel Jais. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) describes it as the highest peak in the UAE at 1,934 metres, usually around 10°C cooler than the rest of the emirate, with marked walking trails and a viewing deck. The drive up is all switchbacks and widening views. Pack a layer for the top.

On the coast are long beaches, resort islands and an old town divided by a creek. Our deeper guide, [Beyond Dubai](/stories/beyond-dubai-quieter-emirates/), covers the forts and the abandoned pearling village in more detail.

**Who it suits:** hikers, adventure seekers, beach-and-mountain combinations, and families wanting resort space. Visit our [Ras Al Khaimah page](/destinations/ras-al-khaimah/).

## Fujairah: over the mountains to the other sea

Come down the far side of the Hajar range and the sea ahead is not the Gulf you left behind. The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/fujairah) calls Fujairah the only emirate located completely on the eastern coast, along the Gulf of Oman, and the UAE's only access to the Indian Ocean. Rugged peaks drop almost to the shore.

The east coast is known for snorkelling and diving, wadis that run with water after rain, and quiet fishing villages. The best-known heritage site is the small Al Bidya Mosque, with its clustered domes, which the [Fujairah Tourism and Antiquities Authority](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-PlacesOfWorship.html) describes as the oldest mosque in the UAE, built from local stone and fired clay.

**Who it suits:** divers and snorkellers, road-trippers, and anyone who wants mountains and sea together without the big-city pace. Read more on our [Fujairah page](/destinations/fujairah/), and see [combining the UAE and Oman](/stories/uae-and-oman-one-trip/) if you are tempted to keep driving.

## The small things that catch first-timers out

- **The weather is the planner.** The season decides how long you can comfortably spend outdoors, so walk early, go indoors at midday and save the corniche for dusk. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you choose dates.
- **Each emirate has its own rules.** Alcohol and dress expectations can change when you cross a border you did not notice. Dress for the most conservative stop of the day.
- **The calendar matters.** Friday is the main prayer day, and during Ramadan daily rhythms shift. Read [visiting during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) if your dates overlap.
- **Photography needs care.** The UK government's advice warns not to photograph people without their permission ([FCDO safety and security](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security)). Ask first.
- **The desert is not one experience.** See [whether a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/)

## Putting your emirates together

If you have four or five days, base yourself in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, add a day in the other, and give one day to Sharjah or a mountain escape. With a week or more, consider splitting your nights: a city first, then Ras Al Khaimah or Fujairah for the mountains and the east coast. Moving base costs time on the road, but your mornings start where you want to be.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Given my dates, which emirates make most sense as bases, and which as day trips?
- How long are the drives between the cities, the mountains and the east coast at the times we would travel?
- Can you arrange a guide for the older districts of Dubai and Sharjah who can explain the history as we walk?
- What exactly should we wear for the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and for a day in Sharjah?
- Is Jebel Jais or Hatta the better mountain day for us, given our fitness and the season?
- Are there public holidays, Ramadan dates or major events during our trip that will change opening hours or traffic?
