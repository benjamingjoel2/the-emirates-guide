The engine cuts out on the crest of a dune, and the quiet arrives all at once. Below, in a hollow of the sand, someone has laid out rugs and cushions around a ring of flickering torches, and there is nobody else in sight. The sky goes from apricot to violet while you are still deciding where to sit.

Moments like that are why so many couples look at the UAE for a honeymoon. In one short trip you can have desert silence, cool mountain air, warm sea and some of the most beautiful buildings in the region, all with excellent places to stay. It is also a country with its own laws on how couples behave in public, and knowing them before you land is part of planning well.

So, is the UAE a good honeymoon destination? For most couples, yes, especially in the cooler months and if you like variety. This guide covers how to shape the trip, where the romance really is, and the rules that matter.

## Choosing the shape of a UAE honeymoon

The best honeymoons here mix one or two busy days with plenty of slow ones. A simple structure works:

- **A city base** for culture, food and evenings out, in Dubai or Abu Dhabi.
- **A quiet base** for the second half: a desert retreat, a mountain lodge or a beach on a calmer coast.
- **One or two set-piece moments**, booked in advance, rather than a packed list of activities.

Moving hotels once is usually enough. Moving every night is not a honeymoon. Our [where to stay](/where-to-stay/) section can help you choose the bases.

### Three ways to put it together

**City and desert.** Spend the first half in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, with a museum morning, a long lunch and an evening out, then move to a desert retreat for the second half. This suits couples who want to finish the trip in near silence.

**City and mountains.** Pair Dubai with Ras Al Khaimah, where Jebel Jais gives you cooler air, sunset decks and walking trails. The transfer is short, which keeps the change of base easy.

**City and the far west.** Pair Abu Dhabi with the Al Dhafra region for dunes, island stays and wildlife. The drive is longer, so this works best on a trip of a week or more, with at least two nights out west.

Whichever you pick, put the more active days near the start, while you still have energy, and leave the last two or three days almost empty.

## Where the romance is

### A desert night for two

The Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve lists a [private desert dinner](https://www.ddcr.org/what-to-do/), a secluded spot arranged with rugs and cushions and lit by traditional torches, as well as camping with professional safari guides and hot air balloon rides over the dunes. A dawn balloon flight followed by a slow breakfast is a very different day from the classic evening safari. The reserve says balloon passengers can look out for oryx, gazelles and camels in their natural habitat. Our guide to [camping overnight in the UAE desert](/stories/overnight-desert-camping-uae/) explains what a night out on the sand is really like.

The reserve describes itself as the UAE's first national park, and its approved tours use guides certified to explain the wildlife and culture of the dunes. That matters on a honeymoon: a quieter, conservation-led evening usually means fewer vehicles and less noise around you.

### Sunset in the mountains

In Ras Al Khaimah, Jais Viewing Deck Park runs along a ridge with [seven viewing decks](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/), looking over the mountains on one side and the Arabian Gulf on the other. The tourism board says the sun tends to set between 17.30 and 18.00 through the year, and on Fridays and Saturdays the park stays open until 11:00pm. Up on Jebel Jais, it is usually [10°C cooler than other parts of the emirate](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/), which makes a welcome change from the coast.

Pack a warm layer for the evening. Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board describes winters, from November to March, as [mild at about 15°C to 28°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), with occasional brief showers mainly in the mountains, and it is cooler again on the ridge after dark.

### Art, water and light in Abu Dhabi

Louvre Abu Dhabi is one of the most quietly romantic places in the country. Under the dome, sunlight passes through layers of geometric stars and scatters across the floor in what the architect called the ["rain of light"](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/culture/museums-and-art/louvre-abu-dhabi). Visit Abu Dhabi also notes guided kayaking tours around and beneath the dome, a memorable way to see the building from the water. The museum is closed on Mondays.

The numbers explain the effect: the same page describes a dome 180 metres wide, made up of 7,850 stars arranged in eight layers. Go in the morning, when the light is sharpest, and allow two to three hours, which the page says is typical.

Later, go to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in the late afternoon and stay into dusk as the white marble changes colour. Visitors need to [pre-book an online access pass](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-visiting-hours). Treat it as a cultural visit rather than a photo shoot, and keep a respectful distance from each other inside. The mosque's own rules say that [expressing intimacy in any form is strictly prohibited](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/worshippers/en-v-mosque-manners-details), and that photographs should be taken only at the designated photo stops.

### Stars over Al Ain

For something quieter, Visit Abu Dhabi suggests [camping or glamping at Jebel Hafit Desert Park](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/article-hub/essential-things-to-do-in-al-ain) near Al Ain, with sunset walks and fully equipped dome tents, and points to stargazing there thanks to the lack of light pollution. The same page describes Jebel Hafit as the second-highest peak in the UAE, and Al Ain as a 90-minute drive from Abu Dhabi city, so it works as a one-night escape from the capital.

### A slow morning in the oasis

If you stay near Al Ain, start one day in Al Ain Oasis before the heat builds. Visit Abu Dhabi describes [shaded pathways lined with benches](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-ain-oasis), the sound of water in the old falaj channels and birdsong among the palms, with free entry and bicycles or golf buggies to rent if you would rather not walk. It is a rare chance to wander for an hour in green shade.

### Islands and open space in Al Dhafra

If you want real distance from the cities, Abu Dhabi's western Al Dhafra region offers desert and island spa stays, the Liwa dunes and wildlife encounters on Sir Bani Yas Island. Visit Abu Dhabi says driving there typically takes [around two to three hours from the capital](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra), so give it at least two nights.

Check the calendar before you book. The same page says the Liwa International Festival runs each December, with three weeks of desert motorsports, concerts and fireworks, which is lively but not quiet. It also says the cooler months are ideal for desert experiences, while the warmer months suit coastal stays. Our [Liwa and Empty Quarter guide](/stories/liwa-empty-quarter-guide/) covers the dunes in more detail.

### A beach on the other coast

On the east coast, Khorfakkan's [crescent-shaped beach](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/beaches/khorfakkan-beach/) faces the Gulf of Oman with mountains behind, and Visit Sharjah mentions snorkelling and diving near Shark Island, a short boat ride away. The same page says the beach runs for 3 kilometres and is lined with palm trees. Remember that Khorfakkan is part of Sharjah, where alcohol is banned.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="uae-honeymoon-guide-3" alts="A broad shade tree on the sand of Khorfakkan beach, with a couple walking at the water edge and the bay beyond" caption="Shade on the sand at Khorfakkan, on the Gulf of Oman coast."></photo-set>

In the evening, walk along the bay to the amphitheatre, where a man-made waterfall carved out of natural rock is [often illuminated at night](https://www.visitsharjah.com/activities/culture/khorfakkan-amphitheater-and-waterfall/). Swim only where there are lifeguards, and stay out of the water when red flags are flying; the UK government says they [mean swimming is dangerous](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

## The rules every honeymooning couple should know

None of this should put you off, but you should know it before you go.

### Affection in public

The UK government says plainly that [showing affection in public is frowned upon, and you can be arrested for kissing in public](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Keep affection for your room. This applies in malls, on beaches and in the desert camp as much as on the street. A kiss for the camera on a busy beach is exactly the kind of moment to avoid.

### LGBT+ couples

The same advice states that [same-sex sexual activity is illegal in the UAE and same-sex marriages are not recognised](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). It notes that private life is respected in many respects, but that there have been reports of people being punished, particularly where there is a public element. Read the full advice carefully before deciding whether the UAE is right for you.

### Dress

The UK government advises dressing modestly in public areas such as malls, with [shoulders and knees covered for women](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and wearing swimwear only on beaches or at pools. Visit Abu Dhabi adds that [regular swimwear, including bikinis, can be worn on Abu Dhabi's beaches](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), and suggests a pashmina or jacket for cold air conditioning in the evening. It also notes that the cities' restaurants see every style of evening dress, from casual to black tie, so check the dress code when you book a special dinner. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) covers the details.

### Alcohol

The UK government says the [legal age for drinking is 21](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) in Abu Dhabi, Dubai and the northern emirates except Sharjah, where drinking and possessing alcohol is illegal. It is illegal to drink or be under the influence in a public place anywhere in the UAE. A celebratory glass belongs in a licensed hotel or restaurant. The same advice says never to leave a drink unattended or accept one from a stranger. Our [alcohol rules guide](/stories/alcohol-rules-in-the-uae/) explains the rest.

### Photos of the two of you

Honeymoon photos are part of the fun, but the rules apply to them too. The UK government advises [not photographing people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and says it is illegal to photograph some government buildings and military sites. Keep strangers out of your frame where you can, keep poses modest in public, and save the more affectionate shots for your hotel or a private desert setting.

### Engaged rather than married?

Some couples travel before the wedding rather than after. The UK government says that consensual relationships between a man and a woman outside marriage are [generally legal if both are aged 18 or over](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), but that a complaint from a spouse or parent can still lead to prosecution. It also notes that medical insurance may not cover a pregnancy outside marriage, so check your policy if that applies to you.

## When to go

Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board names [October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) as the best time for outdoor activities, and that window suits most honeymoon plans: desert nights, mountain sunsets and long beach afternoons. In summer, the trip shifts towards pools, spas and indoor culture. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) will help you choose.

Abu Dhabi's tourism board says rain is sporadic and [falls mainly in winter, from November to March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). A wet day is rare, but keep one indoor plan, such as a museum or a spa, ready for it.

### If your dates fall in Ramadan

A Ramadan honeymoon can be lovely, with evening iftar meals and a calmer mood in the day, but it changes the rhythm. The UK government says it may cause offence to [eat, drink or smoke in public in the daytime](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) during Ramadan, that hotels and restaurants serving food in fasting hours may screen off those areas, and that dress codes matter even more. Check opening hours for anything you plan to book.

### Insurance and health

Check that your insurance covers every activity you plan, from balloon flights to snorkelling. The UK government says having [insurance for local treatment or medical evacuation](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health) is particularly important if you are pregnant or have a health condition. It also advises travellers to be [prepared for flight disruptions](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/warnings-and-insurance) given the current regional situation, so flexible bookings are worth the effort. For an ambulance in the UAE, dial 998.

## Quick answers

**Is the UAE good for a honeymoon?** Yes for most couples, especially in the cooler months, with desert, mountains, beaches and culture close together.

**Can couples kiss in public in the UAE?** No. The UK government says [you can be arrested for kissing in public](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).

**Can I wear a bikini on a UAE honeymoon?** On beaches and at pools, yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says [bikinis can be worn on Abu Dhabi's beaches](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear); cover up away from the water.

**What is the best month for a UAE honeymoon?** Ras Al Khaimah's tourism board recommends [October to April](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) for outdoor activities.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you arrange a private desert dinner or an overnight camp just for the two of us, and what is included?
- Which mountain or desert retreat would you pair with our city stay, and how long are the transfers?
- Can you book a Grand Mosque access pass and a Louvre kayak tour on days that fit our plans?
- Are there any events, holidays or Ramadan dates during our trip that would change opening hours or the mood?
- Which beaches near our hotels have lifeguards and calm water in our month?
- What local customs should we know about for dinners, photos and behaviour in public?
