There is a particular evening, usually some time in October, when you step outside after sunset and realise the air no longer feels heavy. People linger on the corniche. Café tables move back out onto the pavement. The city exhales.

That moment is the heart of the UAE in autumn. From September to November the country moves from the tail of summer into the start of its outdoor season, and the experience changes a lot from one end to the other. September still behaves like summer. October is warm, with a hot middle of the day and lovely mornings and evenings. November opens the season most visitors come for. If you want warm seas, quieter sights and the first of the cooler evenings, autumn is an excellent time to visit, especially from mid-October onwards.

In this guide we take the season a month at a time, then look at heat sense, the events that start filling the calendar, the first desert evenings, and the practical details of packing and pacing an autumn trip.

## How autumn weather changes, month by month

Visit Abu Dhabi defines autumn in the capital as [September to December](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), as the summer heat begins to subside, and says the milder weather calls for more beach outings and watersports around an archipelago made up of hundreds of islands. It places summer from late May to September, so the two seasons overlap at the start.

Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives figures for the north. It says that in October and November [temperatures begin to drop](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/) and the chance of rain increases a little, with typical daily highs of about 35°C by the end of October and around 30°C by the end of November. Days also shorten through the season, heading towards the shortest day on 21 December, which the same guide puts at about 10.5 hours of daylight.

### September: summer with the door ajar

Treat September as a summer trip. Visit Abu Dhabi's summer guide covers June to September and describes [intense heat and relatively high humidity](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer), especially along the coast. The UAE's midday break for outdoor workers is a useful marker: in 2026 it ran [until 15 September](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/06/04/midday-break-for-outdoor-workers-to-begin-on-june-15/), covering the hours between 12.30pm and 3pm, according to The National.

Plan September around indoor sights at midday and the beach or waterfront early and late. Our guide to [visiting the UAE in summer](/stories/uae-in-summer/) has the full playbook.

September does have its advantages. Many attractions are quieter than they will be a few weeks later, and the sea is at its warmest. If you are happy to swim at sunrise, spend the middle of the day in museums and malls, and save the waterfront for the evening, it can be a relaxed and easy month.

### October: the turn

October is the month the year changes direction. The middle of the day is still hot, but Visit Ras Al Khaimah notes that in autumn [early mornings and evenings are very pleasant](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), and suggests it is ideal for sunrises and sunsets, mountain trips and the views from Dhayah Fort. The second half of October is a sweet spot for a beach holiday: warm water, softer light, and fewer people than in midwinter.

It is also a good month to test the outdoors before committing to it. Try a short sunrise walk or an evening on the water early in your trip, see how the heat feels, and then decide whether a longer hike or a full desert afternoon makes sense. Families with young children, in particular, tend to find the cooler edges of the day far easier.

### November: the outdoor season opens

By November the day is comfortable enough for most outdoor plans, from desert evenings to long walks in the old quarters. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that rainfall, while sporadic, falls mainly [between November and March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), averaging 12cm a year in most of the emirate, so this is also when the occasional shower returns. Nights are still mild, but carry a light layer for the desert.

November is also when the calendar fills up and hotels with it. If you want the comfort of the new season without the December crowds, the first half of the month is often the best balance.

## Heat sense for early autumn

Early autumn heat is easy to underestimate because the calendar says the season has turned. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks and avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), as well as wearing light-coloured, loose clothing and avoiding too much alcohol and extreme exercise. It describes heatstroke as a medical emergency, and says to check on children, older people and people with long-term health conditions, who are more at risk.

Know the warning signs. The NHS lists tiredness, dizziness, headache, feeling sick, heavy sweating, cramps and intense thirst among the symptoms of heat exhaustion, and advises moving the person somewhere cool, giving them water and cooling their skin. If they are not feeling better within 30 minutes, or show signs such as confusion or hot skin that is not sweating, treat it as an emergency. In the UAE, the Foreign Office says to [dial 998 and ask for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health).

The sea is at its warmest after the summer, which makes autumn swimming a pleasure, but the Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Swim at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and stay out of the water when red flags are flying. On tourist boats, the Foreign Office advises checking that life jackets are available for all passengers. Our [UAE heat safety guide](/stories/uae-heat-safety-guide/) covers more.

## What autumn does best

- **Beach holidays.** Warm water, softer sun and quieter beaches make late October and November ideal.
- **City breaks with an outdoor edge.** Museums at midday and waterfronts after dark work all autumn.
- **The first desert evenings.** By November, dune drives and camp dinners feel inviting again. See our guide to whether [a desert safari is worth it](/stories/is-a-desert-safari-worth-it/).
- **The northern emirates.** Ras Al Khaimah's coast and mountains suit autumn well, with sunsets the tourism board singles out for this season.
- **Islands and mangroves.** Kayaking and boat trips around Abu Dhabi's islands become comfortable again once the worst of the heat has gone.

### How an autumn day unfolds

The shape of a good day shifts through the season. In September and early October, think of it as a summer day: a swim or walk at sunrise, indoor sights through the middle of the day, and the waterfront after dark. By late October, the outdoor windows stretch: you can stay on the beach until late morning and head out again by mid-afternoon. By November, most of the day works outside, with the strongest sun around lunchtime the only part to plan around.

Sunsets come earlier as the weeks pass, so for desert or mountain trips check the time the sun goes down and work back from it. A dune drive that ends at dusk and a camp dinner under the first cool night air is one of the season's best experiences.

## The events season begins

Autumn is when the UAE's calendar starts filling up again. Visit Abu Dhabi says most major concerts in the capital take place [between October and April](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events), and most major sports events between October and March. Its events listing shows the Abu Dhabi Festival running from 10 October to 19 December, the Abu Dhabi Comedy Season listed on 11 October, Frieze Abu Dhabi from 19 to 22 November and the Formula 1 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix from 3 to 6 December, the last two of which bring crowds and higher demand for rooms.

In Dubai, the Visit Dubai events calendar lists the [Dubai Fitness Challenge](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/whats-on/dubai-events-calendar/dubai-fitness-challenge), a citywide invitation to get active with all of Dubai, running in 2026 from 31 October to 29 November. Check the programme for your dates if you would like to join residents in an organised activity.

If your trip runs into early December, the UAE government lists [National Day on 2 and 3 December](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) as public holidays. Our guide to [December in the UAE](/stories/uae-in-december/) picks up from there.

## Desert trips as the season opens

Desert camps and evening safaris tend to come back into their own as the evenings cool. Visit Abu Dhabi says the cooler months are [ideal for outdoor activities and desert experiences](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) in Al Dhafra, the region that includes the Liwa dunes, and that driving there from Abu Dhabi city typically takes around two to three hours.

Whoever you go with, the Foreign Office says desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and leaving a copy of your plans with someone. For dune drives and quad biking, it recommends a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures and checking that the activity is covered by your insurance. In early autumn, ask what time the trip starts and how much shade and water there is at camp.

## When rain arrives in late autumn

Rain is unusual but not impossible from November onwards, and it can be heavy when it comes. The Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en) and following local advice. Mountain roads and wadis are the places to avoid during heavy rain.

## Who autumn suits, and who should wait

- **Ideal for:** beach lovers who like warm water, couples and families who prefer quieter attractions, and anyone who wants the first cool evenings without peak-season crowds. Late October and November are the sweet spot.
- **Fine with planning:** city breaks in September and early October, as long as you live by the clock and keep the middle of the day indoors.
- **Better to wait if:** you want long hikes, full-day desert trips or walking tours in the midday sun. Those come into their own from November, and more reliably from December.
- **Take extra care if:** you are travelling with babies, older relatives or anyone with a heart or breathing condition in September or October, when the heat still behaves like summer.

### Combining places in an autumn trip

Autumn suits a two-centre trip. A few nights in Dubai or Abu Dhabi for museums, food and waterfront evenings combine well with time on the coast or in the mountains of Ras Al Khaimah, where the tourism board picks out autumn sunsets and mountain trips. On the east coast, early morning swims and snorkelling make the most of the warm sea. Later in the season, add a night in the desert once the evenings have cooled, and save a long drive to Al Dhafra for November, when the dunes are comfortable enough to enjoy in daylight.

Allow for the shortening days. In September the evenings are long; by late November the sun goes down early, so schedule drives and outdoor plans with that in mind.

## Packing for an autumn trip

Pack summer clothes for September and October, with a light layer for November evenings and for the air conditioning. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests taking a [jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), because the air conditioning can be cold. A hat, sunglasses and a refillable water bottle earn their place in every month, and Visit Ras Al Khaimah calls sun protection essential all year round.

Choose clothes that work for the heat and for local custom at the same time. The Foreign Office advises [dressing modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) such as malls, with women covering shoulders and knees, and says swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at pools, so keep the beach kit for the sand and the pool. Loose linen or cotton that covers shoulders and knees does both jobs well.

## Quick answers

**Is autumn a good time to visit the UAE?**
Yes, especially from mid-October. Visit Abu Dhabi says the milder autumn weather calls for beach outings and watersports, and Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives highs of around 30°C by the end of November.

**Is September still too hot?**
For most visitors it feels like summer. Visit Abu Dhabi's summer guide covers June to September, with intense heat and high humidity along the coast.

**Is the sea warm in autumn?**
Yes, the sea is still warm after summer. Swim only at lifeguarded beaches, as the Foreign Office warns rip currents can occur at any beach.

**When does the events season start?**
Visit Abu Dhabi says most major concerts run between October and April and most major sports events between October and March.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which outdoor activities do you run in September and October, and at what times of day?
- When do your desert camps and evening safaris restart their full programme after summer?
- Do our dates overlap with major events such as the Grand Prix or National Day, and how will that affect traffic and availability?
- How much shade and water is provided on boat and beach trips in early autumn?
- If an early-season rain warning is issued, how do you handle rescheduling?
