On the first days of December, the flags appear. They hang from tower blocks and lamp posts, flutter from car windows, and turn up as ribbons, scarves and painted faces. By 2 December the whole country is dressed in red, green, white and black.

That is the start of the UAE's busiest month. December in the UAE brings comfortable weather, National Day celebrations, a run of big festivals and, at the end, New Year. It is one of the best months to visit for sightseeing, the desert and the beach, and also the hardest to book. The short answer: come in December if you want the season at its liveliest, but book early and plan around the busiest days.

Below we cover the weather and daylight, what National Day means and how to join in, the main festival dates for December 2026, New Year's Eve, and the practical side of travelling in the year's peak weeks.

## December weather in the UAE

Visit Abu Dhabi dates winter in the capital [from December to mid-March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), with an average daytime temperature of 25°C and occasional rainfall. It says beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in winter.

In the north, Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives winter daytime highs of [around 25°C to 30°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), with overnight temperatures of around 13°C to 15°C, and winter sea temperatures of around 22°C to 25°C. The same guide notes that December has the shortest day of the year, with 10.5 hours of daylight on 21 December, so plan outdoor trips with an earlier sunset in mind.

Desert evenings and mountain mornings are much cooler than the coast. The tourism board says that high in the Hajar Mountains, [below-freezing temperatures and even snow](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/) in winter are not unheard of, and recommends warm layers and a hat.

### What the short days mean for your plans

With the sun setting early, December days have a definite shape. Mornings are cool and fresh, the middle of the day is warm enough for the beach, and the light starts to go by late afternoon. Put mountain drives, hikes and desert trips at the start of the day or time them to finish at sunset, and keep evenings for markets, waterfronts and dinner. A late start on a day trip can easily mean driving home in the dark.

## National Day: 2 and 3 December

The UAE government lists [National Day on 2 and 3 December](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) as public holidays for both the public and private sectors. It says National Day marks the start of the federal unification of the emirates in 1971, and that "Spirit of the Union" is the official theme of the celebrations. The government explains that the theme comes from the vision of the late Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the Founder President, and is paired with a logo showing the first gathering of the Founding Fathers on 2 December 1971.

### What visitors will notice

- **Flags and decorations** on buildings, cars and streets, often from late November.
- **Celebrations and gatherings** in public spaces, with families out in force.
- **Busier roads and attractions**, as residents enjoy the holiday.

National Day is a warm, proud and welcoming occasion, and visitors are very much part of the crowd. Join in respectfully, dress modestly in public, and be patient with traffic. Our guide to [UAE National Day for visitors](/stories/uae-national-day-for-visitors/) explains how to enjoy it.

### Etiquette in the crowds

A few rules matter more than usual when the streets are full. The Foreign Office advises [dressing modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), with women covering shoulders and knees, and says not to photograph people without their permission. It also notes that swearing and rude gestures are illegal, including online, and that it is illegal to drink or be under the influence of alcohol in a public place anywhere in the UAE. In Sharjah, drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal altogether. Keep celebrations good-humoured, ask before you take close-up photos of families, and cross roads only at designated crossings, which the Foreign Office says is a legal requirement.

## The December festival calendar

December is packed. Visit Abu Dhabi says that [November through March](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events) is when the emirate's events calendar is fullest. A few dates that stand out for December 2026:

### Formula 1 in Abu Dhabi

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="uae-in-december-3" alts="ALT3" caption="CAP3"></photo-set>

The Visit Abu Dhabi events listing shows the [Formula 1 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events) from 3 to 6 December. Yas Island and the capital's hotels are very busy that week, so if you are not going to the race, consider staying elsewhere or visiting Abu Dhabi on different dates. Race week also overlaps with the National Day holiday, which makes the first week of December one of the busiest of the year on the roads between Dubai and Abu Dhabi.

### Culture and family events in the capital

The same listing shows the Abu Dhabi Festival running until 19 December, the Culture Summit Abu Dhabi from 8 to 10 December, and a family festival programme from 9 December into early January. Visit Abu Dhabi also mentions outdoor markets during the winter months among its family events. If you are planning a cultural trip, the second and third weeks of December are a good window, after the Grand Prix and before the New Year rush.

### Dubai Shopping Festival

The Visit Dubai events calendar lists the [Dubai Shopping Festival](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/whats-on/dubai-events-calendar/dubai-shopping-festival), a citywide season of shopping deals and entertainment, running this winter from 4 December to 17 January. Expect busier malls and evening entertainment through the whole second half of the month.

### Liwa International Festival

Visit Abu Dhabi says that [each December the Liwa International Festival](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) brings three weeks of desert motorsports, cultural experiences, concerts and fireworks to the edge of the Empty Quarter. Its events page lists the festival from 11 December to 2 January. If you want the big dunes in peace, go before or after; if you want atmosphere, go during. Our [Liwa and Empty Quarter guide](/stories/liwa-empty-quarter-guide/) has more.

Getting there takes planning. Visit Abu Dhabi says driving from Abu Dhabi city to Al Dhafra typically takes around two to three hours, and that a car is the most convenient way to explore because attractions are spread across a wide area. During the festival, book a room well ahead and allow extra time on the road.

## New Year in the UAE

The UAE government lists [Gregorian New Year on 1 January](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) as a public holiday. New Year's Eve is a major night across the country, with fireworks and celebrations at waterfronts and landmarks, and it is the single busiest evening of the season.

A few practical habits help:

- **Pick your spot early.** Popular viewing areas fill hours before midnight.
- **Leave the car behind.** Roads close and traffic builds; public transport and walking are usually easier.
- **Book dinner well ahead.** Many venues sell special New Year packages and fill early.
- **Check official announcements** close to the date for road closures, transport times and viewing areas.

If you would rather avoid the biggest crowds, consider spending New Year's Eve away from the main city landmarks, on a quieter beach or at a desert camp, and watching a smaller display or simply the stars. Families with young children often find an early-evening celebration and a quiet night far easier than a midnight crush.

## Peak season: booking and crowds

December combines perfect weather with public holidays, festivals and the school holidays of many countries. That means the highest demand of the year for flights, rooms, desert camps and guided trips, especially from National Day through New Year.

- Book accommodation and flights as early as you can.
- Reserve desert camps, mountain trips and popular attractions before you travel.
- Plan big sights for early morning on weekdays, when queues are shortest.
- Leave slack in your schedule for holiday traffic.

If you are deciding where to base yourself, our guide to [Dubai or Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) weighs up the two cities.

### Desert and mountain trips in December

December is one of the best months for the desert and the Hajar Mountains, which is why trips sell out. The Foreign Office says desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and leaving a copy of your plans with someone. For dune drives and quad biking, it recommends a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures, and suggests checking that the activity is covered by your insurance.

For a camp night, pack as if for a cold evening, and ask about heating, blankets and shelter. For a mountain sunrise, leave early, take a warm hat and expect to wait in the cold before the sun appears.

### Escaping the busiest places

December crowds concentrate around the big city landmarks, the Grand Prix, New Year's Eve viewing areas and the main festivals. Quieter options are rarely far away. Weekday mornings at museums and heritage districts are calmer than evenings and weekends. The mountains of Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah, and the beaches of the east coast, make a good break between city stays. Liwa is lively during its festival and far quieter before it begins on 11 December. Al Ain, inland from Abu Dhabi, is another calmer base for a couple of days. Even in the cities, a sunrise walk along a corniche or creek is peaceful while most visitors are still asleep.

### Who December suits

- **Ideal for:** first-time visitors who want comfortable weather for everything, festival lovers, families on school holidays, and anyone who enjoys a lively, celebratory atmosphere.
- **Fine with planning:** travellers on a tight budget or with fixed dates, who should book as far ahead as possible and compare the start of the month with the holiday weeks before committing.
- **Think twice if:** you want empty sights and easy last-minute bookings. Late November or January may suit you better, outside the busiest weeks.

## Packing for December

Visit Abu Dhabi says lightweight clothing suits most of the year, but [something slightly warmer may be needed](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) in the winter months, and suggests a jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas. For December, pack:

- Light layers for daytime and a warm jacket for evenings.
- Proper warm clothing for desert camps or mountain sunrises.
- Swimwear and sun cream, because beach days are still very much on.
- Modest outfits for National Day crowds, mosques and heritage sites. See [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/).

Swimming is still a December pleasure, with the sea in the low to mid twenties. The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and never when red flags are flying.

## If it rains

December falls in the wetter part of the year, though rain is still occasional. Visit Abu Dhabi says rainfall averages 12cm a year in most of the emirate and falls mainly between November and March. When it is heavy, the Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en) and following local advice. Mountain drives and wadi trips are the plans most likely to change.

Keep the emergency numbers to hand. The UAE government lists [999 for police, 998 for ambulance and 997 for civil defence](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays).

## Quick answers

**Is December a good time to visit the UAE?**
Yes, for weather and atmosphere. Visit Abu Dhabi says beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in winter, which runs from December to mid-March. It is also peak season, so book early.

**When is UAE National Day?**
The UAE government lists National Day on 2 and 3 December as public holidays.

**Is New Year's Day a holiday in the UAE?**
Yes. The government lists Gregorian New Year on 1 January as a public holiday.

**What festivals are on in December?**
For 2026, Visit Abu Dhabi lists the Formula 1 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix from 3 to 6 December and the Liwa International Festival from 11 December to 2 January, and Visit Dubai lists the Dubai Shopping Festival from 4 December to 17 January.

**How warm is it in December?**
Visit Abu Dhabi gives an average winter daytime temperature of 25°C, and Visit Ras Al Khaimah says winter nights drop to around 13°C to 15°C.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How will National Day, the Grand Prix or New Year affect traffic, pickups and opening times on our dates?
- How far ahead do we need to book desert camps and day trips in December?
- How cold does it get at the desert camp or mountain viewpoint at night in December, and what should we bring?
- Do you run trips on 2 and 3 December or on New Year's Day, and do timings change?
- If rain or a weather warning disrupts a trip, what are the options to reschedule in such a busy month?
- Can you suggest quieter alternatives to the busiest sights during the holiday weeks?
