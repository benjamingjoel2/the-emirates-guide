In early March you can still sit in the sun at midday and think, this is perfect. By late May the same bench is empty at noon, and the shade under the palms has become the most valuable real estate in town.

Spring is the season of that change. The UAE in spring, from March to May, starts out feeling like winter and ends feeling like summer, so the right trip depends on which end you land in. March is still one of the most comfortable months of the year. April is warm, with lovely mornings and evenings. May belongs to people who are happy to swim early, retreat indoors at midday and come out again at dusk. In 2027 spring also holds the end of Ramadan, Eid Al Fitr and Eid Al Adha, which shape the mood and the crowds.

This guide walks through the season month by month, explains what the 2027 religious calendar means for visitors, and covers the practical side: where to find cooler air, how to pace a day as the heat builds, and what to pack.

## How spring weather unfolds in the UAE

Visit Abu Dhabi places the end of winter in mid-March and describes [spring, from mid-March to May](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), as the season when temperatures start to rise, with sporadic rainfall in March. It says summertime starts in late May.

Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives a clearer sense of the pace. It says that at the end of March typical daily highs [start to reach over 30°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), and that by the end of May the average daily high is around 40°C, while the chance of rain falls to 1 to 2 percent per day. Days lengthen through the season too, towards the longest day on 21 June, which the same guide puts at about 13.75 hours of daylight.

### March: winter's last good weeks

Early March is effectively still winter, with comfortable days and cool evenings. It is a strong month for the things that are hardest in summer: desert evenings, long old-town walks, hikes and day trips to Al Ain or the east coast. By the end of the month the sun has more bite at midday, so start outdoor plans early.

March is also the month most likely to bring a shower, and in 2027 its first days fall at the end of Ramadan. Neither is a reason to avoid it, but both are reasons to keep a little flexibility in your plans.

### April: warm days and golden evenings

April feels properly warm. Mornings and late afternoons are excellent for sightseeing, the sea is pleasant, and beach days come easily. Build a long shaded lunch or a museum into the middle of each day, then head back out as the light softens.

For many visitors, April is the best compromise of the year: warm enough for the beach every day, still comfortable for an early hike, and less crowded than the winter peak. It is also a good month for the east coast, where a morning swim and an afternoon drive through the mountains fit easily into one day.

### May: the day splits in two

May is the turn towards summer. With highs heading towards the figure Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives for late May, the day divides neatly: outdoors early and late, indoors in between. It is still a fine month for a beach-focused trip if you accept that rhythm.

Desert trips in May shift towards the evening, and hikes need a dawn start and a plan to be off the mountain well before midday. If your heart is set on long walks or full outdoor days, March or early April will suit you far better.

## Mountains and sea: where spring stays comfortable

As the coast warms, altitude helps. Visit Ras Al Khaimah suggests that in April and May there are plenty of chances to cool off in the sea, a pool or the mountains, and says temperatures on [Jebel Jais are 10 degrees lower](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/) than in the rest of the emirate. Our [Jebel Jais guide](/stories/jebel-jais-guide/) explains how to plan a day up there.

Hatta, in the Hajar Mountains of Dubai emirate, is another spring favourite, with rocky scenery and the lake at Hatta Dam. Go early in the day, especially from April onwards, and take plenty of water. See our [Hatta guide](/stories/hatta-guide/) for what to do there.

The sea is the other natural air conditioner. Spring water is comfortably warm without the bath-like feel of high summer, and a swim at either end of the day is one of the pleasures of the season. The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and never when red flags are flying.

## Ramadan and two Eids: the 2027 spring calendar

Islamic holidays follow the lunar calendar and move earlier each year, so spring 2027 is busy with them. All of the dates below are projections confirmed only by moon sighting.

- **Ramadan:** According to [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/10/06/ramadan-2027-uae-dates-eid-al-fitr-holiday/), the UAE's Astronomy Centre projects Ramadan 2027 will begin on 8 February 2027 and probably last until 8 or 9 March.
- **Eid Al Fitr:** The same report says Eid Al Fitr is expected to start on 9 or 10 March 2027.
- **Eid Al Adha:** Khaleej Times reports the Astronomy Centre's calculation that [Eid Al Adha is likely on 16 or 17 May 2027](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/ramadan-eid-al-fitr-eid-al-adha-2027-likely-dates), with the Day of Arafah on 15 or 16 May.

The UAE government lists Eid Al Fitr, Arafah Day and Eid Al Adha among its [official public holidays](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays), and notes that Islamic holidays are determined according to moon sighting. It gives Eid Al Fitr as the first to third days of Shawwal, and says that if Ramadan lasts 30 days, the 30th day is also treated as an official holiday. The National points out that, combined with a weekend, this could produce a break of up to six days for residents.

### What that means for visitors

An early March trip may catch the last days of Ramadan. Days are quieter and evenings lively, and the Foreign Office lists behaviours that [may cause offence during Ramadan](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), such as eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime, and advises checking opening hours. It also warns that driving may be erratic around dusk, as people hurry home to break the fast, and that fasting can cause tiredness, particularly in the late afternoon and early evening. Visit Abu Dhabi recommends [dressing more conservatively during Ramadan](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). Our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) covers the details.

The evenings are the reward. The government portal describes iftar, the meal that breaks the fast at sunset, traditionally [beginning with dates](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan), and explains that the firing of the iftar cannon, which signals the moment to break the fast, takes place in many parts of the country. People greet each other with "Ramadan Kareem" or "Ramadan Mubarak", meaning blessed Ramadan, and visitors are welcome to use the greeting too.

Eid brings a festive, family atmosphere, with residents on holiday and popular places busier than usual. Book rooms and tours early if your dates overlap, and read our guide to [Eid in the UAE for visitors](/stories/eid-in-the-uae-for-visitors/).

### Eid Al Adha in May

Eid Al Adha falls a day after the Day of Arafah, which Khaleej Times describes as the most holy day in Islam, a day of fasting for most Muslims not performing Hajj. In 2027 that puts the holiday in the warmest part of spring, so expect families to head for malls, beaches and the mountains, and plan your own outdoor time for the early morning.

## Rain, dust and changing skies

Spring is drier than winter, but March can still bring showers. Visit Abu Dhabi says rainfall is sporadic and falls mainly [between November and March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). When rain is heavy, the Foreign Office warns that it [can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en). Keep wadi walks and mountain drives flexible in March in particular.

Spring can also bring hazy days, when the horizon softens and long views from towers and mountains are less sharp. If a viewpoint matters to you, leave room to try another day.

## Events and the cultural season

Visit Abu Dhabi describes spring in the capital as a busy time for [cultural events and trade shows](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), and says on its events page that most major concerts take place [between October and April](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events). The same page notes that the national exhibition centre hosts the emirate's major trade shows and conferences through the year, and that Louvre Abu Dhabi runs temporary exhibitions alongside its permanent collection.

That makes March and April a good time to combine sightseeing with a performance or an exhibition, but large conferences can also fill hotels, so check the calendar before you fix your dates.

## How a spring day unfolds

The further into spring you travel, the more your day should bend around the sun.

- **Early morning:** the best time for hikes, mountain viewpoints, heritage walks and a swim before the beach fills.
- **Late morning:** in March, keep going outdoors; by May, move inside to a museum, gallery or covered souk.
- **Early afternoon:** a long lunch and a rest, especially with children.
- **Late afternoon:** back out for the golden hour on a beach, corniche or dune.
- **Evening:** waterfronts, markets and dinner outdoors, which stay pleasant right through the season.

### Families and spring travel

Spring is a popular time for family trips, partly because it overlaps with school holidays in many countries. March is easy with children; by May, the heat needs the same respect as summer. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks and avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), and says to check on children, older people and anyone with a long-term health condition, as they are more at risk of heat exhaustion. Plan shaded play, swims and indoor attractions for the middle of the day.

## Day trips and combinations that suit spring

Spring rewards a trip that moves around. In March, an inland day to Al Ain, with its oasis and the drive up Jebel Hafeet, fits comfortably between a morning start and an evening return; Visit Abu Dhabi notes that rain is more common around Al Ain than elsewhere in the emirate because it sits near the Hajar mountains, so check the forecast first. In April, pair a city stay with a few nights on the east coast or in Ras Al Khaimah, where mountains and sea sit close together. By May, think of the mountains and the beach as your outdoor options, and the cities as places for museums, food and evenings.

Whatever you choose, plan drives for the morning, arrive before the midday heat, and leave time for a swim or a rest before heading out again at dusk.

## Packing and pacing for spring

Pack for warmth with a little insurance against cool evenings in March and cold air conditioning all season. Visit Abu Dhabi recommends lightweight clothing for most of the year and a [jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). Visit Ras Al Khaimah suggests light layers for cooler evenings and warm layers for the mountains, and calls sun protection essential all year round.

As temperatures climb through April and May, heat sense becomes important. The NHS also advises wearing light-coloured, loose clothing and avoiding extreme exercise. That is a good rule for late-spring sightseeing as well as for summer, and loose clothes that cover shoulders and knees also suit local dress expectations in malls and public places.

- **March:** plan full outdoor days, with a light layer for the evening.
- **April:** outdoors early and late, with a long break in the middle.
- **May:** swim and sightsee at the edges of the day, museums at midday.

## Quick answers

**Is spring a good time to visit the UAE?**
Yes, especially March and April. Visit Abu Dhabi describes spring as the season when temperatures start to rise, and Visit Ras Al Khaimah says highs pass 30°C by the end of March and reach around 40°C by the end of May.

**Is May too hot for a UAE holiday?**
It is hot but manageable for beach and city trips if you stay out of the midday sun. Visit Abu Dhabi says summertime starts in late May.

**When is Ramadan in 2027?**
The National reports a projected start of 8 February 2027, ending around 8 or 9 March, with Eid Al Fitr expected on 9 or 10 March. Dates are confirmed by moon sighting.

**When is Eid Al Adha in 2027?**
Khaleej Times reports the Astronomy Centre's calculation of 16 or 17 May 2027, subject to moon sighting.

**Does it rain in spring?**
Occasionally in March. Visit Abu Dhabi says rainfall falls mainly between November and March.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How do you adjust start times for desert, mountain and boat trips as the weather warms through April and May?
- Do our dates overlap with the end of Ramadan, Eid Al Fitr or Eid Al Adha, and how will that change opening hours and crowds?
- Which mountain or coastal options would you suggest for a late-spring trip?
- If a rain or haze warning is issued, what is your policy on rescheduling outdoor activities?
- How much shade, water and rest time is built into your full-day tours in May?
