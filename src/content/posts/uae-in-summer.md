The hotel doors slide open and the air hits you like the breath from an oven. Your sunglasses mist over. By the time you reach the taxi, a few steps away, you understand why nobody here walks anywhere at two in the afternoon.

That is summer in the UAE, and it is not for everyone. But visiting the UAE in summer can be worth it if you go in with the right expectations: quieter attractions, a city built to keep you cool indoors, warm seas and long evenings outside once the sun drops. What it is not is a season for long outdoor sightseeing days. If you can live by the clock, summer works. If you want to hike, walk the souks at noon or spend all day on a beach, choose another month.

Below we look honestly at the heat, at the good reasons to come anyway, at how a summer day actually runs, and at the safety habits that matter most, with notes for families and anyone travelling with older relatives.

## How hot is a UAE summer, honestly?

Very. Visit Abu Dhabi's summer guide says that [from June to September you can expect intense heat](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) and relatively high humidity, especially along the coast, with average daytime highs ranging from 38°C in June to 41°C in August. It names July or August as generally the hottest month, with temperatures rising to 41°C to 42°C. The board's main weather page dates summer from late May to September, says daytime temperatures can soar to 45°C and higher, and adds that humidity levels can reach [80 to 90 percent](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi).

The northern emirates are no gentler. Visit Ras Al Khaimah calls June to September [the hottest months](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), with daily highs over 40°C, high humidity because of the Gulf coast, and evenings that only drop to nearer 30°C.

### What the numbers feel like

Figures on a page do not prepare you for humidity. On the coast, the air can feel thick and damp even after dark, cameras and glasses fog when you step out of air conditioning, and shirts stay damp long after a short walk. Inland the air tends to be drier, but the sun is harsher and reflects off sand and stone. Either way, the practical effect is the same: outdoor time comes in short, planned doses.

Days are long, too. Visit Ras Al Khaimah notes that the longest day of the year, on 21 June, has about 13.75 hours of daylight. That gives you a usable early morning and a long, warm evening, with the hottest stretch in between.

### Why the midday hours are different

One sign of how seriously the country takes the heat: every summer the UAE requires a midday break for outdoor workers. The National reported that in 2026 the ban on working outdoors [between 12.30pm and 3pm ran from 15 June to 15 September](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/06/04/midday-break-for-outdoor-workers-to-begin-on-june-15/), as announced by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation. The same report says the break was introduced in 2004 and is officially known as the Occupational Heat Stress Prevention Policy. If people who live here stay out of the midday sun, so should visitors.

## The case for coming anyway

Summer has a quieter, easier side that many visitors never see.

- **Fewer crowds.** Visit Abu Dhabi points to [summer deals and fewer crowds](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) as part of the season's appeal, and the Al Dhafra page describes the warmer months as [quieter](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) and better suited to coastal stays and indoor attractions.
- **Air conditioning everywhere.** The summer guide notes that most attractions, hotels and public spaces offer an air-conditioned escape, from theme parks and malls to museums and galleries.
- **Warm water.** Visit Ras Al Khaimah says the sea reaches [highs of over 30°C in summer](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/). It feels more like a bath than a bracing dip, but early morning and evening swims are lovely.
- **Summer events.** Dubai runs a summer festival, and the Visit Dubai events calendar describes [Dubai Summer Surprises](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/whats-on/dubai-events-calendar/dubai-summer-surprises) as a season of shopping deals, family fun and thrills across the city.

If you are flying through on a stopover, visiting family, or mainly interested in museums, food and hotels, the heat matters much less. Our guide to a [48-hour UAE stopover](/stories/uae-48-hour-stopover/) shows how much fits into a short, mostly indoor visit.

### What summer is honestly bad for

It is worth being clear about the trade-offs. Long walking tours of old districts, mountain hikes, wadi scrambles, full-day desert trips and all-day beach lounging are either unpleasant or unwise between June and September. Outdoor markets and many open-air events take a break. Photography suffers too: midday light is flat and hazy, and lenses steam up when you leave a cool car. If any of those are the reason for your trip, a cooler month will serve you far better.

## How to shape a summer day

The trick is to stop fighting the sun and plan around it. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests limiting outdoor excursions during the day and [visiting popular outdoor attractions in the morning or evening](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer), heading to the beach for a dip or to the desert for an activity as temperatures drop in the evening.

A day that works might look like this:

1. **Early morning:** a walk, a swim or an outdoor landmark, finished before the heat builds.
2. **Late morning to late afternoon:** museums, galleries, malls, a long lunch and a rest.
3. **Evening:** waterfronts, souks, a desert dinner or a boat trip once the sun is low.

Getting around matters as much as the timetable. Use taxis or ride-hailing apps door to door rather than walking between sights, even short distances, and keep each outdoor stretch brief. The Foreign Office advises using only [licensed taxis or other recognised forms of public transport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), including the main ride-hailing apps. If you are hiring a car, park in shade or underground where you can and give it a minute with the air conditioning on before you set off.

### Indoor highlights that carry the middle of the day

The big cultural institutions come into their own in summer. In Abu Dhabi, Louvre Abu Dhabi and the presidential palace at Qasr Al Watan make a full, cool day; in Dubai, the Museum of the Future and the Etihad Museum do the same. Sharjah's museums are another strong choice, and our [Sharjah museums guide](/stories/sharjah-museums-guide/) explains which to pick.

Plan the museum day with the same care as an outdoor one. Arrive soon after opening, when galleries are quietest, take lunch inside, and leave the walk between buildings for the late afternoon if you can. Even short outdoor courtyards and car parks can be punishing at midday.

### Desert and mountains in summer

Desert trips still run, but timings shift towards the evening. Ask what time the dune drive starts, whether there is shade at camp and how much water is provided. The Foreign Office says desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises travelling with other cars, taking water and a mobile phone, and using a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures. In summer, that advice matters even more: a breakdown in the sand at midday is a genuine emergency.

In the mountains, Visit Ras Al Khaimah says temperatures on [Jebel Jais are 10 degrees lower](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/) than in the rest of the emirate, which makes it one of the more comfortable outdoor options. Cooler is relative, though: it is still a summer day in the desert mountains.

### Evenings: when the country comes back outside

Summer evenings have their own character. Once the sun drops, families head to waterfront promenades, parks and malls, and dinner often starts late. The evening is the time for a corniche walk, a boat on the water, a souk or a rooftop with a view. Expect it to stay warm, and on the coast humid, well into the night; Visit Ras Al Khaimah puts summer evenings at around 30°C.

## Staying safe in the heat

Heat illness can creep up quickly, even on short walks. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks, avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), wearing light-coloured, loose clothing, avoiding too much alcohol and avoiding extreme exercise. It lists symptoms of heat exhaustion including tiredness, dizziness, headache, feeling sick, excessive sweating, pale clammy skin and cramps, and says heatstroke is a medical emergency that needs immediate help.

If someone shows signs of heat exhaustion, the NHS advice is to move them somewhere cool, remove unnecessary clothing, give them plenty of water and cool their skin with water and a fan. They should start to feel better within 30 minutes. If they do not, or if they have hot skin that is not sweating, confusion, fast breathing or a seizure, treat it as heatstroke.

In the UAE, the Foreign Office says to [dial 998 and ask for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health). The UAE government's own list of emergency contacts gives [999 for police, 998 for ambulance and 997 for civil defence](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays). The Foreign Office also stresses having appropriate travel insurance, particularly if you have a health condition or are pregnant.

At the beach, warm water does not mean safe water. The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and never when red flags are flying. On tourist boats, it advises making sure life jackets are available for every passenger.

A few simple habits make a big difference:

- Carry water everywhere and keep drinking, even indoors.
- Wear a hat, loose clothing and sun cream for even short hops outside.
- Plan rest into the afternoon, especially with children.
- Keep a light layer in your bag. Visit Abu Dhabi warns that [hotels and cinemas can be cold](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) because of the air conditioning.

Our [UAE heat safety guide](/stories/uae-heat-safety-guide/) goes into more detail.

### Dressing for heat and for local custom

The good news is that cool clothes and respectful clothes are often the same thing. Loose, light-coloured cotton or linen that covers shoulders and knees keeps the sun off your skin and suits the Foreign Office's advice to [dress modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) such as malls, where it says women should cover shoulders and knees. It also says swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools. Our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) has more detail.

## Families, older travellers and health conditions

Visit Abu Dhabi presents summer as a good time for family trips, with family entertainment and summer deals, and much of the region's indoor entertainment is built with children in mind. The heat still needs managing. The NHS says to [check on children, older people and people with long-term health conditions](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), such as diabetes or heart problems, because they are more at risk of heat exhaustion or heatstroke.

In practice, that means shorter outdoor sessions, shade and water at every stop, and an afternoon rest built into the plan rather than squeezed in when someone is already flagging. Pushchairs and car seats heat up quickly in the sun, so keep them covered. If anyone in your group has a heart or breathing condition, or is pregnant, speak to a doctor before you book.

## Holidays and events in summer 2027

Islamic holidays move earlier each year, and in 2027 the Islamic New Year lands in early summer. Khaleej Times reports the Astronomy Centre's calculation that the new Hijri year is [expected to begin on 5, 6 or 7 June 2027](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/ramadan-eid-al-fitr-eid-al-adha-2027-likely-dates), depending on moon sighting. Hijri New Year is one of the UAE's official public holidays, so expect a long weekend feel and busier leisure spots. Summer also overlaps with school holidays in many countries, which keeps family attractions lively even in the hottest weeks.

## So, is it worth it? Who summer suits

- **Worth it for:** museum lovers, food-focused trips, stopovers, families happy with pools and indoor attractions, and anyone who values quieter sights over outdoor freedom.
- **Think twice if:** you want hiking, long walking tours, or a full day of desert and beach. Pick a cooler month instead, using our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/).
- **Take extra care if:** you are travelling with babies, older relatives or anyone with a heart or breathing condition. Speak to a doctor before you book.

## Quick answers

**Is it too hot to visit the UAE in summer?**
It is very hot, but manageable if you plan around it. Visit Abu Dhabi gives average daytime highs from 38°C in June to 41°C in August, and suggests keeping outdoor sights for the morning or evening.

**What is the hottest month in the UAE?**
Visit Abu Dhabi says the hottest month is generally July or August.

**Can you go to the beach in summer?**
Yes, early or late in the day. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says summer sea temperatures exceed 30°C, and the Foreign Office advises swimming only at lifeguarded beaches.

**Is summer cheaper and quieter?**
Visit Abu Dhabi points to summer deals and fewer crowds. Treat that as a general pattern rather than a promise, and compare prices for your dates.

**What number do you call in a medical emergency?**
The Foreign Office says to dial 998 and ask for an ambulance.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What time does each outdoor activity start and finish in our month, and is any of it in the midday heat?
- How much shade, water and rest time is built into desert or boat trips in summer?
- Are your vehicles air-conditioned, and what happens if someone in our group feels unwell from the heat?
- Which indoor sights would you pair with our outdoor plans to fill the middle of the day?
- Are any of your trips suspended or shortened in summer, and what is the alternative?
- Are your guides trained in first aid for heat exhaustion?
