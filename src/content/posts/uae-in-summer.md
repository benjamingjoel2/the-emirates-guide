The hotel doors slide open and the air hits you like the breath from an oven. Your sunglasses mist over. By the time you reach the taxi, a few steps away, you understand why nobody here walks anywhere at two in the afternoon.

That is summer in the UAE, and it is not for everyone. But visiting the UAE in summer can be worth it if you go in with the right expectations: quieter attractions, a city built to keep you cool indoors, warm seas and long evenings outside once the sun drops. What it is not is a season for long outdoor sightseeing days. If you can live by the clock, summer works. If you want to hike, walk the souks at noon or spend all day on a beach, choose another month.

## How hot is a UAE summer, honestly?

Very. Visit Abu Dhabi's summer guide says that [from June to September you can expect intense heat](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) and relatively high humidity, especially along the coast, with average daytime highs ranging from 38°C in June to 41°C in August. It names July or August as generally the hottest month. The board's main weather page adds that summer humidity levels can reach [80 to 90 percent](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi).

The northern emirates are no gentler. Visit Ras Al Khaimah calls June to September [the hottest months](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), with daily highs over 40°C, high humidity because of the Gulf coast, and evenings that only drop to nearer 30°C.

### Why the midday hours are different

One sign of how seriously the country takes the heat: every summer the UAE requires a midday break for outdoor workers. The National reported that in 2026 the ban on working outdoors [between 12.30pm and 3pm ran from 15 June to 15 September](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/06/04/midday-break-for-outdoor-workers-to-begin-on-june-15/), as announced by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation. If people who live here stay out of the midday sun, so should visitors.

## The case for coming anyway

Summer has a quieter, easier side that many visitors never see.

- **Fewer crowds.** Visit Abu Dhabi points to [summer deals and fewer crowds](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer) as part of the season's appeal, and the Al Dhafra page describes the warmer months as [quieter](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) and better suited to coastal stays and indoor attractions.
- **Air conditioning everywhere.** The summer guide notes that most attractions, hotels and public spaces offer an air-conditioned escape, from theme parks and malls to museums and galleries.
- **Warm water.** Visit Ras Al Khaimah says the sea reaches [highs of over 30°C in summer](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/). It feels more like a bath than a bracing dip, but early morning and evening swims are lovely.
- **Summer events.** Dubai runs a summer festival, and the Visit Dubai events calendar describes [Dubai Summer Surprises](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/whats-on/dubai-events-calendar/dubai-summer-surprises) as a citywide season of shopping deals and family fun.

If you are flying through on a stopover, visiting family, or mainly interested in museums, food and hotels, the heat matters much less. Our guide to a [48-hour UAE stopover](/stories/uae-48-hour-stopover/) shows how much fits into a short, mostly indoor visit.

## How to shape a summer day

The trick is to stop fighting the sun and plan around it. Visit Abu Dhabi suggests limiting outdoor excursions during the day and [visiting popular outdoor attractions in the morning or evening](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer), heading to the beach or the desert as temperatures drop.

A day that works might look like this:

1. **Early morning:** a walk, a swim or an outdoor landmark, finished before the heat builds.
2. **Late morning to late afternoon:** museums, galleries, malls, a long lunch and a rest.
3. **Evening:** waterfronts, souks, a desert dinner or a boat trip once the sun is low.

### Indoor highlights that carry the middle of the day

The big cultural institutions come into their own in summer. In Abu Dhabi, Louvre Abu Dhabi and the presidential palace at Qasr Al Watan make a full, cool day; in Dubai, the Museum of the Future and the Etihad Museum do the same. Sharjah's museums are another strong choice, and our [Sharjah museums guide](/stories/sharjah-museums-guide/) explains which to pick.

### Desert and mountains in summer

Desert trips still run, but timings shift towards the evening. Ask what time the dune drive starts, whether there is shade at camp and how much water is provided.

In the mountains, Visit Ras Al Khaimah says temperatures on [Jebel Jais are 10 degrees lower](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/) than in the rest of the emirate, which makes it one of the more comfortable outdoor options. Cooler is relative, though: it is still a summer day in the desert mountains.

## Staying safe in the heat

Heat illness can creep up quickly, even on short walks. The NHS advises [drinking more cold drinks, avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/), avoiding too much alcohol and avoiding extreme exercise. It lists symptoms of heat exhaustion including feeling sick, excessive sweating, pale clammy skin and cramps, and says heatstroke is a medical emergency that needs immediate help.

In the UAE, the Foreign Office says to [dial 998 and ask for an ambulance](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health). It also stresses having appropriate travel insurance, particularly if you have a health condition or are pregnant.

At the beach, warm water does not mean safe water. The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises swimming only at approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and never when red flags are flying.

A few simple habits make a big difference:

- Carry water everywhere and keep drinking, even indoors.
- Wear a hat, loose clothing and sun cream for even short hops outside.
- Plan rest into the afternoon, especially with children.
- Keep a light layer in your bag. Visit Abu Dhabi warns that [hotels and cinemas can be cold](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) because of the air conditioning.

Our [UAE heat safety guide](/stories/uae-heat-safety-guide/) goes into more detail.

## So, is it worth it? Who summer suits

- **Worth it for:** museum lovers, food-focused trips, stopovers, families happy with pools and indoor attractions, and anyone who values quieter sights over outdoor freedom.
- **Think twice if:** you want hiking, long walking tours, or a full day of desert and beach. Pick a cooler month instead, using our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/).
- **Take extra care if:** you are travelling with babies, older relatives or anyone with a heart or breathing condition. Speak to a doctor before you book.

## Quick answers

**Is it too hot to visit the UAE in summer?**
It is very hot, but manageable if you plan around it. Visit Abu Dhabi gives average daytime highs from 38°C in June to 41°C in August, and suggests keeping outdoor sights for the morning or evening.

**What is the hottest month in the UAE?**
Visit Abu Dhabi says the hottest month is generally July or August.

**Can you go to the beach in summer?**
Yes, early or late in the day. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says summer sea temperatures exceed 30°C, and the Foreign Office advises swimming only at lifeguarded beaches.

**Is summer cheaper and quieter?**
Visit Abu Dhabi points to summer deals and fewer crowds. Treat that as a general pattern rather than a promise, and compare prices for your dates.

**What number do you call in a medical emergency?**
The Foreign Office says to dial 998 and ask for an ambulance.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What time does each outdoor activity start and finish in our month, and is any of it in the midday heat?
- How much shade, water and rest time is built into desert or boat trips in summer?
- Are your vehicles air-conditioned, and what happens if someone in our group feels unwell from the heat?
- Which indoor sights would you pair with our outdoor plans to fill the middle of the day?
- Are any of your trips suspended or shortened in summer, and what is the alternative?
- Are your guides trained in first aid for heat exhaustion?
