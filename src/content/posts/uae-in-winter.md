At noon you are eating lunch on a terrace in short sleeves. Six hours later, on a dune in the desert, you are pulling a fleece over your head and wondering where the warmth went.

That swing is the UAE in winter. December to February is the season most visitors picture when they think of the Emirates: blue skies, comfortable days for walking, beaches you can actually lie on, and evenings outdoors. It is also the busiest time of year, with festivals, full hotels and a calendar that rewards a little planning. If you want the short answer, winter is the easiest time to visit the UAE for almost any kind of trip, as long as you pack for the cool nights and book early.

## What the weather does in a UAE winter

Visit Abu Dhabi, the emirate's official tourism board, dates winter in the capital [from December to mid-March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), with an average daytime temperature of 25°C and occasional rainfall. It says beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in this season.

Further north the picture is similar. Visit Ras Al Khaimah describes winter there as [very pleasant](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), with daytime highs of around 25°C to 30°C and overnight temperatures dipping to around 13°C to 15°C. It notes that winter is warm enough to swim but cool enough to explore attractions even in the middle of the day.

### Is the sea warm enough to swim?

For most people, yes. The same Ras Al Khaimah guide says winter sea temperatures [dip to around 22°C to 25°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), and that visitors and locals swim all year. It will feel bracing at first on a breezy morning, then perfectly comfortable by lunchtime.

### Cold nights in the desert and mountains

The coast is mild, but altitude and open sand change things. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says temperatures high in the Hajar Mountains are usually [around 5°C lower than elsewhere in the UAE](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), and that below-freezing temperatures and even snow in winter are not unheard of. It recommends warm layers and a warm hat for the mountains, because conditions can change quickly.

The desert behaves in a similar way after dark, losing its warmth fast once the sun goes. If an overnight camp or a sunrise hike is on your list, pack as though you are going somewhere genuinely cold.

## Packing for warm days and cool nights

Think in layers rather than in seasons. Visit Abu Dhabi says lightweight clothing works for most of the year but that [something slightly warmer may be needed for the winter months](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). It also suggests a jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas, because the air conditioning can be cold.

A practical winter bag looks like this:

- Light, breathable clothes for daytime sightseeing.
- A warm mid-layer and a light jacket for evenings, desert trips and the mountains.
- A hat and gloves if you plan to be up high before dawn.
- Swimwear and sun cream, because the winter sun is still strong.
- A scarf or shawl that doubles as cover for mosque visits.

For what to wear at religious sites and in public, see our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/).

## When the rain comes

Winter is the wettest part of the year, though wet is relative. Visit Abu Dhabi says rainfall is sporadic and [falls mainly between November and March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), and that rain is more common around Al Ain because it sits near the Hajar mountains. In Ras Al Khaimah, the tourism board notes that even in January, the wettest month, it [only rains on two or three days on average](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/).

When it does rain, it can be heavy. The UK Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en) and following local authority advice. Wadi walks and mountain drives are the plans most likely to change, so keep a spare day if you can. Our guide to [rain and flooding in the UAE](/stories/uae-rain-and-flooding-advice/) covers what to do if a warning is issued.

## The winter calendar: festivals and busy weeks

Winter is when the country comes outside, and the events calendar follows. Visit Abu Dhabi says that [November through March is when the emirate's events calendar is at its fullest](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events), with Formula 1, major concerts, cultural festivals and outdoor events clustering in the cooler months. It also mentions outdoor markets during the winter months among its family events.

### Liwa and the desert season

Out in the western region, Visit Abu Dhabi says that [each December the Liwa International Festival](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) brings three weeks of desert motorsports, cultural experiences, concerts and fireworks to the edge of the Empty Quarter. The same page says the cooler months are ideal for outdoor activities and desert experiences. Expect Liwa to be lively during the festival and much quieter either side of it.

### Dubai Shopping Festival

In Dubai, the Visit Dubai events calendar lists the [Dubai Shopping Festival](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/whats-on/dubai-events-calendar/dubai-shopping-festival) from 4 December 2026 until 17 January, citywide, with shopping deals and entertainment. If you want quieter malls, travel either side; if you enjoy a buzz, plan your Dubai days around it.

### Public holidays in the season

Two fixed public holidays fall in or near this window. The UAE government lists [National Day on 2 and 3 December](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) and Gregorian New Year on 1 January. Both bring celebrations, crowds and busier roads, and our guide to [December in the UAE](/stories/uae-in-december/) covers that month in more detail.

## Ramadan in a winter trip

Ramadan moves through the calendar, and for the next few years it falls in the cooler months. The UAE government explains that the Islamic calendar has 354 days, so [Ramadan arrives 11 days earlier](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan) in each subsequent Gregorian year. According to [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/10/06/ramadan-2027-uae-dates-eid-al-fitr-holiday/), the UAE's Astronomy Centre projects that Ramadan 2027 will begin on 8 February 2027. That is a projection, confirmed only by moon sighting.

So a February 2027 trip may well overlap with Ramadan. That is not a reason to cancel. Days are quieter, evenings are social and generous, and visitors are welcome. The Foreign Office lists behaviours that [may cause offence during Ramadan](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), including eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime, playing loud music and swearing in public, and advises checking opening hours of shops and restaurants. Our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains how to enjoy it.

## How to make the most of a winter trip

Winter is the season to do the things that are hard work the rest of the year.

- **Walk the old districts.** Long wanders through the creek-side neighbourhoods of Dubai or the heritage areas of Sharjah are a pleasure in the midday sun.
- **Go high.** The Hajar Mountains of Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah suit hiking and viewpoints, with warm layers for the cold mornings.
- **Sleep in the desert.** An overnight camp gives you stars, silence and a cold, clear sunrise.
- **Mix beaches with culture.** A morning swim and an afternoon at a museum fit comfortably into the same day.

One caution: winter popularity means prices and availability tighten, especially around the holidays. Book flights, rooms and any guided trips well ahead, and do not leave desert camps or mountain tours to the last minute. ## Quick answers

**Is winter the best time to visit the UAE?**
For outdoor sightseeing, beaches and desert trips, it is the most comfortable season. Visit Abu Dhabi says beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in winter, which it dates from December to mid-March.

**How cold does the UAE get in winter?**
On the coast it is mild: Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives daytime highs of around 25°C to 30°C and nights of around 13°C to 15°C. The mountains are cooler, and the tourism board says below-freezing temperatures and even snow are not unheard of up high.

**Can you swim in the sea in winter?**
Yes. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says winter sea temperatures dip to around 22°C to 25°C, and that visitors and locals swim all year round.

**Does it rain in the UAE in winter?**
Occasionally. Rain falls mainly between November and March, and the Foreign Office warns that heavy rain can cause flooding, so check forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology.

**Will Ramadan fall in winter?**
For 2027, The National reports a projected start of 8 February 2027, so a February trip may overlap. The date is confirmed by moon sighting.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How cold will it be at the desert camp or mountain viewpoint on our dates, and what should we bring?
- If rain or a weather warning is issued, will you reschedule, refund or cancel, and who decides?
- Do our dates overlap with the Liwa International Festival, the Dubai Shopping Festival or a public holiday, and how will that affect traffic and crowds?
- If our trip falls during Ramadan, how will the day's programme, meal times and opening hours change?
- What time does sunrise or sunset fall in our month, and how do you plan pickups around it?
- How far ahead should we book desert camps and mountain trips in peak season?
