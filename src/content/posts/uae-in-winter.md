At noon you are eating lunch on a terrace in short sleeves. Six hours later, on a dune in the desert, you are pulling a fleece over your head and wondering where the warmth went.

That swing is the UAE in winter. December to February is the season most visitors picture when they think of the Emirates: blue skies, comfortable days for walking, beaches you can actually lie on, and evenings outdoors. It is also the busiest time of year, with festivals, full hotels and a calendar that rewards a little planning. If you want the short answer, winter is the easiest time to visit the UAE for almost any kind of trip, as long as you pack for the cool nights and book early.

This guide covers what the weather actually does, how to dress for it, which festivals and holidays fall in the season, what Ramadan in early 2027 means for a February trip, and how a good winter day unfolds from sunrise to the first chill of the evening.

## What the weather does in a UAE winter

Visit Abu Dhabi, the emirate's official tourism board, dates winter in the capital [from December to mid-March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), with an average daytime temperature of 25°C and occasional rainfall. It says beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in this season.

Further north the picture is similar. Visit Ras Al Khaimah describes winter there as [very pleasant](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), with daytime highs of around 25°C to 30°C and overnight temperatures dipping to around 13°C to 15°C. It notes that winter is warm enough to swim but cool enough to explore attractions even in the middle of the day, and that skies in this part of the year are mostly clear.

### Is the sea warm enough to swim?

For most people, yes. The same Ras Al Khaimah guide says winter sea temperatures [dip to around 22°C to 25°C](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), and that visitors and locals swim all year. It will feel bracing at first on a breezy morning, then perfectly comfortable by lunchtime.

Cooler air does not change the basic beach rules. The UK Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and can sweep even strong swimmers out to sea, and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags, and never when red flags are flying. Winter winds can whip up the surf on some days, so take the flags seriously even when the water looks inviting.

### Cold nights in the desert and mountains

The coast is mild, but altitude and open sand change things. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says temperatures high in the Hajar Mountains are usually [around 5°C lower than elsewhere in the UAE](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/), and that below-freezing temperatures and even snow in winter are not unheard of. It recommends warm layers and a warm hat for the mountains, because conditions can change quickly.

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="uae-in-winter-2" alts="Layered rocky ridges of the Hajar Mountains near Jebel Jais fading into haze, with a road cut into the slope" caption="Up in the Hajar Mountains it is usually cooler than on the coast, so pack warm layers for early starts."></photo-set>

The desert behaves in a similar way after dark, losing its warmth fast once the sun goes. If an overnight camp or a sunrise hike is on your list, pack as though you are going somewhere genuinely cold. A mountain sunrise is one of the great winter experiences, but you will spend the hour before dawn standing still in the dark, which is when the cold bites hardest.

### Short days and early sunsets

Winter days are shorter than many visitors expect. Visit Ras Al Khaimah notes that the shortest day of the year, on 21 December, has [about 10.5 hours of daylight](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/). That matters more than it sounds. A mountain drive that starts after a late breakfast can leave you coming down in the dark, and a desert trip booked for "the afternoon" may mean only an hour or two of light on the dunes.

Plan the outdoor part of each day to finish before sunset, and treat the evening as the time for markets, waterfronts and dinner. If you are hiking, start early and know when the light goes.

## Packing for warm days and cool nights

Think in layers rather than in seasons. Visit Abu Dhabi says lightweight clothing works for most of the year but that [something slightly warmer may be needed for the winter months](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear). It also suggests a jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas, because the air conditioning can be cold.

A practical winter bag looks like this:

- Light, breathable clothes for daytime sightseeing.
- A warm mid-layer and a light jacket for evenings, desert trips and the mountains.
- A hat and gloves if you plan to be up high before dawn.
- Swimwear and sun cream, because the winter sun is still strong. Visit Ras Al Khaimah calls sun protection essential all year round.
- Comfortable shoes for old-town walks and closed footwear for rocky mountain paths.
- A scarf or shawl that doubles as cover for mosque visits.

### Dressing for sightseeing

Comfortable weather tempts people into beach clothes for the whole day, which is where visitors most often get it wrong. The Foreign Office advises [dressing modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) such as shopping malls, with women covering shoulders and knees, and says swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools. Visit Abu Dhabi adds that it is best to [cover your shoulders and legs](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) when visiting government offices.

In practice, a pair of light trousers or a long skirt and a top with sleeves will take you from a heritage museum to a souk to a café without a second thought. For what to wear at religious sites and in public, see our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/).

## When the rain comes

Winter is the wettest part of the year, though wet is relative. Visit Abu Dhabi says rainfall is sporadic and [falls mainly between November and March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), averaging 12cm a year in most of the emirate, and that rain is more common around Al Ain because it sits near the Hajar mountains. In Ras Al Khaimah, the tourism board notes that even in January, the wettest month, it [only rains on two or three days on average](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/ras-al-khaimah-weather/).

When it does rain, it can be heavy. The UK Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking forecasts from the [National Centre of Meteorology](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/?lang=en) and following local authority advice. Wadi walks and mountain drives are the plans most likely to change, so keep a spare day if you can. Our guide to [rain and flooding in the UAE](/stories/uae-rain-and-flooding-advice/) covers what to do if a warning is issued.

A rainy winter day is rarely a lost one. Swap the wadi for a museum, a long lunch or a covered souk, and save the outdoor plan for the following morning, when the air is often at its clearest.

## The winter calendar: festivals and busy weeks

Winter is when the country comes outside, and the events calendar follows. Visit Abu Dhabi says that [November through March is when the emirate's events calendar is at its fullest](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/events), with Formula 1, major concerts, cultural festivals and outdoor events clustering in the cooler months. It also mentions outdoor markets during the winter months among its family events, and its current listing includes the Kayan Wellness Festival from 29 to 31 January 2027.

### Liwa and the desert season

Out in the western region, Visit Abu Dhabi says that [each December the Liwa International Festival](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/regions/al-dhafra) brings three weeks of desert motorsports, cultural experiences, concerts and fireworks to the edge of the Empty Quarter. The same page says the cooler months are ideal for outdoor activities and desert experiences. Expect Liwa to be lively during the festival and much quieter either side of it.

Getting there takes commitment. Visit Abu Dhabi says driving from Abu Dhabi city to Al Dhafra typically takes around two to three hours, depending on where you are heading, and that a car is the most convenient way to explore because attractions are spread across a wide area. Most visitors are better off staying a night or two rather than attempting it as a day trip. Our [Liwa and Empty Quarter guide](/stories/liwa-empty-quarter-guide/) covers the route and what to see.

### Dubai Shopping Festival

In Dubai, the Visit Dubai events calendar lists the [Dubai Shopping Festival](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/whats-on/dubai-events-calendar/dubai-shopping-festival), a citywide season of shopping deals and entertainment, running this winter from 4 December to 17 January. If you want quieter malls, travel either side; if you enjoy a buzz, plan your Dubai days around it.

### Public holidays in the season

Two fixed public holidays fall in or near this window. The UAE government lists [National Day on 2 and 3 December](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) and Gregorian New Year on 1 January. Both bring celebrations, crowds and busier roads, and our guide to [December in the UAE](/stories/uae-in-december/) covers that month in more detail.

## Ramadan in a winter trip

Ramadan moves through the calendar, and for the next few years it falls in the cooler months. The UAE government explains that the Islamic calendar has 354 days, so [Ramadan arrives 11 days earlier](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan) in each subsequent Gregorian year, and that the month lasts 29 or 30 days depending on the sighting of the crescent moon. According to [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/10/06/ramadan-2027-uae-dates-eid-al-fitr-holiday/), the UAE's Astronomy Centre projects that Ramadan 2027 will begin on 8 February 2027 and probably last until 8 or 9 March. That is a projection, confirmed only by moon sighting.

So a February 2027 trip may well overlap with Ramadan. That is not a reason to cancel. Days are quieter, evenings are social and generous, and visitors are welcome. The government portal explains that there are two main meals: suhoor, eaten before sunrise, and iftar, the meal that breaks the fast at sunset, traditionally with dates. People greet each other with "Ramadan Kareem" or "Ramadan Mubarak", and you are very welcome to do the same.

The Foreign Office lists behaviours that [may cause offence during Ramadan](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), including eating, drinking, smoking or chewing gum in public in the daytime, playing loud music and swearing in public, and advises checking opening hours of shops and restaurants. It also notes that driving may be erratic around dusk as people hurry home to break the fast, and that hotels and restaurants serving food in fasting hours may screen off diners. Our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains how to enjoy it.

## How a good winter day unfolds

Winter is the season to do the things that are hard work the rest of the year, and the best days use all of the light.

- **Early morning:** a sunrise from a mountain viewpoint or a dune, or a swim before the beaches fill. Bring the warm layer.
- **Late morning:** a heritage district or an outdoor landmark, comfortable in the midday sun at this time of year.
- **Afternoon:** a museum, a long lunch, or a drive to your next base, timed to arrive before dark.
- **Late afternoon:** the golden hour on a beach, a corniche or the dunes.
- **Evening:** markets, waterfronts and dinner outdoors, with a jacket over your arm.

### What winter does best

- **Walk the old districts.** Long wanders through the creek-side neighbourhoods of Dubai or the heritage areas of Sharjah are a pleasure in the midday sun.
- **Go high.** The Hajar Mountains of Ras Al Khaimah and Fujairah suit hiking and viewpoints, with warm layers for the cold mornings.
- **Sleep in the desert.** An overnight camp gives you stars, silence and a cold, clear sunrise.
- **Mix beaches with culture.** A morning swim and an afternoon at a museum fit comfortably into the same day.

## Desert, mountain and boat trips in peak season

Winter is when most visitors book a desert trip, which makes it worth choosing carefully. The Foreign Office says desert excursions [can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive vehicle](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), advises travelling with other cars and taking water and a mobile phone, and recommends going with a reputable company with adequate health and safety procedures for dune drives or quad biking. It also suggests checking that such activities are covered by your insurance. On tourist boats, it advises making sure life jackets are available for all passengers.

If you are self-driving into the mountains, leave a copy of your plans with your hotel, fill up before you climb, and avoid wadis and steep roads if rain is forecast. On a guided trip, ask who is driving, how many vehicles travel together and what happens if the weather turns.

### Families and older travellers

Winter is the kindest season for children and older relatives, but the cold desert night catches families out more than anything else. Pack hats and an extra layer for small children, who lose heat quickly once they stop running about, and ask whether a camp has proper shelter and heating. Short days also make it easy to overfill a programme; one big outing a day, with a rest in between, tends to work better than three.

Keep the emergency numbers handy. The UAE government lists [999 for police, 998 for ambulance and 997 for civil defence](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays).

## Booking and crowds

One caution: winter popularity means prices and availability tighten, especially around the holidays. Book flights, rooms and any guided trips well ahead, and do not leave desert camps or mountain tours to the last minute. Weekday mornings are the quietest time at the big sights, and popular viewpoints are busiest at sunset on weekends and public holidays.

## Quick answers

**Is winter the best time to visit the UAE?**
For outdoor sightseeing, beaches and desert trips, it is the most comfortable season. Visit Abu Dhabi says beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are best experienced in winter, which it dates from December to mid-March.

**How cold does the UAE get in winter?**
On the coast it is mild: Visit Ras Al Khaimah gives daytime highs of around 25°C to 30°C and nights of around 13°C to 15°C. The mountains are cooler, and the tourism board says below-freezing temperatures and even snow are not unheard of up high.

**Can you swim in the sea in winter?**
Yes. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says winter sea temperatures dip to around 22°C to 25°C, and that visitors and locals swim all year round.

**Does it rain in the UAE in winter?**
Occasionally. Rain falls mainly between November and March, and the Foreign Office warns that heavy rain can cause flooding, so check forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology.

**Will Ramadan fall in winter?**
For 2027, The National reports a projected start of 8 February 2027, so a February trip may overlap. The date is confirmed by moon sighting.

## Questions to ask your operator

- How cold will it be at the desert camp or mountain viewpoint on our dates, and what should we bring?
- If rain or a weather warning is issued, will you reschedule, refund or cancel, and who decides?
- Do our dates overlap with the Liwa International Festival, the Dubai Shopping Festival or a public holiday, and how will that affect traffic and crowds?
- If our trip falls during Ramadan, how will the day's programme, meal times and opening hours change?
- What time does sunrise or sunset fall in our month, and how do you plan pickups around it?
- How far ahead should we book desert camps and mountain trips in peak season?
