At the end of November, the country quietly changes colour. Flags appear on balconies and lamp posts, cars sprout stickers in red, green, white and black, and children start turning up to school in miniature national dress.

Then, just before the party, comes a pause. Commemoration Day is a day of remembrance, and a couple of days later National Day, now officially called Eid Al Etihad, fills the streets with fireworks, music and family outings. If your trip falls at the end of November or the start of December, you will see both moods, and it is one of the most rewarding times to be a visitor.

The short version: National Day is 2 December and marks the founding of the UAE in 1971. It is a public holiday, with celebrations across all seven emirates that visitors are welcome to enjoy. Commemoration Day, just before it, honours those who died serving the nation, and calls for a quieter, respectful tone.

## What UAE National Day celebrates

On 2 December 1971, the founding rulers came together and the country was born. Every year since, that date has been the high point of the national calendar.

The UAE government portal says National Day [falls on 2 December and marks the formal nationalisation and the start of the federal unification of the emirates in 1971](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays). It describes "Spirit of the Union" as the official theme of the celebrations, derived from the vision and leadership of the late Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan, the Founder President.

### Why it is called Eid Al Etihad

You will see the name Eid Al Etihad on banners and in the media. Gulf News explains that [in 2024 the National Day celebrations were officially named Eid Al Etihad](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/why-uae-national-day-is-officially-called-eid-al-etihad-1.500344864), from the Arabic word for union, which lies at the heart of national identity. In practice, people use both names.

### Where the union was signed

If you want to stand where the story began, head to Dubai. The National describes the Etihad Museum as sitting beside Union House, [where the Constitution was signed in 1971](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/2025/08/29/dubai-visit-etihad-museum-highlights), and notes that the museum's entrance has seven pen-like columns symbolising the signing of the agreement by the seven leaders. Most of the galleries sit underground, with curving white ceilings, light wells and sunken courtyards. The same article suggests allowing time to see Union House as well as the museum. A visit in the days before National Day gives the celebrations around you much more meaning; our [Etihad Museum guide](/stories/etihad-museum-dubai/) has the practical details.

## National Day holiday dates and what they mean for you

The official list of UAE public holidays gives National Day as [2 and 3 December, two days](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays), for both the public and private sectors.

For 2026, Gulf News notes those dates fall on a Wednesday and Thursday, but adds that [the final decision on the public holiday dates will be announced by the relevant UAE government departments](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/uae-national-day-2026-how-to-turn-1-2-or-3-leave-days-into-a-longer-break-1.500645564), and that the Cabinet can move eligible holidays to the start or end of the week. Check for an official announcement shortly before you travel.

What the holiday means on the ground:

- **Busier attractions, parks and beaches**, as many residents have time off. Book popular activities ahead.
- **Heavier traffic** around celebration sites and in the evenings, especially near fireworks.
- **Some government offices closed** for the holiday days.
- **Higher demand for hotels** in popular spots, since many residents travel within the country for the long weekend.

Our guide to [UAE public holidays](/stories/uae-public-holidays-guide/) explains how the rest of the calendar works.

## Commemoration Day: a day of remembrance

Commemoration Day comes just before National Day and has a very different tone. It is solemn, not festive.

Gulf News explains that the day [honours Emirati martyrs who sacrificed their lives in service to the nation across civilian, military and humanitarian roles](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/uae-commemoration-day-2025-1.500361508), and that it was established in 2015 by a decree from the late Sheikh Khalifa bin Zayed Al Nahyan. It notes that the day is not a public holiday.

The National explains that it [originally took place on 30 November](https://www.thenationalnews.com/uae/2021/11/16/when-is-commemoration-day-and-why-is-it-celebrated-in-the-uae) to mark the death on that date in 1971 of the man thought to be the first Emirati killed in military service, and that the day is marked by a minute's silence. Across the country, government institutions and community members hold ceremonies of remembrance.

### Wahat Al Karama

In Abu Dhabi, the national memorial Wahat Al Karama stands [directly opposite the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/uae-commemoration-day-2025-1.500361508). Gulf News says it was unveiled on Commemoration Day in 2016 and honours the UAE's heroes, including soldiers, police, diplomats and civilians. Its centrepiece is a memorial of leaning tablets engraved with poems and quotations, reflected in a shallow pool in front of the mosque. It is worth a quiet visit when you see the mosque; our [Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque visitor guide](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/) covers the visit itself.

### How visitors should behave

On Commemoration Day, follow the lead of people around you. If you are in a public place during a moment of silence, stop and stand quietly. Keep celebrations, loud music and jokes about the occasion for another day.

## How to join the National Day celebrations

National Day is when the country invites everyone to celebrate, residents and visitors alike.

### Fireworks, shows and public zones

Gulf News reports that celebrations take place [across all seven emirates](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/why-uae-national-day-is-officially-called-eid-al-etihad-1.500344864), with fireworks, drone shows, cultural performances and decorations lighting up landmarks, beaches and city streets. Venues and timings change every year, so check the official Eid Al Etihad channels and local tourism boards in the days before.

### Watching the official ceremony

There is an official ceremony each year. For 2025, Gulf News reported that audiences could [watch the official ceremony live through nationwide cinema screenings, local TV channels, the Eid Al Etihad YouTube channel and the official website](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-eid-al-etihad-2025-dates-events-and-how-to-celebrate-across-all-emirates-1.500356339), with public screenings in parks and community majlis venues across the emirates. Expect a similar mix in future years.

### Where celebrations took place in 2025

Locations change from year to year, but the 2025 programme gives a useful idea of how widely the celebrations spread. Gulf News listed public screenings and events in [every emirate](https://gulfnews.com/uae/uae-eid-al-etihad-2025-dates-events-and-how-to-celebrate-across-all-emirates-1.500356339), including:

- **Abu Dhabi:** Khalifa City, Al Falah, Al Shamkha and several majlis venues, with broadcasts across Al Dhafra in places such as Madinat Zayed, Liwa and Delma Island.
- **Al Ain:** viewing near Al Ain International Airport and at majlis locations.
- **Dubai:** Al Khawaneej, among other venues.
- **Sharjah:** Al Siyouh Park and Kshisha Park.
- **Ajman:** Ajman Marina, Al Jurf Family Park and Al Warqa Public Park.
- **Umm Al Quwain:** the Creek Waterfront.
- **Ras Al Khaimah:** Al Qawasim Corniche and Flagpole Plaza.
- **Fujairah:** Umbrella Beach.

The same report describes the 2025 theme as "United", with an online hub offering activity ideas for families and communities. Look for the equivalent programme in the weeks before your trip.

### Small ways to take part

- Wear something in the flag's colours. It is a friendly gesture that people notice.
- Visit a heritage site, such as a fort or old district, where there are often cultural performances.
- Join the crowds at a public fireworks spot, arriving early.
- Say "Happy National Day" or "Eid Al Etihad Mubarak" to the people you meet.

## How the week usually unfolds

The days around National Day have a recognisable shape, and knowing it helps you plan.

In the second half of November, decorations go up. Flags appear on buildings and lamp posts, shopping centres and offices add displays, and schools hold their own celebrations, so you may see children in national dress on the way to class. Heritage areas and parks often start their programmes early.

Commemoration Day comes next, with a solemn tone. Ceremonies take place at memorials and public buildings, and the country pauses for the minute's silence. Shops and attractions usually stay open, so your plans need not change, but this is a day to be quieter in public.

Then the mood lifts. On National Day itself the official ceremony is broadcast, families head out to parks, beaches and corniches, and fireworks and drone shows fill the evening sky in many places. The holiday days that follow tend to be relaxed, with long family outings and busy restaurants.

## What catches visitors out

- **Crowds at fireworks spots.** Popular viewing points fill up well before the show. Arrive early, agree a meeting point with your group and expect slow exits afterwards.
- **Traffic after dark.** Roads near celebration sites can be gridlocked in the evening. Public transport or a walkable hotel makes life easier.
- **Assuming everything is closed.** Most attractions, malls and restaurants stay open over the holiday, often with extra events, but government offices close and some services change.
- **Last-minute bookings.** Many residents travel within the country for the long weekend, so desert camps, mountain stays and east coast hotels can fill up.
- **Noise and late nights.** Hotels near celebration zones can be lively in the evenings. If you need quiet, ask before booking.

## Families and accessibility

National Day is a family occasion, and children are welcome almost everywhere. Large crowds can still be overwhelming, so pick daytime events or smaller neighbourhood celebrations if your children are young, and bring ear defenders if they are sensitive to fireworks. Give each child your phone number on a wristband or card in case you are separated.

If anyone in your group uses a wheelchair or has limited mobility, choose venues with paved paths and step-free access, such as corniches and larger parks, and allow extra time for parking or drop-off. Watching the official ceremony on television or at a calmer screening can be a good alternative to the busiest fireworks sites.

## Heritage visits that add context

Celebrations are more meaningful when you know something of the story behind them, and the holiday period is a good time for a heritage visit. Forts, old districts and museums often host performances and family activities over National Day, and even without an event they help explain how a country of seven emirates came together.

In Abu Dhabi, the oldest stone building in the city is a natural starting point; our [Qasr Al Hosn guide](/stories/qasr-al-hosn-abu-dhabi/) explains the fort and its place in the capital's history. In Dubai, pair the Etihad Museum with a walk through the older districts by the creek. Elsewhere, look for the forts and heritage villages in each emirate, many of which decorate for the holiday.

A few tips for heritage visits over the holiday:

- **Go early in the day.** Popular sites become busier in the afternoon and evening, when families arrive for events.
- **Dress with care.** Heritage sites are family spaces, and modest clothing is expected.
- **Watch the performances respectfully.** Traditional dances and songs are a source of pride. Applaud, enjoy, and ask before photographing performers up close.
- **Check opening arrangements.** Holiday hours can differ from the usual schedule, so confirm before you travel.

## Etiquette and staying safe in the crowds

The mood is joyful, but local rules still apply.

- **Respect the flag.** Do not use it as a costume, step on it or leave it lying around.
- **Dress modestly.** The UK Foreign Office advises visitors to [dress modestly in public areas](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), covering shoulders and knees, and the same guidance applies at public celebrations.
- **Ask before photographing people.** The Foreign Office also advises [not to photograph people without their permission](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), which matters in crowds with families.
- **Take care on the roads.** Police publish rules for decorated cars and celebratory driving. An earlier Abu Dhabi Police notice, for example, prohibited [changing a vehicle's colour, covering number plates, blocking windscreens with posters and carrying more passengers than permitted](https://adpolice.gov.ae/en/Media-Center/News/2021/06/29/Abu-Dhabi-traffic-police-set-car-decoration-guidelines-for-National-Day-). If you are driving a hire car, avoid decorating it and expect slow, crowded roads.

## Quick answers

**When is UAE National Day?**
National Day falls on 2 December, and the official public holiday covers 2 and 3 December, according to the UAE government portal. Dates can be adjusted by official announcement, so check shortly before you travel.

**What is Eid Al Etihad?**
It is the official name for UAE National Day celebrations, adopted in 2024 according to Gulf News. It means the celebration of the union.

**Is Commemoration Day a public holiday?**
Gulf News reports that Commemoration Day is not a public holiday. It is a solemn day honouring those who died in service to the nation.

**Can tourists join National Day celebrations?**
Yes. Celebrations take place across all seven emirates, with fireworks, cultural performances and public screenings of the official ceremony.
