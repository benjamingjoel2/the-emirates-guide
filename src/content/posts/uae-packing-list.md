You step out of the airport and the warm air folds around you like a towel from a dryer. Twenty minutes later you are in a hotel lobby so cold you wish you had kept your jumper in your hand luggage. Welcome to packing for the UAE, where the challenge is rarely one climate but two: the one outside and the one indoors.

Get it right and you will spend your trip comfortable, respectful and ready for anything from a mosque visit to a desert evening. Get it wrong and you will be buying a scarf at the souk on day one.

The short answer to what to pack for the UAE: light, loose, breathable clothes for daytime; at least one outfit that covers shoulders and knees; a warm layer for air conditioning and winter evenings; serious sun protection; and any medicines checked against UAE rules before you fly. What changes by season is how much of each. Here is the full UAE packing list, season by season and activity by activity.

## How the seasons shape your suitcase

The UAE's seasons are not the ones you may be used to. Visit Abu Dhabi describes [winter as December to mid-March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), with comfortable temperatures, occasional rain and an average daytime temperature of 25°C. Spring runs from mid-March to May as temperatures start to climb. Summer, from late May to September, brings average daytime temperatures of around 45°C and humidity of 80 to 90%. Autumn, from September to December, sees the heat gradually ease.

It describes the climate as [sub-tropical and arid](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), with sporadic rain falling mainly between November and March. For a fuller picture of each month, see our guide to [the best time to visit the UAE](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/).

## The core UAE packing list for any time of year

Whatever the month, these earn their place.

### Clothes

- **Light, breathable tops and trousers or skirts**, in cotton or linen.
- **At least one outfit that covers shoulders and knees.** The UK Foreign Office says women should [cover shoulders and knees in public areas like shopping malls](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), that underwear should not be visible and that transparent clothing should be avoided.
- **Plain clothes without slogans.** The same advice warns against clothing with imagery or phrases that could offend.
- **Swimwear, plus a cover-up.** The Foreign Office says swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools. On the beach itself, Visit Abu Dhabi says regular swimwear, including [full-piece swimsuits or bikinis](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) for women and shorts for men, can be worn on Abu Dhabi's private and public beaches.
- **A jacket, cardigan or pashmina.** Visit Abu Dhabi advises taking [a jacket or sweater for hotels and cinemas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) because the air conditioning can be cold.

### Shoes

- Comfortable walking shoes or trainers for souks, heritage areas and long museum days.
- Sandals or slip-ons that come off easily, which help at mosques and on boats.
- Smarter shoes if you plan evenings out. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that restaurants and clubs range [from casual to black tie](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), depending on their dress code.

### Sun kit

- High-factor sun cream, lip balm with SPF and after-sun.
- Sunglasses and a wide-brimmed hat.
- A refillable water bottle.

## Winter: December to mid-March

This is peak season, and the easiest to pack for. Days are warm, but evenings can feel cool, especially in the desert, in the mountains and on the water.

Add:

- A warm jumper or fleece and a light jacket for evenings outdoors
- Long trousers for desert trips and mountain walks
- A light waterproof or compact umbrella, given the chance of winter rain
- Closed shoes if you plan to hike

Visit Abu Dhabi notes that something slightly warmer may be needed in the winter months, and says the beaches, desert trips and mountain adventures are [best experienced in winter](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi). For ideas, see our guide to [the UAE in winter](/stories/uae-in-winter/).

## Spring: mid-March to May

Spring starts pleasant and ends hot. Pack as for winter early in the season, then shift steadily towards summer kit.

- Fewer warm layers, but keep one for air conditioning and evenings
- More breathable clothing as the weeks go on
- Extra sun protection as the days heat up

Visit Abu Dhabi says spring sees [sporadic rainfall in March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi), so a light waterproof is still useful early on.

## Summer: late May to September

Summer is about managing heat. Visit Abu Dhabi says that from June to September you can expect [intense heat and relatively high humidity](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi/summer), especially along the coast, with average daytime highs from 38°C in June to 41°C in August. It advises staying hydrated and using plenty of sunscreen when outdoors.

Pack:

- The lightest, loosest clothes you own. The NHS recommends [light-coloured, loose clothing](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/) and avoiding the sun between 11am and 3pm to help prevent heat exhaustion.
- Breathable long sleeves and trousers, which protect from the sun and still meet modest dress expectations.
- Rehydration sachets.
- A cooling towel or small fan, if you feel the heat.
- An extra layer anyway, because indoors is where you will spend much of your time.

Our guide to [the UAE in summer](/stories/uae-in-summer/) has more on planning around the heat.

## Packing for what you will actually do

Seasons decide the weight of your clothes; activities decide the rest. Think through your plans day by day and add what each one needs.

### Desert trips

- Closed shoes or sandals you do not mind filling with sand
- A light scarf, useful against sun and wind-blown sand
- Sunglasses that wrap around a little
- A warm layer for the evening, especially in winter

If you are staying overnight, pack as you would for a cool evening outdoors, with a layer you can sleep in.

### Boat days and beaches

- Swimwear worn under a cover-up, so you can change quickly
- A dry bag for your phone and documents
- Reef-friendly habits: leave nothing behind on the sand or in the water

The Foreign Office warns that rip currents [can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags. That does not change what you pack, but it does change where you unpack it.

### Mountains and wadis

- Proper walking shoes with grip
- A refillable bottle and more water than you think you need
- A light layer, as it can feel cooler at height and in the shade
- A small first aid kit with plasters for blisters

### Malls, museums and evenings out

- Smart casual outfits that cover shoulders and knees
- A light layer for cold air conditioning; Visit Abu Dhabi advises women to take [a pashmina or jacket because of cold air](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) in the evenings

The same page notes that malls, health clubs and resorts are generally more accepting of fashionable clothing, but that it is best to cover your shoulders and legs when visiting government offices. It also says short or tight clothing may be worn but will attract attention, most of it unwelcome. Packing a couple of longer, looser outfits saves you from having to think about it each morning.

## Autumn: September to December

Autumn begins hot and ends gloriously mild. Early in the season, pack as for summer. By November and December, add a light layer for evenings and think about the water: Visit Abu Dhabi says the milder autumn weather calls for [more beach outings and watersports](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/weather-in-abu-dhabi).

## Rain: rare, but worth a thought

Rain is not the first thing most people pack for, but it does happen. Visit Abu Dhabi says rain is [more common in Al Ain](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) than elsewhere in the emirate, because of its closeness to the Hajar Mountains. The Foreign Office says heavy rain in the UAE [can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and advises checking forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology. A compact umbrella or light waterproof takes up little room in the winter months. Our guide to [rain and flooding in the UAE](/stories/uae-rain-and-flooding-advice/) explains what to do if the weather turns.

## Special occasions that need their own outfit

### Mosque visits

If you plan to visit the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque, pack specifically for it. The mosque's own rules ask women to wear [loose-fitting clothing to the ankles with sleeves to the wrists](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/mosque-manner), with full hair coverage, avoiding transparent, tight or revealing clothes. Men are asked to avoid transparent, tight-fitting or sleeveless shirts, and clothing that covers the entire legs is preferred. The rules also ask visitors to avoid excessive jewellery, heavy make-up, vibrant colours and clothing with slogans. A long, loose dress or wide trousers, a long-sleeved top and a large scarf will cover most mosque visits.

The same rules list items visitors may not carry, which is worth knowing before you fill a day bag. They include [electronic cigarettes of all kinds, lighters and sharp items](https://www.szgmc.gov.ae/en/mosque-manner), and, perhaps surprisingly for families, dolls and stuffed animals. Professional photography equipment needs a permit, while personal photos on non-professional equipment are permitted. If you are travelling with a small child who never lets go of a favourite toy, leave it in the car or at the hotel for this visit.

### Ramadan

If your trip falls during Ramadan, pack a little more conservatively. Visit Abu Dhabi says it is [especially recommended to dress more conservatively](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) during the holy month. Our guide to [what to wear and UAE etiquette](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) covers more situations.

## What to check before it goes in your bag

Some items need more than a second thought.

### Medicines

The Foreign Office says there are [strict rules about medications](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) you can bring into the UAE, and that you must get approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health to bring medication. It says visitors can bring up to 3 months' supply, reduced to one month's supply for medicines on the list requiring approval. Arriving without evidence of prior approval can mean the medication is not allowed in and you may be prosecuted. Start this early: the Foreign Office says applications are normally processed within 5 working days.

### Skincare, vapes and food

The Foreign Office warns that some skincare products and e-cigarette refills may contain ingredients that are [illegal in the UAE, such as CBD oil](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that you could face criminal charges if found with them. It also says it is [illegal to bring pork products](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) into the UAE. Check labels before you pack, and leave anything doubtful at home.

### Cameras, binoculars and drones

The Foreign Office says you may need a licence to use equipment such as [powerful cameras or binoculars](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), satellite phones or recording devices, and advises getting guidance from the UAE Embassy. If you are thinking of bringing a drone, check the rules carefully before you pack it.

### Weapons and related kit

This one rarely affects holidaymakers, but it is worth knowing. The Foreign Office says you need permission from UAE customs and the Ministry of Defence to bring in [weapons and related equipment](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), however small the quantity, and that this includes items such as ammunition, body protection, cleaning kits and gun belts. If you shoot as a hobby, check your bags carefully for anything left over from a previous trip.

## Documents and paperwork

Not everything that matters fits in a suitcase. Before you zip it shut, check:

- **Your passport.** The Foreign Office says it must have an expiry date [at least 6 months after the date you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) in the UAE.
- **Medicine approvals**, printed and saved on your phone, alongside prescriptions.
- **Insurance details**, including the policy number and the insurer's emergency number. Our [travel insurance guide](/stories/uae-travel-insurance-guide/) explains what your policy should cover.
- **Booking confirmations** for tours and hotels, especially for side trips into Oman, where border officers may ask to see them.

## What catches people out

- **Only packing for heat.** Air conditioning and winter evenings catch out almost everyone.
- **No modest outfit.** One long, loose outfit and a scarf open up mosques, heritage sites and government buildings.
- **Too many shoes, not the right ones.** One good walking pair, one pair that slips off easily and one smart pair usually covers it.
- **Unchecked toiletries.** Read the labels on skincare and vape refills before they go in your bag.
- **Leaving medicines until the last minute.** Approval takes time, so start early.

## Quick answers

**What should I wear in the UAE?**
Light, breathable clothes, with shoulders and knees covered in public areas like malls. The Foreign Office says swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at pools.

**Do I need warm clothes for the UAE?**
Yes, at least one layer. Visit Abu Dhabi advises a jacket or sweater for cold air conditioning and something slightly warmer in the winter months.

**What should women wear to the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque?**
The mosque asks women to wear loose clothing to the ankles, sleeves to the wrists and full hair coverage.

**Can I bring my medication to the UAE?**
Often, but the Foreign Office says you must get approval in advance from the UAE Ministry of Health, and there are limits on quantity.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What should we wear for each tour on our itinerary, including any mosque or heritage site visits?
- How cool does it get in the desert or on the water in the evening in the month we are travelling?
- Will we need closed shoes, swimwear or a change of clothes for any activity?
- Is water, sun shade or a scarf provided on tours, or should we bring our own?
- Are there any items we should not bring on a tour, such as drones or alcohol?
