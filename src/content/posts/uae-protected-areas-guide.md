Drive east out of Dubai and the city stops abruptly. One minute there are interchanges and warehouses; the next, a reserve sign, a ridge of orange sand and, if you are lucky, a herd of white oryx grazing in the distance. That boundary is a protected area, and the UAE has far more of them than most visitors realise.

Protected areas in the UAE cover deserts, mountains, mangroves, wetlands and coral reefs, and they are some of the best places in the country to see wildlife. But they do not all work the same way. Some you can walk into, some need a booking or an approved guide, some are open only to guests of a resort inside them, and many are closed to everyone except researchers. This guide explains how to tell which is which, and where to start.

## What protected areas in the UAE actually protect

Each emirate manages its own reserves, under federal and local laws. Dubai Municipality lists federal laws including a [ban on wildlife hunting, gathering and destruction](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/about-dubai-protected-areas/), and says Dubai's local rules prohibit disturbing wildlife, removing shells, coral, rocks or soil, damaging plants, introducing non-native species and polluting the land, water or air in a protected area.

The other thing to grasp is scale versus access. The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi says its Sheikh Zayed Protected Areas Network has [19 protected areas, four of them open to the public](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi). The rest are closed because of their fragility and are open only to researchers with permits. Nationally, the UAE government portal says the country has [ten Ramsar sites](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/environment-and-energy/Nature-and-wildlife/topography-and-ecosystems), wetlands designated as being of international importance.

### Why the rules are stricter than they look

It is easy to treat a reserve as a park with fewer facilities. It is not. Desert crusts, mangrove roots and nesting beaches take years to recover from a single careless visit, and many of the animals inside are rare or recovering from near extinction. That is why access is often controlled, why vehicles are limited to fixed tracks, and why the penalties for breaking the rules are real. Thinking of a reserve as a working conservation site, rather than a playground, will help you understand most of the restrictions you meet.

## Four ways in: a quick key

Before you set your heart on a reserve, work out which group it belongs to:

- **Open to visitors.** Walk in during opening hours, sometimes with registration.
- **By booking or approved operator.** You go with a licensed guide or reserve a slot.
- **Resort guests only.** The protected area surrounds a hotel, and access comes with a stay.
- **Closed.** For research and conservation only, or shut for works.

Status can change, so always check the official page close to your trip.

## Dubai: a third of the emirate, protected

Dubai Municipality says its [eight protected areas cover approximately 31% of Dubai](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/about-dubai-protected-areas/) and represent all of the emirate's major ecosystems: mountain, desert, coastal and marine. Its visitor rules are short and clear: [take nothing but memories and leave nothing but footprints](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/plan-your-visit/), keep to the fenced areas, and do not approach or harm wildlife, with heavy fines for breaches.

The municipality says the eight reserves were formally established under a decree in 2014 and are managed under [Law No. 11 of 2003](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/about-dubai-protected-areas/) on protected areas in Dubai. Alongside the better known names, its list includes the Al Wohoosh Desert Conservation Reserve and the Jabal Nazwa and Ghaf Nazwa conservation reserves, which see far fewer visitors.

### Open year round: Ras Al Khor and Al Marmoom

The municipality says [Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary and Al Marmoom Desert Conservation Reserve](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/) are open to the public year round. Ras Al Khor is a wetland on the edge of the city, watched from hides. Al Marmoom is described as [the largest protected area in the emirate](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/wildlife/), a desert reserve with lakes, gazelles and oryx. Our guide to [birdwatching in the UAE](/stories/birdwatching-in-the-uae/) covers both hides.

### With an approved operator: the Dubai Desert Conservation Reserve

The DDCR divides its land into [four zones](https://www.ddcr.org/about/), from a wilderness zone with no human intervention except research, to a zone where approved safari operators with registered guides can drive pre-planned routes. You visit with one of those operators. Our [DDCR guide](/stories/dubai-desert-conservation-reserve-guide/) explains how.

### Mountains and coast: Hatta and Jabal Ali

In the Hajar Mountains, the municipality describes the Hatta Mountain Reserve as a [Ramsar wetland since 2019](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/wildlife/), with the Arabian tahr as its flagship species and some of the best hiking routes in the UAE. Visit Dubai lists [hiking, kayaking, mountain biking and camping](https://www.visitdubai.com/en/beyond-dubai/nature-adventures-in-hatta) in Hatta.

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="uae-protected-areas-guide-4" alts="A blue reservoir in the Hatta Mountain Reserve surrounded by steep brown mountains" caption="Hatta's reservoir sits inside a mountain reserve that is also a Ramsar wetland."></photo-set>

Hatta works well as an introduction to the mountain side of the protected network because it combines managed activities with open landscape. Walk early, carry more water than you think you need, and keep to marked routes, since the slopes are loose and the reserve is home to animals that avoid people. See our [Hatta guide](/stories/hatta-guide/) to plan a day there.

On the coast, the municipality says the beaches of [Jabal Ali Wildlife Sanctuary](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/wildlife/) are the last remaining breeding grounds in Dubai of the critically endangered hawksbill turtle. The municipality mentions educational bookings there for schools and companies, but it is not among the reserves it lists as open to the public year round.

## Abu Dhabi: big reserves, selective access

The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi's network ranges from coral reefs to the Empty Quarter. It includes an [Arabian Oryx Protected Area](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Discover-Our-Biodiversity/Sheikh-Zayed-Protected-Areas-Network) that it says holds the world's largest population of the species, and several reserves dedicated to the houbara bustard. Most of these are not for visitors. The ones you can reach:

### Jabal Hafit National Park

The agency calls this [the only mountainous protected area in the emirate](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Discover-Our-Biodiversity/Sheikh-Zayed-Protected-Areas-Network), home to the Arabian tahr and the dwarf palm. Its visitor page adds the [Blandford's fox and the UAE's only concentration of Egyptian vultures](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Jabal-Hafit-National--Park). At its foot, Visit Abu Dhabi says Jebel Hafit Desert Park is [open daily from 8am to 6pm](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/parks/jebel-hafit-desert-park), with hiking, mountain biking and camping. Our [Jebel Hafit guide](/stories/jebel-hafit-guide/) covers the drive.

The agency's visitor page gives more reason to linger. It describes Jabal Hafit as [Abu Dhabi's highest peak and the UAE's second highest](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Jabal-Hafit-National--Park), at 1,240 metres, and says more than 500 ancient burial tombs dating back 5,000 years have been found in its foothills. It lists Brandt's hedgehog among the threatened species, reports that an Arabian caracal was recently sighted there for the first time in 35 years, and recommends visiting during the day to take in the terrain.

### Mangroves and wetlands

The agency says Al Wathba Wetland Reserve, famous for its flamingos, is [temporarily closed for development until further notice](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Al-Wathba-Wetland-Reserve). Close by, Visit Abu Dhabi says the [Al Wathba Fossil Dunes opened as a free attraction in 2022](https://www.visitabudhabi.ae/en/things-to-do/nature-and-wildlife/natural-wonders/al-wathba-fossil-dunes), with lit walking trails among wind-sculpted dunes. The same page says the protected area holds more than 1,700 fossil dunes over 7 square kilometres, formed by wind and sediment roughly 120,000 to 150,000 years ago, with trails that extend to 3 kilometres. It is one of the few protected sites in the emirate where you can simply turn up and walk, and late afternoon light shows the layered sand at its best.

### Resort-only reserves

Some of the most striking reserves come with a room key. The agency says you must be a guest of the neighbouring resort to see oryx in the [Qasr Al Sarab Protected Area](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/Experience-Green-Abu-Dhabi/Places-To-Go/Qasr-Al-Sarab-Protected-Area), and the same applies on Sir Bani Yas Island.

### At sea

Visit Abu Dhabi says Saadiyat Marine National Park is [open for non-motorised activities](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/where-to-go/marine-protected-areas) such as kayaking, paddleboarding and snorkelling, while Marawah is reached on specialised eco-tours.

## Sharjah and the east coast

Sharjah's reserves are among the easiest to visit. Visit Sharjah says Wasit Nature Reserve [won the Aga Khan Award for Architecture in 2019](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/wasit-nature-reserve/) for turning wasteland into a thriving ecosystem, and that it is closed on Sundays.

On the east coast, the [Al Qurm Protected Area](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/al-qurm-protected-area/) protects mangroves where hawksbill and green turtles nest nearby, with guided kayak tours. Visit Sharjah says the area covers more than 500 hectares of protected mangroves, where you may spot the endangered Arabian collared kingfisher, and that an overnight tented stay there is linked by boardwalks built to protect the turtle nesting grounds. The [Al Hefaiyah Mountain Conservation Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/al-hefaiyah-mountain-conservation-centre/) shows mountain species such as the Arabian leopard, wolf and tahr in landscaped enclosures. Visit Sharjah says the centre spreads over 12 square kilometres near Kalba, at the base of the Hajar Mountains, with around 30 native species, indoor exhibits of snakes, lizards, hedgehogs and scorpions, and an enclosure for the caracal. Guided tours suit families, and golf carts can speed things up for larger groups.

Inland, the [Mleiha Archaeological Centre](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) offers guided tours of ancient sites and Fossil Rock; see our [Mleiha guide](/stories/mleiha-sharjah-desert-guide/). Visit Sharjah says tours start at the Umm an-Nar tomb, a circular Bronze Age grave, and that the centre sits near Jebel Fayah, 65km south east of Sharjah city, with return transfers available if you do not have a car.

In Fujairah, the local tourism authority describes Wadi Wurayah as a boulder-strewn wadi [protected because of its unique flora and fauna](https://www.fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-NaturalSites.html). The authority places it 42km north of Fujairah and gives its area as 192 square kilometres.

## Planning a protected area day

Most reserve visits follow the same pattern, whichever emirate you are in. Check the official page a few days before you go, since closures for works or breeding seasons are common. Plan to arrive early, when wildlife is active and the heat has not built. Bring water, sun protection and binoculars, and expect limited food on site; at Ras Al Khor, for example, the municipality says [food and drink are not allowed inside the hides](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-protected-areas/plan-your-visit/).

Distances catch people out. The reserves are spread across the country, from the wetlands in the cities to the mountains on the Oman border and the islands of the far west. Trying to see three in a day usually means more time in the car than on foot. A better approach is to pair one reserve with something nearby: Ras Al Khor with a morning in old Dubai, Jabal Hafit with Al Ain, or Al Qurm with the Al Hefaiyah centre on the same stretch of coast.

### Seasons and timing

The cooler months from autumn to spring are the obvious time for desert and mountain reserves, and they coincide with the main bird migration. In the hotter months, keep visits short and early, and favour wetlands and coastal sites where you can watch from shade. Turtle nesting beaches are sensitive at certain times of year, which is another reason some coastal reserves restrict access.

### Families and accessibility

For families, the easiest reserves are the ones with structure: hides, boardwalks, visitor centres and short trails. Wasit, Ras Al Khor, the Al Wathba Fossil Dunes and the conservation centres in Sharjah all fit that description. Remote desert, mountain and island reserves are more demanding and depend on a vehicle or boat. If anyone in your group has limited mobility, ask specifically about paths, steps, seating and shade before you book, and favour sites with golf carts or boardwalks.

### Photography and drones

Photography for personal use is generally part of a visit, but commercial shoots and research need permission from the managing authority. Treat drones with caution: do not fly one in a reserve unless you have confirmed that it is permitted, since noise and movement can disturb nesting birds and grazing animals,.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming open means open every day.** Some reserves close on particular days or for seasonal works.
- **Confusing a resort reserve with a public one.** At Qasr Al Sarab and Sir Bani Yas, access to the protected area comes with a stay.
- **Driving off track.** In desert reserves, vehicles stick to permitted routes, and fragile ground does not recover quickly.
- **Collecting souvenirs.** Shells, coral, rocks and plants stay where they are under Dubai's rules, and the same principle applies elsewhere.
- **Underestimating the heat.** Even a short trail can be hard work in the middle of the day.

## Being a good guest

The rules across the emirates are close enough to sum up in one list:

- Stay on marked paths, tracks and boardwalks.
- Never feed, chase or handle wildlife.
- Take nothing: shells, coral, rocks and plants stay where they are.
- Leave no litter, and keep noise down.
- Drive only where vehicles are permitted, and only with a properly equipped vehicle. The UK Foreign Office warns that [desert excursions can be dangerous](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) without one.

## Quick answers

**How many protected areas does Abu Dhabi have?** The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi says its network has 19, of which four are open to the public.

**How much of Dubai is protected?** Dubai Municipality says its eight protected areas cover approximately 31% of the emirate.

**Which Dubai reserves can I visit without a tour?** Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary and Al Marmoom Desert Conservation Reserve, which the municipality says are open to the public year round.

**Can I visit the Al Wathba Fossil Dunes?** Yes. Visit Abu Dhabi says they opened as a free attraction in 2022, with lit walking trails.

**Is Al Wathba Wetland Reserve open?** The Environment Agency Abu Dhabi says it is temporarily closed for development until further notice.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Is this trip inside a protected area, and are you approved or licensed to operate there?
- Which parts of the reserve are open to us, and which are off limits?
- Are any of the sites on our list closed for works or seasonal reasons right now?
- Do you stick to fixed routes, and how close do your vehicles or boats go to wildlife?
- Does the reserve need advance registration or ID on arrival?
- How do you handle waste and keep group sizes small?
