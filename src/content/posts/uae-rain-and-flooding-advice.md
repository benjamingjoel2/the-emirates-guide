The sky over the mountains turns the colour of slate. A few fat drops hit the windscreen, then the rain arrives all at once, drumming on the car roof, and within minutes the dry channel beside the road is running brown and fast.

It does not happen often in the UAE, which is exactly why it catches visitors out. A country built for sun can struggle when a heavy storm arrives: roads pool, underpasses fill and wadis that were dry an hour ago start to flow. This guide covers UAE rain and flooding advice for travellers, from when storms are likely to what to do if one hits.

## UAE rain and flooding: what you need to know first

Rain is unusual but not rare, and it tends to come in short, heavy bursts rather than steady drizzle. Visit Abu Dhabi says rainfall is sporadic and [falls mainly in winter, from November to March](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear), and that rain is more common in Al Ain because it sits close to the Hajar mountains.

The UK Foreign Office warns that [heavy rain in the UAE can cause flooding](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). Its advice is to check forecasts from the National Center of Meteorology, follow advice from the local authorities and keep an eye on local radio and online news.

The short version for visitors:

- Check the official forecast and warnings each day, especially in the cooler months.
- If heavy rain is forecast, change plans for mountains, wadis, deserts and boats.
- Never walk or drive through flood water.
- Call **999** for police in an emergency anywhere in the UAE.

## Where to get official warnings

The National Center of Meteorology is the country's federal weather authority. Its [website](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/) carries current weather conditions, a warnings map, weather and marine bulletins, and awareness pages on rain and floods, sand storms and thick fog. It is the place to check before a day out of the city.

You may also receive warnings straight to your phone. The UAE government explains that the [National Early Warning System](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies) sends text messages to warn people of dangerous weather conditions and tell them what to do to stay safe. According to the same page, sending these warnings is limited to the national emergency and crisis authority, the Ministry of Interior, the police of the seven emirates and the Ministry of Health and Prevention. If a message arrives, read it and follow it.

### Local news and your hotel

The Foreign Office points visitors to [local radio and online media](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) for up-to-date information. Your hotel front desk is also a practical source: staff will usually know about road closures, cancelled tours and changes to public transport before you do.

## Before the storm: changing your plans

When the forecast looks unsettled, a bit of flexibility goes a long way.

### Mountains and wadis

Wadis are dry valleys that channel water off the mountains, and they can fill quickly when rain falls upstream, even if the sky above you is clear. On a day with heavy rain forecast, skip wadi walks, mountain hikes and the drive up to high viewpoints. Our guides to [hiking in the UAE](/stories/hiking-in-the-uae/) and [Fujairah's forts and wadis](/stories/fujairah-forts-and-wadis/) have more on the terrain.

### Desert trips

Desert excursions can be risky in any weather. The Foreign Office says they can be dangerous unless you are in a properly equipped four-wheel-drive vehicle, and advises that you [travel with other cars, take water and a mobile phone](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and leave your travel plans with someone. If the forecast is poor, ask your operator whether to move the trip.

### Boats and the sea

Storms can make the sea rough. The Foreign Office advises care on tourist boats, which may not meet UK safety standards, and says to [make sure life jackets are available](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) for everyone. If a cruise or snorkelling trip is cancelled for weather, take it as a sensible call.

### Swap in an indoor day

The cities are full of good wet-weather options: museums, galleries, covered souks and long lunches. Our [Louvre Abu Dhabi guide](/stories/louvre-abu-dhabi-guide/) is a good place to start.

## During heavy rain and flooding

The UK government's guidance on extreme weather abroad sets out simple rules for floods. In a flood, or if one is expected, it says you should [follow local alerts and emergency advice](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tropical-cyclones) as soon as they are issued, evacuate if told to and take key documents and medication, and otherwise shelter in a safe building, moving to a higher level if it starts to flood.

The same guidance says you should not:

- walk or drive through flood water, [even if it looks shallow](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tropical-cyclones);
- move through flood water if you are unsure whether it is safe, because it may hide other hazards;
- climb into attic spaces where you could become trapped.

If you become trapped, call the emergency services or use your phone to let others know where you are.

### On the road

Driving in a storm is where many visitors get into trouble. Roads can flood in low spots and underpasses, visibility drops, and other drivers do not always slow down. Practical habits help:

- Slow down, leave plenty of space and use your headlights.
- Avoid underpasses and low-lying roads that already have standing water.
- If water is across the road, turn around rather than testing the depth.
- If the rain is extreme and you are not sure, pull over somewhere safe and wait.

The UAE government advises that if you have a minor accident and no one is hurt, you can [move your car to the shoulder lane](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies) and wait for the police at a distance from the car. If anyone is injured, leave the vehicles where they are and wait for an ambulance. Our guide to [driving in the UAE as a visitor](/stories/driving-in-the-uae-as-a-visitor/) covers the rules of the road in more detail.

## Sand storms and fog

Rain is not the only weather that disrupts plans. The UAE government notes that the national emergency authority has [guidelines for sand storms and fog](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies) as well as heavy rain, floods and lightning, and the National Center of Meteorology publishes [awareness pages](https://www.ncm.gov.ae/) on all three. Both can cut visibility sharply on the roads, so the same rule applies: slow down, keep your distance and wait it out if you need to.

## Emergency numbers to save

The UAE government lists these numbers, which work [anywhere in the UAE](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/justice-safety-and-the-law/Safety/handling-emergencies):

- **999** for police
- **998** for ambulance
- **997** for fire and civil defence
- **996** for the coastguard
- **995** for search and rescue

Save them in your phone before you set out, along with your hotel's number and your travel insurer's emergency line.

## After the storm

Once the rain passes, roads can stay flooded or closed for a while, and some attractions, tours and public transport may take time to return to normal. Check with your hotel or operator before heading out, and carry on avoiding wadis and low roads until the water has gone down. If your flight or tour is affected, contact your travel provider and insurer.

## Quick answers

**When does it rain in the UAE?**
Rain is sporadic and falls mainly in winter, from November to March, according to Visit Abu Dhabi, with Al Ain seeing more because it lies near the Hajar mountains.

**Where can I check for weather warnings in the UAE?**
The National Center of Meteorology website carries forecasts and a warnings map, and the National Early Warning System sends text alerts about dangerous weather.

**Is it safe to drive through flood water?**
No. UK government guidance says never walk or drive through flood water, even if it looks shallow, because it may hide other hazards.

**What is the emergency number in the UAE?**
Call 999 for police, 998 for an ambulance and 997 for fire and civil defence, from anywhere in the UAE.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Do you check the National Center of Meteorology forecast and warnings before each trip, and who decides whether it goes ahead?
- What is your policy if a tour is cancelled or shortened because of rain, wind or fog?
- Does this route pass through wadis or low-lying roads, and what is the alternative if they flood?
- For desert or mountain trips, how many vehicles travel together and what communications do you carry?
- For boat trips, are life jackets available for every passenger, including children, and what sea conditions would stop the trip?
