The flight is booked, the hotel is paid for, and then a small doubt arrives at midnight: do I actually need a visa for this? A search turns up a dozen websites, half of them selling something, and none of them agreeing.

For many travellers the honest answer about a UAE tourist visa is reassuringly simple. Some nationalities get a visa stamped on arrival, while others need one arranged before they fly. The trick is knowing which group you are in, and checking it on an official source rather than a forum thread. This guide walks through how to do that, what the main visa types are, and the small rules about passports and overstays that catch people out.

One caveat before we begin. Visa rules are set by the UAE authorities and they change. Treat everything here as a map to the official pages, not a substitute for them.

## The quick check: which group are you in?

The UAE government portal puts it plainly. [GCC citizens do not need a visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Visa-information/do-you-need-an-entry-permit-or-a-visa-to-enter-the-uae) to enter the UAE. Citizens of several other countries can get a visa on arrival. Everyone else needs to apply for a visa in advance.

That gives you three possible starting points:

- **GCC citizen:** no visa needed.
- **Visa on arrival nationality:** your visa is issued at immigration when you land.
- **Everyone else:** a pre-arranged tourist or transit visa before you travel.

The same page warns that [lists of countries eligible for visa on arrival may vary from time to time](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Visa-information/do-you-need-an-entry-permit-or-a-visa-to-enter-the-uae), and suggests checking with the UAE embassy in your country before travelling. So even if a friend with the same passport flew in last year without a visa, check again.

## Visa on arrival: what to expect at the desk

You walk off the plane, follow the signs to immigration and hand over your passport. If your nationality is on the list, that is largely it.

Visit Abu Dhabi publishes a list of nationalities that can get a [free-of-charge entry visa upon arrival](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs) at Abu Dhabi International Airport. It includes the United Kingdom, the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and many European countries, among others. The same page notes that an eye scan will be required at the airport.

For British citizens, the UK Foreign Office says the visitor or tourist visa is issued [free of charge when you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) and you do not need to apply in advance. Your passport is stamped as you go through immigration. It is valid for up to 90 days over a 180-day period beginning from your first entry, and those days can be used continuously or across several visits.

Other nationalities on the arrival list may get a different length of stay, so check the terms for your own passport on the official channels rather than assuming the British rule applies to you.

## Pre-arranged tourist visas: who applies and how

If your passport is not on the arrival list, the process starts before you leave home. The UAE government describes a [tourist visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa) as the route for people who are not eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry.

### The official application routes

According to the government portal, you can apply online through the [Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security or the General Directorate of Identity and Foreigners Affairs in Dubai](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa). You can also apply through airlines, licensed travel agents or hotels.

A few details from the same page are worth knowing:

- UAE-based airlines can arrange visas, but each sets its own conditions, and one of them is that you fly with that airline.
- Licensed travel agents and hotels in the UAE can arrange a tourist visa provided you buy the ticket through them and keep a reservation at the specific hotel.
- The UAE's embassies do not issue tourist visas.

That last point surprises people. The embassy can help you check eligibility, but the application itself goes through the official authorities or one of those sponsors.

### The five-year multiple entry option

Frequent visitors may want to look at the [multiple entry five-year tourist visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa). The government says it is available to all nationalities on self-sponsorship and allows stays of 90 days on each visit, extendable for a further 90 days. Applicants need a bank balance of 4,000 US dollars or the equivalent during the last six months, valid UAE health insurance and an onward or return ticket. Read the full conditions on the official page before you apply.

## Just passing through? Transit visas explained

A long layover can be a tempting chance to see the city. If you are not eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry and want to step out of the airport, the government says you need a [pre-arranged transit visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/transit-visa).

These are issued for either 48 or 96 hours, are sponsored by UAE-based airlines, and must be processed and approved before you enter the country. The same page says a transit visa is single-entry and cannot be extended. If you want to make the most of a short stop, our [48-hour stopover guide](/stories/uae-48-hour-stopover/) shows what fits.

## Your passport: the six-month rule

This is the detail that turns people away at check-in. The government portal says [passports must be valid for at least six months](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Visa-information/do-you-need-an-entry-permit-or-a-visa-to-enter-the-uae) from the date of entry into the UAE.

The UK Foreign Office gives the same rule for British passports and adds two further points. If you are only transiting without passing through immigration, your passport needs an expiry date [at least three months after the date you transit](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). And you will be denied entry if you try to use a passport that has been reported lost or stolen.

Check the expiry date the day you book, not the week you fly. Renewals take time.

## Staying longer, and what happens if you overstay

Plans change. A week becomes ten days, a flight is cancelled, a family visit runs on.

The Foreign Office warns that you could face [fines for exceeding your authorised stay](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), which may build up for each day of overstay. If you want to extend, it points to the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs for Dubai and the Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship for the rest of the UAE.

Dubai's directorate lists services online for [extending a tourist visa](https://gdrfad.gov.ae/en), issuing single-entry and five-year tourist visas, and verifying entry permits. Use those official channels if you need to stay, and do it before your permitted stay runs out.

Two more points from the Foreign Office are worth keeping in mind. Working on a visit visa is not allowed and can lead to a fine, prison or deportation. And you may be prevented from leaving the UAE if you are subject to a travel ban, involved in legal proceedings or have unpaid debt.

## Avoiding visa scams

Search for a UAE visa and you will find plenty of sites that look official. The federal authority's own website carries a [warning about fraudulent calls and fake websites](https://www.icp.gov.ae/en/) that use its name and logo to steal personal data, and urges people to use only its official channels.

The government portal adds similar advice about tour operators: [check the authenticity of travel agents](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa) and do not pay or send copies of official documents until you are sure the operator is genuine. Our guide to [booking a UAE tour operator safely](/stories/booking-a-uae-tour-operator-safely/) has more on spotting the real thing.

## Before you fly: a short checklist

- Confirm your nationality's visa status on the official portal or with the UAE embassy.
- Check your passport has at least six months of validity from your arrival date.
- If you need a pre-arranged visa, apply through an official authority, your airline, a licensed agent or your hotel.
- Keep a copy of your approved visa with your travel documents.
- Make sure your travel insurance is in place. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that, under UAE and Abu Dhabi law, [all visitors must have medical insurance cover](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs). Our [travel insurance guide](/stories/uae-travel-insurance-guide/) covers what to check.
- Read up on [what to bring, and what not to, through customs](/stories/bringing-medicines-into-uae/), especially if you take regular medication.

## Quick answers

**Do UK citizens need a visa for the UAE?** British citizens get a visitor visa free of charge on arrival, with no advance application. The UK Foreign Office says it is valid for up to 90 days over a 180-day period.

**Can I get a UAE tourist visa from the embassy?** No. The UAE government says its embassies do not issue tourist visas. Apply through the official authorities, a UAE-based airline, a licensed travel agent or your hotel.

**How long must my passport be valid for the UAE?** At least six months from the date you enter the UAE, according to the government portal.

**Can I leave the airport during a layover?** Yes, if your nationality qualifies for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry. If not, you need a pre-arranged 48-hour or 96-hour transit visa, sponsored by a UAE-based airline.

**What happens if I overstay my UAE visa?** You may face fines that build up for each day of overstay. Contact the immigration authorities to extend before your permitted stay ends.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you confirm, from the official sources, which visa route applies to each passport in our group?
- If you or our hotel are sponsoring the visa, how long does approval usually take and when should we apply?
- Does our itinerary keep every traveller within their permitted length of stay?
- If we want to cross into Oman during the trip, how does that affect our UAE visa and re-entry?
- What happens to our bookings if a visa application is delayed or refused?
- Which documents should we carry on tours, and is a copy of the passport and visa enough?
