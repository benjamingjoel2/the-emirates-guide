The flight is booked, the hotel is paid for, and then a small doubt arrives at midnight: do I actually need a visa for this? A search turns up a dozen websites, half of them selling something, and none of them agreeing.

For many travellers the honest answer about a UAE tourist visa is reassuringly simple. Some nationalities get a visa stamped on arrival, while others need one arranged before they fly. The trick is knowing which group you are in, and checking it on an official source rather than a forum thread. This guide walks through how to do that, what the main visa types are, and the small rules about passports, overstays and leaving the country that catch people out.

One caveat before we begin. Visa rules are set by the UAE authorities and they change. Treat everything here as a map to the official pages, not a substitute for them.

## The quick check: which group are you in?

The UAE government portal puts it plainly. [GCC citizens do not need a visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Visa-information/do-you-need-an-entry-permit-or-a-visa-to-enter-the-uae) to enter the UAE. Citizens of several other countries can get a visa on arrival. Everyone else needs to apply for a visa in advance.

That gives you four possible starting points:

- **GCC citizen:** no visa needed.
- **Visa on arrival nationality:** your visa is issued at immigration when you land.
- **Resident of a GCC country, but not a citizen:** an eVisa applied for online before you travel.
- **Everyone else:** a pre-arranged tourist or transit visa before you travel.

The same page warns that [lists of countries eligible for visa on arrival may vary from time to time](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Visa-information/do-you-need-an-entry-permit-or-a-visa-to-enter-the-uae), and suggests checking with the UAE embassy in your country before travelling. So even if a friend with the same passport flew in last year without a visa, check again.

### Mixed groups need checking one passport at a time

Families and groups often travel on more than one nationality: a parent with dual citizenship, a partner on a different passport, a friend who lives in another country. The visa route follows the passport you enter on, not the person who booked the trip, so run the check for every traveller separately. It is common for one member of a group to need a pre-arranged visa while the others are stamped in on arrival, and that is the person whose paperwork needs starting first.

## Visa on arrival: what to expect at the desk

You walk off the plane, follow the signs to immigration and hand over your passport. If your nationality is on the list, that is largely it.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="uae-tourist-visa-guide-1" alts="Overhead bilingual Arabic and English sign in Abu Dhabi International Airport Terminal 3 pointing towards US pre-clearance flights, a lounge and a smoking area" caption="Airport signage is in Arabic and English, so finding passport control after landing is straightforward."></photo-set>

Visit Abu Dhabi publishes a list of nationalities that can get a [free-of-charge entry visa upon arrival](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs) at Abu Dhabi International Airport. It includes the United Kingdom, the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and many European countries, among others. The same page notes that an eye scan will be required at the airport.

For British citizens, the UK Foreign Office says the visitor or tourist visa is issued [free of charge when you arrive](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) and you do not need to apply in advance. Your passport is stamped as you go through immigration. It is valid for up to 90 days over a 180-day period beginning from your first entry, and those days can be used continuously or across several visits, provided the total stays within 90 days in that 180-day window.

That rolling window matters if you visit often. Two long trips a few months apart can add up faster than you expect, so keep a note of your entry and exit dates. Other nationalities may get different terms, so check your own passport's rules on the official channels.

### How the arrival day actually unfolds

The flow is much the same wherever you land: passport control, the eye scan Visit Abu Dhabi mentions, then baggage reclaim, customs and the arrivals hall.

Customs is the step people forget is part of entering the country. The UAE government says passengers carrying [more than AED 60,000 in cash](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Tourist-services-and-information/Customs-clearance), or the equivalent in other currencies, financial instruments, precious metals or valuable stones, must declare it. For travellers under 18, the amount they carry is added to the limit of the parent, guardian or adult travelling with them. The same page lists banned and restricted goods, and our guide to [UAE laws every tourist should know](/stories/uae-laws-for-tourists/) covers what not to pack.

## Pre-arranged tourist visas: who applies and how

If your passport is not on the arrival list, the process starts before you leave home. The UAE government describes a [tourist visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa) as the route for people who are not eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry.

### The official application routes

According to the government portal, you can apply online through the [Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security or the General Directorate of Identity and Foreigners Affairs in Dubai](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa). You can also apply through airlines, licensed travel agents or hotels.

A few details from the same page are worth knowing:

- UAE-based airlines can arrange visas, but each sets its own conditions, and one of them is that you fly with that airline.
- Licensed travel agents and hotels in the UAE can arrange a tourist visa provided you buy the ticket through them and keep a reservation at the specific hotel.
- Travel agencies in your own country may offer packages to the UAE in collaboration with a local tour operator.
- The UAE's embassies do not issue tourist visas.

That last point surprises people. The embassy can help you check eligibility and verify an agent, but the application itself goes through the official authorities or one of those sponsors.

### Applying through your hotel

Visit Abu Dhabi adds some useful detail for the capital. It says [five, four and three-star hotels can apply for visas](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs) on behalf of guests who have booked a stay with them, but that not all of them have this system in place, so check with your hotel first. The service does not currently apply to guests staying in hotel apartments.

The same page makes a point that applies to every sponsor. Airlines, travel agencies and hotels can apply on your behalf, but you need to give yourself enough time for approval, and they do not take responsibility if the visa is declined. Build that into your planning: apply well ahead, and keep bookings flexible until the approval arrives.

### The five-year multiple entry option

Frequent visitors may want to look at the [multiple entry five-year tourist visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa). The government says it is available to all nationalities on self-sponsorship and allows stays of 90 days on each visit, extendable for a further 90 days. Applicants need a bank balance of 4,000 US dollars or the equivalent during the last six months, a valid health insurance policy that applies in the UAE and is valid for 180 days, and an onward or return ticket. The portal also links to a family or group version of the same long-term visa through the federal authority.

## Living in another Gulf country? The GCC resident eVisa

If you live and work in another GCC country without being a citizen, there is a separate route. The government portal says GCC residents and their companions need to [apply online for a visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa) and must obtain it before arriving in the UAE.

The notes on the same page set out the main conditions:

- The entry permit is valid for 30 days from the date of issue, and allows a stay of 30 days from the date of entry.
- It can be extended once, for 30 days.
- Your GCC residency must be valid for at least one year from your arrival date.
- Your passport must be valid for at least six months from your arrival date.
- Your profession must be on the approved list, subject to evaluation by the authority.

## Just passing through? Transit visas explained

A long layover can be a tempting chance to see the city. If you are not eligible for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry and want to step out of the airport, the government says you need a [pre-arranged transit visa](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/transit-visa).

These are issued for either 48 or 96 hours, are sponsored by UAE-based airlines, and must be processed and approved before you enter the country. The same page says you apply through the UAE-based airline you are travelling on, a travel agency or an airport hotel, and that a transit visa is single-entry and cannot be extended. If you want to make the most of a short stop, our [48-hour stopover guide](/stories/uae-48-hour-stopover/) shows what fits.


## Your passport: the six-month rule

This is the detail that turns people away at check-in. The government portal says [passports must be valid for at least six months](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Visa-information/do-you-need-an-entry-permit-or-a-visa-to-enter-the-uae) from the date of entry into the UAE.

The UK Foreign Office gives the same rule for British passports and adds further points. If you are only transiting without passing through immigration, your passport needs an expiry date [at least three months after the date you transit](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). If you hold a UAE residence permit, the expiry date must be at least three months after you arrive. And you will be denied entry if you do not have a valid travel document or try to use a passport that has been reported lost or stolen.

Check the expiry date the day you book, not the week you fly.

## Other entry checks people overlook

### Yellow fever certificates

Most visitors never need one, but the Foreign Office says you must have a certificate proving a [yellow fever vaccination](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements) if you are coming from a country listed as a transmission risk. If your trip routes through such a country before the UAE, check this well ahead.

### Working on a visit visa

A tourist visa is for tourism. The Foreign Office warns that if you are [caught working on a visit visa](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), you could get a fine or a prison sentence and risk deportation. If a trip involves any paid work, check the correct permit route with the immigration authorities first.

### Returning after living in the UAE

Former residents need to take more care. The Foreign Office says you risk arrest if you return when your previous visa is not in order, for example if a work visa was never cancelled, or if you have outstanding debts or an unresolved legal issue. It suggests getting advice from a local lawyer before travelling if you have any doubt.

## Staying longer, and what happens if you overstay

Plans change: a flight is cancelled, a family visit runs on.

The Foreign Office warns that you could face [fines for exceeding your authorised stay](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements), which may build up for each day of overstay. If you want to extend, it points to the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs for Dubai and the Federal Authority for Identity and Citizenship for the rest of the UAE.

Dubai's directorate lists services online for [extending a tourist visa](https://gdrfad.gov.ae/en), issuing single-entry and five-year tourist visas, and verifying entry permits. Use those official channels if you need to stay, and do it before your permitted stay runs out.

### Leaving can be blocked too

It is easy to think of visas only as a way in. The Foreign Office notes that people subject to a travel ban, involved in legal proceedings or with unpaid debt, and children subject to a custody dispute, [may be prevented from leaving the UAE](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/entry-requirements). Its health advice adds that you may be [prevented from leaving the UAE if you are unable to pay your medical bill](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/health), which is one more reason to arrive with proper insurance.

## Your passport during the stay

Once you are in, keep your passport somewhere safe but reachable. The Foreign Office says it is [normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) of your passport or Emirates ID when you check in. It also notes that you cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult, which is worth knowing if older teenagers are travelling separately from the rest of the family.

## Avoiding visa scams

Search for a UAE visa and you will find plenty of sites that look official, some of them charging for services you do not need. The safest habit is to start from the government portal and follow its links to the federal authority, Dubai's directorate, the airlines and the other official channels, rather than clicking on whichever result appears first.

The government portal adds similar advice about tour operators: [check the authenticity of travel agents](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visa-and-emirates-id/Types-of-visas/tourist-visa) and do not pay or send copies of official documents until you are completely sure the operator is genuine. It suggests the UAE embassy in your country can help with verification. Our guide to [booking a UAE tour operator safely](/stories/booking-a-uae-tour-operator-safely/) has more on spotting the real thing.

A few warning signs to look out for:

- A site that charges for a visa your nationality receives free on arrival.
- Pressure to pay quickly, or by an unusual method.
- Requests for scans of your passport before you have confirmed who you are dealing with.
- An address or phone number you cannot match to a licensed business or an official authority.

## Before you fly: a short checklist

- Confirm each traveller's visa status on the official portal or with the UAE embassy.
- Check every passport has at least six months of validity from your arrival date.
- If you need a pre-arranged visa, apply through an official authority, your airline, a licensed agent or your hotel, and allow time for approval.
- Keep a copy of your approved visa with your travel documents, and a digital copy on your phone.
- Count your days if you visit more than once within a few months.
- Make sure your travel insurance is in place. Visit Abu Dhabi notes that, under UAE and Abu Dhabi law, [all visitors must have medical insurance cover](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/faqs). Our [travel insurance guide](/stories/uae-travel-insurance-guide/) covers what to check.
- If you take regular medication, read our guide to [bringing medicines into the UAE](/stories/bringing-medicines-into-uae/).

## Quick answers

**Do UK citizens need a visa for the UAE?** British citizens get a visitor visa free of charge on arrival, with no advance application. The UK Foreign Office says it is valid for up to 90 days over a 180-day period.

**Can I get a UAE tourist visa from the embassy?** No. The UAE government says its embassies do not issue tourist visas. Apply through the official authorities, a UAE-based airline, a licensed travel agent or your hotel.

**How long must my passport be valid for the UAE?** At least six months from the date you enter the UAE, according to the government portal.

**Can I leave the airport during a layover?** Yes, if your nationality qualifies for a visa on arrival or visa-free entry. If not, you need a pre-arranged 48-hour or 96-hour transit visa, sponsored by a UAE-based airline.

**What happens if I overstay my UAE visa?** You may face fines that build up for each day of overstay. Contact the immigration authorities to extend before your permitted stay ends.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Can you confirm, from the official sources, which visa route applies to each passport in our group?
- If you or our hotel are sponsoring the visa, how long does approval usually take and when should we apply?
- Does our itinerary keep every traveller within their permitted length of stay?
- If we want to cross into Oman during the trip, how does that affect our UAE visa and re-entry?
- What happens to our bookings if a visa application is delayed or refused?
- Which documents should we carry on tours, and is a copy of the passport and visa enough?
