On a Friday afternoon in Dubai, the office towers along the main highway are already emptying. By Saturday morning the beaches, parks and malls are full of families, and the roads into the mountains are busy with people heading out for the day.

If you last read about the Gulf a few years ago, you might expect the weekend to fall on Friday and Saturday. In the UAE that is no longer the pattern. Getting it right matters more than it sounds, because it decides when government offices answer, when attractions are at their busiest and when a quiet museum visit is easiest to find.

## The UAE weekend and working hours in one paragraph

Here is the short answer. According to the UAE government's own [fact sheet](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/fact-sheet), government and semi-government offices work from Monday to Friday, while Sharjah government entities work from Monday to Thursday. The same page says most private companies close only on Sundays, and others close on both Saturdays and Sundays. So for most visitors the weekend is effectively Saturday and Sunday, with Friday as a working day that winds down earlier for many people, and a longer weekend in Sharjah's public sector.

For a traveller, that has three practical effects:

- **Saturdays and Sundays are the busy days** at beaches, parks, malls and family attractions.
- **Weekday mornings are usually the calmest time** for museums, heritage sites and popular viewpoints.
- **Anything official** (an embassy, a government office, a licensing desk) should be planned for Monday to Thursday if you can, with Friday as a backup.

## Why Friday still feels different

Friday is a working day on paper, yet it has a rhythm of its own. It is the day of the main weekly congregational prayer, held around midday, and much of the country organises the day around it.

### What that means for sightseeing

Some mosques that welcome sightseeing visitors adjust their visiting arrangements on Fridays. If a mosque visit is high on your list, check its official website for Friday arrangements before you build your day around it. Our [Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque visitor guide](/stories/sheikh-zayed-grand-mosque-visitor-guide/) covers the most visited one, and our [travel tips](/travel-tips/) section has more practical planning help.

Around the middle of the day, you may also notice small shops in older neighbourhoods closing for a while, especially in traditional souks. Malls, large attractions and hotels generally carry on as normal.

### Friday afternoon is a good time to start a trip out of town

Because many people finish early on Friday, roads out of the cities can be busy that afternoon as residents head to the mountains, the east coast or the desert. If you are driving to Hatta, Jebel Jais or Fujairah, an early Friday start or a Monday trip will usually feel calmer. Our guide to [driving in the UAE as a visitor](/stories/driving-in-the-uae-as-a-visitor/) has more on the roads themselves.

## Sharjah's longer weekend

Sharjah is the exception worth knowing. The [fact sheet](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/fact-sheet) notes that Sharjah government entities work from Monday to Thursday, which gives the emirate's public sector a three-day weekend from Friday to Sunday.

For visitors, this mostly matters if you have business with a Sharjah government office. Museums and heritage sites in Sharjah set their own hours, so check each venue's official page rather than assuming it follows the government week. Our [Sharjah destination page](/destinations/sharjah/) has ideas for a visit.

## Embassies, consulates and other official business

If you lose a passport or need consular help, timing becomes very real. The UAE fact sheet says embassies and consulates are [usually open from Monday to Friday](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/fact-sheet), the UAE work week, and closed on Saturdays, Sundays, UAE public holidays and the public holidays of their own country. It adds that opening times vary by embassy and by the purpose of your visit.

Two practical habits help:

- Note your embassy's contact details before you travel, including any out-of-hours emergency line.
- Remember that a holiday at home can close your embassy even when the UAE is working normally.

## Opening hours: how to read them

There is no single opening-hours rule for shops, attractions or restaurants across the seven emirates. Each venue sets its own, and they change with the seasons, school holidays and special events. Treat any hours you see in an older blog, including this one, as a starting point rather than a fact.

### Attractions and museums

Official attraction websites are the only reliable place for current hours, last entry times and any weekly closing day. Many big sights sell timed tickets, so the slot you choose matters as much as the opening time. Weekday mornings tend to be quieter, and on Saturdays and Sundays it is worth booking ahead.

### Shops and souks

Malls in the big cities keep long hours and are busiest in the evenings and at weekends. Traditional souks often feel liveliest in the late afternoon and evening, when the heat eases. If you want unhurried browsing, a weekday morning is often your best chance.

### Government offices and dress

If you do visit a government office, dress more conservatively than you might for the beach or a mall. Visit Abu Dhabi advises that it is best to [cover your shoulders and legs](https://visitabudhabi.ae/en/plan-your-trip/essential-info/climate-and-what-to-wear) when visiting government offices. 

## How Ramadan changes the working day

During Ramadan the whole country moves to a different clock. The UAE government portal says that under UAE labour law, [working hours are reduced by two hours per day](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan), for Muslim and non-Muslim employees alike. Offices close earlier, and the city comes alive after sunset instead.

The same page says amusement parks, restaurants, cultural and tourist sites [remain open throughout the day](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/ramadan). It also notes that paid parking hours change during Ramadan, with details shown on parking meters, and that taxis can be hard to find in the evenings because many drivers are breaking their fast, so booking ahead is better.

The UK Foreign Office advises visitors to [check opening hours of shops and restaurants](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) during Ramadan, and warns that driving may be erratic around dusk as people hurry home. Our full guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains the rest.

## Public holidays and the weekend

Public holidays add another layer. The UAE government lists the [official public holidays](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) for both the public and private sectors: Gregorian New Year, Eid Al Fitr, Arafah Day, Eid Al Adha, Hijri New Year, the Prophet's birthday and National Day. Islamic holidays are set by moon sighting, so their exact dates are confirmed close to the time.

One detail is useful for planning. According to the same page, under Cabinet Resolution No. 27 of 2024 a public holiday [cannot be transferred to another day](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/public-holidays-and-religious-affairs/public-holidays) if it falls on the weekend or coincides with another holiday. In practice, holidays that land next to a Saturday and Sunday can create long weekends, when hotels and attractions fill and roads to the mountains and coast are busy. Our [public holidays guide](/stories/uae-public-holidays-guide/) looks at how to plan around them.

## A simple way to plan your week

Put together, the pattern is easy to work with:

1. **Monday to Thursday:** museums, heritage sites, the busiest viewpoints and anything official.
2. **Friday:** a flexible day. Good for malls, beaches and hotel time around midday, with an early start if you are leaving town.
3. **Saturday and Sunday:** expect crowds at family attractions and beaches. Book ahead, or use the weekend for a desert trip or a quieter emirate.

The UAE is [four hours ahead of GMT](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/fact-sheet), which is handy to remember if you need to reach a company or bank at home during its working hours.

## Quick answers

**What days are the weekend in the UAE?**
Government offices work Monday to Friday, and most private companies close on Sunday, with some also closing on Saturday, according to the UAE government fact sheet. For visitors, Saturday and Sunday are the busy weekend days.

**Is Sharjah's weekend different?**
Yes. Sharjah government entities work Monday to Thursday, so the emirate's public sector has a Friday to Sunday weekend.

**Are attractions open during Ramadan?**
The UAE government portal says amusement parks, restaurants, cultural and tourist sites remain open throughout the day during Ramadan, though hours can change, so check each venue.

**Are embassies open on Fridays?**
The UAE fact sheet says embassies and consulates are usually open Monday to Friday, but hours vary by embassy, so check yours before you go.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which days of the week are quietest at the attractions on our list, and should any visit move to a weekday morning?
- Does any mosque or heritage site we plan to see change its visiting arrangements on Fridays?
- Will a public holiday or long weekend fall during our trip, and how might that affect crowds and tour availability?
- If we travel during Ramadan, how will tour timings, pickups and meal stops change?
- For a day trip out of the city, which day and departure time will help us avoid the heaviest weekend traffic?
