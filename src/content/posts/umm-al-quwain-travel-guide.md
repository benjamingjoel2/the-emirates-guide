At first light the lagoon is so flat it doubles everything: the mangroves, the low islands, a lone heron standing on one leg. A fishing boat putters out of the creek and its wake takes a long time to fade. Nobody is in a hurry here, because nobody needs to be.

Umm Al Quwain is the emirate people overlook between Ajman and Ras Al Khaimah, and that is exactly its appeal. This Umm Al Quwain travel guide is for travellers who like water, birds and small old towns. In short: come for the lagoon and its mangroves, ideally by kayak in the early morning, then spend an hour in the old fort museum and a slow afternoon by the sea. It will not fill a week, but it can fill a very good day or a restful overnight.

## Umm Al Quwain in a nutshell

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/umm-al-quwain) describes Umm Al Quwain as the second smallest and least populated emirate, covering about 720 sq km. It lies between Sharjah to the south-west and Ras Al Khaimah to the north-east, and its main town is built on a narrow peninsula called Khor Al Bidiyah.

Fishing has long been central to life here. The portal says it is a key contributor to the economy, and lists traditional activities such as dhow building, falconry and camel racing as popular in the emirate. The government's emirate guide adds that Umm Al Quwain is [about 20 minutes' drive north of Ajman](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), which makes it easy to combine the two.

What you will not find is a big resort strip or a skyline. The town is low-rise, the roads are quiet, and the pleasure of the place lies in water, light and space. Come with that expectation and it is one of the most restful corners of the country.

## The lagoon, the islands and the mangroves

This is the heart of the place. Water wraps around the town on almost every side, and the edges are fringed with green.

The [government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/umm-al-quwain) describes rich coastal mangroves on the Arabian Gulf coast and many islands lying east of the mainland. The largest, Al Seniah, is home to Arabian gazelles, falcons and turtles, it says. As part of its Sustainable Blue Economy Strategy, the emirate also plans to expand its already strong mangrove cover threefold by 2031.

The emirate guide lists the islands by name, including Al Sinniya, Jazirat Al Ghalla, Al Keabe, Al Sow, Al Qaram, Al Humaidi, Al Chewria and Al Harmala, and calls Al Sinniya the most famous, [blessed with several environmental privileges](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-). Treat the islands as protected nature rather than a beach stop, and only land where a licensed guide says you may.

### Kayaking at Mangrove Beach

The easiest way into the mangroves is from the shore. Emirates Nature-WWF describes the [Mangrove Beach area within Khor Al Yeefrah](https://www.emiratesnaturewwf.ae/en/page/khor-al-yeefrah) as a place of dense mangrove forest and rich biodiversity, with kayaking near lagoon-side beaches. It says the lagoon is the breeding ground for the largest colony of the Socotra cormorant in the Gulf region.

The same organisation has been helping the mangroves grow. It reports that 14,000 saplings were planted in Khor Al Yeefrah in 2023 and 24,000 in 2024, with volunteers joining the planting and surveys. It says more than 300 volunteers took part in outreach and planting at Mangrove Beach in February 2024, and that the restored areas are revisited regularly to check how the young trees are doing. If you see fenced or marked planting areas along the shore, keep clear of them.

A paddle here is gentle. Expect calm channels, the plop of fish and crabs, and long views across the lagoon. A few habits help:

- **Go early.** The light is soft, the water calm and the birds active.
- **Wear a life jacket and cover up.** The sun off the water is strong in any season.
- **Give birds space.** Do not paddle towards nesting colonies or flush flocks for a photo.
- **Leave the roots alone.** Do not stand on or pull at mangrove roots.

### How a guided paddle usually runs

Most trips start with a short briefing on the beach, a quick lesson in holding the paddle and steering, and a few minutes in open water before you head into the channels. Guides tend to stop where the trees open out, to point out birds and explain how the mangroves trap sediment and shelter young fish. Bring a dry bag for your phone, water, a hat, and shoes you do not mind getting muddy. If you have never kayaked before, a double kayak with a confident partner is the easiest start.

If wildlife is your main interest, our guide to [birdwatching in the UAE](/stories/birdwatching-in-the-uae/) puts the lagoon alongside the country's other wetlands.

## Siniya Island and the pearling town

Out in the lagoon, Al Sinniya has turned out to hold one of the most interesting recent discoveries in the region. In 2023 the emirate's tourism and archaeology department announced what Gulf News reported as [the oldest pearling town yet found in the Arabian Gulf](https://gulfnews.com/uae/arabian-gulfs-oldest-pearling-town-discovered-in-umm-al-quwain-1.94600893), a settlement of about 12 hectares that flourished from the late 6th to the mid-8th centuries AD, close to an ancient Christian monastery found on the island the year before. The report describes discarded oyster shells, pearls and a pearl diver's weight among the finds.

Excavation is ongoing, and the island is not set up as a visitor attraction. Enjoy it from the water, and look out for displays and news from the emirate's museum rather than trying to land.

## The old town and the fort museum

Back on the peninsula, the old quarter is small and low-rise, with the fort as its anchor. Inside the walls it is shaded and still.

<photo-set layout="wide" keys="umm-al-quwain-travel-guide-4" alts="The walls and round tower of Umm Al Quwain Fort seen from the street in the old town" caption="Umm Al Quwain Fort once guarded the entrance to the old town and now houses the museum."></photo-set>

The government's emirate guide explains that [Umm Al Quwain Fort, once home to the Ruler](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), guarded the entrance to the old town and later became a museum. It adds that the emirate has six other forts. The Ministry of Economy and Tourism describes the [Umm Al Quwain National Museum](https://www.moet.gov.ae/en/-/umm-al-quwain-national-museum) as housed in Al-Ali Fort, one of the oldest buildings in the emirate, with artefacts spread through many rooms that show how people here once lived.

An hour at the museum is the best way to make sense of the lagoon, the pearling and fishing past, and the archaeology found nearby. Take it slowly: the rooms are small and the details, from doors to wall niches, are part of the story.

Walk the waterfront afterwards. Fishing boats, nets and the smell of the sea keep the old-town feel, especially in the late afternoon. Dress modestly in the old town and ask before photographing anyone, particularly fishermen at work.

## Deep history: Ed Dur and Tell Abraq

Umm Al Quwain has some of the most important archaeology in the country. The government's emirate guide describes it as [the most ancient emirate in the country](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), with archaeological traces showing links to Mesopotamia in the 3rd millennium BC. It lists Tell Abraq, dating from the Bronze Age, Alokaaab Island, and Ed Dur, a site with significance in the first century AD.

These are archaeological sites rather than visitor attractions with paths and signs. The fort museum is the place to see what has been found; if you want to visit a site itself, go with a guide who knows what access is allowed.

## Inland to Falaj Al Mualla

Umm Al Quwain also reaches into the desert. The emirate guide describes Falaj Al Mualla as an [attractive natural oasis about 50 km south-east of the town](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) on the road to Al Dhaid, with a camel racetrack. The government portal notes that the first poultry farm in the UAE was established there.

It is a quiet drive through farmland and dunes. If camel racing interests you, our guide to [watching camel racing in the UAE](/stories/camel-racing-uae-guide/) explains how race days work. Otherwise, treat it as a detour on the way to the east coast.

## Beaches, water sports and staying safe

Umm Al Quwain's sea is usually calm inside the lagoon, but open beaches are another matter. The Foreign Office warns that rip currents can occur at any beach and advises [swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). On boat trips, it advises checking that life jackets are available for all passengers.

<photo-set layout="inset-left" keys="umm-al-quwain-travel-guide-7" alts="The sun setting over the sea at a beach in Umm Al Quwain" caption="Evenings on the Umm Al Quwain shore are quiet and unhurried."></photo-set>

For something more active, the government's emirate guide says windsurfing, waterskiing and sailing can be enjoyed at the [Umm Al Quwain Marine Club](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), which also has a horse-riding centre. Book ahead and ask what experience you need for each activity.

Dress modestly away from the beach. This is a traditional, family-centred town, and our guide to [what to wear in the UAE](/stories/what-to-wear-uae-etiquette/) covers the basics.

## Getting there and getting around

Most visitors arrive by car from Dubai, Sharjah or Ajman along the coast, and a car or driver makes the most sense once you are here, since the beach, the old town and the kayak launch points are spread around the lagoon. Driving is unhurried compared with the big cities, but the usual UAE rules apply. The Foreign Office reminds visitors that it is [illegal to drink and drive, no matter how small the amount](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that you should only cross roads at designated pedestrian crossings.

If you would rather not drive, book a transfer or a guided trip that includes pick-up, and agree the return time before you set off. Taxis are less plentiful here than in Dubai, so do not count on finding one at a launch point.

## A day in Umm Al Quwain

If you have one day, this order follows the light and the heat:

1. **Dawn on the lagoon.** A guided kayak through the mangroves while the water is still.
2. **Breakfast and a rest.** Dry off, rehydrate and change into clothes for town.
3. **Late morning at the fort museum.** Shade, history and context for what you saw on the water.
4. **Lunch near the old town.** Fish is the natural choice on this coast.
5. **Afternoon at leisure.** A sleep, a swim at a lifeguarded beach, or water sports at the marine club.
6. **Sunset on the shore.** Walk the waterfront as the fishing boats come in.

## Umm Al Quwain with children

The lagoon suits families well. Calm water makes a short guided paddle realistic for older children, and spotting crabs, fish and herons keeps younger ones busy from the shore. Keep outings short and early, carry more water than you think you need, and plan the middle of the day indoors at the museum or back at your hotel. The fort's small rooms and towers are easy to explore with children, but keep a hand on little ones near the water's edge in the old town.

## What tends to catch visitors out

- **Arriving too late.** By late morning the light is harsh and the birds quieter. Dawn is the reward for staying nearby.
- **Expecting big-city choice.** Restaurants and shops are simple and fewer, so plan meals around the town centre.
- **Treating islands as picnic spots.** Many are protected, and some are active archaeological sites.
- **Underdressing in town.** Swimwear is for the beach. Cover up when you head to the museum or the waterfront.

## When to go and how long to stay

The cooler months are the most comfortable for paddling and walking, and they are also when birdlife is at its liveliest. Our [month-by-month guide](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) helps you pick dates.

A day trip from Dubai or Sharjah works, but an overnight is what lets you be on the lagoon at dawn. If you are heading north anyway, Umm Al Quwain makes a natural pause before Ras Al Khaimah. ## Quick answers

**Is Umm Al Quwain worth visiting?** Yes, if you enjoy quiet water, mangroves and birdlife. The lagoon, its islands and the old fort museum are the highlights, and the pace is far slower than in Dubai.

**Can you kayak in Umm Al Quwain?** Yes. Emirates Nature-WWF describes kayaking near lagoon-side beaches at Mangrove Beach, within Khor Al Yeefrah.

**How far is Umm Al Quwain from Ajman?** The UAE government's emirate guide puts it at about 20 minutes' drive north of Ajman.

**What wildlife lives around Umm Al Quwain?** The government portal says Al Seniah island is home to Arabian gazelles, falcons and turtles, and Emirates Nature-WWF says the lagoon hosts the Gulf region's largest Socotra cormorant colony.

**Can you visit the pearling town on Siniya Island?** It is an active excavation rather than a visitor site, so enjoy the island from the water and see the emirate's history at the fort museum.

## Questions to ask your operator

- What is the best time on our date to kayak the lagoon, given the tide and the heat?
- Do your kayak trips keep a safe distance from bird colonies, and are guides trained in local wildlife?
- Is the fort museum open when we plan to visit?
- Can we see any of the archaeological sites, such as Ed Dur, and what access is allowed?
- Would an overnight in Umm Al Quwain fit our route north, and what can you arrange at dawn?
