Step out of the lift on your first morning in Dubai and the view tells you which trip you have booked. A wall of glass towers and a fountain lake. A creek busy with wooden boats. A marina full of yachts, with the beach a short walk away. Same city, very different days.

Choosing where to stay in Dubai matters more than in many cities, because Dubai is long and spread out along the coast, and the journey between areas can eat into your time. The short answer for first-timers: stay in Downtown if you want the skyline and easy metro links, by the Creek in Old Dubai if you want heritage and atmosphere, and in Dubai Marina, JBR or Jumeirah if the beach is the point of the trip. Below we compare each, with the transport links that make them work.

## The quick way to choose where to stay in Dubai

Ask yourself three questions before you look at a single hotel:

- **What will you do most?** Sightseeing, beach time, shopping or culture.
- **How will you get around?** Metro and tram, taxis, or a hire car.
- **Who is travelling?** Couples, families and friends' groups tend to want different things.

Then match your answers to an area. Dubai Metro runs on [two lines, Red and Green](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), so a hotel within walking distance of a station makes a car-free trip far easier. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) explains the network in more detail.

## Downtown Dubai: the skyline on your doorstep

It is dusk, the lake below the tower lights up, and the crowds gather along the waterfront to watch the fountain. This is Downtown, and for many first-time visitors it is the obvious base.

The UAE government portal names the [Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the world](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), among Dubai's landmarks, and Downtown is built around it. You are close to big shopping, the fountain and a run of restaurants, and the area is served by the Red Line.

RTA also runs an electric traditional abra serving [Dubai Mall, Burj Plaza and The Palace](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), a gentle way to see the lake from the water.

**Best for:** first-timers who want the modern city, couples, short stays.
**Think twice if:** you want a beach on your doorstep or a slower, older feel.

## Old Dubai by the Creek: Al Fahidi, Deira and the souks

Here the morning starts with the putter of an abra engine and the smell of spices drifting across the water. Old Dubai, on both banks of Dubai Creek, is where the city's trading story began.

The government portal recommends [a visit to the historic Al Fahidi district, an abra ride across the creek and a bargain in the traditional souqs](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) to complete a Dubai trip. Stay nearby and all three are a stroll away. RTA's heritage abras link [Al Fahidi, Al Seef, Baniyas, Al Sabkha and the old souks in Deira](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), and the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash.

Al Fahidi is also home to the Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding, which runs [cultural meals, heritage tours and mosque visits](https://cultures.ae/) under the motto "Open Doors. Open Minds." If you want to understand Emirati culture, waking up in this part of town is a head start.

**Best for:** culture lovers, photographers, travellers who like to walk, repeat visitors.
**Think twice if:** you want a resort pool and beach, or very quiet streets after dark.

## Dubai Marina and JBR: waterfront living

Late afternoon in the Marina: joggers on the promenade, boats coming in, towers turning gold. Dubai Marina and neighbouring Jumeirah Beach Residence (JBR) give you a beach, a long waterfront walk and plenty of places to eat, all in one compact area.

Getting around is easier than it looks. The Dubai Tram [links Dubai Metro and the Palm Monorail and runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram/about-tram). Within the Marina itself, the Dubai Water Taxi runs a [hop-on, hop-off service with four stops](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine): Marina Terrace, Marina Walk, Dubai Marina Mall and Marina Promenade.

The catch is distance. Old Dubai is a long way off, so plan a full day when you head there rather than popping across.

**Best for:** beach lovers, groups of friends, longer stays.
**Think twice if:** your plans centre on Old Dubai and the souks.

## Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim: beaches and a lower skyline

Between Downtown and the Marina, the Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim districts feel lower and more residential, with villas, small shopping streets and long public beaches.

Beach time here can stretch well past sunset. Dubai Municipality has opened [beaches for night swimming in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting, safety screens and qualified lifeguards. It recommends swimming at night only in the designated zones.

This is also where you will find Jumeirah Mosque. The Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a [guided visit every day except Friday](https://cultures.ae/program/jumeirah-mosque-visit/), at 10am and 2pm, lasting 1 hour 15 minutes.

**Best for:** families, beach-focused trips, travellers who prefer a calmer base.
**Think twice if:** you will rely only on the metro, as stations are inland from the beaches.

## Palm Jumeirah: the resort option

The government portal lists [Palm Jumeirah, a palm-shaped island](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), among Dubai's modern landmarks. Staying here feels like a resort holiday: large hotels, private beaches and sea views.

It is the most self-contained option. The Palm Monorail connects to the tram, which in turn connects to the metro, but most visitors use taxis for longer trips. Choose the Palm if your plan is mostly pool, beach and the occasional outing.

**Best for:** honeymooners, families who want everything on site, relaxation.
**Think twice if:** you want to explore every day.

## Around Zabeel and the city centre

If you want something in between, the areas around Zabeel Park sit between Old Dubai and Downtown. The Dubai Frame stands in [Zabeel Park, reached through Al Jafiliya Metro Station](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), and from here both the Creek and Downtown are a short ride away. It suits travellers who want central access without paying for a skyline view.

## Before you book: checks that save surprises

### The extra charges on your bill

The UAE government portal explains that hotels and other tourist facilities may add [taxes, service charges and fees](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae), and that Dubai hotels charge a Tourism Dirham Fee per room per night, depending on the hotel's category. Check whether the price you see includes them.

### Passports, ages and alcohol

The UK Foreign Office notes that it is [normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that you cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult. It also says you can buy and drink alcohol in licensed venues such as hotels, restaurants and clubs, and that it is illegal to drink or be under the influence of alcohol in a public place anywhere in the UAE.

### Location versus price

A cheaper room far from a station can cost you in taxis and time. Look at a map before you book and note the walk to the nearest metro or tram stop. ## Quick answers

**What is the best area to stay in Dubai for first-timers?** Downtown suits most first-timers, with the Burj Khalifa nearby and links to the Red Line. Choose Old Dubai for heritage, or the Marina and Jumeirah for the beach.

**Can I get around Dubai without a car?** Yes, if you stay near a station. Dubai Metro runs on the Red and Green lines, and the tram connects the metro with the Palm Monorail along the coast.

**Is it better to stay near the beach or in the city?** It depends on your plans. Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim have public beaches, including night swimming beaches with lifeguards, while Downtown and Old Dubai are better for sightseeing.

**Do Dubai hotels charge extra fees?** Dubai hotels charge a Tourism Dirham Fee per room per night, and other taxes and service charges may apply, so check what your rate includes.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which neighbourhood suits the specific activities on our itinerary, and how long are the transfers from each?
- How far is our hotel from the nearest metro or tram stop, on foot?
- Does the room rate include the Tourism Dirham Fee and other charges?
- If we split our stay between the beach and Old Dubai, which order makes most sense?
- Which areas are quietest at night, and which are best for families with young children?
- Can you arrange airport transfers that match our arrival time?
