Step out of the lift on your first morning in Dubai and the view tells you which trip you have booked. A wall of glass towers and a fountain lake. A creek busy with wooden boats. A marina full of yachts, with the beach a short walk away. Same city, very different days, which is why where to stay in Dubai is the first decision worth getting right.

Choosing where to stay in Dubai matters more than in many cities, because Dubai is long and spread out along the coast, and the journey between areas can eat into your time. The short answer for first-timers: stay in Downtown if you want the skyline and easy metro links, by the Creek in Old Dubai if you want heritage and atmosphere, and in Dubai Marina, JBR or Jumeirah if the beach is the point of the trip. Below we compare each, with the transport links that make them work, what a normal day looks like from each base, and the checks worth making before you pay.

## The quick way to choose where to stay in Dubai

Ask yourself three questions before you look at a single hotel:

- **What will you do most?** Sightseeing, beach time, shopping or culture.
- **How will you get around?** Metro and tram, taxis, or a hire car.
- **Who is travelling?** Couples, families and friends' groups tend to want different things.

Then match your answers to an area. Dubai Metro runs on [two lines, Red and Green](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), so a hotel within walking distance of a station makes a car-free trip far easier. Our guide to [getting around Dubai](/stories/getting-around-dubai/) explains the network in more detail.

A useful way to picture the city is as a long strip running roughly along the coast. Old Dubai and the Creek sit at one end, Downtown and the business district sit in the middle, and the Marina and JBR sit much further along towards the other end, with Jumeirah's beaches and the Palm in between. Wherever you stay, the places at the far end of that strip will be your longest journeys, so put the base closest to the things you will do most often.

## Downtown Dubai: the skyline on your doorstep

It is dusk, the lake below the tower lights up, and the crowds gather along the waterfront to watch the fountain. This is Downtown, and for many first-time visitors it is the obvious base.

The UAE government portal names the [Burj Khalifa, the tallest building in the world](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), among Dubai's landmarks, and Downtown is built around it. You are close to big shopping, the fountain and a run of restaurants, and the area is served by the Red Line.

RTA also runs an electric traditional abra serving [Dubai Mall, Burj Plaza and The Palace](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), a gentle way to see the lake from the water.

### A day from Downtown

A typical day here starts with a short metro ride to whatever you are seeing that morning, because the Red Line runs through the middle of the city. The Red Line also serves stations near Old Dubai, and the Marina is further along the same line. You come back in the afternoon heat, rest, then walk out in the evening to the lake and the fountain without needing transport at all. That rhythm is why Downtown works so well for short stays.

What catches people out is the scale. The towers look close together on a map, but the blocks are big and the roads are wide, so a hotel described as near the fountain may still be a long, warm walk away. Check the walking route, not only the straight line.

**Best for:** first-timers who want the modern city, couples, short stays.
**Think twice if:** you want a beach on your doorstep or a slower, older feel.

## Old Dubai by the Creek: Al Fahidi, Deira and the souks

Here the morning starts with the putter of an abra engine and the smell of spices drifting across the water. Old Dubai, on both banks of Dubai Creek, is where the city's trading story began.

The government portal recommends [a visit to the historic Al Fahidi district, an abra ride across the creek and a bargain in the traditional souqs](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) to complete a Dubai trip. Stay nearby and all three are a stroll away. RTA's heritage abras link [Al Fahidi, Al Seef, Baniyas, Al Sabkha and the old souks in Deira](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), and the motorised traditional abra between Bur Dubai and Deira Old Souq accepts cash.

<photo-set layout="full" keys="where-to-stay-in-dubai-2" alts="A wooden abra carrying passengers across Dubai Creek from Bur Dubai towards Deira, with low buildings on the far bank" caption="Staying by the Creek puts the abra crossings between Bur Dubai and Deira a short walk away."></photo-set>

Al Fahidi is also home to the Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum Centre for Cultural Understanding, which runs [cultural meals, heritage tours and mosque visits](https://cultures.ae/) under the motto "Open Doors. Open Minds." If you want to understand Emirati culture, waking up in this part of town is a head start.

### A day from the Creek

Mornings here are made for walking. You can be in the lanes of Al Fahidi before the day warms up, cross the water by abra and browse the souks in Deira, then be back in your room by the middle of the day. Evenings are lively along the waterfront at Al Seef, and the abras keep crossing after dark. The trade off comes when you want the beach or the Marina, which are a longer journey from here, so plan those as dedicated outings rather than quick hops.

Streets in the older districts are busier and noisier than the resort areas, and the hotels are generally smaller. If you like a bit of bustle outside your door, that is part of the appeal.

**Best for:** culture lovers, photographers, travellers who like to walk, repeat visitors.
**Think twice if:** you want a resort pool and beach, or very quiet streets after dark.

## Dubai Marina and JBR: waterfront living

Late afternoon in the Marina: joggers on the promenade, boats coming in, towers turning gold. Dubai Marina and neighbouring Jumeirah Beach Residence (JBR) give you a beach, a long waterfront walk and plenty of places to eat, all in one compact area.

Getting around is easier than it looks. The Dubai Tram [links Dubai Metro and the Palm Monorail and runs along Al Sufouh Road and Jumeirah Beach Road](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/tram/about-tram). The government portal adds that it is considered [the first tram service outside Europe powered by a ground-based electric supply system](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport), which is why you will not see overhead wires. Within the Marina itself, the Dubai Water Taxi runs a [hop-on, hop-off service with four stops](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine): Marina Terrace, Marina Walk, Dubai Marina Mall and Marina Promenade.

### A day from the Marina

Here the day often runs the other way round. You start with a swim or a walk along the beach while it is still cool, spend the hot hours by a pool or indoors, and come out again for the promenade in the evening. Sightseeing days in Old Dubai or Downtown mean a metro ride, so leave earlier and accept that you will see fewer things in one go.

The catch is distance. Old Dubai is a long way off, so plan a full day when you head there rather than popping across.

**Best for:** beach lovers, groups of friends, longer stays.
**Think twice if:** your plans centre on Old Dubai and the souks.

## Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim: beaches and a lower skyline

Between Downtown and the Marina, the Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim districts feel lower and more residential, with villas, small shopping streets and long public beaches.

Beach time here can stretch well past sunset. Dubai Municipality has opened [beaches for night swimming in Jumeirah 2, Jumeirah 3 and Umm Suqeim 1](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/), with lighting, safety screens and qualified lifeguards. It says each night swimming beach is 800 metres long, that night swimming runs from sunset to sunrise, and it recommends swimming at night only in the designated zones.

This is also where you will find Jumeirah Mosque. The Centre for Cultural Understanding runs a [guided visit every day except Friday](https://cultures.ae/program/jumeirah-mosque-visit/), at 10am and 2pm, lasting 1 hour 15 minutes. Its page says prior booking is not required and that registration starts 30 minutes before the programme, so staying nearby makes an unhurried morning visit easy.

**Best for:** families, beach-focused trips, travellers who prefer a calmer base.
**Think twice if:** you will rely only on the metro, as stations are inland from the beaches.

## Palm Jumeirah: the resort option

The government portal lists [Palm Jumeirah, a palm-shaped island](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-), among Dubai's modern landmarks. Staying here feels like a resort holiday: large hotels, private beaches and sea views.

It is the most self-contained option. The Palm Monorail connects to the tram, which in turn connects to the metro, but most visitors use taxis for longer trips. Choose the Palm if your plan is mostly pool, beach and the occasional outing.

**Best for:** honeymooners, families who want everything on site, relaxation.
**Think twice if:** you want to explore every day.

## Around Zabeel and the city centre

If you want something in between, the areas around Zabeel Park sit between Old Dubai and Downtown. The Dubai Frame stands in [Zabeel Park, reached through Al Jafiliya Metro Station](https://www.dubaiframe.ae/en/plan-your-visit), and from here both the Creek and Downtown are a short ride away. It suits travellers who want central access without paying for a skyline view.

## Splitting your stay between two areas

On a longer trip, many visitors find that two bases beat one. A few nights by the Creek or in Downtown for sightseeing, followed by a few nights at the beach to slow down, means you spend less time in taxis and more time doing things. It also gives the trip a natural shape: busy first, restful at the end.

If you split, think about the order. Sightseeing first suits most people, because you arrive with energy and finish rested before the flight home. The beach first can work if you arrive tired from a long journey and want a day or two to recover. Either way, leave the move between hotels for the middle of the day, when you would be resting indoors anyway. If your trip continues to other emirates, our guide to [Dubai or Abu Dhabi](/stories/dubai-or-abu-dhabi-where-to-stay/) helps you decide where the second half belongs.

## Staying with children

Families tend to do best in Jumeirah, the Marina and JBR, or on the Palm, where the beach and pool are close and the evenings are spent outdoors. The night swimming beaches are a real help in warmer months, and Dubai Municipality stresses the [constant monitoring of children and following lifeguards' instructions](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-expands-its-beach-tourism-offering-with-the-opening-of-three-new-beaches-for-night-swimming/) there.

If you plan to use the metro with children, note RTA's rule that [children under 8 cannot use public transport without an adult](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), and that trains have cabins reserved for women and children. Boats have their own rule: RTA says children under 12 travelling with an adult [must wear a life jacket on board](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine) its marine transport. Our guide to [Dubai with children](/stories/dubai-with-kids/) covers the rest.

## Accessibility and step-free travel

If anyone in your group uses a wheelchair or has limited mobility, a hotel close to a metro station is worth even more. RTA lists [dedicated spaces for wheelchair users on all trains, and lift access to all metro station levels](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), along with tactile guidance paths and audible door alerts. Its Dubai Ferry seats [up to 98 people, including two wheelchairs](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/marine/about-marine), and the water taxi has a wheelchair area.

For door-to-door trips, the government portal notes that there are [special taxis for people of determination](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport), as well as for women and children and for families. When you book a hotel, ask specifically about step-free entrances, accessible bathrooms and the route from the room to the nearest station, rather than relying on a general description.

## Arriving and getting to your hotel

Most visitors take a taxi from the airport to their first hotel, especially with luggage. The UK Foreign Office advises using [only licensed taxis or other recognised forms of public transport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), including recognised ride-hailing apps, and says authorised Dubai taxis are painted cream with different coloured roofs. The government portal says you can book a Dubai taxi [through RTA or apps](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/transportation/Modes-of-transport/public-transport), and that booking charges differ by type of taxi and time of day.

If you would rather use the metro, check the luggage rule first. RTA allows [two suitcases per passenger](https://www.rta.ae/wps/portal/rta/ae/public-transport/metro/about-metro), one large and one small, with maximum sizes listed on its page, and asks that they are kept in the luggage area of each cabin. Travellers with large cases are usually happier in a taxi.

## Before you book: checks that save surprises

### The extra charges on your bill

The UAE government portal explains that hotels and other tourist facilities may add [taxes, service charges and fees](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae), and that Dubai hotels charge a Tourism Dirham Fee per room per night, for a maximum of 30 consecutive nights, depending on the hotel's category. Check whether the price you see includes them.

### Passports, ages and alcohol

The UK Foreign Office notes that it is [normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and that you cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult. It also says you can buy and drink alcohol in licensed venues such as hotels, restaurants and clubs, and that it is illegal to drink or be under the influence of alcohol in a public place anywhere in the UAE.

### Staying during Ramadan

If your dates fall in Ramadan, the Foreign Office suggests you [check opening hours of shops and restaurants](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), and notes that hotels and restaurants serving food in fasting hours may separate you from Muslim guests, for example with screens. It is a calm and interesting time to visit, and our guide to [visiting the UAE during Ramadan](/stories/visiting-uae-during-ramadan/) explains what changes.

### Location versus price

A cheaper room far from a station can cost you in taxis and time. Look at a map before you book and note the walk to the nearest metro or tram stop. [The best time to visit](/stories/best-time-to-visit-uae-month-by-month/) explains how the season affects demand.

### Day trips from your base

Wherever you stay, a day out of the city is easy to add. The government portal suggests a [1.5 hour drive to Hatta](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Discover-the-UAE/explore-the-uae-by-emirate-) for its natural water pools, and Sharjah and Abu Dhabi are both common day trips. Hotels on the side of the city closest to where you are heading save you time on the road. For more on the city, see our [Dubai destination page](/destinations/dubai/).

## Quick answers

**What is the best area to stay in Dubai for first-timers?** Downtown suits most first-timers, with the Burj Khalifa nearby and links to the Red Line. Choose Old Dubai for heritage, or the Marina and Jumeirah for the beach.

**Can I get around Dubai without a car?** Yes, if you stay near a station. Dubai Metro runs on the Red and Green lines, and the tram connects the metro with the Palm Monorail along the coast.

**Is it better to stay near the beach or in the city?** It depends on your plans. Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim have public beaches, including night swimming beaches with lifeguards, while Downtown and Old Dubai are better for sightseeing. On a longer trip, consider splitting your stay.

**Is Dubai accessible for wheelchair users?** The metro has wheelchair spaces on all trains and lifts to all station levels, and there are special taxis for people of determination. Check step-free access with your hotel directly.

**Do Dubai hotels charge extra fees?** Dubai hotels charge a Tourism Dirham Fee per room per night, and other taxes and service charges may apply, so check what your rate includes.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which neighbourhood suits the specific activities on our itinerary, and how long are the transfers from each?
- How far is our hotel from the nearest metro or tram stop, on foot?
- Does the room rate include the Tourism Dirham Fee and other charges?
- If we split our stay between the beach and Old Dubai, which order makes most sense?
- Which areas are quietest at night, and which are best for families with young children?
- Does the hotel have step-free access and accessible rooms, if we need them?
- Can you arrange airport transfers that match our arrival time?
