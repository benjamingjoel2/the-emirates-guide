Picture two mornings in Ras Al Khaimah. In the first, you open the curtains onto flat, pale water and a strip of sand, with the mountains a blue outline far behind. In the second, you step outside into cool, thin air, and the sea is a distant silver line far below. Both are the same emirate, a short drive apart. Which one you wake up to depends on where you stay.

Choosing where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah comes down to one question: what will you do most? For a beach holiday with children, the resort islands of Al Marjan and Mina Al Arab are the obvious choice. For heritage, food and evening walks, base yourself in or near the city. For the mountains, a night on or near Jebel Jais gives you the cool early hours when the trails are at their best. Most first-timers do well on the coast and drive inland for the day.

## How the emirate is laid out

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) describes a capital split by a creek into the western old town of Ras Al Khaimah and the eastern district of Al Nakheel. It names Al Jazirah Al Hamra, Digdaga, Khatt and Masafi as other key towns, and describes Al Marjan Island, to the south, as a group of four coral-shaped man-made islands with hotels and leisure attractions.

Distances are short. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says the emirate is [about 45 minutes by car](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) from Dubai International Airport, and that getting around is easiest by car, with metered taxis and a more limited bus network. Wherever you stay, expect to drive or take taxis to most sights.

## Where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah for the beach

### Al Marjan Island: the family resort strip

Al Marjan is built for easy beach days. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/why-you-should-visit-al-marjan-island/) describes it as the emirate's first man-made archipelago, with 23 km of waterfront, including 7.8 km of white sandy beaches, and a palm-lined corniche with play areas, cafés and a running track. It says the island is always lively, and especially busy at weekends.

**Suits:** families who want pools, kids' clubs and a beach on the doorstep. **Less good for:** anyone who wants local streets and old buildings within walking distance.

### Mina Al Arab: islands and mangroves

A little closer to the city, Mina Al Arab feels calmer. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/mina-al-arab/) describes it as a twin-island community of two landscaped man-made islands, with white-sand beaches, mangroves and an ecological reserve, reached by two bridges or by boat. It mentions kayaking along the shores and wetlands.

Nearby, the public [Flamingo Beach](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/flamingo-beach/) lies between the city centre and Al Hamra Village. The tourism board says the greater flamingo lives in the Mina Al Arab mangroves.

**Suits:** couples and families who want a quieter beach base with some nature. **Less good for:** a busy social scene.

### Al Hamra: a marina and old stones

Further south along the coast, the Al Hamra area combines resorts with a marina. The tourism board describes [Al Hamra Waterfront](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-hamra-waterfront/) as a lifestyle area on the north side of the Al Hamra Lagoon with places to eat, water sports and a marina.

Its big advantage is history on the doorstep. The abandoned pearling village of [Al Jazeera Al Hamra](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-jazeera-al-hamra/) is close by, just off the E11 highway, so you can walk its lanes in the early morning before the heat builds. Our [guide to the pearling village](/stories/al-jazirat-al-hamra-guide/) covers it.

**Suits:** travellers who want a resort but also some culture, and anyone driving up from Dubai who wants a shorter journey.

## Staying in town

The city is where daily life happens. Base yourself here and you can walk [Corniche Al Qawasim](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-qawasim-corniche/), which the tourism board describes as a waterfront promenade along the mangroves, lined with restaurants and cafés, that comes alive at night. From stations along the corniche, the RAK Transport Authority runs a [heritage-style abra service](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/activities/abra-in-ras-al-khaimah-creek/) on the creek.

In the old town, the [National Museum of Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/national-museum-of-ras-al-khaimah/) occupies a historic fort. City hotels tend to be more business-like than the beach resorts, but you trade a private beach for evenings among residents rather than other holidaymakers.

**Suits:** independent travellers, food lovers and anyone on a shorter stay who wants to see the emirate's everyday side.

## Staying in the mountains

For the mountains, staying high changes the trip. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) says you can stay overnight on Jebel Jais, with options that include camps with air-conditioned cabins and glamping. It says the mountain is usually around 10°C cooler than elsewhere in the emirate, and recommends visiting from October to April.

The appeal is time. You can be on a trail at first light, or watch the sunset from the [Jais Viewing Deck Park](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/) without a long drive back down in the dark. Our [Jebel Jais guide](/stories/jebel-jais-guide/) explains the road and the trails.

**Suits:** walkers, cyclists and anyone who wants cool air and quiet. **Less good for:** a beach holiday, or anyone who dislikes winding roads.

## Desert and wilder coast

Ras Al Khaimah also has dunes inland, and the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) lists rolling sand dunes among its landscapes. A desert stay suits people who want silence and stars more than a buffet and a pool. Our guide to choosing a [desert stay or city hotel](/stories/desert-stay-or-city-hotel-uae/) weighs it up.

In the far north, [Al Rams Beach](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-rams-beach/) and Saraya Island are popular for camping, but the tourism board notes you need a four-wheel drive, or a guide to drive you, to reach the island.

## What to check before you book

- **Location against your plans.** A beach resort far south of the city adds driving time to every trip north to Dhayah Fort or the mountains.
- **Beach safety.** The UK Foreign Office advises [swimming only from approved beaches](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) with lifeguards and flags, so check whether your hotel's beach is supervised.
- **Travelling teenagers.** The Foreign Office says you cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult.
- **Season.** The tourism board says October to April is the [best time to visit](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), so book early for those months.

## Quick answers

**What is the best area to stay in Ras Al Khaimah for families?** Al Marjan Island, which the tourism board describes as one of the emirate's most family-friendly areas, with beaches, play areas and a corniche.

**Can you stay on Jebel Jais?** Yes. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says overnight options on the mountain include camps with air-conditioned cabins and glamping.

**Is Ras Al Khaimah a good base for exploring the emirate?** Yes. The tourism board says it is about 45 minutes by car from Dubai International Airport, and most sights are a short drive from the coast.

**Do I need a car if I stay in a beach resort?** It helps. The tourism board says getting around is easiest by car, though metered taxis are available.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which area would put us closest to the things we most want to do, and how long are the drives from there?
- Is the hotel beach lifeguarded, and is the water calm enough for young children in our month?
- Can you arrange a driver for a day in the mountains and a day at the heritage sites?
- Would one or two nights on Jebel Jais make sense for our group, and what are the facilities like?
- How busy is the area at weekends or during holidays on our dates?
- Can you arrange transfers from Dubai International Airport or Ras Al Khaimah International Airport?
