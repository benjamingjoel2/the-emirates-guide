Picture two mornings in Ras Al Khaimah. In the first, you open the curtains onto flat, pale water and a strip of sand, with the mountains a blue outline far behind. In the second, you step outside into cool, thin air, and the sea is a distant silver line far below. Both are the same emirate, a short drive apart. Which one you wake up to depends on where you stay.

Choosing where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah comes down to one question: what will you do most? For a beach holiday with children, the resort islands of Al Marjan and Mina Al Arab are the obvious choice. For heritage, food and evening walks, base yourself in or near the city. For the mountains, a night on or near Jebel Jais gives you the cool early hours when the trails are at their best. Most first-timers do well on the coast and drive inland for the day.

## How the emirate is laid out

The [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) describes a capital split by a creek into the western old town of Ras Al Khaimah and the eastern district of Al Nakheel. It names Al Jazirah Al Hamra, Digdaga, Khatt and Masafi as other key towns, and describes Al Marjan Island, to the south, as a group of four coral-shaped man-made islands with hotels and leisure attractions.

Distances are short. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says the emirate is [about 45 minutes by car](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) from Dubai International Airport, and that getting around is easiest by car, with metered taxis, ride-hailing apps and a more limited bus network. It adds that several hotels offer airport transfers and that major resorts often run shuttles and excursions to the main sights and malls. Wherever you stay, expect to drive or take taxis to most sights.

A useful way to think about it: the coast runs roughly north to south, with Al Rams and the heritage sites of Dhayah at the northern end, the city and its corniche in the middle, and Mina Al Arab, Al Hamra and Al Marjan stretching away to the south towards Dubai. The mountains rise inland behind all of them. The further south you stay, the shorter your drive from Dubai, and the longer your drive to the northern sights.

## Where to stay in Ras Al Khaimah for the beach

### Al Marjan Island: the family resort strip

Al Marjan is built for easy beach days. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/blog/why-you-should-visit-al-marjan-island/) describes it as the emirate's first man-made archipelago, with 23 km of waterfront, including 7.8 km of white sandy beaches, and a palm-lined corniche with play areas, cafés and a dedicated 2 km running track. It says the island is always lively, especially busy at weekends, and home to the Ras Al Khaimah Half Marathon. The resorts here lean heavily towards families, with kids' clubs, large pools and all-inclusive options.

**Suits:** families who want pools, kids' clubs and a beach on the doorstep. **Less good for:** anyone who wants local streets and old buildings within walking distance.

### Mina Al Arab: islands and mangroves

A little closer to the city, Mina Al Arab feels calmer. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/mina-al-arab/) describes it as a twin-island community of two landscaped man-made islands, with white-sand beaches, mangroves and an ecological reserve, reached by two bridges or by boat. It mentions kayaking along the shores and wetlands, and says the islands include the emirate's first overwater villas.

The same page gives handy drive times: about 20 minutes to the city centre, about 30 minutes to Ras Al Khaimah airport and to Al Marjan Island, and a little over an hour to Dubai. That central position is its quiet advantage.

Nearby, the public [Flamingo Beach](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/flamingo-beach/) lies between the city centre and Al Hamra Village. The tourism board says the greater flamingo lives in the Mina Al Arab mangroves, and that the beach has lifeguards, shallow water and walkways suited to prams and wheelchairs.

**Suits:** couples and families who want a quieter beach base with some nature. **Less good for:** a busy social scene.

### Al Hamra: a marina and old stones

Further south along the coast, the Al Hamra area combines resorts with a marina. The tourism board describes [Al Hamra Waterfront](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-hamra-waterfront/) as a family lifestyle area on the north side of the Al Hamra Lagoon with places to eat, water sports and a 222-berth marina. Its clubhouse, the same page says, is open daily from 8am to 8pm and is family and pet friendly.

Its big advantage is history on the doorstep. The abandoned pearling village of [Al Jazeera Al Hamra](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-jazeera-al-hamra/) is close by, just off the E11 highway, so you can walk its lanes in the early morning before the heat builds. Our [guide to the pearling village](/stories/al-jazirat-al-hamra-guide/) covers it.

**Suits:** travellers who want a resort but also some culture, and anyone driving up from Dubai who wants a shorter journey.

## Staying in town

The city is where daily life happens. Base yourself here and you can walk [Corniche Al Qawasim](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-qawasim-corniche/), which the tourism board describes as a three-kilometre waterfront promenade along the mangroves, lined with restaurants and cafés, that comes alive at night. From stations along the corniche, the RAK Transport Authority runs a [heritage-style abra service](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/activities/abra-in-ras-al-khaimah-creek/) on the creek, with four stations and boats running from 10am to 10pm, seven days a week.

In the old town, the [National Museum of Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/national-museum-of-ras-al-khaimah/) occupies a historic fort, open Tuesday to Sunday. City hotels tend to be more business-like than the beach resorts, but you trade a private beach for evenings among residents rather than other holidaymakers, and you are well placed for the drive north to Dhayah Fort and the pearl farm at Al Rams.

**Suits:** independent travellers, food lovers and anyone on a shorter stay who wants to see the emirate's everyday side.

## Staying in the mountains

For the mountains, staying high changes the trip. [Visit Ras Al Khaimah](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) says you can stay overnight on Jebel Jais, with options that include a camp with air-conditioned, en-suite cabins and a glamping camp it describes as the highest in the UAE. It says the mountain is usually around 10°C cooler than elsewhere in the emirate, and recommends visiting from October to April.

The appeal is time. You can be on a trail at first light, or watch the sunset from the [Jais Viewing Deck Park](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/jais-viewing-deck-park/) without a long drive back down in the dark. Our [Jebel Jais guide](/stories/jebel-jais-guide/) explains the road and the trails.

**Suits:** walkers, cyclists and anyone who wants cool air and quiet. **Less good for:** a beach holiday, or anyone who dislikes winding roads.

## Desert and wilder coast

Ras Al Khaimah also has dunes inland, and the [UAE government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/ras-al-khaimah) lists rolling sand dunes among its landscapes. The tourism board's [FAQs](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) mention desert trips with camel rides, falconry displays and Bedouin-style dinners, including overnight options. A desert stay suits people who want silence and stars more than a buffet and a pool. Our guide to choosing a [desert stay or city hotel](/stories/desert-stay-or-city-hotel-uae/) weighs it up.

In the far north, [Al Rams Beach](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/discover/attractions/al-rams-beach/) and Saraya Island are popular for camping, but the tourism board notes you need a four-wheel drive, or a guide to drive you, to reach the island. It describes Al Rams as a fishing community with a limited number of shops, so this is a place for self-sufficient campers, not a hotel base.

## Matching your base to the season

The tourism board says October to April is the [best time to visit](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), with winter temperatures of around 15°C to 28°C between November and March. In those months every base works, and the choice is simply about what you want to do.

In summer, which the tourism board puts at June to September, the balance shifts. Inland temperatures can exceed 45°C, so a resort with a pool and shade becomes the centre of the day, and outings shrink to early mornings and evenings. The mountain is cooler but still hot: the tourism board's [Jebel Jais page](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/location/place/jebel-jais/) gives an average of 31°C at the peak in the daytime. Book early for the cooler months, when demand is highest.

## A day from each base

It helps to picture an ordinary day before you book.

**From Al Marjan Island**, mornings are for the beach and the pool, and the corniche is busy with runners and families in the cooler hours. A heritage day means a drive north past the city to the museum, Dhayah or Al Rams, so you will want an early start and a car or driver.

**From Mina Al Arab or Al Hamra**, you sit in the middle. The pearling village is close, Flamingo Beach is on the way into town, and the city is about 20 minutes away, which makes half-day outings easy. You can come back for a swim in the afternoon and still go out again for the corniche in the evening.

**From the city**, you can walk the corniche after dark, ride the abra across the creek and reach the National Museum without a car. Beach time means a short drive to a public beach.

**From Jebel Jais**, the day starts and ends on the mountain. You walk early, rest at midday and watch the sun set over the Gulf, and the coast becomes the day trip rather than the base.

## Splitting your stay

On a stay of four nights or more, consider two bases. A common pattern is three nights on the coast for the beach, the museum and the heritage sites, then one night on Jebel Jais for sunrise and an early walk. Alternatively, combine one night in town for the corniche and the old town with the rest of the stay at a beach resort. Moving once costs you a morning, but it saves several long drives and lets you see the emirate at its best times of day.

## Families and accessibility

For families, the resort islands make life easy: beaches, pools and kids' clubs in one place, and short transfers. Al Marjan is the busiest and most family-focused; Mina Al Arab is quieter. If anyone in your group uses a wheelchair, look at Flamingo Beach, which the tourism board says has walkways suited to prams, walking aids and wheelchairs, and check step-free access at your hotel directly. The mountain camps and the heritage sites are much harder going.

## What to check before you book

- **Location against your plans.** A beach resort far south of the city adds driving time to every trip north to Dhayah Fort or the mountains.
- **Beach safety.** The UK Foreign Office advises [swimming only from approved beaches](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) with lifeguards and flags, so check whether your hotel's beach is supervised.
- **Travelling teenagers.** The Foreign Office says you cannot stay in a hotel if you are under 18 and not accompanied by an adult.
- **Your passport at check-in.** The Foreign Office notes that it is normal practice for hotels to take a photocopy of your passport or Emirates ID.
- **Transfers and shuttles.** Ask whether the hotel runs airport transfers or shuttles to sights, which the tourism board says many do.
- **Season.** The tourism board says October to April is the [best time to visit](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/), so book early for those months.

## Getting around from your base

Once you have chosen, plan your transport. The tourism board's [FAQs](https://visitrasalkhaimah.com/faqs/) mention the transport authority's Sayr app for booking buses and taxis and checking routes, and recommend a four-wheel drive only for mountain and desert areas. ## Quick answers

**What is the best area to stay in Ras Al Khaimah for families?** Al Marjan Island, which the tourism board describes as one of the emirate's most family-friendly areas, with beaches, play areas and a corniche.

**Can you stay on Jebel Jais?** Yes. Visit Ras Al Khaimah says overnight options on the mountain include a camp with air-conditioned cabins and glamping.

**Is Ras Al Khaimah a good base for exploring the emirate?** Yes. The tourism board says it is about 45 minutes by car from Dubai International Airport, and most sights are a short drive from the coast.

**Do I need a car if I stay in a beach resort?** It helps. The tourism board says getting around is easiest by car, though metered taxis are available and many resorts run shuttles.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which area would put us closest to the things we most want to do, and how long are the drives from there?
- Is the hotel beach lifeguarded, and is the water calm enough for young children in our month?
- Can you arrange a driver for a day in the mountains and a day at the heritage sites?
- Would one or two nights on Jebel Jais make sense for our group, and what are the facilities like?
- How busy is the area at weekends or during holidays on our dates?
- Can you arrange transfers from Dubai International Airport or Ras Al Khaimah International Airport?
