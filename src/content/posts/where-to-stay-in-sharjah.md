The call to prayer drifts across Khalid Lagoon, the domes of Al Noor Mosque begin to glow, and families settle onto the grass along the corniche with flasks of tea. Nobody is in a hurry. This is Sharjah after dark, and it feels like a different country from the towers a short drive south.

That contrast is exactly why people choose to stay here. Sharjah is the UAE's culture-first emirate, with museums, souks and restored heritage streets close together, and a calmer, more traditional feel than its neighbour. It also has its own rules, and one of them matters a great deal when you choose a hotel.

So, where to stay in Sharjah? For most visitors, the best base is near the corniche, between the Heart of Sharjah heritage area and Khalid Lagoon, where the main museums and souks are within easy reach. Beach lovers should look at Al Khan or Al Heera, and anyone wanting mountains and sea should consider the east coast at Khorfakkan or Kalba.

## Why stay in Sharjah at all?

Sharjah describes itself as [the UAE's UNESCO Cultural Capital](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/), and the city's heritage museums, souks and waterfronts reflect that. Staying here, rather than visiting on a day trip, lets you see the evenings, when the lagoon and corniche are at their liveliest and the heat has eased.

<photo-set layout="inset-right" keys="where-to-stay-in-sharjah-1" alts="A wind tower and arcaded upper gallery around the courtyard of Bait Al Naboodah, seen through a doorway in the Heart of Sharjah" caption="Bait Al Naboodah, a restored merchant house with a wind tower, is one of the coral-walled houses of the Heart of Sharjah."></photo-set>

It is also a quieter, more conservative place. Most of all, the UK Foreign Office notes that [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). That applies to hotels too. If a drink with dinner is part of your holiday, stay elsewhere or plan accordingly; if it is not, Sharjah's calm can be a real plus. Our guide to [Sharjah's rules for visitors](/stories/sharjah-rules-for-visitors/) covers the details.

What you gain is a city that rewards slow travel. Mornings can go on museums and old lanes, afternoons on a long lunch and a rest, and evenings on the waterfronts, where you will see more local families than tour groups.

## Heart of Sharjah and the corniche: for culture lovers

This is the area to choose if museums and old streets are why you came. The Heart of Sharjah heritage district holds the [Sharjah Heritage Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-heritage-museum/), set in a restored 18th-century pearl merchant's house opposite Souk Al Arsa. The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization sits [on the Corniche, just north of the Heart of Sharjah district](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/sharjah-museum-of-islamic-civilization/).

<photo-set layout="full" keys="where-to-stay-in-sharjah-2" alts="A restored coral-stone building beside a paved square with palm trees and lamps in the Heart of Sharjah" caption="The Heart of Sharjah is the city's old merchant quarter, restored as a heritage and arts district."></photo-set>

Visit Sharjah describes the district as the city's original merchant centre, [reborn as a heritage and arts district](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/heart-of-sharjah/), with coral-walled houses such as Bait Al Naboodah, the Sharjah Calligraphy Museum, and art spaces and museums set in traditional wind tower buildings. It suggests looking out for Al Hisn Fort and the only round wind tower in the UAE, and notes that the area has been listed as a potential UNESCO World Heritage Site. The same page says the district is just off the Corniche and about 20 minutes from Sharjah International Airport, which makes it practical for short stays too.

The Central Souk, with its blue-tiled facade, is [a short walk from the corniche](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/shopping/central-souk-blue-souk/), and the tourism board says it is open until late every evening, making it a good after-dinner stroll. It describes more than 600 shops across two levels, with crafts, textiles and carpets upstairs and a row of jewellery shops on the lagoon side.

**Best for:** first-time visitors to Sharjah, culture fans, anyone without a car.
**Think about:** older streets can be busy with traffic, and this is a city setting rather than a resort one.

Our [Heart of Sharjah guide](/stories/heart-of-sharjah-guide/) and [Sharjah museums guide](/stories/sharjah-museums-guide/) help you plan the days.

## Khalid Lagoon and the Buhaira Corniche: for evening walks

A little inland from the old quarter, the lagoon is where Sharjah comes out to play after sunset. Al Noor Mosque stands [next to Khalid Lagoon on the Buhaira Corniche](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/al-noor-mosque/), and the tourism board says it offers guided tours for visitors and is lit up at night. Al Noor Island, with its butterfly house and art installations, is just across the water.

The mosque is open to non-Muslims for guided tours, and Visit Sharjah says traditional Emirati dress is offered to visitors at the entrance and that photography is allowed. Guides cover Islamic culture, Emirati heritage and the building itself, and there is time for questions at the end, so it works well with children.

If you are staying nearby, it is worth knowing what else is close. Visit Sharjah's city guide describes the [Al Noor Island Butterfly House](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/) as home to more than 20 butterfly species, and lists the Islamic Botanical Garden, which displays plants mentioned in the Holy Qur'an, among the city's nature stops. The tourism board's mosque page also points to Sharjah Mosque, the largest in the emirate, which it says opened in 2019.

**Best for:** couples, families who like an evening walk, anyone who wants water views without being on the beach.
**Think about:** check how far your hotel is from the souks and museums if you plan to walk.

## Al Qasba and Al Majaz: for lively evenings

If you want a little more going on after dark, look at hotels near Al Qasba or the Al Majaz Waterfront, both on the lagoon side of the city. Visit Sharjah describes Al Qasba as a [kilometre-long canal connecting Khalid Lagoon and Al Khan Lagoon](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/al-qasba/), with pedestrian bridges, restaurants and cafes, the Maraya Art Centre, a theatre, a children's fun zone and abra rides. It suggests arriving by boat from Al Noor Island, and says the area is easy to reach from the Sharjah bus station.

Close by, the [Al Majaz Waterfront](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/al-majaz-waterfront/) is a lakeside area with the Sharjah Musical Fountain, mini-golf and the Maraya Art Park, which the tourism board suggests for evening strolls and family picnics.

**Best for:** families with children, anyone who likes to eat out and walk in the evenings.
**Think about:** these areas can be busy on weekends and holidays, so ask for a room away from the main walkways if you sleep lightly.

## Al Khan and Al Heera: for a beach base

Sharjah has a Gulf coastline, and two areas suit travellers who want sand close by.

### Al Khan

Al Khan Beach is in the Al Khan neighbourhood close to Sharjah city, with a [dedicated swimming area, water sports and showers and changing rooms](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/al-khan-beach/). The Sharjah Maritime Museum and Sharjah Aquarium are a short walk away. It is also the part of Sharjah nearest to Dubai, which helps if you want to split your time.

### Al Heera

Further north, Al Heera Beach is [the longest beach in the city](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/al-heera-beach/), with a jogging track, bicycle track, sports fields, children's play areas and a skate park, plus places to eat.

**Best for:** families, active travellers, longer stays.
**Think about:** you will need taxis to reach the heritage area. As everywhere in the UAE, the Foreign Office reminds visitors that [swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), so cover up when you leave the sand. It also warns that rip currents can occur at any beach, and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and checking for red flags.

## Staying in Sharjah to visit Dubai

Many visitors look at Sharjah because it is next door to Dubai. It can work, especially if you want a quieter base, but the drive between the two cities can be slow at busy times. If you will be in Dubai most days, weigh the commute honestly against the calmer evenings. Our [Sharjah day trip from Dubai](/stories/sharjah-day-trip-from-dubai/) guide looks at it from the other direction.

A useful rule of thumb: if Sharjah's museums, souks and waterfronts are the main reason for your trip and Dubai is the side visit, stay in Sharjah. If it is the other way round, stay in Dubai and come to Sharjah for a long day, ideally ending with an evening at the lagoon.

## Getting around from your hotel

Sharjah is a city to explore in short hops. Within the heritage area and along the corniche, walking works well in the cooler months and in the evenings. For anything further, such as the beaches, Al Qasba or the airport, taxis are the simplest option, and the Foreign Office advises using only licensed taxis or recognised ride-hailing apps. Visit Sharjah also notes that Al Qasba is easy to reach from the Sharjah bus station. If you plan day trips to the east coast or Mleiha, a hire car or a booked transfer saves a lot of time.

## How two days from a Sharjah base might look

This is one way to use a central base; adjust it to your interests and the season.

1. **Day one, morning:** the Heart of Sharjah lanes and the Sharjah Heritage Museum while it is cooler.
2. **Day one, afternoon:** the Museum of Islamic Civilization on the corniche, then a rest at the hotel.
3. **Day one, evening:** the Central Souk and a walk along the lagoon to see Al Noor Mosque lit up.
4. **Day two, morning:** a guided tour of Al Noor Mosque, then Al Noor Island across the water.
5. **Day two, evening:** a boat or walk to Al Qasba, or a beach sunset at Al Heera or Al Khan.

## Further afield: the east coast and the desert

Sharjah is not only a city. The emirate also has a coastline on the Gulf of Oman, with seaside towns at [Khorfakkan and Kalba](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/), where Visit Sharjah highlights scuba diving, kayaking, snorkelling, boat trips to spot dolphins and the mangroves of Kalba. A night or two here, with mountains behind and the sea in front, is a very different experience from the city. See our guide to the [Khorfakkan](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) area for more.

Inland, Mleiha is home to an archaeological centre and dunes, and Visit Sharjah says visitors love to [spend a night under the stars](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) there, with the centre able to advise on camping and glamping. ## Booking a hotel in Sharjah

The UAE government portal lets you [search for and book a hotel, hotel apartment or resort in Sharjah](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae) and the other emirates, and lists the taxes and charges that hotels across the UAE may add, such as service charges and municipality fees. Check what your quoted rate includes.

A few practical checks before you book:

- **Location against your plans.** Mark the museums, souks and beaches you want on a map, then choose a hotel between them.
- **Ramadan timing.** During the holy month, hotel restaurants may serve differently in daylight hours. The Foreign Office notes that hotels serving food in fasting hours [may separate you from Muslim guests, for example with screens](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).
- **Dress.** The same advice asks visitors to dress modestly in public areas, with shoulders and knees covered for women, which is sensible to follow anywhere in Sharjah.
- **Families.** Ask about connecting rooms, cots and pools with shade. The Foreign Office also notes that under-18s cannot stay in a hotel unless accompanied by an adult.
- **Paperwork at check-in.** It is normal practice for UAE hotels to take a photocopy of your passport, so keep it to hand when you arrive.

## What catches people out

- **Expecting a bar.** No hotel in Sharjah serves alcohol, and bringing it in is not allowed either.
- **Underestimating traffic to Dubai.** The two cities are close on a map, but rush hour can turn a short hop into a long one.
- **Booking a beach hotel for a museum trip.** Al Heera and Al Khan are pleasant, but you will spend time in taxis if most of your plans are in the old town.
- **Forgetting the weekend.** Waterfronts, souks and Al Qasba are at their busiest when local families are off work, which is lovely for atmosphere but less so for quiet.

## Quick answers

**What is the best area to stay in Sharjah?** For culture and walking, the corniche between the Heart of Sharjah and Khalid Lagoon. For lively evenings, Al Qasba or Al Majaz. For the beach, Al Khan or Al Heera.

**Can you drink alcohol in Sharjah hotels?** No. The UK Foreign Office says drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah.

**Is Sharjah good for families?** Yes. Al Heera Beach has play areas, bicycle and jogging tracks, Al Qasba has a children's fun zone, and Al Khan is close to the Maritime Museum and Aquarium.

**Can non-Muslims visit Al Noor Mosque?** Yes. Visit Sharjah says the mosque is open to non-Muslims for guided tours, with traditional dress offered at the entrance.

**Can you stay on Sharjah's east coast?** Yes. Khorfakkan and Kalba are Sharjah's seaside towns on the Gulf of Oman, with diving, kayaking, boat trips and mangroves nearby.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which area of Sharjah best suits the museums and souks we want to see, and can we walk between them?
- How long should we allow for transfers to Dubai at the times we would travel?
- Can you arrange a guided visit to Al Noor Mosque or the Heart of Sharjah museums during our stay?
- Is a night on the east coast at Khorfakkan or Kalba realistic within our itinerary?
- How will Ramadan or any public holiday during our dates affect restaurants and opening times near our hotel?
