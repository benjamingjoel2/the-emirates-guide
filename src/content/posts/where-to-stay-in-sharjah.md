The call to prayer drifts across Khalid Lagoon, the domes of Al Noor Mosque begin to glow, and families settle onto the grass along the corniche with flasks of tea. Nobody is in a hurry. This is Sharjah after dark, and it feels like a different country from the towers a short drive south.

That contrast is exactly why people choose to stay here. Sharjah is the UAE's culture-first emirate, with museums, souks and restored heritage streets close together, and a calmer, more traditional feel than its neighbour. It also has its own rules, and one of them matters a great deal when you choose a hotel.

So, where to stay in Sharjah? For most visitors, the best base is near the corniche, between the Heart of Sharjah heritage area and Khalid Lagoon, where the main museums and souks are within easy reach. Beach lovers should look at Al Khan or Al Heera, and anyone wanting mountains and sea should consider the east coast at Khorfakkan or Kalba.

## Why stay in Sharjah at all?

Sharjah describes itself as [the UAE's UNESCO Cultural Capital](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/sharjah-city/), and the city's heritage museums, souks and waterfronts reflect that. Staying here, rather than visiting on a day trip, lets you see the evenings, when the lagoon and corniche are at their liveliest and the heat has eased.

It is also a quieter, more conservative place. Most of all, the UK Foreign Office notes that [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). That applies to hotels too. If a drink with dinner is part of your holiday, stay elsewhere or plan accordingly; if it is not, Sharjah's calm can be a real plus. Our guide to [Sharjah's rules for visitors](/stories/sharjah-rules-for-visitors/) covers the details.

## Heart of Sharjah and the corniche: for culture lovers

This is the area to choose if museums and old streets are why you came. The Heart of Sharjah heritage district holds the [Sharjah Heritage Museum](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/sharjah-heritage-museum/), set in a restored 18th-century pearl merchant's house opposite Souk Al Arsa. The Sharjah Museum of Islamic Civilization sits [on the Corniche, just north of the Heart of Sharjah district](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/sharjah-museum-of-islamic-civilization/).

The Central Souk, with its blue-tiled facade, is [a short walk from the corniche](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/shopping/central-souk-blue-souk/), and the tourism board says it is open until late every evening, making it a good after-dinner stroll.

**Best for:** first-time visitors to Sharjah, culture fans, anyone without a car.
**Think about:** older streets can be busy with traffic, and this is a city setting rather than a resort one.

## Khalid Lagoon and the Buhaira Corniche: for evening walks

A little inland from the old quarter, the lagoon is where Sharjah comes out to play after sunset. Al Noor Mosque stands [next to Khalid Lagoon on the Buhaira Corniche](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/culture/al-noor-mosque/), and the tourism board says it offers guided tours for visitors and is lit up at night. Al Noor Island, with its butterfly house and art installations, is just across the water.

**Best for:** couples, families who like an evening walk, anyone who wants water views without being on the beach.
**Think about:** check how far your hotel is from the souks and museums if you plan to walk.

## Al Khan and Al Heera: for a beach base

Sharjah has a Gulf coastline, and two areas suit travellers who want sand close by.

### Al Khan

Al Khan Beach is in the Al Khan neighbourhood close to Sharjah city, with a [dedicated swimming area, water sports and showers and changing rooms](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/al-khan-beach/). The Sharjah Maritime Museum and Sharjah Aquarium are a short walk away. It is also the part of Sharjah nearest to Dubai, which helps if you want to split your time.

### Al Heera

Further north, Al Heera Beach is [the longest beach in the city](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/al-heera-beach/), with a jogging track, bicycle track, sports fields, children's play areas and a skate park, plus places to eat.

**Best for:** families, active travellers, longer stays.
**Think about:** you will need taxis to reach the heritage area. As everywhere in the UAE, the Foreign Office reminds visitors that [swimwear should only be worn on beaches or at swimming pools](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security), so cover up when you leave the sand.

## Staying in Sharjah to visit Dubai

Many visitors look at Sharjah because it is next door to Dubai. It can work, especially if you want a quieter base, but the drive between the two cities can be slow at busy times. If you will be in Dubai most days, weigh the commute honestly against the calmer evenings. Our [Sharjah day trip from Dubai](/stories/sharjah-day-trip-from-dubai/) guide looks at it from the other direction.

## Further afield: the east coast and the desert

Sharjah is not only a city. The emirate also has a coastline on the Gulf of Oman, with seaside towns at [Khorfakkan and Kalba](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/), where Visit Sharjah highlights scuba diving, kayaking, snorkelling, boat trips to spot dolphins and the mangroves of Kalba. A night or two here, with mountains behind and the sea in front, is a very different experience from the city. See our guide to the [Khorfakkan](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) area for more.

Inland, Mleiha is home to an archaeological centre and dunes, and Visit Sharjah says visitors love to [spend a night under the stars](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/adventure/mleiha-national-park/) there, with the centre able to advise on camping and glamping.

## Booking a hotel in Sharjah

The UAE government portal lets you [search for and book a hotel, hotel apartment or resort in Sharjah](https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/visiting-and-exploring-the-uae/Arrival-and-accommodation/where-to-stay-in-the-uae) and the other emirates, and lists the taxes and charges that hotels across the UAE may add, such as service charges and municipality fees. Check what your quoted rate includes.

A few practical checks before you book:

- **Location against your plans.** Mark the museums, souks and beaches you want on a map, then choose a hotel between them.
- **Ramadan timing.** During the holy month, hotel restaurants may serve differently in daylight hours. The Foreign Office notes that hotels serving food in fasting hours [may separate you from Muslim guests, for example with screens](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security).
- **Dress.** The same advice asks visitors to dress modestly in public areas, with shoulders and knees covered for women, which is sensible to follow anywhere in Sharjah.
- **Families.** Ask about connecting rooms, cots and pools with shade.

## Quick answers

**What is the best area to stay in Sharjah?** For culture and walking, the corniche between the Heart of Sharjah and Khalid Lagoon. For the beach, Al Khan or Al Heera.

**Can you drink alcohol in Sharjah hotels?** No. The UK Foreign Office says drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah.

**Is Sharjah good for families?** Yes. Al Heera Beach has play areas, bicycle and jogging tracks, and Al Khan is close to the Maritime Museum and Aquarium.

**Can you stay on Sharjah's east coast?** Yes. Khorfakkan and Kalba are Sharjah's seaside towns on the Gulf of Oman, with diving, kayaking, boat trips and mangroves nearby.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which area of Sharjah best suits the museums and souks we want to see, and can we walk between them?
- How long should we allow for transfers to Dubai at the times we would travel?
- Can you arrange a guided visit to Al Noor Mosque or the Heart of Sharjah museums during our stay?
- Is a night on the east coast at Khorfakkan or Kalba realistic within our itinerary?
- How will Ramadan or any public holiday during our dates affect restaurants and opening times near our hotel?
