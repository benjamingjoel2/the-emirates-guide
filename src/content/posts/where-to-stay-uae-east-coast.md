On the east coast, the sun rises out of the sea. You open the curtains to a pale gold horizon, fishing boats heading out across the bay, and a wall of bare brown mountains behind the town catching the first light. It is the opposite of the Gulf side's sunsets, and for many visitors it becomes the reason to come back.

The UAE's east coast faces the Gulf of Oman, with the Hajar Mountains pressing close to the shore. It is shared between two emirates: Fujairah, and Sharjah's eastern towns of Khorfakkan and Kalba. That split matters more than you might think when choosing where to stay.

The quick answer on where to stay on the UAE east coast: pick Khorfakkan for a lively town beach and mountain walks, Fujairah city for forts, transport and a central base, the coast north towards Dibba for a quiet resort stay, and Kalba for mangroves and birdlife. Two or three nights lets you see most of it.

## Getting your bearings on the east coast

Fujairah's tourism authority describes the emirate as sitting [between the Hajar Mountains and the Indian Ocean](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-Overview.html), overlooking the Gulf of Oman, and lists Dibba, Murbeh, Qidfa, Al Bidiyah and Masafi among its main towns and villages. Sharjah's share of the coast includes [the seaside towns of Khorfakkan and Kalba](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/regions/east-coast/), which Visit Sharjah says are a short drive from Sharjah city.

On the map the two emirates interlock, so you may cross between them several times on one drive along the coast without noticing. Your hotel's emirate decides some of the local rules, which we come to below.

## Khorfakkan: a town beach with mountains behind

Khorfakkan curls around a bay, with a [crescent-shaped beach lined with palm trees](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/khorfakkan-beach/) that Visit Sharjah calls one of the emirate's most popular weekend getaways. The tourism board lists fishing, diving, parasailing, kayaking and boat and jet ski rentals from huts near the centre of the corniche, plus snorkelling near Shark Island, a short boat ride away. It also mentions waking to a sunrise over the water, which is the east coast in a sentence.

Above the town, a marked mountain path leads to Al Rabi Tower, built as part of a [network of towers that defended the city](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/heritage/al-rabi-tower/), with views over the mountains and beaches.

**Best for:** families, walkers, anyone who wants a town with a promenade, cafes and things to do in the evening.

**Know before you go:** Khorfakkan is part of Sharjah, and the UK Foreign Office says [drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal in the Emirate of Sharjah](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security). That applies here and in Kalba, not only in Sharjah city. See our [Khorfakkan guide](/stories/khorfakkan-guide/) for more on the town.

## Fujairah city: forts and a central base

Fujairah city sits roughly in the middle of the coast, which makes it a practical base if you want to drive north and south on different days. Fujairah Fort stands [on a high hill in the old Fujairah area](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-MuseumAndForts.html), overlooking the city, and the tourism authority describes its unusual design of round towers and a square tower around a central hall.

Getting here is straightforward. The authority says the Sheikh Khalifa road [connects Fujairah with Sharjah and Dubai](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-GettingThere.html), and that the emirate has its own international airport.

**Best for:** history lovers, road-trippers, business-and-leisure trips.

**Think about:** a city hotel may be a drive from the best beaches, so check where the sand is before you book.

## North towards Dibba: for a quiet resort stay

North of Fujairah city, the coast becomes quieter, with beach resorts, small villages and the mountains close behind. This is the stretch to choose if your plan is to unwind by the sea for a few days.

It also puts you close to Al Bidya Mosque, which Fujairah's tourism authority calls [the oldest mosque in the United Arab Emirates](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-PlacesOfWorship.html), on the road to Dibba. Our [Al Bidya Mosque guide](/stories/al-bidya-mosque-guide/) explains how to visit respectfully.

Water sports are the main activity, and the tourism authority notes that [most hotels can arrange the water sport of your choice](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-SportsAndCruises.html), from snorkelling and diving to kayaking and parasailing.

**Best for:** couples, snorkellers and divers, anyone after a slower pace.

**Think about:** you will need a car or transfers to explore beyond your resort.

## Kalba: mangroves, birds and a long corniche

At the southern end of the coast, Kalba is [the southernmost inhabited area of Sharjah](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/beaches/kalba-beach-corniche/) before the border with Oman. Its corniche has shaded benches facing the sea and a jogging track, and at the southern end the coast and mangroves form part of the Al Hefaiyah Conservation Area, an important nesting site for hawksbill turtles.

The Khor Kalba Mangrove Centre sits among [the oldest mangrove forest in the UAE](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/nature-and-wildlife/khor-kalba-mangrove-centre/), where visitors can look for birds such as the rare Arabian collared kingfisher.

**Best for:** nature lovers, birdwatchers, families who like quieter beaches.

**Think about:** it is the most local-feeling of the four bases, with fewer international hotels.

## Sea safety and seasons

The east coast is all about the water, so the safety basics matter.

- **Swim only where it is safe.** The Foreign Office warns that [rip currents can occur at any beach](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/united-arab-emirates/safety-and-security) and advises swimming only from approved beaches with lifeguards and flags.
- **Check the boat.** It also says the safety of tourist boats may not be up to UK standards, and to make sure life jackets are available for everyone.
- **Diving needs a certificate.** Visit Sharjah says [taster sessions do not count](https://www.visitsharjah.com/en/activities/sports/diving-in-khorfakkan/) and many dive operations will ask to see your diving certificate.
- **Watch the weather.** Heavy rain can cause flooding in the UAE, and the Foreign Office recommends checking forecasts from the National Centre of Meteorology.

Fujairah's tourism authority describes summer, from [May to September](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-Overview.html), as hot and sometimes humid, with very pleasant weather in winter. For wadi walks, it notes that the dry beds of [Wadi Ham can turn into a rushing stream when it rains](https://fujairahtourism.ae/index_action-NaturalSites.html), another reason to watch the forecast. Our [snorkelling and diving guide](/stories/snorkelling-diving-uae-east-coast/) has more on the underwater side.

## Combining bases on a road trip

With two or three nights, you can split your stay: one base in the north for the beach, and one in Khorfakkan or Fujairah city for history and town life. Kalba fits well as a day out from Fujairah city. Our [east coast road trip](/stories/uae-east-coast-road-trip/) lays out a route, and the [Fujairah destination page](/destinations/fujairah/) gathers everything on the emirate.

## Quick answers

**Which town is best to stay in on the UAE east coast?** Khorfakkan suits families and walkers, Fujairah city suits road-trippers and history lovers, and the coast north towards Dibba suits a quiet beach stay.

**Can you drink alcohol in Khorfakkan or Kalba?** No. Both are part of Sharjah, where the UK Foreign Office says drinking or possessing alcohol is illegal.

**When is the best time to visit the east coast?** Fujairah's tourism authority says winter weather is very pleasant, while summer, from May to September, is hot and sometimes humid.

**Do I need a certificate to dive in Khorfakkan?** Visit Sharjah says many dive operations require a diving certificate, and taster sessions do not count.

## Questions to ask your operator

- Which emirate is our hotel in, and how does that affect what is available at the hotel?
- Which beaches near our base have lifeguards and flags, and when is the sea usually calmest?
- Do your boats carry life jackets for every passenger, including children?
- Can you arrange snorkelling or diving to suit our experience, and what certification do you need to see?
- How long are the drives between Khorfakkan, Fujairah city, Dibba and Kalba at the times we would travel?
- What is your plan if rain or rough seas cancel a boat trip or wadi walk?
